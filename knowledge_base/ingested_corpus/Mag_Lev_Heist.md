# Mag_Lev_Heist.pdf

Overview
This supplement is a lightweight heist system meant to be run on its own as a one-shot. It consists
of the system rules, 5 Ready Made Player Characters, 4 Races, 3 Classes, 5 Locations, 2 NPCs,
world lore and a heist. The world, the characters, and the lore are meant to be used in a variety of
systems. I've added some suggestions throughout for ways to use them in a system-neutral way.

World and Setting
The planet of Vexil is a desolate wasteland, largely uninhabited outside of its singular sprawling
city of Sovix. Founded by and home to the Intergalactic Committee of Experimental Medicine, or
ICEM for short, this towering metropolis is the seat of all medical research in the galaxy, and the
center of ICEM power. And where there's power, there's profit.
High above the filth ridden alleys and the musty stench of the streets runs a feat of modern
engineering: The ICEM Maglev Commuter and Cargo train. Using magnets set into various
rooftops of the committee-owned skyscrapers, this train runs at an astonishing speed between
various offices, departing from their high security MedLab and ending within the loading docks in
their Biotech R&D facility.
Rumors abound on what the central carriages contain that warrant the accompanying drones,
soldiers, and scanners. Could it be money? Drugs? Coveted ICEM weapons grade body mods?
Well your fixer, AuntI, has a hunch. And a way for your crew to find out.
This work contains themes of futuristic technology, body modification, and a touch of western
inspired gunslingers. Some of my other works are designed to fit into this thematically, such as
Varth's Veingud Vaxis and Fungal Scavengers. Both are available for free on both Itch.io and
DriveThruRPG. Not necessary, but would work well for a GM trying to build out the world more.
The System
This system uses 2d6, with an optional 3d6 for the Edge mechanic. When a player attempts an
action that is uncertain or risky, the GM can call for a roll. The player would then roll 2d6, or 3d6
with Edge, to determine the outcome. On contested rolls, the GM and the player both roll 2d6. The
higher result succeeds and a tie results in a partial success for both sides. If no roll is called for, the
action automatically succeeds.
9+ Clear Success: Player or GM accomplishes their action as intended.
5-8 Partial Success: Player accomplishes their task but incurs a Penalty. Player can choose their
penalty from the Penalty Table.
2-4 Fail: Player fails their task and incurs a Penalty. GM chooses the penalty.
There are three core mechanics in this game, dials that can be turned with each action the player
takes. Envision it as a pressure system, where each action raises or lowers the overall pressure
during the mission. These core mechanics are Alarm, Time and Heat.


Alarm
The environmental pressure. Every action has the chance to raise the alarm level, resulting in
increased hostility in the environment. This can come in the form of
Alarm Levels: there are six alarm levels, each with their own flavor. At different points in the
mission, the Alarm level may shift without player input. I've outlined the reasons behind alarm
levels raising later in the supplement, along with guides for how to manage alarm levels as a GM.
Time
Time resource is a pool, with each Exchange costing a baseline of 1 unit of time. Unless otherwise
specified, each Exchange costs 1 Time, with each Exchange allowing for 1 action per character.
Different mission starts have a different total time given to complete the objective. These time
totals are listed in the Locations section.
Before the mission start, roll 1d6. On an even number, add the roll to the total Time pool. On odd,
subtract 1 from the Time pool.
If Time reaches 0, the mission does not automatically fail. Instead, each Exchange raises Alarm or
gains another penalty at the GM's discretion. Penalties can include things such as additional
reinforcements, increased security, or restricted movement.
When In Doubt
Raise Alarm to apply environmental pressure, raise Heat to apply pressure to a specific
character, and spend Time to move the mission forward.
New obstacles appearing
Existing obstacles growing in difficulty
Restricted movement
Options closing

Note that this is NOT damage. It is a way to mutate the terrain in a way to increase the difficulty
for the players and use up one of the other resources: Time.


Heat
Personal threat focus. Each player has their own Heat dial, which raises and lowers depending on
the actions of the player. This is how much attention the player draws to themselves during
infiltrations and combat. When your Heat is high, enemies begin to prioritize you over other
players.
Heat Levels:
0 Ghosted - You're beneath notice.
Enemies will not target you unless you are the only visible threat.
You can move freely through contested spaces.
1 Small Fry - You're noticed, but not worth enemy resources.
Enemies will target you if you are adjacent or obviously interfering.
Moving through contested spaces requires a roll. You have Edge with this roll.
2 Target (Default) - You're noticed by the enemy.
Enemies will engage you if they can do so safely. For example, from a distance, behind
cover, etc.
Moving through contested spaces requires a roll.
3 Threat - You become an active threat.
Enemies prioritize you over lower Heat targets.
Moving through contested spaces requires a roll.
Only a target with Equal or Higher Heat can pull attention from you.
4 Hunted - You're a wanted threat.
Enemies will continue to target you even if you break line of sight.
You draw coordinated actions, such as Flanking, Focused Fire, Target Locking.
You cannot move through contested spaces without risking penalty.
5 Deadly - You're now the priority.
Enemies will target you unless another target with a Heat of 6 is within their sight.
Enemies can move freely through contested spaces to get to you.
You cannot move through contested spaces without incurring penalty.
Lowering Heat requires Time or Specials.
6 Kill Command - You become marked for immediate termination.
Enemies will ignore all threats to kill you.
Enemy attacks gain Edge against you.
Heat cannot be lowered using Time, only Specials.
Cannot move through contested spaces.


Penalty
A penalty is incurred when a player performs a risky or failed action. Penalties should feel like an
increase to the overall pressure of the mission without resulting in a total loss or defeat.
When a Penalty is incurred, choose from the options below or roll on the table.

Edge - A condition gained at the discretion of the GM or from special items/abilities. Allows the
player or GM to roll 3d6 and take the higher two. Edge can apply to any roll and does not stack.
Other Terms
Contested Spaces - Spaces which are currently occupied, defended or otherwise monitored by an
Enemy.
Units - I recommend using hex units with each hex being approximately the size of a person. Some
enemies and items take up multiple hexes of space. Unless otherwise specified, every player
character, NPC, and enemy unit is 1 hex worth of space.
Exchange - Round of actions taken by both players and GM.
Scene - Individual sections of the mission.
Conditions - A negative status applied to a player, NPC or enemy. These commonly include
Blinded, Stunned, Knocked Down, Flanked, Poisoned, and Burned.


Combat and HP
HP is meant to be a secondary system, not the primary focus. That being said, lasers are only
scary if they hurt so I've added a simple HP and Combat system for when things go south.
Each Class and Enemy has a set HP listed with them, along with a selection of weapons to use
during combat. On a Clear Success, any attack does the full damage specified from the weapon
type. On a Partial Success, the attack does half damage and gains a Penalty. On a Fail, no
damage is done and gains a Penalty.
Movement
Each class has an amount of units they can move before it costs Time. Some classes, like the
Gunslinger, are specifically designed to move without costing Time as part of their design. The
Armed Turrets are stationary Enemies and thus cannot move.
Mission Brief
The Client
Fixer AuntI contacts your crew with a lucrative offer. A client wants a crate of materials lifted from
ICEM Maglev Cargo Cart. A high risk and an even higher reward, the offer comes with a bonus of
contributing to an underground movement working against the Committee. Understandably, the
client requests to remain anonymous.
The crew must choose an infiltration point, locate the cargo container, plan an extraction, and
escape with the cargo to obtain the reward. The client was clear that the only thing that matters is
that the cargo is extracted undamaged. Stealing, fighting, damaging anything else is acceptable as
long as the full case of cargo is delivered.

Classes and Races
The Conduits
The forgotten caretakers of Vexil herself.
Origins
The Conduits are a race of beings formed from the planets own soil to be guardians and
cultivators. Across the span of their life, each Conduit develops a network of metallic lines and
symbols across their bodies, allowing them to interface with the planet directly and eventually other
technologies. Each individual develops a unique pattern, as the lines form and adapt to match
skills, habits, and survival needs.
Once a peaceful and thriving culture, they were eventually discovered by the Intergalactic
Committee for Experimental Medicine and promptly enslaved as experiment subjects. Stripped of
autonomy, the majority were plugged into the living motherboard the capital was built upon. Others
were used in the Committees experiments to replicate the technology, resulting in the ink used to
create the Interfacers (see Classes).
Ties to the Crew
Quincy was part of a team of researchers who discovered how to extract the “ink” and is
contributed with the creation of the first Interfacer, Rez. It wasn't until their twenties when the
recessive trait of the Conduits began manifesting itself, leading to their defection from the
Committee out of fear of becoming another piece of the Motherboard.

Culture
For most of their history, the Conduits didn't have machines in the traditional sense instead
interfacing directly with the planet. After the collapse brought on by the Committee, the Conduits
faded from history and are now largely forgotten. The few that persist freely live lives plagued by
paranoia, guilt, and a yearning for their lost history.
Soulforged
The unnatural side of Evolution.
Origins
The Soulforged are a race of ritualistic inventors with a unique and extremely taboo history of
imbuing their souls into machines. Over the course of a Soulforged's life, they replace each body
part with an invention of their own making, slowly transforming themselves from organic lifeforms
to inorganic machines. The last piece to be transformed is the brain, where upon reaching
adulthood a Soulforge transplants the remainder of their soul and consciousness completely into
something like a CPU.
Ties to the Crew
Gears, who was orphaned at a young age, grew up with a knack for inventing and no knowledge of
her origins. Given of her lack of training and guidance, she has only made one modification to her
body: Clamps. The robotic companion infused itself into her spine, joining with her soul splitting it
in half. As a unprecedented side effect, Clamps gained sentience for itself and is considered a
living being.
Physiology
Racial Traits
Varying degrees of cyborgification, with some of the newer generations deciding to opt for a
sleeker, more mainstream look. Older generations favor mechanical appearance.
Overtime, the Soulforged's soul begins to decay. Most choose to shut down before this
happens. A select few, however, try to outlast their parts. These are hunted and shutdown by
their brethren.
Throughout the transformation, Soulforged beings begin to supplement and eventually replace
their diets from organic material to power sources. Power sources are largely determined by
the individuals lineage.
Cyborgification - Once per Mission - Start play with one soul-bound prosthetic. For example,
iron lung that can filter toxins, ocular lens that can see heat signatures, or a voice box that can
mimic sounds.


Culture
Among the Soulforged are those known as Priestsmith, beings who forgo the augmentations in
order to care for the rest of the race. They can heal others by temporarily 'patching' an injured
Soulforged with a portion of their own soul. This hastens recovery, but the individual must return
the Priestsmith's soul or their augmentation will begin to rapidly decay.

We are Starfall, caretakers of the Great Expanse.

Once a peaceful, spacefaring race now scattered amongst the surface of Vexil. Without the aid of

Culture
