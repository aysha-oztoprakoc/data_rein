Sinless is a cyber-sorcery tabletop role-playing 
game. You create a special operator, outside 
the system, without a System Identification 
Number known as Sinless. You develop a 
brand to acquire resources and territory. You 
perform shadow operations to raise 1 billion 
zuzu’s, a cryptocurrency based on a posh dog. 
They create their own enclave to make the 
world a better place. It supports long sandbox 
campaigns in sessions of 90-180 minutes long. 
A sample character sheet is included, and 
the core rules contain both pre-generated 
characters and rules for making your own.
Sinless rules are descriptive and not prescriptive. 
They provide a structure and options your table 
can change to your taste.
Your gamemaster, who we call an Agonarch, will 
design several operations. These operations are 
employment opportunities for your brand—Your 
group of Sinless. 
You can use your skills to find out who is the 
corporation and person who is funding the 
job, find out the specifics of the job contract, 
and negotiate for extra pay. 
Preparation
Players gather information and plan their assault. 
They can use their skills and assets to discover 
the map, locations of loot, cameras, the 
types of opponents, home addresses, vices, 
and more in order to develop an approach 
to the operation. They also have options including 
infiltrating the site, planting explosives or 
other items and more. 
Infiltration
This is the most familiar type of gameplay, 
with a battlemat and figures. But instead of 
exploring and fighting, you’re sneaking into 
hostile territory. Every round when you 
move, you make a shadow check to avoid 
raising alert. Deckers and other digital specialists 
can affect alert levels making you more 
stealthy. As you trigger certain alert levels, 
things occur on site that make it more difficult 
to remain hidden.
Once alert is capped or you are discovered 
Heat is triggered. Like many crime games it 
indicates the severity of the conflict. Heat will 
rise as on-site opposition engages with you, 
and if something blows up or someone dies, 
maximum heat is triggered and High Threat 
Response teams (HTR or heater teams) ar­
rive in 2d6 rounds. Players will know three 
rounds before they arrive. If any players are 
still one site when HTR arrives, they are lost.
Escape
The third phase of operations is escape in a 
vehicle. This is much like a normal combat, 
except the action is centered around your 
moving vehicle. This escape uses the same 
rules for conflict that the infiltration phase 
uses, with additional options. If your vehicle 
is wrecked, you have to fight your way free, 
alternately you can complete a set number 
of legs successfully and escape. 
Sector play
Sector play covers downtime and activities 
between operations. When players return 
from the mission they have a limited number 
of actions they can take during a sector turn. 
This involves things like fencing loot, im­
proving their ghost rating and hiring assets 
that are in town.
But you can’t get rich or change the system 
working for other people. So soon your play­
ers will engage in operations to take over 
various resources that allow them to expand 
their power and influence.

The game alternates between the operation phase and the sector phase. The operation phase is 
where players complete a contract for an employer, or employ those same skills to advance the 
interests of the brand. The sector phase is used for downtime and brand domain activities. Each 
phase abstracts the passage of time, instead of tracking time day by day. 
Operations are the core component of play in Sinless. They represent a contract to complete a 
task, such as extraction or assassination. After acquiring an operation, the reconnaissance and 
preparation framework provides an opportunity to use assets and develop a plan of attack.	

Once the operation begins, players take steps to accomplish their goals, and the Agonarch models 
the world’s response to their actions utilizing the game rules. This continues until the players are 
successful, all dead, or Heater (High Threat Response) units arrive which ends the operation, and 
the survival of anyone who didn’t escape comes down to a die roll.	

Operations consist of espionage, extractions, demolitions, technology and historical artifact retrieval 
delves, investigations, opportunities for profit from theft, and other nefarious activities.

During conflict players draw dice from pools to 
attack and defend.
Your skill + gear feature represents the limit of 
dice you may draw from a pool for a test. Bonus 
dice are free. Penalty dice lower the limit.

Your dice refresh at the start of the round. If you do not 
have dice in your pool, you cannot attempt the skill 
or defend. Pools are only used during conflict.
During conflict, players can take actions. 
All players get two simple actions and a reflex action.
Complex actions require two simple actions.
Certain special features grant exploit actions. 
Exploit actions are identical to simple actions, but can only be spent on their stated purpose.
A powerful vehicle control rig grants exploit actions that can only be used to control vehicles and drones. The rigger will 
still get their normal simple actions, which they can use for anything (including controlling drones). The exploit actions 
allow him to spend additional actions during his turn, as long as they are used for controlling a vehicle or drone.

Brand Development
Brands grow and develop.
As they take over resources, their Market Capital (MC) rises. 
When the Brand reaches 10 Market Capital, they can select a third brand trait.
When the Brand reaches 100 Market Capital, they can select a final, fourth brand trait.
Market Capital is separate from cash; it’s legal operating capital. 
You can turn banked market capital into cash at a rate of 1 MC per 5,000 Zuzus. 
You can turn cash into banked market capital at a rate of 20,000 Zuzus for 1 MC.
The number of actions you can take in a sector phase is also determined by your Brand size.
At 10 MC you can take a fourth sector action, and at 100, you can take a fifth.
Your total Market Capital also determines how many assets you can support.
You do not pay your assets upkeep. Assets need a certain amount of infrastructure and support. The upkeep represents 
the size of the brand's market cap necessary to provide for them. If you have more asset upkeep than upkeep available, 
they will leave,  moving on, until your Market Capital can support all that are employed. Otherwise they work for you, at no 
ongoing cost.
Certain resources, boons, and arcanatech can raise your brands statistics.

Resources
Every resource has a condition track.
Resources can be built, destroyed, and upgraded (CRB p. 222) 
during the sector turn.
Each box costs 10 banked MC to construct. Using the upgrade 
action allows constructing a maximum of six boxes per sector turn. 
At certain thresholds the resource improves its class, from E-D-C-B-A. 
This improves both the quality of the sector, along with increasing 
both upkeep and profit (usually) and bonus effects. 
See Sinless CRB pp. 225-227 for details on each resource.
Resources can be damaged and influenced. Damage is 
tracked with X’s from the left, and influence is tracked with 
/'s from the right.
When a resource is completely damaged, completely 
influenced, or any combination thereof, it changes 
ownership and becomes part of the players brand as a 
controlled resource.
The brand then gets all benefits from the resource. 
Resources with damage or unpaid upkeep do not function.
Players have a great variety of actions their brands can engage in during a sector turn. (see CRB pp. 222–223.)
They can use their brand statistics to apply damage and influence to local resource. 	

They can demolish, repair, or upgrade owned resources.
Brands can choose to do crime to raise funds, use a persuadertron sweep to brainwash people into your 
cults/gang/cabal, and use ragebait media to exploit them for cash. 
It also requires a sector action to increase your ghost rating, purchase items with a rarity value, transfer 
funds between cash and banked market cap, explore other sectors, and purchase an asset or activate an asset's 
sector action.
Purchasing an item also requires either a relevant resource or asset. 
No way to get an illegal sniper rifle unless you make them or know a guy.

to get your limit.
•	Total any bonus dice from firing mode, 
bright light, point-blank range, etc. 
•	Total any penalty dice from range, cover, 
situations, and lighting.
•	Apply the modifiers. If there are more penalty 
dice than bonus dice, once the bonus dice are 
eliminated, penalty dice lower the limit.

Brawn Pool
The Brawn pool equals a character’s Strength 
+ ½ Body + ¼ Willpower.
Melee Weapons covers the use of hand-to-
hand weapons (swords, knives, etc.).
Unarmed Combat covers the use of martial 
combat. Anyone may do ½ their Strength in 
damage to the stun condition track on a 
successful Unarmed Combat roll.
Martial Arts* is a replacement skill for un­
armed combat. Martial Arts costs 2 skill 
points per rank. Players select a martial art 
style that replaces normal unarmed attacks 
(see p. 72). At each rank, they gain a martial 
ability. Your Martial Arts skill can never be 
higher than your Unarmed Combat skill.
Characters with Martial Arts may do ½ their 
Strength to a target’s physical condition track, or 
their full Strength to a target’s stun condition 
track.
Cybertechtronic Combat (Cyber Combat)* 
covers the use of implanted cybertechtron­
ics in melee combat, including razors, spurs, 
as well as the monofilament whip.
Throwing Weapons covers the use of throw­
ing weapons. Throwing an item (not a weap­
on) will do ¼ your Strength in damage to the 
stun condition track.
Athletics Jump 1 m (.5”) from a standing start, 
or 2 m (1”) from a running start. You can also 
jump vertically, every success letting you 
jump 1 m (.5”).	

To climb, every success on an Athletics test 
allows you to move 2 m (1”) on any vertical 
surface.	

To swim, your movement is 2 m (1”) + 2 m (1”) 
per Athletics test success.

Operational Procedure
Note that the information given to the play­
ers is just the name of the operation, and a 
brief description of the work (assault, theft, ex­
tractions, et. al. q.v.) 
The players may make a Reconnaissance 
test to determine the etiquette hiring (Crimi­
nal, Aristocracy) and a Coercion test to deter­
mine the brand or individual who is hiring for 
the operation. This costs no time or resources to 
do, but only one attempt by a character in the 
brand can be made for each roll.
This can be done for each available operation. 
Once the players agree on an operation, 
they go to the meet with the Mr. Johnson. 
Note that the specifics of getting the meet, the 
safety of the meet, not getting tailed, etc. its all 
obviated. Choose a site in the sector for the

normal operating procedure for brands and

this communication channel. 
This type of safety is inherent in the game.

STARTING SINLESS


If there is a of twist involving a mission 
(~16%), it is always possible to find out about 
it with an espionage dossier during opera­
tion preparation  
Sinless isn't a game about trying to protect 
yourself from every possible eventuality. It is 
explicitly about the players choosing an oper­
ation, researching it, completing it, then 
using that success to upgrade and ex­
pand brand influence, ultimately, making 
a choice about how they change communi­
ties. Events outside of this loop are not re­
lated to the game. During the meet negotia­
tion, taking or refusing the contract are the only 
choices. 
Until the contract is signed, players can back 
out of the mission. Once they accept a con­
tract, if they refuse to complete it, their Kis­
met Pool is reduced by one. Note that this 
penalty only applies to bad faith attempts. 
Players are given agency during the mission 
to make choices, such as deciding to de­
stroy research data instead of recovering it if 
they deem it too dangerous. This is not a  fail­
ure—the penalty applies for taking an opera­
tion and then refusing to attempt it.
