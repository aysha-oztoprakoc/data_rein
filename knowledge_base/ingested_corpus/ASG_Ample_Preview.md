So, you got this game and want to know what it’s all about, right? Well, I’m not

almost every genre or setting you can think of. You won’t find information about X

making characters, resolving conflicts and a framework for you to hang a setting 
onto with minimal fuss. It’s flexible, small, and really, really easy to play.

love to see anything you come up with and would like to share. All we ask is that

Character Generation
Character Concept
The Player comes up with a sentence or two to describe the character, such as 
their demeanour or bearing, something about their background or about who they 
are now. This can be what drives them, or what complicates their life or simply a 
description of who they appear to be.
Archetype
The Player chooses an Archetype, which defines how they operate within the game 
world. The GM is encouraged to come up with custom Archetypes that are themed 
to their own setting, or to use one of the basic ones provided. Each Archetype 
grants a bonus Skill point in one or more Skills.
Attributes
Players choose one Attribute as a Forte and one Attribute as a Weakness. The 
Forte has a value of 8 (for d8s) and Weakness has value of 4 (for d4s). The other 
two Attributes have a value of 6 (for d6).
Attributes are listed as a code called a ‘SCIPcode’, pronounced as skipcode. 
A SCIPcode of 4686 for example means a Weakness of Strength, Forte of 
Intelligence, while Cunning and Personality are normal. Each number denotes the 
type of die associated with Skill checks that use the Attribute. See Using Skills 
later for more info.
SCIP = Strength, Cunning, Intelligence and Personality
Strength – The physical prowess of a character, both how strong and how tough 
they are. 
Cunning – The agility and dexterity of a character, in both body and mind. 
Intelligence – The depth and breadth of a character’s Mind, both their intuitive 
and deductive powers.
Personality – The charisma and manipulative powers of a character, both how 
likeable and influential a person is. A character’s strength of will.
Skills
Players have 5 points to distribute among their Skills. Archetypes have favoured 
Skills, which they may use Bumps on during play
Bumps
A starting character begins with 1 Bump, and gains more during play. These Bumps 
may be used on Skills rolls when needed. A character starts each game session 
with a minimum of 1 Bump, and may keep any unused Bumps from previous 
sessions. A character starting a session with no Bumps, instead gains 1 Bump. 
These Bump ‘tokens’ may be purchased using XP. See Spending XP later in the 
rules for more info, and Using Skills for more for information about using Bumps.

Advantages
Players begin with 3 points in Advantages. These are quirks and features that flesh 
out both a background and special powers that belong to each character. GMs are 
encouraged to invent their own Advantages and make them thematically relevant 
to their settings, as well as disallowing any of the provided Advantages.
Equipment & Notes
Depending on the setting, different things can go into this section. Most commonly 
it is where players list the weapons, armour and other equipment their characters 
have but it can also list information such as spells known or quest/mission specific 
things. See Cash, Wealth & Currency for more information.
Archetypes
An Archetype is kind of like a class in other RPGs, and kind of like a personality at 
the same time. A character can be The Scholar while still referring to themselves 
as a Warrior, it’s just that Academics and Mechanics appeals to them more, and 
they have a drive to discover lore. An Archetype does not define their profession, 
but it does define their passions and aptitudes.
The Warrior
This Archetype views the world as a series of conflicts and battles. They measure 
their success through the number of victories they hold on the battlefield. Glory 
is something that should be strived for, through the defeat of their adversaries, 
although this is not the only virtue of importance to them. More than just a brute, 
The Warrior seeks to perfect their body and skills, often showing great discipline 
in these areas. The Warrior Archetype gains +1 Skill on Combat Skill and 
Athletics. Once per game session, a single free Bump may be used on these 
Skills.
The Rogue
The Rogue Archetypes prefers the indirect approach to conflict. Through guile 
and careful planning, The Rogue seeks to overcome adversity and get rich in 
the process. Although they may have a heart of gold, having an ACTUAL golden 
heart would be pretty neat too, and The Rogue can always be counted on to not 
always be counted on. Whether it's jewels, information or a maiden's favour that 
The Rogue seeks, they pursue their passion with craftiness and precision. The 
Rogue Archetype gains +1 Skill on Stealth and Stunts. Once per game session, 
a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.
The Hero
The Hero can't help but put them self in harm’s way, so that others may be spared 
certain doom/debts/bad reputation/indigestion. It is in their nature to right wrongs, 
defend the weak and be a general pain in the arse of villains. They make fine leaders, 
excellent friends and strong allies. Especially for The Rogue. They are in their 
element taking point in an assault, as much as they are in a courtroom or audience 
chamber for the King. The Hero Archetype gains +1 Skill on Communication 
and Combat Skill. Once per game session, a single free Bump may be used on 
these Skills.

The Scholar
The Scholar understands that knowledge is power, and that fore-warned is fore-
armed. Whether for the betterment of life, or for its own sake, the pursuit of 
knowledge takes a special kind of person to perform, and The Scholar is it. The 
magical and the mundane both have mysteries that need solving, and nothing 
drives The Scholar more than a hint of wider, yet hidden truth yet to be discovered. 
The Scholar gains +1 Skill on Academics and Mechanical. Once per game 
session, a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.
The Bard
Never let the truth get in the way of a good story, and never get between The Bard 
and his free meal. The Bard seeks experiences in order to enliven the world about 
them with tales of colour and enchantment. They seek to entertain and to inform, 
to enrich the lives of others. Often mistaken for The Rogue, The Bard can be a bit 
of a scoundrel as well, but it's all in the execution that matters. The Bard gains +1 
Skill on Communication and Stunts. Once per game session, a single free 
Bump may be used on these Skills.
The Acrobat
Honing their bodies in order to perform graceful moves and daredevil feats, The 
Acrobat is a master of getting into and out of danger. The Acrobat sees a mountain 
as a challenge, a chasm as an opportunity and a collapsing wall trap as a motivation. 
Thrill seeker and constant self-improver, The Acrobat solves problems by avoiding 
them entirely and they do so with grace. The Acrobat gains +1 Skill on Athletics 
and Stunts. Once per game session, a single free Bump may be used on these 
Skills.
The Sleuth
The disciplines of deduction and reason are the tools of The Sleuth. They have 
an uncanny knack for rooting out the truth, uncovering details unnoticed by others 
and to following a trail like a bloodhound. They are investigators and trouble 
shooters, with an eye for the unseen and an ear for the unheard, they know the 
right questions to ask and the correct light to hold to a crime scene. The Sleuth 
gains +1 Skill on Perception and Communication. Once per game session, 
a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.
The Burglar
No lock is an obstacle, no treasure is safe. The Burglar prides themselves on their 
ingenuity and daring by stealing things from people who believed they were safe. 
The bigger the boast, the richer the score, the more tempting the heist. The Burglar 
differs from The Rogue in that the challenge, the plan, is more important than 
the score. They are professional thieves who value skill and perfection as an art 
form. The Burglar gains +1 Skill on Mechanical and Stealth. Once per game 
session, a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.

The Artisan
Creation is the milk and bread of The Artisan. Anything from a work of art to the 
more practical applications of engineering excites The Artisan and drives them. 
They seek to find new ways to build things, better ways to modify and improve 
equipment and can be found in mounds of sawdust and swarf well into the long 
hours of the night. The Artisan gains +1 Skill on Mechanical and Crafting. 
Once per game session, a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.
The Inventor
A blending of The Scholar and The Artisan, The Inventor is always coming up with 
new toys and crazy ideas. Never content to tinker with the completed works, they 
cast aside finished projects eager to begin the process afresh. Always seeking 
new ideas and a chance to study the magnificent and complex, The Inventor is 
a marvellous amalgam of scientist and craftsman. The Inventor gains +1 Skill on 
Crafting and Academics. Once per game session, a single free Bump may be 
used on these Skills.
The Assassin
Whether for money, revenge or pleasure, The Assassin is death incarnate. They 
care not for fancy swordplay, witty banter or fancy flourishes; they care only for 
the kill. Masters of the shadows, they strike without warning from afar with a high 
powered rifle or from a potted plant with a needle. Efficiency of the kill is everything, 
and they strive for faster, cleaner and harder to trace methods of execution. The 
Assassin gains +1 Skill on Stealth and Combat Skill. Once per game session, 
a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.
The Guardian
A protector, body guard or watchman, The Guardian excels at spotting danger and 
moving to combat it. Ever alert for foes, The Guardian never sits with their back 
to an entrance and always has an eye on their charge. They can be counted on 
to have your back, identifying the source of the greatest danger, and suggesting 
a course of action to get to safety. The Guardian gains +1 Skill on Perception 
and Combat Skill. Once per game session, a single free Bump may be used on 
these Skills.
The Archaeologist
At home in both the library and the wilderness, The Archaeologist is the perfect field 
scientist. They gather their knowledge from dusty tombs, ancient relics and well 
equipped universities. From the laboratory to the ruins of long dead civilisations 
the Archaeologist plies their trade, a seeker of the unknown and a true adventurer. 
The Archaeologist gains +1 Skill on Academics and Athletics. Once per game 
session, a single free Bump may be used on these Skills.

Using Skills
A Skill check is performed by rolling a number of dice equal to the Skill, of a type 
equal to the Aptitude associated with the action. For example, attempting to pick a 
lock requires the use of the Mechanical Skill, and uses Cunning. If a character has 
a skill of 2 and a Cunning of 6, the character rolls 2d6. A task will have a difficulty, 
and a requirement of 'successes'. Each die that scores the difficulty is a success, 
and if enough successes are rolled the task is successful.
Example – Picking a Hard lock has a difficulty of 4 and requires 3 successes. 
If an action scores at least 1 success in a skill roll, it is classed a 'partial success' 
and if the action being attempted allows for it, another attempt may be made 
the following round to get the remaining successes. The more successes a 
character gets in a roll, the faster the action can be performed. Complex, time 
consuming tasks require many successes. If a skill check nets no successes in 
a roll, regardless of any successes scored prior to the roll, the skill check fails or 
becomes partially successful, depending on the action. Using the example above, 
if a character scores two 4s in the first roll but none in the second, they have failed 
to open the lock but may attempt the skill check again later. Retrying a skill resets 
all successes acquired previously.
If the first roll in a skill attempt fails to net any successes, the attempt is a complete 
failure and something occurs to prohibit or complicate further attempts. Using the 
same example a failure would mean either the lock jams or their pick tools snapped.
Skill roll has all successes required for action = Success
Skill roll has at least 1 success, but not all successes = Partial 
Success, keep trying
Skill roll has no successes, but scored at least 1 in a prior round 
= Partial Failure, all success rolls are lost but may attempt again
First Skill roll has no successes = Failure, may not attempt again
Using this system, a GM may rule that only a single attempt may be made on 
an action, and that gaining only a partial success is insufficient for any degree of 
success. They may also rule that a partial success is enough to succeed, but the 
action has a cost to it or the attempt is sloppy and ungraceful. Perhaps a Partial 
Success on an Stunts roll causes a Slide on the next Skill check, or Consider the 
circumstances involved in the action, and which rule should apply.

Slides 
A Slide forces a player to decrease a single die to the next lowest value; a d12 
becomes d10, d10 a d8, d8 a d6, and d6 becomes a d4. A Slide is caused by 
characters attempting an action and being disadvantaged in some way, such as 
firing a bow in strong winds. Another common source of Slides is from Armour 
in combat. If a Slide ever reduces a d4, it instead removes the die from the roll 
completely. Any die roll always has a minimum of 1 die.
Chokes 
A Choke is a penalty that ‘soaks’ successes on a Skill check. A Choke of 1, for 
example, on an Stunts Skill check means that whenever performing an action 
using the Stunts Skill the first success they score is ignored. A Choke of 2 would 
mean they ignore the first 2 successes and so on. These successes do not count 
towards the total needed for success. The most common source of a Choke is 
when skills oppose each other in a contest or conflict. 
Tips
A Tip is a bonus die granted for a Skill check. Gaining two Tips from a single source 
is called a Righteous Tip. Generally, they can come from outside assistance, 
such as Players assisting each other in tasks, but sometimes magical items or 
exceptional quality items can generate Tips. Dice gained in this way are the same 
type used by the other dice in the roll.
Critical Success
A Critical Success occurs when one of the dice in a Skill check, including attack rolls, 
scores exactly a 4 on a roll. Only one die may be counted as a Critical Success, so 
if multiple dice types roll a 4, only the lowest is counted. When a critical success 
is rolled, an additional die of the same type may be rolled in order to gain an extra 
success. Basically, it’s a Tip, but only for the type of die rolled. Only 1 Critical Success 
is allowed per roll, however if the extra die rolls a 4 as well then the player may roll 
another extra die. Each time the extra die rolls a 4, a success is recorded and another 
die is rolled until a 4 is not rolled, or a number of bonus successes is reached equal 
to half the maximum value of the die -1; 1 extra success for d4s, 2 for d6s, 3 for d8s, 
4 for d10s and 5 for d12s.
For example, Ryan is making an attack with his Pistol and gains a Bump on the 
attack. His Cunning is 6 and his Pistol is 3, so with his regular rolls he would roll 3d6 
for the attack. He gains a Bump, and rolls 2d6 and 1d8. He rolls a 3 and a 4 on the 
2d6, and a 4 on the d8. As 2 different dice scored 4, he only counts the lowest for 
the critical success and so rolls an extra d8, scoring a 5. His total roll is 3, 4, 4 and 
5 – 3 successes, with a Critical Success. If Ryan has rolled another 4 with the d6, he 
would be allowed to roll again and continue rolling every time he rolls a 4.
Opposing Skills
Sometimes, using a Skill becomes a contest. When the skill directly opposes 
another character, usually they have the option to resist the action in some way. The 
most direct example of this is during combat, but there are other times when this is 
appropriate, such as attempting to convince a character that a lie is the truth.

In situations where opposing a Skill is necessary, both parties to the attempt make 
Skill checks and whoever gains the most number of successes is the victor. A 
tie means a ‘Partial Success’ for the instigator only if they managed to score at 
least 1 success. If the instigator of an opposed Skill check gains more successes 
than the defender, and also gains enough successes for the action to succeed, 
the action goes ahead as planned. Each success by the defender essentially 
generates a Choke for the instigator, increasing the number of successes required 
to perform the action. Should a defender score no successes while opposing 
a skill, the instigator’s actions are essentially unopposed. If a defender scores 
enough successes to reduce the amount of successes by the instigator to zero or 
less, they have defended the action and the instigator fails in the task.
For example, a character is trying to climb a tree and needs to get 2 Athletics 
successes to reach the top. On the ground is another character throwing rocks 
and sticks at the character trying to make them fall. The climber makes an Athletics 
Skill check while the defender, the guy on the ground, makes an Athletics or even 
a Combat Skill check. Each success that the defender gets makes the number of 
successes needed by the climber to increase by 1. Should the guy on the ground 
get more successes than the climber, a rock or stick knocks the climber loose and 
he falls to the ground. 
On the first round, the climber scores 2 successes, but the guy on the ground 
scores 1; the climber suffers from a Choke of 1. The next round the climber once 
again scores 2 successes, however this time the guy on the ground scores 3 
successes. With a Choke of 3, even with the previous successes of the last round 
the climber is reduced to zero successes and falls. 
Difficulty
Most actions have a difficulty of 4. Gaining just 1 success is usually enough to 
perform the most basic of tasks and for actions that are more complex or time 
consuming more successes are needed.

Academics
This Skill encompasses all lore, knowledge and formal learning. It represents a 
character’s study habits as well as their retention of information. Use this skill 
whenever a character is called upon to know a fact, or apply knowledge to a 
concept in order to learn more.
Athletics
This Skill is used to describe any number of physical actions, from sports to track 
and field, climbing, hiking and jumping. It represents not only a character’s capacity 
for physical activity, but also their form and technique in performing them.
Combat Skill
This Skill represents a character’s training and expertise in different forms of 
combat. All characters begin play with 1 point in this skill, as with all skills, and 
may select 1 specific Weapon Skill to start play knowing. This is recorded on the 
character sheet.  Only individual Weapon Skills, or Styles, are improved through 
spending XP, with the base Combat Skill automatically set to the highest Weapon 
Skill value. Each specific Weapon Skill is treated as a separate Skill. For example, 
having Pistols 3 and Rifles 2 are both separate Skills and the Combat Skill is set 
to 3. Increasing your highest Weapon Skill also increases your base Combat Skill.
Communication
This Skill represents a character’s aptitude with passing information, either verbally 
or written. It shows a character’s understanding of human (or at least sapient) 
communications and language, how people interact and how to connect with 
others.
Mechanical
This Skill reflects a character’s technical aptitude, their prowess with tools and 
the practical application of mechanisms, electronics, steam power or whatever 
technology your game setting uses. It is the go – to Skill for picking locks, repairing 
starships, getting a car to start and the techniques for structuring poetry and prose.

Perception
This Skill represents how aware a character is, their attention to detail and their 
ability to notice things that stand out. Heavily tied to the concept of consciousness 
and self-awareness,  Perception is a representation of how connected a character 
is to the world around them.
Stealth
This Skill describes a character’s understanding of various techniques and concepts 
used to avoid detection. It includes moving quietly, moving between shadows, 
using camouflage and other pattern disruption devices, but it also covers the 
techniques of misdirection and hiding in plain sight. Sleight of hand, prestidigitation 
and legerdemain all fall under this Skill.
Stunts
This Skill incorporates a variety of actions, from acrobatics, gymnastics and other 
open displays of physical prowess. If a character is ever unarmed and makes an 
attack, unless they have a specific Weapon Skill incorporating unarmed attacks 
they use the lower of this Skill or Combat Skill, with the Strength or Cunning 
Attribute. This skill is also used when ever a character wishes to ‘amaze’ people 
with flair.
Crafting
As the name suggests, this Skill represents a character’s expertise in building and 
making things. Different disciplines require the use of different Attributes, but if the 
end result is the construction, brewing, forging or piecing together of something, 
then Crafting is the Skill.

Advantages
Advantages are quirks and perks that improve a character in some way, that are 
not related to Skills. They are sometimes fluidic, in that their value can rise and 
fall depending on a story rather than on expending XP. When called to make an 
Advantage check, the player simply rolls as many d6s as they have in the value 
of the Advantage. For example, when called to make a Contacts check and a 
character has a Contacts value of 2; they roll 2d6. Success is almost always 
determined by the amount of 4 or more rolled on each die. 
Advantages can be used for a number of things; They can allow characters access 
to information, places or people that help further a story along, they can grant 
abilities such as spells and magic, they can also represent special training or 
equipment. Below are a few examples of good Advantages.
Wealth
This Advantage denotes that the character has a financial benefit that they can use. 
This can be used, for example, in the place of a Communications check to bribe 
a guard, or to purchase expensive items. When called to make a Wealth check, 
success means they have access to the required amount of funds at that time, 
while a failure means they are unable to liquidate assets to fulfil the requirements. 
A Partial Success could mean that Wealth checks for the remainder of the game 
session suffer an automatic Slide, while an outright failure could mean a Choke for 
the remainder of the story. 
Contacts
This Advantage shows that the character is well connected. If a character believes 
that they might know someone that can help out the party in the area, they make 
a Contacts check. This should only be allowed if the player can tie the situation 
or area into the character’s back story or Concept. It makes sense for a character 
with a ‘Martial Arts Monk from old China’ Concept to know people in Chinatown, 
but it would be a stretch to believe they’d know a scientist that can help them with 
a nanotechnology question. 
Aid provided by Contacts should rarely be given free of charge, and should usually 
involve some form of deal, payment or convincing to get it. This Advantage only 
covers the connection to the Contact, not the nature of the relationship.
Region
This Advantage represents familiarity with a region (usually the one the characters 
are playing in). It represents knowledge of the geography, historical footnotes 
and political issues affecting the area. Streetsmarts and regional secrets are also 
covered by this Advantage. The area covered by the Region Advantage can be as 
small as a neighborhood, or as large as a region of wilderness, but must be clearly 
defined. Examples are; West Street Ghetto, The Old Fang Forrest, or New York 
City.
Region checks are made when a character wishes to recall some fact or other 
piece of information about the Region, such as a safe way to travel through the 
region, who’s in charge or where they are located within the region.

Status
This Advantage represents a character’s authority and public presence. A high 
Status might indicate nobility or aristocracy, military rank or a senior position at a law 
firm or other public office. This Advantage flows two ways; it grants the character a 
measure of authority, and an equal measure of responsibility. A character may be 
able to call on the assistance of the local police force for back up, but they’re going 
to need help with their own investigations at some point. Status checks are made 
when a character wishes to ‘pull rank’ or muster official or public assistance.
Reputation 
This Advantage measures either notoriety or fame, whether deserved or unwanted. 
Characters gain and lose reputation depending on their actions, and on the 
perception of the public. A reputation check is made when a character wants to 
take advantage of the fact that they are well known, or when they want the weight 
of their words to carry meaning. A threat is so much more potent when the victim 
knows what you are capable of, likewise a reputation of honour means they will 
know that your word is good.
Magic
This Advantage measures a characters access to the supernatural. How the magic 
manifests in the game is determined by the setting, but generally a Magic check 
is made to cast a spell, utter a curse or to otherwise activate a magical effect. 
Depending on the setting, this could represent psychic powers or alien technology 
instead of actual Magic, but the results are the same. A Magic check can be called 
when a character wishes to attempt some feat of supernatural ability, when they 
wish to know if their understanding of esoteric forces can give them more clues, 
or to sense the presence of other magical or otherwise supernatural forces and 
entities. Generally, having this Advantage requires a reference to their power in the 
character’s Concept. See Magic In Your Games later on in the rules for more 
info.
Custom Advantages 
Depending on the setting, adding Advantages to the game can add depth and 
flavour. The idea behind Advantages is that they provide a mechanic that reflects an 
advantage, a leg up so to speak, available to the character. In science fiction setting, 
for example, having an Augmented Advantage may allow a character extra abilities 
gained through cybernetic replacements and devices. A fantasy setting about leading 
a nation may have a Fortress Advantage, helping to repel invaders from the lands. 
A strategic game about leading an army or squad of troops may have a Tactics 
Advantage, measuring a unit’s effectiveness on the battlefield. 
Another use for custom Advantages is to create special effects for characters. A 
character finds a magical sword and you need a way to measure the power of the 
artefact? Make an Advantage of Magical Sword. A character has a powerful computer 
they use to hack into enemy systems? Give them the Ultra-Deck 2000 Advantage. 
The key to using Advantages is that they must be clearly defined in what they 
represent and when a check is to be made. Finally, they need a way to increase 
them, or reduce them.
