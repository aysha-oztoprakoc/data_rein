Welcome to the Storm 
What is this, anyway? 
This is not a heroic epic. This is not a rulebook-worshipping occultist’s manual.  
This is a world where blades sing, magic moans, machines swear, and gods sometimes whisper 
inside your skull. 
Fantasia Vixens™ is a dark-fantasy, techno-surreal, mythomagical roleplaying game - but most 
of all, it’s a space where character stories matter more than dice rolls. Here, the edges of reality 
are frayed. The world used to be whole - now it’s just strong. And the harder you hold onto who 
you are… the more you shape the matter around you. 
This game isn’t a caste-puppet show. It starts from your style. From the body you wear. From 
the oath you made. From the memory you forgot. From your choices - that ignite into 
consequences. 
And yes, there are big swords. And other kinds of exaggeration. 
The world has seen too much to be clean. Here, armor can be decoration or defense, desire or 
dread. Bodies - of any shape - aren’t objects, but vessels of stories. Scars, curves, motion - all 
tell a tale. 
Characters don’t represent - they shape. 
This game allows for sexuality, passion, and instinct - but it isn’t built on them. 
It’s built on who you are when the world tears off your wrist. 
And what you say - when you can’t say anything anymore. Just strike.

How do you play it? 
The system is D6-based - fast, intuitive, adaptable. The dice decide - you shape the meaning. 
Here, success isn’t everything. What matters is the price you pay for it. Because every action 
has Vice. Every character has an Oath. 
And this world - does not forget. 
Why would you play this? 
Because something lives here. Something that doesn’t smooth out.  Something that’s not classic 
fantasy, not cyberpunk, not post-apocalypse - but drinks the blood of them all. 
 
This is a world that lets you be yourself - ​
 but always asks: “Are you sure this is what you want?”

Fantasia Vixens characters aren’t just collections of numbers - they're vow-swearing survivors, 
stage-born warriors, and fledgling legends soaked in sorcery. But in this Quickstart, we're using

The Mechanics – How the World Works (or Doesn’t) 
Fantasia Vixens runs on d6 dice pools. 
Whenever your character takes an action where the outcome isn’t guaranteed, you roll a 
number of six-sided dice equal to an Attribute + an appropriate Skill. 
For example, if you have Blade 2 and Melee 1, roll 3d6. 
Each roll is judged by a target number - you succeed if at least one die meets or beats the

Surge Dice – Drawn from the Shadow 
of Vice 
Your character has a Vice - a dark obsession, 
craving, or flaw. If your action aligns with your Vice, 
you may be granted or request +1 Surge Die - an 
extra d6 added to your roll. 
But there’s a catch: If the Surge Die rolls a 1, the 
Vice backfires. Something goes wrong - maybe 
narratively, magically, or internally (the GM decides 
how). The Surge Die is like the Vice itself: tempting, 
powerful, and dangerous. 
If you want power, you’ll get it - ​
 but there will be a cost.

A short, stylish idea. Think “Drifting memory-dealer

Assign Attributes 
Distribute 10 points between the following five Core

●​ Bust (physical presence, charisma) 
●​ Blade (combat prowess, destructive power) 
●​ Booty (survival, cunning, scavenging) 
●​ Brains (intellect, strategy, knowledge) 
●​ Bitchcraft (magical aura, ritual potency) 
Then calculate:​
 Stamina = Bust + Brains (Max 8 at creation)

Character Archetype – Vicepunk 
(Subtype of ViceShade) 
For the Player: A Vicepunk isn’t a hero - 
they’re a mirror. They scratch at reality so you 
can see the lie underneath. They live in the 
cracks of society, where only rage and wit 
remain. Vice doesn’t control them - they forge 
it into a weapon. 
Their behavior is erratic, their style loud or 
deliberately too much: too colorful, too 
ragged, too sharp, too noisy. A Vicepunk lives 
as if the world has already fallen - and now 
they’re rewriting the aftermath. 
Play it like this: Break the forms: Laugh 
when others fear, insult when they flatter, ask 
questions when they command. Your Vice 
isn’t a weakness - it’s a weapon. Make it 
visible. Make it disruptive. Let it affect others. 
Use words like blades. A Vicepunk often 
crafts their comebacks from other people’s 
own sentences. 
Suggested Vice Types: Dependency (drugs, danger, adrenaline), Rebellion (pushing limits, 
breaking rules, defying order) 
Special Ability: Implosive Humor (1/scene): If you use a Surge Die on a Booty or Brains roll 
and roll a 1, the GM triggers a moment of social or emotional disarray. This could be: an 
awkward truth slipping out, sudden panic, spontaneous sympathy or hysteria, a strange 
misunderstanding that opens a new opportunity. The world is ridiculous - you just laugh at it first. 
For the GM: Vicepunks destabilize the world - not to destroy it, but to strip it bare. They’re not 
anarchists. They’re reflections. They don’t beg for the world to change - they force it to respond. 
Build scenes around them with something to subvert: A ritual that’s too rigid. A rule taken too 
seriously. An authority that’s too self-assured. 
The Vicepunk’s presence bends these things -  and in doing so, they don’t just shift the 
narrative. They hold a mirror to the world and to the other characters. 
Important: Don’t treat them as comic relief. They are a medium through which the world 
critiques itself. Society can’t look at them for too long -  but it can’t look away either.

Schools of Magic 
Cindrel: The Language of Fractures 
For the Player: Cindrel magic whispers from the folds of reality - and you understand what it’s 
saying. This school isn’t simply about teleportation or spatial distortion. You’re not moving 
elsewhere - the place itself becomes different. A Cindrel user steps through a door that never 
existed, and invokes an event that didn’t happen here, or now, but somewhere. 
Cindrel is never precise, but always probable. It vibrates the edges of the real, shifts a moment, 
bleeds a space into its own past. The mage doesn’t command energy - they persuade the world 
that it could be otherwise. 
●​ What does a Cindrel user do? They step out of space - and return changed. They 
summon a moment that didn’t happen here - but now it does. They point to one place - 
and something opens elsewhere. 
●​ Style: strange attention to empty spaces, slow movements, words after which the room 
changes. 
●​ Best for you if: you don’t want to fit - you want to slip, bend, and break the world until it 
finally fits you. 
For the GM: Cindrel magic is not about action - it’s about displacement. Characters who use 
this school bring constant tension to space and time. Cindrel users don’t “do” things - they evoke 
realities where those things already happened… or are about to. 
As GM, that means Cindrel isn’t linear, isn’t predictable - and doesn’t need to be. The key isn’t 
asking “What happens?” - but “What could have happened?” 
This school shines where cause and effect, space and time, past and present all coexist. Don’t 
give the player full control - but offer them possibilities only they can feel. And the world will 
answer: sometimes slower, sometimes with a crack, sometimes in ways it shouldn’t.

overview of the setting, basic rules for magic and equipment, and a 
small selection of NPCs and threats. It is designed as an accessible 
entry point for both players and Storytellers who want to experience 
the system before committing to the full line. 
 
Fantasia Vixens: Shape Your Vice, Forge Your Path

characters with strong narrative identity, supported by pre-generated 
examples for immediate play. The book serves as the primary 
foundation for players, combining mechanics with guidance on 
roleplaying within the setting.

intended to expand character creation options while reinforcing the
