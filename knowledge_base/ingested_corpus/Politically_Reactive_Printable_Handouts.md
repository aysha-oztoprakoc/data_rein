Story 2: 
Bay Area Politicos Watch, May 16th, 2124 
"Del. Dorian Delgado Defends Jacuzzi Borrowed Well Past Due Date"  
by Ralph Alonza 
 
Summary: The article describes Delgado taking a popular hot tub out of the city library’s furniture 
collection on March 17th for a five week loan, but still having not returned it four weeks past its 
scheduled due date. Dorian is quoted as having explained that he was holding on to it because the 
aquatics librarian asked him to do so to save them the trouble of storing it while the aquatics wing was 
undergoing repairs. The article notes that the librarian is Dorian's cousin, as well as that the request 
violated library protocols regarding storage procedures and appearance of preferential treatment. It 
also notes that the repairs started 10 days after the hot tub was due and had completed at the time of 
print. An update notes that Delgado returned the hot tub the day the article was published. 
 
Response: Delgado says that he took the hot tub out for a birthday party. When he called to schedule 
a pickup, the librarian -- his cousin Umaya -- asked if he'd just hold on to it to save her the trouble of 
moving it into storage. He didn't realize that she wasn't technically supposed to use borrowers as a 
substitute for storage, but she said afterwards that despite being against protocols as written it was a 
common practice that all the librarians used to avoid extra paperwork, especially because the storage 
depot was often slow to return calls and emails. Dorian considers the timing suspicious, as he was 
contacted to give a response a day after the repairs had finished and before he'd even been told so, 
and then the article came out later that day before he had a chance to return the hot tub.

Summary of Ordinance 2125-M17 
This ordinance would instruct the county treasurer to establish a process for members of 
qualifying tenant unions to simultaneously opt out of collecting their guaranteed income 
and opt out of paying property tax.  
There are existing programs in which individuals collectively pool their incomes and then 
use these funds to pay all of their property taxes. They then direct the remainder into 
shared pools used to fund their preferred food coops and various Communist projects, 
such as the local chapter of the Anarchy Everywhere party. 
If passed, the effects would be limited to the district which Delgado, Oakjoy, and 
Postmasterless represent – District 2 – which covers most of East Oakland. It’s projected 
that ~5,000 out of 280,000 residents might participate during the next five years, which is 
slightly less than 2% of residents. After five years, the program sunsets unless renewed. 
The primary argument in favor is that this would help facilitate a smooth transition off 
of currency (as has been done in the Mississippi Watershed Union).  
The primary argument against is that it would undermine the highly efficient system 
that makes a high-tech post-capitalist city without wealth inequality possible, with no 
material benefit.
