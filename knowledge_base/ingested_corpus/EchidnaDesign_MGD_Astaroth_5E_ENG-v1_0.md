Content Warning
Blood, biblical demons, exorcisms, and rituals are 
referenced in some parts of the pdf. Reader discre-
tion is advised. The pdf contains a fictional reinter-
pretation of demons, rituals and information from 
ancient real-world texts, still, it is intended as a 
game supplement, and nothing more.
What is this Book
Malamorte’s Goetic Demons (abbreviated in MGD) is 
a TTRPG compendium designed to add the biblical 
demons of Ars Goetia into your games as adver-
saries, sources of power, and story hooks. The com-
pendium features the statistics of the demons as 
monsters that can be encountered, fought and de-
feated, the magic items related to each demon, in-
formation on their cults, and so on. Each demon is 
represented with an original artwork, freshly rein-
terpreting the physical features and symbolism of 
each, based on the original description.
This is Just a Game
Malamorte’s Goetic Demons is a TTRPG supplement, 
and nothing more. It is not intended as a book 
about real-world magic, nor as an historically accu-
rate edition of the original texts of Ars Goetia.
What is Ars Goetia
“Goetia” is an Ancient Greek word that stands for 
“magic” in a derogatory sense. In modern language, 
it can be roughly interpreted as “black magic”.
“Ars Goetia” is the practice of summoning demons 
through sorcery to gain favors and power, a prac-
tice going back at least to the 17th century.
Sources and History
Ars Goetia is part of The Lesser Key of Solomon, a 
17th century grimoire also known by its Latin title 
Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis. The original text has 
been translated and published several times by many 
authors throughout history, with different levels of 
reliability and historical accuracy. For the fabrica-
tion of Malamorte’s Goetic Demons, we have been 
relying on the following editions:
•   The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King. Trans-
lated into the English tongue by a dead hand (1904),  
transcribed by S. L. MacGregor Mathers and 
edited by Aleister Crowley. Even if this is not the 
most reliable edition, it is one of the most acces-
sible: the text is in modern English, and the en-
tire body is Public Domain, enabling us to use its 
content as part of our book. The texts in gothic 
font that you will find at the beginning of each 
demon’s section are taken from this edition. 
•   The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula 
Salomonis by Joseph H. Peterson (2001). This is 
considered to be the most philologically reliable 
edition, being the result of a remarkable work of 
consultation of the original manuscripts, and 
comparison between many different previous 
versions. The Peterson edition is currently under 
copyright, so we cannot include any portion in 
MGD, but we are reading it as part of our re-
search in order to create this volume. If you are 
interested in the Ars Goetia’s history and sources, 
we recommend purchasing the Peterson edition 
or looking for it in your local library.
While we care about creating MGD starting from 
solid, well-informed sources, we also mean for it to 
be a game manual first of all. Therefore we consider 
the source material more as a starting point to build 
on, allowing us to change and develop in order to 
create engaging content for your TTRPG sessions.

Cult of Astaroth
Goals: Taking revenge to restore honor and re-
spect; learning about the past, present, and future.
Typical Cultists: Archmage, Cultist, Cultist Fa-
natic, Guard, Guard Captain, Knight, Mage, No-
ble, Spy. 
Astaroth attracts the loyalties of the “fallen”, people 
and creatures who have been cast down in one way 
or the other, whether it was their fault or not. The 
cult often makes use of poisons, and treats the viper 
as a symbol. The promise of knowledge, premoni-
tions and visions often attracts who deals in secrets 
and other learnings.
Signature Spells
A Warlock who made a pact with Astaroth can 
switch any of their subclass spells with one the fol-
lowing of the same level. Other spellcasters can gain 
access to these spells with the Magical Secrets Dark 
Blessing. If the GM wishes to, a creature of the 
listed CR can know one or more of the related 
spells.

A Commoner trying to break a personal curse

5
An archmage affected by an incurable disease 
asked to Astaroth for the strength to overcome 
their affliction. The ritual went wrong, and 
Astaroth killed the archmage and reincarnated in

6
A powerful lord, magically warded against poison, 
just died of poisoning without an apparent 
explanation. The court Mage believes the Viper 
Fluid of Astaroth was involved.

Dark Blessing
The following are examples of the kind of gifts, fa-
vors and rewards that Astaroth can deliver to their 
cultists and servants, in exchange for worship, ser-
vices, or successful rituals. They are similar in 
power and scope to magic items, therefore a “rarity” 
is provided for each of them, so the GM can assess 
them accordingly.
Foul Breath
Dark Blessing (Permanent), Common
This Dark Blessing allows you to cast Poison Spray, 
and you gain proficiency with the Poisoner’s Kit.
Glimpse of the Future
Dark Blessing (Consumable), Uncommon
This Dark Blessing allows you to cast Augury. Once 
used three times, the Dark Blessing vanishes from 
you.
Lore of Liberal Arts
Dark Blessing (Permanent), Rare
This Dark Blessing grants you one proficiency of 
your choice between the following: Alchemist’s 
Supplies, History, Musical Instrument (any), Na-
ture, Tinker’s Tools.
Magical Secrets
Dark Blessing (Permanent), Common (cantrip, level 1), 
Uncommon (level 2 and 3, or Rare (level 4 and 5)
If you can cast spells, you add all of the Astaroth 
Signature Spells up to a certain level (depending on 
the rarity of the Dark Blessing) to the list of spells 
you can prepare or know. See “Signature Spells” 
earlier in this document.
Mental Acuity
Dark Blessing (Permanent), Very Rare
Your Intelligence Score increases by 2, up to a max-
imum of 30.
Past Revealed
Dark Blessing (Consumable), Rare
This Dark Blessing allows you to cast Legend Lore.
Once you do so, the Dark Blessing vanishes from 
you.
Pierce the Future
Dark Blessing (Consumable), Legendary
This Dark Blessing allows you to cast Foresight.
Once you do so, the Dark Blessing vanishes from 
you.
Spit of the Viper
Dark Blessing (Consumable), Common (1 - 50 gp), 
Uncommon (51 - 200 gp), or Rare (201 - 20000 gp)
Astaroth grants you a vial of magically enhanced 
poison of your choice, with a gp value in range of 
the chosen rarity (see above). The poison within the 
vial ignores Resistance and Immunity to Poison 
damage and the Poisoned condition, and saving 
throws against it have Disadvantage.
Wings of Astaroth
Dark Blessing (Permanent), Very Rare
This Dark Blessing allows you to cast Summon 
Dragon (level 7 version) to call a fiendish spirit that 
resembles the draconic part of Astaroth. The sum-
moned spirit has the Fiend type (instead of 
Dragon), no Swim speed, and has Resistance to 
Fire, Necrotic, and Poison damage (instead of the 
ones from the spell description).

Dark Relics of Astaroth
The following magic items can be recovered from 
the remains of a physical manifestation of the de-
mon, or fiven by the demon as a particularly mean-
ingful reward.
(A second magic item will be added in the future)
Viper Fluid of Astaroth
Potion, Very Rare
This vial contains the very venom from the mouth 
of the viper that Astaroth hold in their hand.
You can use it in the following ways: 
Weapon. You can take a Utilize action to apply the 
entire content of the vial on a weapon or ammu-
nition. The next successful attack using it exposes 
the target to the poison detailed below, and the 
target has Disadvantage on the initial saving 
throw against it. On a failed save, the target gains 
Vulnerability to Poison damage while Poisoned, 
in addition to any other effect.
Drink. Anyone drinking the content of the vial is 
exposed to the poison detailed below, then re-
ceives the effects of the Contact Other Plane spell to 
pose questions to Astaroth (no Intelligence saving 
throw required).
Throw. You can take the Utilize action to throw 
the vial up to 60 feet. The vial explodes in a 20-
foot-radius Sphere of poisonous gas on impact and 
is destroyed. Each creature in the Sphere is ex-
posed to the poison detailed below. At the end of 
your turn, the gas dissipates and leaves behind 1d4 
+ 1 Swarms of Venomous Snake, appearing in as 
many 5-foot squares within the Sphere. The 
swarms act after your turn following your verbal 
commands, then disappear after 1 hour.
Poison Effect. The creature makes a DC 18 Con-
stitution saving throw. On a failure, the creature 
takes 10d6 Poison damage and has the Poisoned 
condition for 1 minute. Resistance or Immunity to 
Poison or the Poisoned condition don’t work 
against these effects. A creature can repeat the sav-
ing throw at the end of each of their turns, ending 
the condition on themselves on a success. On a suc-
cess on the initial saving throw, the creature takes 
half as much damage but is not Poisoned. 
Harvested Item. A freshly harvested Viper Fluid 
of Astaroth has all of the powers above, but loses 
them after seven days, unless properly refined and 
crafted into an actual magic item.
