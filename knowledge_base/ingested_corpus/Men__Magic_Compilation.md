INTRODUCTION: 
 
    These rules are an amalgamation of the earliest published volumes 
of the oldest fantasy role-playing game; those little booklets that 
ushered in an age of adventure and magic for so many. What you 
have before you is an attempt to gather, organize, edit, and customize 
all that written material into a set of similar volumes that retains the 
old-school feel and form, while offering more ease of use and 
reference. The first three volumes of this series will collect and edit all 
relevant data from those original tomes, plus the prequel and the 
supplements that followed, into a cohesive set of guides that will be 
self-contained and complete. The final result will honor the original 
spirit of the source material in every possible way.  
     
    Men & Magic (Vol. I) gives the player everything needed to create 
a character, including lists of spells for those who wield magic. It is the 
only volume intended for the benefit of the player. Monsters & 
Treasure (Vol. II) is a tome for the Referee. It details the various 
adversaries and creatures populating the dungeon, as well as the 
options for the treasures, magical and mundane, they may guard. The 
Underworld & Wilderness Adventures (Vol. III) is the guide to 
creating and judging a campaign, from the dungeon to the world 
above. For the most part, this final volume is also intended for the use 
of the Referee.

SCOPE: 
 
    This presentation of the rules will provide full coverage of the game, 
as originally presented. Any changes or edits are included for clarity 
and completeness. All relevant rules or additions from supplements 
and magazines (The Strategic Review and The Dragon) are also 
provided, in proper place and context. For the sake of brevity and 
continuity, Chainmail is not used as a reference, as those rules were 
modified in the earliest supplements, and these rules incorporate the 
alternate combat system. Still, this volume assumes at least a passing 
familiarity with the RPG hobby and a modicum of common sense. 
Setting aside guidelines or suggestions for proper age levels or 
number of participants, how about we jump right in!

PREPARATION FOR THE CAMPAIGN: 
 
    While the Referee is responsible for presenting the environment 
and guidance for the adventures themselves, each player must learn 
the rules that apply to his own character and keep his character sheet 
accurate and current. Rules and information for creating dungeons, 
complete with deadly foes, confounding traps, and valuable treasures 
will be found in Volumes 2 and 3 of this set. What each player must 
do is create a heroic adventurer for their very own character.

Fighting Men includes human warriors, also dwarves, elves, and 
halflings. Fighters have access to all weapons and armor, both 
mundane and magical. They have more hit dice to endure damage 
taken in battle, but use few non-martial magic items and no spells. 
Their training and prowess grants fighting men advantages from high 
strength and dexterity scores in combat. The prime requisite for a 
fighter is strength, and a fighter with an 18 strength may roll d100 for 
"percentile" strength - see DETERMINATION OF ABILITIES, below. 
 
Fighters of a truly lawful nature, with a high charisma score, may 
aspire to the rank of Paladin (a sub-class), detailed after the main 
classes and non-humans. Fighting Lords (9th level) are known to build 
strongholds of their own to possibly become Barons, or other minor 
nobility. Rules for the construction and maintenance of strongholds 
can be found in Volume 3, The Underworld & Wilderness 
Adventures.

Magic-Users, both humans and elves, are the keepers and wielders 
of arcane spellcraft. Though powerful casters of magical spells later in 
their careers, mages have it tough at lower levels, relying often upon 
the courage and protection of armored warriors. Magic-Users have 
access to all enchanted items, except those martial arms and 
armaments beyond their training, which includes most everything 
except daggers. The prime requisite for a magic-user is intelligence. 
 
Spell research is one province of magic-users, and those of any level 
may create new spells, as resources allow. Spells of a level permitted 
to the mage may be researched and it is then merely a matter of time 
and gold invested. Exact rules and amounts are given in the 
MAGICAL RESEARCH portion of the section on SPELLS, later in this 
volume. Those magicians who reach the lofty rank of Wizard (11th 
level) are also capable of crafting enchanted items of their own. Rules 
will be given in the magical treasure section of Volume 2.

Clerics are exclusively human or half-elven as player characters, with 
the advantage of both martial prowess and divine spellcasting ability. 
Along with a selection of any non-edged weapon, clerics also gain the 
use of any magical armor, in addition to their own listing of divine 
spells. The list of magical items permitted to clerics is also fairly 
extensive. Clerics of a rank greater than Bishop (6th level) must be 
devoted to either Law or Chaos and will immediately lose all benefits if 
they change sides. The prime requisite for a cleric is wisdom. 
 
Clerics of a neutral alignment, with a high wisdom and charisma 
score, may become Druids (a sub-class), detailed after the main 
classes and non-humans. 
Clerics with high scores in wisdom, strength, and dexterity may 
become Monks (a sub-class), detailed after the main classes and non-
humans. Only humans may become monks.

Thieves are scouts and troubleshooters, of any race (without level 
limit), who gather tactical information and deal with locks, traps, or 
other impediments. The nature of their training and their work leads 
thieves to be only Neutral or Chaotic, though other Lawful characters 
may employ a thief for single missions where the law is generally not 
broken. Thieves can wear leather armor, cannot use shields, but are 
able to wield magic daggers and swords. They cannot cast spells, but 
they can read magical scrolls at Master Thief (10th) level. The prime 
requisite for a thief is dexterity. 
 
The advantages of a thief lie in numerous abilities useful in the 
dungeon environment, plus the sneaky backstab and facility with 
languages and magical writings. 


    A strike without warning (usually from behind) increases a thief's 
chance to hit by +4 and inflicts double (x2) damage if successful. At 
5th level (Cutpurse), the damage is tripled (x3). At 9th level (Thief), 
the damage is quadrupled (x4). The progression continues, with 
damage increased every four levels. 
    At 3rd level (Robber), a thief can read languages with an 80% 
chance of success. At 10th level (Master Thief), a thief can read 
arcane (not divine) magical writings, enabling them to use scrolls. 
Attempting to cast a scroll spell of 7th level or greater imposes a 10% 
chance of the result being accidentally reversed due to error.   
    The other abilities of the thief are as follows. 
 
 
--- open locks 
 
 
--- remove traps 
 
 
--- listen for noise 
 
 
--- move stealthily 
 
 
--- filch items and pick pockets 
 
 
--- hide in shadows 
 
 
--- climb sheer surfaces 
    Climbing sheer surfaces imposes a 13% base chance for a 1st 
level thief to slip and fall during the attempt. This chance is reduced 
by 1%/level after 1st.

NON-HUMANS: 
 
The other four races of adventuring characters embody one or more 
of the four main classes, often with restrictions and limitations to 
balance their natural traits and abilities. 
 
Dwarves: 
 
Physical: These stocky folk stand about 4 feet in height and weigh 
roughly 150 pounds. Their coloring tends to be in earthy tones and 
they are known as miners and metalworkers par excellence. Tending 
toward underground communities, dwarves have infravision that 
allows them to see up to 60' in the dark.  
Adventuring: Dwarves are at +1 to hit and -1 to be hit when fighting 
"Giant Class" creatures. Such creatures have their damage halved on 
a successful hit upon a dwarf. Being naturally resistant to magic, 
dwarves enjoy a +4 level bonus to appropriate saving throws. 
Additionally, a dwarf may detect slanting passages, traps, shifting 
walls and new construction in underground settings. Detect sound 
behind a closed door on a 1-2 on d6. In addition to their own language 
(Dwarvish), Dwarves also speak the languages of Gnomes, Kobolds 
and Goblins, plus their alignment language.  
Class: The standard dwarf advances as a fighting man, up to a 
maximum of 6th level (Myrmidon). A dwarf with a 17 strength may 
achieve 7th level (Champion), while a dwarf of 18 strength can reach 
8th level (Super Hero).  
    Though dwarf clerics (also advancing as fighters) as high as 7th 
level (Lama) may cure and resurrect their own kind, they are never 
found as player characters. 
    As with all player character races, the dwarf may also advance as a 
thief, with no level limit. While a dwarf may advance as both a fighter 
and a thief, no bonuses for exceptional ability scores are given, and 
all experience is divided evenly between the two classes, even when 
no further progress can be made as a fighter. When acting as a thief, 
only leather armor may be worn.     
 
 
Elves:  
 
Physical: These graceful people reach five feet or more in height and 
weigh around 120 willowy pounds. They can be tanned or pale, 
depending upon their background or type. Elves are capable warriors 
and arcane spellcasters, often in equal measure. They have 
infravision which allows them to see up to 60' in the dark.  
Adventuring: When wielding a sword or a bow, an elf is at +1 to hit. 
The paralysis effect of ghouls does not affect elves. They note secret 
and hidden doors more easily, locating such on a 1-4 on d6, and may 
be allowed the chance to sense secret doors in passing, on a 1-2 on 
d6, success indicating they feel something is there. An elf may detect 
sound behind a closed door on a 1-2 on d6. 
Elves also speak their own language (Elvish), as well as those of 
Orcs, Hobgoblins, and Gnolls, plus their alignment language. 
Class: The typical elf advances as a fighting man and a magic-user, 
switching from one to the other as they will, from adventure to 
adventure, though not during the course of one. An elf with a 17 
strength can reach 5th level (Swashbuckler) as a fighter. An elf with 
an 18 strength may achieve 6th level (Myrmidon). An elf with an 18 
intelligence is able to gain 9th level (Sorcerer) as a magic-user. 
    Elves may choose to advance solely as thieves, or to function 
equally as fighters, magic-users, and thieves. In the latter case, all 
experience is divided equally among all three classes, even when the 
elf can no longer advance in one or more classes. When acting as a 
thief, only leather armor is permitted. 
    Elven clerics exist among other elves, able to advance as high as 
6th level (Bishop), but not as player characters. These fighter/magic-
user/clerics are limited to 6th level (Magician) in arcane ability. 
 
Half-Elves: 
 
Physical: Being half elf and half human, members of this race tend to 
resemble slightly smaller and more graceful humans, or somewhat 
bulkier and taller elves. 
Adventuring: Note secret and hidden doors as elves, locating such 
on a 1-4 on d6. May be allowed the chance to sense secret doors in 
passing, on a 1-2 on d6, success indicating they feel something is 
there. Half-elves also speak Elvish, as well as those of Orcs, 
Hobgoblins, and Gnolls, plus their alignment language. 
Class: Much like elves, a half-elf may advance as both a fighter and a 
magic-user, with the weapon and spell ability of each. They may 


advance to 6th level (Myrmidon) as a fighter, or as high as 7th level 
(Champion) if possessing a 17 strength, and as high as 8th level 
(Superhero) with an 18 strength. A half-elf may reach 6th level 
(Magician) as a magic-user, or as high as 7th level (Enchanter) with a 
17 intelligence, and up to 8th level (Warlock) with an 18 intelligence. 
Half-elves with a Wisdom of 13 or more may become clerics, with 
experience divided evenly between fighter, magic-user, and cleric 
classes. They may advance as high as 4th level (Vicar) in the cleric 
class, and may not act chaotically, nor associate with chaotic 
characters or creatures. 
 
Halflings:     
 
Physical: The smallest of the adventuring races, a halfling stands 
about 3 feet tall and will usually weigh less than 60 pounds. 
Adventuring: Halflings add +3 to hit when attacking with a sling. 
They have magic resistance equal to dwarves, adding 4 levels to all 
such saving throws. They detect sounds behind closed doors with a 2 
in 6 chance of success. Of all the races, only halflings receive 
bonuses to all thieving abilities (see the Demihuman Bonuses to Thief 
Abilities table, above). 
Class: Members of this race are limited to the fighting-man class or 
the thief class, with progression to 4th level (Hero) in the former, and 
no limit in the latter.

Paladins (Fighting-Men): A human fighter with a 17 or higher 
charisma score and a consistently lawful alignment may choose to 
continue advancement as a paladin. If such a character ever acts in a 
chaotic fashion, the status of paladin is immediately and permanently 
lost. 
 
Once per day, a paladin can lay on hands to cure wounds (2 
points/paladin level). Also, once per day, the paladin can lay on hands 
to cure disease (1/5 paladin levels). A paladin is himself immune to 
disease. A paladin enjoys a 10% bonus to saving throws.  
    At 8th level, the paladin can dispel evil on command and may 
detect evil within 6". 
    Wielding a Holy Sword grants virtual immunity to all magic. In the 
hands of a paladin, this item will negate all spells (including wands, 
staves, and such) in a 1" radius. 
    At any time, the paladin may obtain a heavy horse with the same 
spell negation ability as the Holy Sword, Armor Class 5, Move 18", 
5+1 Hit Dice, and High intelligence. If killed, the horse is not 
automatically replaced, and another cannot be gained within ten 
years.  
    Paladins cannot own more than four magic items, excluding armor, 
shield, and up to four weapons. Give away all excess treasure not 
necessary for upkeep of self, any retainers (up to 200), and a modest 
stronghold (up to 200,000 GP). All else must be donated - not given to 
another PC.

they cease to be Lawful, becoming normal fighters. A fighter with an 
intelligence and wisdom of at least 12, and a constitution of at least 
15, may become a ranger. A ranger's prime requisite is strength.

Druids (Clerics): Members of this cleric sub-class are devoted to the 
harmony and sanctity of nature itself, an ethos suited to their 
dominantly neutral alignment. A cleric with a wisdom of at least 12 
and a charisma of at least 14 may choose to become a druid. With the 
worship of nature comes the reverence of mistletoe as a holy symbol 
to be employed in ceremony and spell casting. These predominantly 
nature-oriented spells will be found in an exclusive druid SPELLS 
TABLE, listed with those of the magic-user and the cleric, later in this 
volume. The concerns of civilization and humankind fall behind those 
of animals and plants.  
 
Druids may use the following weapons: Dagger, curved sword, spear, 
sling, and oil. They may employ leather armor and wooden shields, 
but none of metal. They are permitted magic items usable by "all 
classes," any not prohibited to druids, and all those permitted to 
clerics - except for those of a written nature. A druid fights and saves 
as a cleric, except that the druid gains a +2 bonus to all saves vs. fire. 
 
Though druids cannot turn the undead, they do gain the following 
powers. 
    At 2nd level (Initiate of the 1st Circle): Identify pure water, plants, 
and animals. Pass through overgrowth. 
    At 6th level (Initiate of the 5th Circle): Shape change 3x/day, once 
each into any reptile, bird, or mammal, from a size as small as a raven 
to as large as a small bear. During such a change, the druid loses 


from 10% to 60% of any sustained damage. Unaffected by the charm 
spells of woodland and water "fae." such as dryads or nixies. 
 
In addition to the common tongue, druids have a secret language of 
their own (Druidic). At 5th level (Initiate of the 4th Circle), the druid 
can learn an additional language from the following, +1/level 
thereafter: Centaur, Dragon (Green), Dryad, Elvish, Giant (Hill), 
Manticore, Nixie, Pixie, or Treant. 
 
The druidic code stresses the protection of woodland animals and 
plants, especially trees. A druid is more likely to punish those who 
destroy nature, rather than risk their own lives to defend it. A druid will 
not willingly or deliberately harm an animal or woodland. The druid will 
defend himself and may attempt to remove any harmful enchantments 
placed upon a creature or place.

Bards (Jacks-Of-All-Trades): A well-rounded adventurer with some 
skills of the thief, limited access to magic-user spells, and worthy 
fighting talent, the bard is also gifted with the ability to charm through 
song. To become a bard, the character must have at least average 
strength and intelligence, and a below-average dexterity reduces 
thieving abilities by ½. Also, the bard must have above-average 
charisma, increasing charming abilities by 5%/point above 14.  
    The bard is not a sub-class of any kind and is presented at the end 
of this section as something of an added profession. Dwarves, Elves, 
and Halflings may advance as bards up to the 8th level (Minstrel). 
Humans have no level limit.

They also save as a cleric. While they may wear leather or chainmail 
armor, the latter will reduce the chances of climbing walls or moving 
silently to zero. Though generally neutral in nature, bards may be 


lawful or chaotic. A bard who becomes lawful loses all thieving 
abilities. Bards and druids belong to the same sect and are obligated 
to come to the aid of the other in times of need.

section of Volume 2 for details), with magic books dealing damage to 
bards the same as with fighters. Bards may not use "fighter-only" 
potions, though they may read scrolls. All defensive "plusses" from 
magic items will subtract 5% from the bard's charming attempts (see 
below). 
 
A bard has the skills of a thief, but at half level (rounded down). 
Dwarves, elves, and halflings receive their usual bonuses to these 
thief skills. The backstab ability of the thief is not gained by a bard. 
 
The signature ability of the bard is that of charming creatures within 
hearing distance (about 60') through song, once per day/level, at a 
10% chance/level. Creatures with more than three hit dice, and 
humans (or non-human character races - except the bard's allies) of 
5th level or greater, resist the bard's charm (lowering the percent 
chance of success) at 5%/hit die above three (or level above 4th for 
men-types). Monks subtract 10%/level instead of 5%. Undead 
subtract 10%/hit die instead of 5%.                       
    A charmed creature will stop and do nothing but listen to the music 
until attacked, interrupted by a loud noise or similar effect, or the bard             
stops playing. If a creature is enraged, ravenous, or otherwise in an 
agitated state, the chances of charming may be reduced accordingly. 
    When charmed, the creature is subject to suggestion from the bard. 
For those with lower intelligence, the suggestion should be a simple 
one, and can be made more complex for those with higher 
intelligence. In all other ways, this functions as the magic-user spell, 
Suggestion, with applicable saving throws for the target to 
immediately come to its senses 
    A bard receives experience for charming and suggesting, according 
to the hit dice of the creature(s) involved - treated as an assist.. 
    The song of a bard can also counter or cancel sound-based effects 
from monsters (such as a harpy's song) for as long as the bard plays. 
 
Along with the ability to charm, the bard is known for an extensive 
knowledge of legends, magic, and other such lore that may help 
identify specific objects or tell of locales and folklore inside and 
outside of the dungeon. The percentage change of success is 
10%/level of the bard, with the DM determining the likelihood of the 
bard knowing specific outdoor regions. The bard is especially adept at 
determining the backgrounds and properties of weapons (without the 
need to draw or use the weapon), including the "plusses" of a magical 
weapon or the intelligence and ego of an intelligent one - gaining 
experience of 100 points/"plus" or point of intelligence/ego in the 
process. In attempts to discern the qualities of miscellaneous magic 
items or artifacts, the bard's chances are at least half, especially 
regarding items not usable by the bard 
    A bard's extensive knowledge and proficiency for learning allows 
the character to know a number of languages equal to his or her 
intelligence score.

Experience Points and Character Levels: Defeating monsters in 
combat and gaining treasure earns those heroes involved a number of 
experience points, which will be adjusted by the DM based upon the 
prime requisite scores of the PCs, and the level of actual challenge 
involved. As seen on each character class level chart, earned 
experience points will eventually advance a character in levels. 
Adventurers will earn proportionately less experience when the 
challenge is less than typical for their experience level. Thus, a 6th 
level character fighting a 4th level monster, or adventuring on the 4th 
level of a dungeon would receive 4/6 of the possible experience 
award. Experience awards for magic items gained during the 
adventure will be less than optimal, to reflect the usefulness of such 
items in achieving further goals. Also, it is suggested that no more 
experience be awarded in a single adventure beyond that necessary 
to advance a character by one level. Levels beyond those given on 
the class progression charts are generally possible. 
 
Hit Dice: The number and type of dice rolled for a character's total hit 
points are given on each class progression chart (as presented in 
GREYHAWK, instead of the uniform d8+ system of Volume 1, Men & 
Magic). Hit points are a measure of the damage an individual or 
creature can take before death. Numbers given after a plus (+) are 
added to the total, after the roll(s). 
 


Spells & Levels: For those classes able to cast spells, at the top of 
each column is the spell level - a relative measure of power and 
complexity. Level 1 spells are the weakest and simplest. The numbers 
in the columns represent how many spells of each level the character 
can cast according to experience level. Spell level and experience 
level are not otherwise connected. For example, a 9th level magic-
user cannot yet learn 9th level spells.

Character Alignment: During character creation, it is necessary to 
select an alignment to determine the values and ethos which will help 
govern the character's actions. The three alignments are Law, 
Neutrality, and Chaos. In many cases, these absolutes are tempered 
with Good and Evil. There can be Lawful Evil as well as Chaotic 
Good. 
 
Lawful Alignment indicates a trustworthy and cooperative attitude. 
This alignment is associated with Good, and is a requirement for 
some clerics and all paladins or rangers. 
Neutral Alignment implies a sense of impartiality, or simply a lack of 
moral preference - such as with animals or mindless automatons. 
Chaotic Alignment encourages lawless and selfish action. This 
alignment is associated with Evil. 
 
Each alignment is also defined by its own unique language, detailed 
below. 
 
LANGUAGES:  
 
    For game purposes, most humans will share a "common tongue" 
that allows them to communicate wherever they may be. Other races 
and speaking creatures will have their own languages, though they 
will have a 20% chance of knowing Common as well. 
    As mentioned above, Each alignment has a shared language. 
Those of other alignments cannot speak an alignment language not 
their own, but they will recognize it for what it as - thus determining 
the alignment of the speaker. 
    Intelligence of 11 or more allows a character to learn added 
languages at a rate of 1 per point above 10. 
 
 


NON-PLAYER CHARACTERS:  
 
    Every individual encountered by the player characters is either a 
monster or a non-player character (NPC). An NPC is generally 
another person, under the DM's control, that can be interacted with 
and possibly hired into service. 
    NPCs can be hired by advertising in town, seeking hirelings out in 
person, or sending message to other lands. Only lower level 
individuals will work for player characters as hirelings or mercenaries 
and they will want money, magic, or other treasures for their service. 
An expenditure of 100 GP minimum may be required to tempt a 
human into service. Dwarves also accept gold, while magic-users and 
elves prefer magic items. Clerics want a church. 
    All experience earned by hirelings or followers is divided by 50%, 
representing the fact they serve under another. Guidelines for 
calculating experience awards for slaying monsters are found in 
Volume 2, Monsters & Treasure. 
    Generally, only individuals of the same basic alignment as the 
player character will enter into service. Retainers in the service of 
chaotic characters have a base -2 on loyalty scores, while chaotic 
retainers will never have a loyalty score higher than 15. Monsters of 
similar alignment may be drawn into service, or magically charmed to 
obey. Such creatures may be tempted or mollified by rewards, just as 
npc retainers. In the end, after modifiers for charisma and such, the 
monster's reactions may be determined with a 2d6 roll on the

Relatives and Inheritance: In the event of a character' death or 
disappearance, a will may be put into place designating a relative as 
the heritor of any legacy that may remain. This relative would pay a 
10% legacy tax and start play as a 1st level character. 
    Should the original character somehow turn up, he reclaims his 
estate (with another 10% tax to be paid) and the DM determines how 
gracious the relative may be in giving it up. The disinherited relative 
may be kept on as an NPC follower of the player character, but his 
loyalty would suffer a penalty of 0 to -6, and there might be the 
possibility of betrayal at a future date. 
    Without a will or a relative to inherit, a player character's 
possessions are lost upon their demise or disappearance.

creatures, such as giants, can break through in two turns. Less 
powerful creatures take proportionately longer. A Flaming Sword cuts 
through in one turn. Duration: 8 game hours. Range: 3".

Magic-User at least three levels higher than the caster can pass

the level of the caster of the original spell, thus a 5th level caster

magic-user. Duration: 1 turn. Range: 12". 
 
Explosive Runes: Magical characters scribed upon a page that

higher may detect them with a 50% chance, and remove them with a 
75% chance.

24".

Protection From Evil 10': A Protection From Evil spell extending in a

Protection From Normal Missiles: Recipient invulnerable to missiles

Rope Trick: A 6' to 24' length of rope is made to stand upright and

Water Breathing: Confers the ability to breathe normally underwater.

Extension I: Increase duration of 1st-3rd level spells by 50%. Range:

MAGICAL RESEARCH: 
 
Magic-users and clerics may attempt to formulate new spells not 
otherwise listed, with the amount of investment dependent upon the 
level of the desired spell. Monetary investment is 2,000 gp for a first 
level spell, doubled for each spell level thereafter. For every amount 
spent equal to the basic investment, there is a cumulative 20% 
chance of success. Time investment is one week per spell level. 
 
The level of the researched spell must be one that the character can 
cast himself. Once the new spell is created, it can be added to the 
researcher’s spell list as a spell of the appropriate level. 
 
BOOKS OF SPELLS: 
 
Spellcasters need books containing the spells they use, one book for 
each level. Duplicate or replacement books cost the same amounts 
per spell level as those listed above for magical research.
