INTRODUCTION
This is an attempt to update the classic dystopian and social science 
fiction of yore to a contemporary role-playing game. There is a bit of 
everything popular in here (as the name of the game betrays). Half-life 2, 
1984, Children of Men, Brave New World, Fallout, Black Mirror, you name 
it, it‘ll have a place in this game.
It is not just about the action, it‘s about exploring a world in the near fu-
ture, where the most hideous ideas humanity can come up with became 
reality. Nothing here is as much invented, as it is satirically exaggerated.
Why would you want to play such a game? Honestly, I’m too lazy to even 
be sneaky about this. We firmly believe that exposure to unwanted ideas 
is not only better than hiding from them, it‘s outright necessary if you 
want to reflect on and demystify them. In that sense, the game will not 
be everyone‘s cup of tea and definitely implies a readership mature and 
willing enough to explore strange (taboo, even) places. What better way 
to do this than in a game of make-believe and satire?
The setting for this game is as fucked up as one would expect after 
reading the above, and we encourage readers to explore this as deep as 
they dare. Anything goes, as long as it stays in the game. Yes, we don‘t 
care if you play enforcers for the political party in charge, or rebels, or 
anarchists, or just people trying to get by. You could play this as seriously 
as you can manage, or as the satire it is written as (Paranoia, anyone?) - 
just try to bring it all as close to its natural conclusion in all its aspects 
as possible. Let that world come alive and have fun doing so. We will 
provide the tools for that.
Our game is designed to accumulate stress for the characters in multiple, 
mostly random, ways as their stories progress, so it’ll induce that drop of 
tragedy even if you go for comedy. The game is somewhat rigged against 
the characters, as the genre-conventions dictate. Not that they don‘t have 
a chance for a happy ending, but it‘ll be a rare occurrence, and probably 
not as the players intended or imagined.
We hope you enjoy this game for what it is.
Take care, friends and neighbors – Ed Jester Punk (Eddy)

A FRIENDLY REMINDER
To be perfectly clear: this is a work of satire. The authors condemn vio-
lence, fascism and extremism in every form other than fictional and you 
should understand that distinction.
If you are just here for some very dark science fiction, do not worry. We 
got you covered (and you can skip this). However, we are not claiming 
that any of the concepts, ideas or predictions come only with this one 
interpretation or are the single, most likely way how the world will be in 
a couple of years. It‘s a thought experiment, nothing more. A satire, if you 
will. Our contribution to the ongoing cultural dialogue about these things.
In that sense, you should get a fair chance of finding something out 
about your position towards its themes. Because, if you think about it, 
that‘s what it‘s all about in the end: opening a dialogue through fiction for 
ideas that are already out there looking for people to carry them around 
unquestioned.
Everyone can play this game and all are encouraged to explore what they 
desire to explore (as long as everyone else is on board).
The last thing we would like to add is that the best experience in gaming 
stems from people being nice to each other. So be just that, even when 
the world turns very dark.

THE SETTING
The premise of the narrative we present here is built on what humanity 
has established in understanding how it all works, and not necessarily 
where it might be going. However, it is entirely possible that the iden-
tity conflicts western civilization is struggling with at the time of this 
writing is part of humanity’s transition to something that goes beyond 
the established. A transhuman society like that, as it were, would have 
to function on very different sets of assumptions. So please keep in 
mind: this is dark satire coming from a very specific interpretation of 
the world. It‘s neither wrong nor right, it‘s just a humble contribution to 
that discourse. Humanity needs to find non-violent solutions to progress 
towards a better world. Here‘s hoping. But for now…
THE SETTING
The year 2081 was a common year starting on a Wednesday. Only that it 
wasn‘t common at all. Imagine a world where every disaster humanity 
could come up with actually became reality. Global warming made the 
world practically a desert in most places, pollution killed off the rest. 
Nature, as we know it, is kept artificially alive where people managed to 
make geo-engineering work. Where geo-engineering didn’t work, nature 
turned very dangerous, and beyond that, it has turned truly strange. 
Add weaponized artificial intelligence gone rogue, failed attempts of 
gene-editing, as well as freak accidents with nano-technology, and you 
get a nice backdrop for this dystopia.
Its stage is a politically shattered Europe. The devastating immigration 
crisis in the early 2020s paired with a devastating pandemic had made 
Europe one of the main fighting grounds between the alt-right and ctrl-
left movements. While most of the population ended up radicalized 
towards the political right end of the spectrum, it was the extreme left 
that eventually managed to gain political control in Europe by taking 
over the universities, the media and corporate culture, with a profound 
impact on capital flow and influence.
It was a time of conflict, fueled by hyper-morality, Cancel Culture and 
celebrated victimhood on the one side, and the extremes of everything 
opposite to that on the other side, screaming just as loud. There were mi-
litary actions against the millions of people fleeing their homes because 
of disasters. There were purges in the streets as the political parties, the 
banks and the elites abandoned all pretense and came down on their ene-
mies with open force. The great pandemic offered the right opportunity 
for them to execute long-prepared plans.
After Identity Politics and Brosocialism (BroSoc for short) had finally 
manifested in the form of the party known as The Family, it didn‘t take 
long for the purges to become one-sided. Very much like the Nazis did in 
Germany, The Family started by attacking a wealthy minority, labeling 
theirs “The Patriarchy”. A common and calculated economical move to 
reduce a population while redistributing wealth, as it is always occurring 
in times where economic growth is restrained. People are reduced to 
tribes, and the big tribes loot and exploit the small ones for all they have.
It all happened well-received and supported by the then thoroughly-in-
doctrinated population (what was left of it after purges and catastrophes, 
anyway). This process revealed another obvious parallel to Germany after 
the Great War: a disowned people easily falls prey to ideology. By then, 
the refugees of the biggest migration of the 21st century had either died 
or fallen in line as well.

THE RULES
If you have to ask this question, you probably have the wrong game in 
your hands. At least some experience with role-playing games is recom-
mended to play Ø2\\‘3|| (sorry). That being said, there are different schools 
or approaches, so it might be useful to clarify where we come from.
While this is a game about telling stories, we would also consider it to 
be firmly rooted in the early traditions of the hobby. The story is driven 
by the players and their characters, but the District Master (DM) is the 
last arbiter of the rules, always, and the rest is up to chance. Unlike other 
systems, this game will create everything a DM needs randomly, and 
gives him tools and advice on how to bring it all to life.
Unlike other RPGs, Ø2\\‘3|| has a system that generates narratives based 
on the interplay between the players and DM, based on a shared currency 
(Pennies) that denotes the ability to influence the game in a variety of 
ways. This imposes rules on the DM, but also organically generates fitting 
challenges. Being the DM in Ø2\\‘3|| is less about being an omniscient 
narrator, and instead more like the experience of playing a game.
It‘s aimed to free a DM from the consequences of bad decisions in that 
the dice decide (almost) every major outcome. The DM just has to play it 
out and is free to focus on the narration. The stories will always be firmly 
rooted in the dystopian tradition, while the individual interpretation of 
the narrator is unhindered (or rather, not preoccupied with the set-up) 
in their individual version of the manifesting narrative.
We hope you appreciate the irony that you have to trust the system in 
this game …

General overview over the game
We will start with a pitch what a game of Ø2\\‘3|| looks like in very general 
terms and from the players’ perspective. That first section closes with 
a complete set of all the terms the game uses in the form of a glossary. 
After that, we dive into character generation and information on how 
Advancement and Combat work.
Ø2\\‘3|| uses five different dice: d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12 rolled to match or 
exceed Difficulty Tiers (DT) divisible by 4 (4, 8, 12, 16 …) in order to achieve 
a success. Characters are defined by 5 Attributes that indicate a potential 
rather than a value (high, medium and low potential). Players and District 
Master (DM) never roll more than 3 dice per Test. DMs have their own dice.
With High Potential Attributes you basically roll 3d12 and put the die that 
fell most to the right aside. It is your Optional Die (OD). The remaining two 
dice give you a Leading Die (LD), which will be used as result against the 
difficulty of a Test, and a Secondary Die (SD), which has several functions.
Players are using the Leading Die versus a so-called Difficulty Tier to 
achieve whatever Test is worth rolling the dice for to determine Success 
or Failure (a character’s Potentials decide if the lower or the higher d12 is 
the LD) and there are situations in which the SD and the OD may be added 
to raise the result. There are, however, a few more tricks to how players 
are allowed to enhance their results towards the difficulty, namely with 
the so called Defined Potentials (DP):
	
1. Players can use skills (a value added to a roll) or
	
2. use items (a depletable resource called Usage they might have 
at their disposal) or
	
3. activate contacts (another pool of Usages) to enhance their 
chances before or after a roll.
A feature that differentiates Ø2\\‘3|| from other games is that players can 
come up with those Defined Potentials as they need them. They can do so 
10 times per ‘Season’ (Seasons are basically chapters in the characters’ 
lives and somewhat determine the rhythm of the narrative) in what is 
called ‘Slots’. When a Potential a character might have is specified, the

otherwise it might be added to the Anger Pool, trigger a Social Media Feedback 
or support another character. Another feature is that the OD can be bought to 
add it against the difficulty. Triples always activate all dice against a difficulty.

On the player side, the costs for activating the third Die is what in the 
game is called a ‘Pennie’ (which is the currency the District Master uses 
to bring the world to life). The DM uses Pennies to either make the game 
more difficult, or to establish the world and obstacles surrounding the 
characters. Partial Successes are possible as well and are achieved with 
results meeting the next lower Difficulty Tier. ‘Damage Effects’ denote 
the amount of damage a result will produce in a fight.
DIFFICULTY TIERS
Difficulties come in steps of 4, so the difficulties in Ø2\\‘3|| are:
	
(Very Easy)
	
(Easy/ Partial: 4+ / Stage 1 Damage Effect)
	
(Moderate / Partial: 8+ / Stage 2 Damage Effect)
	
(Challenging / Partial: 12+ / Stage 3 Damage Effect)
 
(Difficult / Partial: 16+ /Stage 4 Damage Effect)
 
(Very Difficult / Partial: 16+ /Stage 4 Damage Effect)
	
(Almost Impossible / Partial: 20+ /Stage 5 Damage Effect)
	
…
Results can furthermore be altered by using Insanity (no Pennie, but the 
risk of Damage) or Relief  (support Tests by other players) Dice.
There also are Opposing Rolls in which the difficulty of a Test is deter-
mined by an opposing random result (a roll of the dice either by the DM 
or other players).
This is the entirety of the basic rules. Some of them will have exceptions 
or can change over the course of the game, but it is all built on the pat-
terns outlined above.
