Herbs of the Tropics Biome
Carrion Flower (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: none
Appearance: This enormous red bloom smells like
rotting meat.
Effect: When chewed as a standard action, the chewer's
skin becomes quite foul­smelling indeed. The next time
the chewer is struck in melee, this scent is unleashed all at
once, nauseating the attacker for 1 round if he occupies a
square adjacent to the chewer. A DC 10 + 1/2 the
herbalist's class level + the herbalist's Wisdom modifier
Fortitude save negates this effect. The scent dissipates
without an opportunity to be used if the chewer is not
struck within 1 round per herbalist class level of chewing.
Chameleon Bloom (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 2
Recipes: Twisted Fate
Appearance: These small, unassuming flowers are the
color
of
their
direct
surroundings,
making
them
particularly difficult to identify by any means other than
careful inspection of their silhouette.
Effect: When chewed as a standard action, the chewer's
aura becomes muddled and confused. The chewer selects
one of his alignment axes. This axis has no value for 1
round for every 2 herbalist class levels, minimum 1 round.
The chewer is not merely immune to detection in this axis
­ there is quite literally nothing to detect! Spells that deal
damage or apply effects based on alignment treat the
muddled alignment as the alignment that receives the
lowest amount of damage or general "bad stuff". As a side
effect, spells with an alignment descriptor cannot be cast
by the chewer while under the effects of chameleon
bloom.
Cocoa (fruit)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: none
Appearance: Dull and brown, the pod of the cocoa plant
must be cracked open to get to the unprocessed chocolate
inside.
This
contains
traces
amounts
of
caffeine.
Effect: When chewed as a standard action, the chewer
gains a bonus to Acrobatics checks equal to the herbalist's
class level. This effect persists for 1 minute.
Dragon's Maw (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: Chromatic Dragon's Maw
Appearance: Long, majestic curves of stem and stalk
terminate in large, red blooms that are hot enough to
scorch flesh with a mere touch. The air around a dragon's
maw is cool and refreshing to downright cold depending
on the heat of the ambient air that day. Given its need to
draw heat from external sources, dragon's maw prefers
warm to swelteringly hot climates with plenty of water.
Sun is not a concern.
Effect: One unit of dragon's maw can be swung in a wide
arc as a standard action, creating a 15­foot cone of flame
that deals 1d4 points of fire damage per herbalist class
level with a DC 10 + 1/2 the herbalist's class level + the
herbalist's
Wisdom
modifier
Reflex
save
for
half.
Emperor's Candlesticks (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: Candlelit Lichen
Appearance: Long clusters of small, brilliantly yellow
blooms on thick stalks are the namesake of the emperor's
candlesticks.
Effect: One unit of Emperor's Candlesticks can be
applied to a weapon as though it were a poison. Creatures
dealt
damage
by
a
weapon
coated
in
emperor's
candlesticks must make a DC 10 + 1/2 the herbalist's class
level + the herbalist's Wisdom modifier Fortitude save or
suddenly start glowing. Creatures that are glowing shed
light like a candle and take 1d6 points of fire damage per
round. Each round, an affected creature can attempt a
new Fortitude save to end the effect. This effect persists
for rounds equal to the herbalist's class level. Unlike most
other poisons, emperor's candlesticks affects pretty much
anything and will affect any and all corporeal creatures.
Harmony Fruit (fruit)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 1
Recipes: none
Appearance: Found where the classic elements all meet
in one place, such as embedded in the sand of the
shoreline of a river just before the edge of a waterfall that
cascades over an extinct volcano, harmony fruits grow on
scraggly little trees that embody the fact that the classic
elements can indeed get along.
Effect: A branch of the harmony fruit tree can be hoisted
into the air and shaken about, causing the fruits to glow in
a multitude of colors. For one round, all creatures within
30 feet of the branch have resistance equal to the
herbalist's class level against acid, cold, electricity, fire,
and sonic damage. The herbalist must keep hold of the
harmony fruit branch for the duration of this effect in
order to gain this benefit.

Liana (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: Powdered Liana
Appearance: Rooted to the ground, the liana vine coils
up trees and other rigid structures to break through to the
canopy above. It is also the primary ingredient for a
particularly potent psychoactive tea.
Effect: When one unit of liana is chewed as a standard
action, the chewer's vision warps and twists, making
everything hard to discern. Strangely, the rest of the
world notices a warping and twisting on the part of the
chewer. Attacks made against and by the chewer are
treated as though the target has 20% concealment. This
effect persists for 1 round per herbalist level.
Madagascar Periwinkle (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: none
Appearance: Small, unobtrusive flowers of red, white,
and purple grow low to the ground in a blanket of green.
Though there are various species with different petal
shapes, all Madagascar periwinkles have four petals.
Effect: When one unit of Madagascar periwinkle is
chewed as a standard action, the chewer gains a +2 bonus
to saving throws against gaze attacks. This effect persists
for 1 round per herbalist level.
Manmelter Vine (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 1
Recipes: none
Appearance: A rather unremarkable vine with some
darker green striping along its length, manmelter vine
produces a liquid that can liquefy most organic material,
including human flesh.
Effect: When one unit of manmelter vine is thrown as a
splash weapon, it wraps around whatever creature is in
the square in which it lands, dealing 1d4 points of damage
per herbalist class level each round for 2 rounds with a
DC 10 +1/2 the herbalist's class level + the herbalist's
Wisdom modifier Fortitude save to negate damage. Each
time the victim makes a successful Fortitude save, it takes
a ­2 penalty to subsequent saves against that particular
manmelter vine. At 11th level, manmelter vine's duration
is increased to 3 rounds. Ripping manmelter vine off of a
creature is a DC 10 + 1/2 the herbalist's class level + the
herbalist's
Wisdom
modifier
Strength
check.
If
manmelter vine lands in an empty square as a result of
being thrown, it can be picked back up and used again.
Inorganic creatures and creatures that are already liquid,
such as elementals, incorporeal creatures, constructs not
made of flesh and bone, and oozes, are totally unaffected
by manmelter vine.
Given manmelter vine's homicidal nature, it cannot be
placed into a cultivation pot.
Night­blooming Jasmine (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 1
Recipes: Powdered Liana
Appearance: The night­blooming jasmine features thin,
elongated leaves and cream­colored balls for blooms.
Extremely fragrant, this plant does much to make itself
appealing
despite
its
particularly
potent
toxicity
to
humanoids.
Effect: As a standard action, one unit of night­blooming
jasmine can be held aloft and shaken to let loose all of its
fragrance at once. Extremely calming, this fragrance
forces each creature within 30 feet of the herbalist to
make a DC 10 + 1/2 herbalist's class level + herbalist's
Wisdom modifier Will save or lose the benefit of all
morale bonuses and the detriment of all fear effects
currently in effect on that creature. This is considered a
suppression effect and not a hard dispel. Morale and fear
effects with a limited duration continue to expend
duration while suppressed in this fashion. This effect lasts
as long as the creature is in the fragrance and for 1d4+1
rounds after it leaves. Any creature that succeeds on its
save but remains in the fragrance must continue to save
each round on the herbalist's turn. The fragrance persists
for 1 round per herbalist level and can be blown away as
though it were a fog cloud.
If Night­blooming Jasmine is placed in a cultivation pot,
that herbalist is considered to be permanently under the
effects of Night­blooming Jasmine with no saving throws
allowed. This effect is ignored if the herbalist has the
Cultivator Savant (Night­blooming Jasmine) feat. As this
represents a limited immunity to the night­blooming
jasmine, an herbalist with such a feat gains a +4 bonus to
saving throws against the plant.
Pineapple (fruit)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: Assorted Fruit Wine
Appearance: A large, spiny aggregate fruit with juicy
yellow flesh underneath, the ubiquitous pineapple is the
most popular produce projectile among halflings the
world over.
Effect: Pineapple has two uses.
1: When one unit of pineapple is chewed as a standard
action, the chewer's mouth is flooded with juicy positive
energy, healing 1d8 + twice the herbalist's class level
points of damage.
2: An herbalist can brew a batch of fresh fermented
pineapple juice in a mere 10 minutes. A batch of
fermented pineapple juice contains one dose, which can
be consumed as though it were a potion. When consumed,
the drinker's mouth is flooded with positive energy,
healing 1d8 + the herbalist's class level points of damage.

Pink Wood Sorrel (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: none
Appearance: The pink wood sorrel is characterized by
its elongated pink blooms that grow in an umbel­like
configuration. The stem of the pink wood sorrel is very
thin
and
less
springy
than
would
be
expected.
Effect: As a standard action, an herbalist can will pink
wood sorrel to become a highly toxic ingested poison for
10 minutes per herbalist class level. A creature with a
bone skeleton who consumes one unit of pink wood sorrel
must make a DC 10 + 1/2 herbalist's class level +
herbalist's Wisdom modifier Fortitude save or have its
skeleton attacked by the oxalic acid within the sorrel,
rendering it staggered until it receives one day of bed rest
or any effect that restores ability damage, such as the
lesser restoration spell. There is a one hour delay between
the consumption of the herb and the poison taking effect.
Creatures who consume only a partial dose receive a +4
bonus to their saving throw.
Root Spore (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 2
Recipes: Leech Spore
Appearance: Root spore is a root parasite that looks like
the spherical fruit of a brown fungus, but it is, in fact, a
flowering plant.
Effect: When one unit of root spore is thrown as a splash
weapon, it clings to whatever creature is in the square in
which it lands, dealing acid damage equal to 1d4 + 1/3 the
herbalist's class level each round for rounds equal to half
the herbalist's class level, minimum 1. As it deals damage,
a fat nodule begins to grow from the base of the root
spore. If eaten by the herbalist as though it were a potion,
this nodule heals the herbalist for an amount equal to the
damage dealt by the root spore. A root spore "potion"
retains its potency for 1 minute. Ripping root spore off of
a creature is a DC 10 + 1/2 the herbalist's class level + the
herbalist's Wisdom modifier Strength check. If root spore
lands in an empty square as a result of being thrown, it
can be picked back up and used again.
Screaming Creeper (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 2
Recipes: Chromatic Dragon's Maw, Elemental Cattail,
Elemental Chokevine
Appearance: This bright green vine lets out an ear­
piercing scream whenever it is disturbed. Given most
creatures find this sound to be quite unpleasant, animals
leave it alone. Many other plants have adapted to show
this exact same hue in an effort to not get eaten.
Effect: When one unit of screaming creeper is minimally
processed and applied to a metal weapon as though it
were an oil, the weapon begins to hum. The weapon deals
additional sonic damage equal to 1 + 1/4 the herbalist's
class
level
(max
6).
These
effects
last
for
1
minute
per
herbalist
class
level.
Seerbrush (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 2
Recipe: Twisted Fate
Appearance: Like the outstretched arms of an ancient
oracle toward an unseen goal, seerbrush's dry and
scraggly brown stems are always splayed out awkwardly,
but in a common direction.
Effect: When chewed as a standard action, seerbrush
grants the chewer insight into the future. As an immediate
action, the chewer may elect to reroll a single d20 roll,
albeit at a ­4 penalty, before the results of the roll are
known. This reroll must be used within 1 round per
herbalist level.
Shaman's Assistant (herb)
Biome(s): Tropics
Capacity: 4
Recipes: Leech Spore
Appearance: Found near the phosphorous­rich remains
of campfires in the hot and wet areas of the world, the
fast­growing shaman's assistant is a thin creeper with
pure
white
blooms
and
a
very
potent
scent.
Effect: When chewed as a standard action, the next
source of ability drain dealt to the chewer deals ability
damage instead. This effect persists for 1 round per
herbalist level.
Ubiquitine (herb)
Biomes: All
Capacity: 4
Recipes: All, Extremophilic Elixir, Heart's Blood,
Ubiquitium
Appearance:
Ubiquitine
is
thoroughly
generic
in
appearance and needs. In fact, it is so generic that it can
grow in any biome; however, it is specific about its generic
needs. In other words, given it has become hardy enough
to impose upon itself a sense of aesthetics, it only grows in
places that are themselves suitably generic.
Effect: Ubiquitine has two uses.
1: A single unit of ubiquitine can replace a single unit of
any other herb in a recipe. Use of two or more units of
ubiquitine in this fashion will cause the concoction to be
too generic and the recipe will fail. As it is, the use of one
unit of ubiquitine in this fashion increases the Craft DC of
the recipe by +5.
2: A unit of ubiquitine can mimic the effects of any one
other herb found with it in a particular compress.
If two plants in cultivation pots other than ubiquitine
itself ever share a biome of origin, any ubiquitine in
cultivation
pots
gets
upset
about
how
aesthetically
unpleasing its surroundings are and promptly dies.

Craft DC: 24
Capacity: Special
Effect: An herbalist can brew a batch of fresh assorted
fruit wine in a mere 10 minutes. A batch of assorted fruit
wine contains 2 doses, which can be consumed as though
they were potions. When consumed, the drinker's mouth
is flooded with positive energy, healing 1d8 + twice the
herbalist's
class
level
points
of
damage.
Candlelit Lichen
Ingredients: 1 Acidbite Lichen, 2 Emperor's Candlesticks
Craft DC: 25
Capacity: 2
Effect: When one unit of candlelit lichen is thrown as a
splash weapon, it clings to whatever creature is in the
square in which it lands, dealing acid damage equal to 1d6
+ 1/2 the herbalist's class level and 1d4 fire damage each
round for 1 minute. Ripping candlelit lichen off of a
creature is a DC 10 + 1/2 the herbalist's class level + the
herbalist's Wisdom modifier Strength check that deals
2d6 points of fire damage if unsuccessful. If candlelit
lichen lands in an empty square as a result of being
thrown, it can be picked back up and used again.
Chromatic Dragon's Maw
Ingredients: 2 Dragon's Maw, 1 of any combination of ­
Flametongue, Frostcap, Puckerberry, Screaming Creeper
Craft DC: 24
Capacity: 2
Effect: One unit of chromatic dragon's maw can be
swung in a wide arc as a standard action, creating a 15­
foot cone of energy that deals 1d6 points of energy
damage of the appropriate type (fire if made with
flametongue, cold if made with frostcap, acid if made with
puckerberry, 1d4 sonic if made with screaming creeper)
damage per herbalist class level with a DC 10 + 1/2 the
herbalist's class level + the herbalist's Wisdom modifier
Reflex save for half.
Elemental Cattail
Ingredients: 1 Cattail, 1 of any combination of ­
Flametongue, Frostcap, Puckerberry, Screaming Creeper
Craft DC: 24
Capacity: 2
Effect: The herbalist may swing one unit of elemental
cattail as though it were a club of her size. If it hits, it
deals bludgeoning damage and explodes in a cloud of
elementally­charged fluff. This fluff sticks to the creature
damaged by the cattail and deals 1d6 points of the
appropriate
energy
damage
(fire
if
made
with
flametongue, cold if made with frostcap, acid if made with
puckerberry, 1d4 sonic if made with screaming creeper)
each round for rounds equal to the herbalist's class level.
In addition to the elemental damage, the fluff behaves as
the glitterdust spell, but can be scraped off by dealing
damage equal to twice the herbalist's level to it. Given it is
immobile and stuck to the creature in question, the
creature automatically hits the fluff. Others trying to help
the creature must hit an AC of 10. Given this not a magical
effect, the fluff is permanent and will not go away unless
removed manually.
Note that although elemental cattail can be wielded as
though it were a club, it cannot be affected by herbs that
act as oils or poisons.
Elemental Chokevine
Ingredients: 1 Chokevine, 1 of any combination of ­
Flametongue, Frostcap, Puckerberry, Screaming Creeper
Craft DC: 24
Capacity: 2
Effect: The herbalist may swing a unit of elemental
chokevine as though it were a whip of her size. If it hits,
the herbalist immediately makes a grapple attempt as a
free action with a bonus to her CMB equal to 1 + 1/5 the
herbalist's class level for using the chokevine (max CMB
+5). If the grapple check is successful, the vine leaves the
herbalist's
hand
and
the
victim
is
strangled
and
constricted, dealing 2d4 points of constriction damage
and the herbalist's level / 2 points of the appropriate
energy damage (fire if made with flametongue, cold if
made with frostcap, acid if made with puckerberry, sonic
if made with screaming creeper) each round for rounds
equal to half the herbalist's class level (minimum 1).
Further, the victim cannot speak, breathe, or cast spells
with a verbal component for the active duration of a
choking
chokevine.
Unique
anatomical
features
can
render this strangulation effect partially to completely
ineffective. This choke may be broken by a successful
combat maneuver check against a CMD of the herbalist's
CMD + 1 + 1/5 her herbalist class level (max CMD + 5). If
the grapple check is not successful, the chokevine goes
limp and cannot be used again.
Note that although elemental chokevine can be wielded as
though it were a whip, it cannot be affected by herbs that
act as oils or poisons.
