The Game 
 
The core rulebook is everything you need to 
get started on your epic fantasy adventure, 
with emphasis placed on customizability 
and creativity. 
 
 
The Players 
 
The players are, well, you. One of the two 
most important aspects of the game, the 
players are the ones who drive the story 
forward. Through their characters, players 
explore and interact with the intricate (or not 
so intricate) world. The other of the two 
most important aspects is the one who 
builds the world, that being… 
 
 
The Architect 
 
Across the vast plane of reality exist many 
different universes, each shaped and 
overseen by their Architect. Sometimes 
referred to as Game Master or GM, the 
Architect is responsible for their world and 
managing everything upon it. They control 
the shape and flow of everything the players 
touch, they craft a reaction to every player 
action, they control every NPC (Non Player 
Character). The Architect’s goal is not to 
beat the players, but to challenge them. 
Their role is to oversee the campaign, the 
adventure on which the players will embark. 
 
 
 
 
 
The World 
 
The world of Calamity Mythos is not one, 
but many. Some are ancient; teeming with 
adventure or destruction, ripe for a new 
Architect. Others are newly born into the 
cosmos, able to be shaped to any desire. 
Ultimately, the choice is yours. 
 
 
Calculations 
 
This game requires the usage of addition 
(+), subtraction (-), multiplication (*), and 
division (/). Anytime a decimal or fraction 
comes up, always round down. A calculator 
is not required, but is recommended. In 
addition, there are aspects of the game that 
require variable amounts of chance. Two 
different types of dice are used in order to 
fulfill this; D6s (six-sided dice), and D20s 
(20-sided dice). 
 
 
 
Exploration 
 
Gameplay is divided into two basic sections; 
exploration, and combat. Exploration is all 
about roleplaying; you have full control over 
your choices, you decide how your 
character acts or reacts to a situation. The 
Architect controls the narrative, but the 
players control how their characters act. 
 
 
Timescale 
 
Time is something that no one can avoid, 
and there are certain aspects of both 
exploration and combat that are based on 


 
4 
the flow of time. Whether it be 2 hours until 
bandits attack a village, a volcano that will 
erupt in 1 hour, or the moon falling to earth 
in 3 days, there are many situations that rely 
on the passage of time. While not an issue 
in combat, it falls to the Architect’s 
discretion to determine how fast game time 
passes during exploration. For cases of less 
than 20 minutes, the Architect might have 
things play out in real time. For cases of 
several hours, the Architect might instead 
separate a day into 6 hour blocks of 
morning, midday, evening, and night. 
 
 
Traveling 
 
Players will often journey across long 
distances, which can be very boring to 
depict. In these cases, it's simply faster to 
skip to the destination. Other times 
however, the journey itself is the campaign. 
 
 
Skill Checks 
 
When a character attempts a task that they 
cannot easily do, the Architect will 
determine whether or not they must perform 
a skill check. For more on skills, see page 8. 
When performing a skill check, the Architect 
will decide a number called a difficulty score 
that determines the difficulty of what you are 
trying to do. They are not required to say 
how high the difficulty score is. Roll a D20, 
then add the skill’s level to the result. If the 
total is equal to or greater than the difficulty 
score, the attempt succeeds. Otherwise, it 
fails. Some skill checks might even require 
more than one skill. Generally, difficulty 
scores will adhere to the following baseline: 
 
 
Easy - less than 9. 
Normal - 9-12. 
Moderate - 13-16. 
 
 
Below are some example difficulty scores. 
 
Climbing - Athletics 10. 
 
Tracking a creature through woods - 
Survival 16. 
 
Resisting poison - Fortitude 14. 
 
Fixing a clock - Repair 15. 
 
Binding a leg wound - Medicine 15. 
 
Using nearby plants to treat a leg wound - 
Medicine 10, Survival 7. 
 
Pro tips for Architects 
 
When deciding a difficulty score, keep in 
mind the average roll of a D20 is 10.5, 
and the average skill level at level 1 is 3.  
 
Skill checks should only be used when 
there is a reasonable chance of failure, 
you wouldn’t expect a seasoned 
locksmith to fail at picking a simple lock. 
 
If you want to keep your players on their 
toes, ask for Perception skill checks at 
random intervals. 
 
 
 
 
Skill Checks Against NPCs 
 
Whether it’s interrogation through 
persuasion or intimidation, or a duel of wits, 
there will be times when you are required to 
make skill checks against NPCs (Non

Player Characters). In most cases your skill 
+ 1D20 must be greater than the NPC’s skill 
+ 1D20. 
 
 
Advantage and 
Disadvantage 
 
There are many things which might help or 
hinder you when rolling for some specific 
task, in which case you would have either 
advantage or disadvantage. When you have 
advantage on a roll, roll 2 D20s and use the 
higher roll. When you have disadvantage on 
a roll, roll 2 D20s and use the lower roll. If 
you gain advantage or disadvantage from 
more than one source, roll 1 additional D20 
per source; if you have two different abilities 
that grant advantage, roll 3D20 and use the 
highest roll. Advantage and disadvantage 
also negate each other; if you gain 1 stack 
of advantage and 1 stack of disadvantage, 
only roll 1D20. 
 
 
Resting and Sleeping 
 
Unless your character is nonliving, they can 
only function for so long without rest. When 
out of combat, characters can either rest or 
sleep. Resting can happen so long as 
nothing more strenuous than a light stroll is 
done for at least 20 minutes. While resting, 
characters who are below 1/4th of their max 
Health will heal up to that point. Characters 
who are below 1/4th of their max Mana will 
also recover up to that point. In addition, 
you can also eat 1 meal while resting. 
Different meals will heal you further. Resting 
can be done 3 times per day. 
 
Characters must sleep for a certain number 
of hours to fully heal and regain all spent 
Mana and psionic arts. Most species must 
spend 6 hours per day sleeping, during 
which they are completely unaware of their 
surroundings. A character does not gain any 
benefits of sleeping more than once per 
day, nor do they gain any if sleep is 
interrupted. 
 
 
Conditions 
 
Conditions are effects that can be imposed 
by a variety of scenarios. Many of these will 
make more sense after the Character 
Creation section. 
 
Blinded - You cannot see, -10 to defense 
and -20 to any rolls that require sight. 
 
Deafened - You cannot hear, you fail any 
checks that require hearing and are 
considered outside of earshot of any powers 
or effects. 
 
Hungry - You gain disadvantage on all rolls. 
This condition can be gained by not eating 
for a prolonged period, left to the Architect’s 
discretion. 
 
Prone - You are knocked to the ground; 
gain disadvantage on all attack rolls, and all 
attack rolls against you gain advantage. 
Standing up consumes 1 Pace. 
 
Restless - You gain disadvantage on all 
rolls. This condition can be gained by not 
sleeping for a prolonged period, left to the 
Architect’s discretion. 
 
Restrained - You are unable to use any 
Stamina, except to resist restrainment. 
 
Stunned - You are unable to make any 
actions, and fail any checks.

Unconscious - You are unaware of your 
surroundings, unable to use any actions, fail 
any checks, and any attacks against you 
automatically succeed.  
 
 
 
Character Creation 
 
Everything you need as a player to create 
your avatar. Several stats are reliant on 
other stats, so make sure to review all 
aspects of character creation before you try 
actually making one. 
 
 
 
Core Stats 
 
These are the first basic elements of all 
characters.  
 
 
Health 
 
Health Represents your life force, how 
much damage you can take. Health is 
depleted by taking damage. Dropping to 0 
Health means you are unconscious, and 
going into negative Health means you’re 
dead. A character who causes another to 
drop to 0 or negative Health can choose 
whether to kill or knock them out. 
Max Health is determined by the following 
formula: Vitality + lineage bonus + path 
bonus + (lineage bonus per level + path 
bonus per level) * level. 
 
 
Shield 
 
Shield acts similarly to Health; unless stated 
otherwise, it drains in place of Health and is 
independent of both wellbeing and any rules 
involving Health or damage. Shield is not 
inherent to a person, it typically represents 
the usage of a technique or outside force to 
negate or soften a blow. For a mage, it 
might represent a personal barrier created 
by a spell. For a barbarian, it might 
represent their near supernatural 
endurance. If you have multiple sources of 
shield, they do not stack and you must 
choose which to apply.  
 
 
Mana 
 
Mana is what fuels spells, and is consumed 
when using magic. Magic requires Mana to 
use, with the amount depending on the 
spell. Max Mana is determined by the 
following formula: Wisdom + lineage bonus 
+ path bonus + (lineage bonus per level + 
path bonus per level) * level. 
 
 
Defense 
 
Your first line of, well, defense. This 
represents your ability to either block or 
evade an attack. Defense deals in 
absolutes, you either nullify damage or get 
hit in full.  
 
Your base defense is 10 + current level / 3 
rounded down. Additional sources of 
defense might include feats, and powers. 
 
 


 
7 
Armor 
 
Armor acts as a damage absorber, subtract 
it from any damage taken before applying. 
However, it cannot reduce damage below 1. 
Armor typically comes from items such as 
suits of armor, but can also come from feats 
and powers. 
 
 
Pace 
 
Pace represents how many yards you can 
move per turn in combat. 1 Pace is 
equivalent to 1 yard, or 1 inch on a 
battlemap. Pace is determined by the 
following formula: (Vitality + Reflex) / 4 
rounded down + lineage bonus + path 
bonus 
 
 
Stamina 
 
Stamina represents how many actions you 
can take during a single round in combat. 
Your Stamina is Reflex / 3 rounded down. 
 
 
Size 
 
Different species grow to different sizes, 
even two people in the same species might 
vary greatly in height and weight. Size 
determines many things, such as where you 
can fit, how hard you are to grapple, etc. 
Each race and monster will have a size 
value of tiny, small, medium, large, gigantic, 
or colossal.  
 
Tiny - Up to 4 tiny creatures can occupy the 
same 1 square yard area as another 
creature. In other words, 5 tiny creatures or 
4 tiny and 1 small or medium creature can 
occupy the same square yard. 
 
Small, Medium - Occupies a 1 square yard. 
These sizes are mostly similar, with one 
difference being ease of grappling. 
 
Large - Occupies a 2 square yard area. 
 
Gigantic - Occupies an area of 3-4 square 
yds. 
 
Colossal - Occupies an area of 5 square 
yds or more.  
 
Each lineage also gives an average for both 
height and weight. 
 
 
 
Attributes 
 
Attributes are another core aspect of your 
character. They represent your physical and 
mental attributes, and determine how well 
you can perform various tasks. Each 
attribute also has a modifier, which is used 
for skills and skill checks. At level 1, a 
character has 73 points that can be divided 
among the attributes in any way. 
 
Attribute modifier = attribute / 3 rounded 
down. 
 
Strength - Represents your physical 
strength; how much you can lift, how hard 
you can swing a sword, how large of a bow 
you can wield, etc. 
 
Vitality - Represents your durability, 
resistance to ailments, how much Health 
you have, etc.

Reflex - Represents your reaction speed 
and precision. 
 
Charisma - Represents your ability to 
convince, intimidate, charm, and lie. 
 
Intelligence - Represents your perception 
and ability to learn and reason. 
 
Wisdom - Represents your common sense, 
knowledge, and spell casting ability. 
 
Will - Represents your willpower and ability 
to resist intimidation, magic, and mind-
altering effects. 
 
Luck - Unlike the other attributes, luck does 
not contribute to any skills or have a 
modifier, and starts at 0 instead of 1. Luck 
controls how many fate dice you have, 
which are D6s that you can add to any roll 
you make.  
 
Only one fate die can be added to a roll 
initially. Once used, a fate die is consumed 
and cannot be used again until sleeping. If a 
6 is rolled on a fate die, you can roll an 
additional fate die without expending one, 
up to a maximum of 7 fate dice (+42 in 
total). The number of fate dice you gain is 
Luck / 3 rounded down.

perform various tasks. Each skill will fall 
under the category of one of the attributes, 
and uses that attribute’s modifier.

restrain your opponent, or prevent your 
opponent from restraining you. 
 
Heavy Weapons - Represents your skill 
with heavy weapons such as great swords 
and polearms. 
 
Unarmed - Represents your skill in hand to 
hand combat. 
 
 
Vitality 
 
Athletics - Represents your skill in such 
things as swimming, climbing, etc. 
 
Fortitude - Represents your resistance to 
fatigue and toxic substances. 
 
 
Reflex 
 
Archery - Represents your skill with bows, 
crossbows, and firearms. 
 
Light Weapons - Represents your skill with 
weapons such as daggers and one-handed 
swords. 
 
Thievery - Represents skills such as 
lockpicking, pickpocketing, and stealthiness. 
 
 
Charisma 
 
Deception - Represents your ability to lie. 
 
Intimidation - Represents your ability to 
convince with more than just words.

Powers 
 
Powers are abilities that can be used in and 
out of combat. You have a total number of 
powers equal to Level / 2 rounded down + 
3, selected from your lineage or path lists. 
Upon leveling up, you can reselect any 
powers. 
 
Powers are read as the following: 
 
(Name) | (Power type) 
Requirements: (Any requirements needed 
to use or unique qualities) 
Range: (Maximum number of yards it can 
reach) 
Refresh: (Number to roll or exceed to use 
again) 
Cost: (Amount of Mana needed) 
Charge: (Number of turns it takes to 
activate) 
 
(Any effects the power has) 
 
 
Refresh 
 
Even if you have the Stamina to do so, 
some powers can’t be immediately reused; 
after using a power with a refresh value, roll 
1D20. If the result is greater than or equal to 
the refresh value, the power becomes 
usable again on the same turn. Otherwise, 
continue rolling 1D20 at the start of each of 
your turns until the roll is greater than or 
equal to the value. If you have multiple 
powers that need to refresh, you can use 
the same 1D20 roll for all of them.

Cost 
 
Many powers, most notably spells, consume 
Mana upon activation. With exception to 
psionic arts, any power that has a cost will 
not activate if you do not have enough 
Mana. 
 
 
Charge 
 
Some powers have a charge up time, which 
determines how many turns it takes for the 
power to activate.  
 
No charge means that the power activates 
immediately. For a charge up time of more 
than 0, any additional actions taken will 
reset the number of turns needed. Mana is 
only consumed after the charge up time 
finishes. You can choose to cancel the 
power while in the process of charging. 
 
 
Power types 
 
Powers fall into one of four categories; 
martial, spellcasting, psionic, and feats.  
 
 
Martial 
 
Martial powers rely on your physical or 
martial prowess, typically forgoing Mana 
usage in favor of cooldowns. 
 
 
Spellcasting 
 
Spells are the primary consumer of Mana. 
Some spells require that certain items be 
consumed in order to cast them. items are 
only consumed upon successful casting. 
Some spells can be enhanced by spending 
more Mana than required, this requires the 
Magic Overcharge feat. 
 
 
Psionics 
 
Psionic arts channel a character’s willpower, 
allowing them to shape reality with their 
mind. While willpower can never be fully 
depleted, overuse can place a strain on 
your mind. Psionic arts require Mana, 
similarly to spells. Unlike spells however, 
psionic arts can be used by consuming 
Health instead of Mana at a one-to-one 
exchange rate. 
 
 
Feats 
 
Feats are powers that act passively, 
providing different bonuses such as adding 
Reflex modifier to defense, dual wielding, or 
granting access to certain other powers.  
Some examples are below: 
 
 
Dual Wielding | Feat 
Requirements: Barbarian, Knight, Ranger, 
level 6  
 
Allows you to dual wield single handed 
weapons; make one attack with each 
weapon, using only one Stamina. For 
powers that use a melee weapon, perform 
the attack once per weapon.

Strength equal to or greater than the sum

Allows you to dual wield any two

Anti feats 
 
Anti feats are ones that give restrictions or 
negative effects, in exchange you gain 1 
additional  power point for every anti feat. 
 
 
Free powers 
 
Free powers are automatically applied when 
selecting certain lineages or paths, or 
meeting certain prerequisites; they do not 
use power points, and cannot be deselected 
or removed.  
 
 
Sometimes a power will have [Weapon], this 
indicates to use your weapon’s stat. 
 
 
Leveling up 
 
Defeating an enemy rewards the whole 
party with Experience Points (Exp, or 
sometimes simply XP). Each player can 
also receive Exp by succeeding skill checks 
outside of combat, gaining Exp equal to the 
difficulty score. After gaining enough Exp, 
you level up.  
 
When increasing your max Health or Mana, 
our current Health or Mana will increase to 
the new max only if you are at full Health or 
Mana.  
 
You have a number of attribute points equal 
to Level + 72 and a number of skill points 
equal to Level - 1, both of which can be 
used to increase any of their respective 
stats. You also have a number of power 
points equal to Level / 2 rounded down + 3, 
and may reselect any of your powers upon 
leveling up. 
 
Many powers, and even some items may 
have a level restriction; this is the minimum 
level needed to be able to use it. Starting 
from level 1 is recommended, but not 
required.

Combat 
 
The second basic section of the game, 
combat begins when two or more 
characters are hostile towards each other. 
Unlike exploration, combat is turn based. 
 
 
Rounds 
 
A round encompasses every character's 
turn. Each round represents 5 seconds of 
time, with each turn being nearly 
simultaneous. Turn order is decided by 
highest to lowest of 1D20 + level for each 
participant in the battle. Taking damage, 
being grappled, or otherwise getting caught 
off guard before combat starts will force 
disadvantage on a character's roll. 
 
 
Environment 
 
Unless stated otherwise, an attack or power 
requires line of sight.  
 
Unstable terrain - Unstable terrain imposes 
a -2 to all rolls that use Archery, Athletics, 
Grappling, Heavy Weapons, Light 
Weapons, Spellcasting, or Unarmed. 
 
 
Difficult terrain - Every 1 yard of difficult 
terrain requires 2 Pace to move through. 
 
 
Mounted combat 
 
Mounts, such as horses, have their own 
Pace that you will use. Riding a mount 
grants you advantage on melee attack and 
power rolls. 
 
 
Damage 
 
Although it is possible to take damage 
outside of combat, that vast majority of it will 
be dealt during it. 
 
 
Damage types 
 
Damage comes in many different types; 
swords slash, arrows pierce, maces use 
bludgeoning, etc. All damage types are as 
follows: Slashing, piercing, bludgeoning, 
fire, frost, electric, poison, acid, holy, 
necrotic, demonic, magical, and psychic. 
 
 
Resistances and 
weaknesses 
 
Some creatures might be weak to one type 
of damage, and some powers or items 
might grant resistance to another type of 
damage. If a character has resistance to a 
damage type, any damage taken from that 
type is halved. If a character has weakness 
to a damage type, any damage taken from 
that type is doubled. 
 
Many attacks will have multiple damage 
types; i.e., magic attacks usually have at 
least 1 other damage type, and holy, 
necrotic, and demonic attacks are almost 
always considered magic by default. In 
cases where a character takes damage that 
they are both resistant and weak to, both 
effects cancel each other out. 
 


 
13 
Rarely, some creatures will be fully immune 
to certain damage types. Immunity only 
counts towards 1 specific damage type; if a 
creature has immunity to fire damage but 
not electric damage, an attack that uses 
both types will deal full damage. 
 
 
Regeneration and fast 
healing 
 
Regeneration allows you to recover a set 
amount of Health at the start of each of your 
turns. Taking fire, acid, frost, necrotic, or 
holy damage without resistance to it will 
disable regeneration for your next turn.  
 
Fast healing is similar to regeneration, 
however losing any Health regardless of 
resistance or damage source will disable it 
for your next turn. 
 
 
Critical hits 
 
When making an attack roll, rolling a 20 on 
the die results in a critical hit. Critical hits do 
twice as much damage. 
 
 
Wounded 
 
When a character drops to below half 
Health, they are considered wounded. Many 
creatures will change how they act when 
wounded; they might become stronger, 
attempt to flee, gain new abilities, lose some 
abilities, etc. 
 
 
 
 
 
Actions 
 
As stated in Core Stats, each character can 
use Stamina to perform actions in combat. 
Stamina resets at the start of your next turn. 
Unless otherwise stated, every action 
consumes 1 Stamina. 
 
 
Movement 
 
Movement is best done by using square 
grids, with each square representing 1 yd. 
Alternatives can include hexagon grids, or 
forgoing a grid and measuring by hand. 
When using a square grid, you cannot move 
diagonally. 
 
Taking a movement action allows you to 
move up to your max Pace. You are able to 
halt your movement to take other actions, 
then continue up to your unused Pace. 
 
 
Attacking 
 
Attacking is the most common action taken 
in combat. Basic attacks use Unarmed, 
Heavy Weapons, Light Weapons, or 
Archery, depending on the weapon used. 
First compare your specific skill + 1D20 plus 
any other modifiers vs the target’s Defense. 
If your total is higher than your target’s 
Defense, you will do damage equal to the 
skill used by your weapon plus any 
modifiers, or your Unarmed skill if 
weaponless.

Powers 
 
All powers can be used outside of combat, 
but they will most often be used during it. 
Some powers use values other than 
Defense, in which case they will state which 
one. 
 
 
Grappling 
 
Your grappling skill + 1D20 vs your 
opponent’s grappling skill + 1D20. If 
successful, your opponent is restrained, and 
you can choose to either move them one 1 
yd, or attempt an unarmed attack if you 
have enough Stamina. Every subsequent 
turn, repeat the skill check. Attempting to 
grapple a target 2 sizes smaller than you 
grants you advantage, while attempting to 
grapple a target 2 sizes bigger than you 
grants you disadvantage. Tiny creatures 
cannot grapple non tiny creatures, but 
cannot be grappled by creatures of size  
equal to or greater than Large. 
 
 
Trading items 
 
Spend 1 Stamina to either pass, throw, or 
otherwise move an item to a nearby ally.  
 
 
Talking 
 
Unlike other actions, talking costs 0 
Stamina, and can be used any number of 
times. 
 
 
 
Fleeing 
 
There might be times where you get into a 
fight you simply cannot win. To exit combat 
early, consume all Stamina to make an 
Athletics check against each enemy within 
range to hit you. Succeeding every check 
allows you to escape, failing any of them 
forces you to remain. 
 
 
Counter Actions 
 
When targeted by an action, characters can 
use their Stamina to make certain actions. 
Creatures who attempt to flee from you, and 
thus make an Athletics check against you, 
count as targeting you with an action. 
 
 
Attacking 
 
Basic attacks and powers function mostly as 
normal, however any damage you inflict is 
dealt after your opponent’s action. 
 
 
Defending 
 
Spend 2 Stamina to add 1D20 to your 
Defense. 
 
 
Trading items 
 
Spend 1 Stamina to receive an item.
