Perplexing Puzzles #2: Puzzle Chests
What are they?
Puzzle chests are richly­decorated containers, ranging
from small enough to be jewelry boxes to enormous safes,
whose locking mechanism is tied to solving some sort of
logic puzzle rather than by inserting a key or entering a
combination. Typically guarded against magical intrusion
by a simple lead lining and featuring some of the finest
mechanical locking mechanisms ever to be produced,
puzzle chests invariably begin their existence as luxury
items commissioned by individuals of aristocratic means,
for they are the only individuals with the funds necessary
to purchase one. Some puzzle chests have since found
their way into the strange and unusual corners of the
world through theft and accident. Those who find them,
particularly poor, downtrodden creatures like kobolds,
may revere puzzle boxes simply for being shiny, and their
obvious association with being wealthy and powerful
dictates where most of them eventually end up within the
hierarchy of such a group.
Depending on who finds one and how well­constructed
the puzzle mechanism is, a puzzle box can remain locked
for years, if not centuries, its adopted owner considering
it to be nothing more than an attractive knickknack.
Given their sometimes­otherworldly designs, it is not
unheard of to have a puzzle box become an important
ritual object in a tribal religion that just so happens to use
similar iconography. Similarly, given the high artistry
typically exhibited by their construction, some cultures
may integrate a small puzzle box into their leader's badge
of office, be it the centerpiece of his crown, the physical
emblem of his shield, the head of his scepter, and so on.
Anatomy of a Puzzle Chest
Puzzle chests are represented by a "mechanism sheet" and
an "internal sheet". When the player characters encounter
a puzzle chest and wish to solve it, the GM passes out the
mechanism sheet, but keeps the internal sheet to himself.
The Mechanism Sheet
The mechanism sheet contains the following sections.
1: The Description ­ The description states what the chest
looks like and is largely there for fluff, though advanced
puzzle chests, those that use alternate geometry, also state
their special rules in this section.
2: Initial State ­ The initial state is the starting position
for a puzzle chest. To play a puzzle chest, line up dice to
match what is listed in this section. It is this line of dice
that the player manipulates when making moves.
3: Unlocking Combination ­ If your dice ever match what
is listed in this section, the puzzle is solved and the chest
opens! Well done!
4: Legal Moves ­ Legal moves show how the player
character attempting to solve the puzzle can exchange
dice for other dice in an effort to make his line of dice
match
the
unlocking
combination.
This
is
discussed in Section: Solving a Puzzle Chest.
The Internal Sheet
The internal sheet contains the following sections.
1: CR and Experience ­ A puzzle chest counts as an
encounter, giving it a CR and a corresponding experience
value all its own. Advanced puzzle chests, found in the
back of the book, have no standardized CR as they do not
have loot and traps already assigned to them.
2: Solution ­ Each puzzle chest has one or more shorthand
solutions written out for the GM.
3: Treasure ­ Each puzzle chest includes a flavorful stash
of treasure whose value is equivalent to standard loot for
its CR.
4: Additional Treasure ­ Each puzzle chest also includes
additional treasure whose value is equivalent to half
standard loot for its CR. This loot is awarded at GM's
discretion.
5: Lore ­ A lore section will be present if the treasure of a
puzzle chest has a provenance of some sort. This section
contains standard Knowledge skill challenges that award
paragraphs of background information.
6: Special Rules and Trap Triggers ­ If a puzzle chest
contains a trap, then that trap has a trigger. These triggers
are tied to the puzzle itself and tend to punish players for
trying to take the easy way out on the puzzle. Special rules
are optional.
7: Traps ­ If a puzzle chest contains one or more traps,
they are detailed here.

Solving a Puzzle Chest
When attempting to solve a puzzle chest, first line up
some dice so they match the initial state detailed by the
mechanism sheet. Let's assume the initial state of a
particular puzzle chest is 1 2 1 3, as shown below. Feel free
to line up some dice to match in order to follow along.
Let's also assume that one of the legal moves for a
particular puzzle is 1 3 ­­­> 2 1, as shown below.
The dice to the left of the arrow are known as the input,
while the dice to the right of the arrow are known as the
output. Input is done from left to right, but the dice
chosen do not need to be adjacent to each other. To
reiterate, if the input is 1 3, any combination of dice
including a 1 and a 3 is perfectly acceptable so long as the
1 is to the LEFT of the 3. By extension, if a legal move
requires three or more dice for input, say 3 2 1 ­­> 4, then
the 3 must be to the LEFT of the 2, which is to the LEFT
of the 1. The highlighted sets of dice below illustrate all
legal inputs for our example puzzle.
Remove the dice chosen as input.
Add the output dice for the chosen move. Output comes in
from the RIGHT in all standard puzzle chests and is
added in the order shown in that move.
And there you have it! That's all you need to know to play
any non­advanced puzzle chest. Just keep making moves
until it's solved! At any time, you can choose to reset the
puzzle to its initial state ­ just in case you get into a no­
win situation.
Reading Solution Shorthand
In order to save space, all solutions are written out in a
sort of shorthand. Those dice chosen for the input are
bolded, while those output dice to be added to the current
configuration are bolded and placed after a vertical bar ( |
). Remove all bolded dice to the left of the vertical bar and
add all dice to the right of the vertical bar to find the new
configuration. Let's take the two examples used in the
previous column for Section: Solving a Puzzle Chest to
illustrate.
1 2 1 3 | 2 1
1 2 1 3 | 2 1
In the first shorthand example, which corresponds to the
first configuration with yellow highlighting, the leftmost 1
and the 3, both bolded, are used as the input for the move
1 3 ­­> 2 1. The 2 and the 1, output for the move, are to the
right of the vertical bar and are bolded. The second
configuration
uses
the
rightmost
1
instead,
but
is
otherwise identical. This leaves us with the following
configurations.
2 1 2 1
1 2 2 1
Advanced chests may have their own special shorthand,
particularly if they start having dice come in from the left
or support vertical movement. This advanced shorthand is
discussed with puzzles that use it.
Unlocking by Alternate Means and Traps
Puzzle chests are differentiated from typical treasure
chests in that they have what is known as a puzzle
mechanism in place of a more conventional keyhole or
lock. Puzzle mechanisms are entirely self­contained and,
as such, cannot be unlocked in the traditional lockpicking
sense. There's simply no hole. To dissuade wizards, a thin
shell
of
lead
is
built
into
each
and
every
puzzle
mechanism, making unlocking a puzzle chest by magical
means utterly impossible. As lead is also the poor man's
divination blocker, it is impossible to use divination spells
to determine anything about the contents of the puzzle
chest, including any integrated traps. Divination spells
that contact a deity or other such entity are an exception
to this rule, as the deity is assumed to have been watching
the last time the puzzle chest was opened.
Despite these precautions, a savvy trapper can still detect
and disarm any traps that are integrated with a puzzle
chest as though it were a normal trap. Whenever a trapper
detects such an integrated trap, he also learns that trap's
triggering special rule. If there is a special rule that is not
attached to a trap, the only way to learn it is by trial and
error, meaning a trap­free puzzle chest can be more
frustrating than one that actively tries to kill. If a puzzle
chest is destroyed, it counts as having been opened,
though any integrated trap is triggered, even if it had
already been disarmed. No XP is awarded when a chest is
destroyed. Further, the chest's value is reduced by 75%
and half of the chest's contents are destroyed. If a chest
holds one item, there is a 50% chance it is ruined.
