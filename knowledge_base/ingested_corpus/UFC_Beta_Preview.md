2) CHARACTER CREATION
The Urban Fantasy Codex uses the standard 
character creation rules for normal (or 
normie) humans, familiar to both players 
of Everywhen and Barbarians of Lemuria. 
Not so standard, however, is the possibility 
for players to create monster heroes 
(creatures), or those on the cusp of the 
supernatural (exotics). 
While there are rules mechanics to 
balance these origins, they do not succeed 
completely. ‘Balance’ becomes abstract 
when characters can turn into a bat and 
shrug off small-arms fire! Asymmetric power 
levels are part of the fun in running an urban 
fantasy game, though this requires special 
consideration by the GM (see pg. XX).
•	 Normies are vanilla humans with no 
supernatural abilities. Though they 
may have had a brush with eldritch 
forces, none of it rubbed off. Virgins are 
normies who haven’t encountered the 
supernatural—yet. Any skepticism they 
possess won’t survive their first adventure! 
•	 Exotics are people with a connection to 
the supernatural, though not to the point 
of being a creature. This connection can 
be subtle. Sorcerers, psychics, and priests 
with Faith Points require the exotic origin, 
as do certain boons (Second Sight, True 
Believer, etc.).

•	 Creatures can be straight-up monsters, 
though not necessarily evil. Ghosts, 
vampires, werewolves, zombies, 
dhampir, ghouls, demons, et al., number 
themselves among creatures. They can 
be very powerful, though at the same 
time constrained by powerful flaws.
Even if player characters start with a normie 
origin, that doesn’t mean they stay that 
way. For example, a normal human might 
come across a grimoire of ancient spells 
during play. After spending XP to take a 
rank in the sorcerer career and the Arcane 
boon, the character effectively becomes 
an exotic. Likewise, a normie bitten by a 
vampire and Turned (see Powers, pg. XX) 
becomes a creature.
Creatures are so fundamentally different 
they get their own chapter for character 
creation (see pg. XX). 
The Urban Fantasy Codex uses the resolve 
mechanic (see Everywhen, pg. 15). Resolve 
simulates willpower and a person’s overall 
grip on reality. It also serves as lifeblood for 
incorporeal creatures like ghosts or demons, 
at least until they can find a body to possess!
Finally, players can design their own factions 
as part of character creation, with the GM’s 
approval. Factions are organizations within 
the setting, from local to world-spanning, 
with their own agendas and impact on play. 
See chapter XX for more details.
YOUR ‘FIRST’
When creating an initial character concept, 
a good starting point is to consider how 
and when your hero first contacted the 
supernatural—assuming they have. Even 
most creatures were human, once. What 
happened that set them on their current 
path?
A related technique for supernatural 
characters is to imagine a ‘thread’ or link 
connecting them to the world of the 
mundane.
Examples
Reggie Trevino used to work security on 
the graveyard shift. One night his usual 
subway line went down, forcing him 
to take an alternate home. This route 
stretched through an older section of 
tunnels—older, and not well maintained. 
His subway car came to a sudden, 
screeching stop. Reg has only a dim 
memory of what happened next, but he 
recalls pale, cadaverous faces peering 
in through the car’s windows . . . and 
screams. 
Sabine Deroy grew up in a crumbling 
chateau somewhere in the Loire Valley. 
Though her childhood was a blur, she 
remembers her mother warning her to 
“never leave the castle.” By reading 
old books, Sabine deduced she is a 
dhampir; the product of an unholy 
union between life and death. She has 
since escaped to Paris with the help of 
Claude, a groundskeeper, but has found 
the City of Lights to be anything but 
accommodating.
YOU CAN’T GO BACK
Once you’ve encountered the 
supernatural—or, if a creature, made some 
kind of connection to the mundane world—
your old, ‘safe’ existence is gone. Imagine 
trying to resume life as an accountant after 
you discover your stepsister is a werewolf; 
what if there are others like her? What 
happens at the next family reunion?
This conflict between your old life (or unlife) 
and a new one makes a great entry point 
for starting a campaign. In a modern setting, 
having actual motives to adventure is 
important, and usually more complex than 
‘killing things and taking their treasure.’

ORIGINS
In the Urban Fantasy Codex, origins aren’t 
based on geographical location. Instead, 
they define a character’s relationship with 
the supernatural. All these origins, including 
creatures, use the standard character 
creation rules, except where noted.
Normies don’t have access to supernatural 
abilities, like spells, creature powers, 
or certain boons. They are still quite 
competent, thanks to the cinematic nature 
of the Everywhen system. Heroic hunters, 
regular folks who’ve had their world turned 
upside down by the supernatural, helpful 
college profs, students turned investigators—
these are all normies.  
•	 Use the standard character creation 
rules from Everywhen. For ‘gritty’ or lower-
powered characters, see the sidebar on 
pg. XX. 
•	 Virgins (normies who haven’t had any 
contact with the supernatural) get the 
Skeptic flaw for ‘free.’ If they survive their 
first adventure, they can trade Skeptic 
for a free boon, subject to the GM’s 
approval.
Exotics can tap into supernatural forces 
when needed. This might be anything from 
a master sorcerer capable of summoning 
demons to a waitress with the Second Sight 
boon. Mystics who possess psychic abilities, 
sorcerers, and priests with access to Faith 
Points require the exotic origin, as do boons 
like True Believer and Sensitive. 
•	 Exotics don’t get a ‘free’ boon at 
character creation. Every boon they 
take requires a flaw or a reduction in 
Hero Points, as standard. 
•	 Some careers, like sorcerer, are 
considered corrupting (see Magic, pg. 
XX). 
Creatures are the stuff of dark legends. 
These might be skulking ghouls, ‘soldiers’ 
from a vampire crime family, antiquarian 
mummies, etc. Creature is its own career; 
ranks indicate general power and 
acclimation to ‘monster culture.’ In addition 
to special boons, all creatures have access 
to powers, which work like arcana and are 
fueled by Creature Points (CP).
•	 The maximum career rank for starting 
creatures is determined by the GM.
•	 For each creature rank, deduct 1 Hero 
Point from the total available. Rank 0 
counts as 1 for this purpose.
•	 Creatures receive a number of creature 
boons equal to 1 + their rank.
•	 For each creature boon taken, a 
creature flaw must be selected.
•	 For each standard or exotic flaw taken, a 
flaw must be selected.
See the Creature chapter (pg. XX) for more 
information.

Option: Gritty Characters
Some GMs may wish to run a low-
powered, ‘humancentric’ game where 
PCs are just average people; baristas, 
construction workers, homemakers, 
college students, etc., thrust into a dark 
world by fate. Any supernatural abilities 
are acquired through the course of 
play, and survival depends on sound 
planning—and luck. 
All such ‘gritty’ characters take the 
normie origin. The number of starting 
attribute and career points depends on 
age: 
Character start with a number of 
combat attribute points equal to 2 + 1 
for each rank in a martial career chosen 
(see pg. XX), maximum of 4.
Characters have 2 Hero Points total 
and can acquire more during play 
by accepting the consequences of 
Calamitous Failures, roleplaying flaws 
effectively, and GM’s decree. The Defy 
Death option (see Everywhen, pg. 33) 
may not be available, to emphasize the 
‘play for keeps’ nature of the setting.

CAREERS
25 careers are detailed in the Urban Fantasy 
Codex as representative of the genre. 
Players may wish to make up new ones, 
subject to the GM’s approval, though 
generally, combining existing careers with 
select boons and flaws allows for almost any 
heroic concept imaginable. See What, No 
Slayer Career? on pg. XX for an example.
Some careers are geared towards fighting, 
like solider, while others, like criminal, 
involve routine exposure to violence. 
These careers are designated as martial. 
Though all characters receive the same 
number of points to invest in their combat 
abilities, martial careers can grant an edge 
in combat. An agent, for example, can 
add their rank to damage when striking 
unnoticed. These abilities are noted in the 
career descriptions.
Scientists, sorcerers, and techies accrue 
downtime project points (DP) that can be 
used between adventures (see Equipment, 
pg. XX, and Magic pg. XX). Depending on 
the career, DPs allow the creation of nifty 
inventions, groundbreaking experiments, 
magic potions, etc.
Finally, being a creature like a vampire or 
ghost constitutes its own career. Ranks in 
creature indicate how powerful they are 
relative to others. This has a mechanical 
effect; some creature boons scale by rank, 
and special creature powers (see pg. XX) 
work like arcana, with rank affecting the 
activation roll and number of creature 
points (CP) available.  
RANK 0 REVISITED
Though it might not seem obvious, taking a 
career at rank 0 doesn’t make you ‘useless’ 
in that field. You have a familiarity with the 
basics, which can make all the difference 
when trying to land a plane or attempt CPR!
Rank 0 creatures can be thought of as 
neophytes. For example, a newly Turned 
vampire might have only a vague idea 
of how their powers work—but they can 
attempt to use them, all the same. 
Rank 0 can also be thought of as a hobby 
or amateur interest. A rank 0 artist who 
practices in their spare time might know the 
basics of drawing. Or a rank 0 entertainer, 
working as an intern at TV station, could 
know how to set up for a camera shoot.

ACADEMIC
Alternative Names: Historian, Professor, 
Scholar
Pairs Well With: Artist, Mystic, Scientist 
A genre staple, the academic devotes 
lifelong study to a particular field of 
knowledge, usually a ‘soft’ science like 
folklore, linguistics, anthropology, etc. (cf.  
scientist). They are attached to a body of 
higher learning like a university or research 
center. A knowledge of esoterica makes 
them a useful Contact, and easy to involve 
in supernatural adventures. 
Academics get an additional language pick 
for each rank in their career. They are good 
at research, library and archive use, and 
writing. Academics are well-read and have 
a broad knowledge base that exceeds their 
specialty. 
Attributes: Mind
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Contacts, First-Rate Facilities, Genius, 
Knowledge, Linguist 
Poor Eyesight, Skeptic, Slow to Act, Timid, 
Urbanite
AGENT (Martial Career)
Alternative Names: Assassin, Operative, Spy
Pairs Well With: Cop, Investigator, Soldier
Sometimes in uniform, sometimes operating 
undercover, agents do the dirty (and 
occasionally wet) work associated with 
a large organization. The latter can be 
anything from a formal government agency 
to a shady corporation. Though their specific 
role can vary, the job is always dangerous. 
Agents are good at tactics, stealth, and 
surveillance. Undercover types generally 
have some persuasive and deceptive skills 
as well. At the GM’s option, agents can add 
their rank to damage when striking unseen. 
Attributes: Agility and Mind
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Alert, Disguise, Detect Deception, Stealthy, 
Tools of the Trade 
Bounty, Enemy, Fanatic, Hatred of . . ., 
Stressed
ARTIST
Alternative Names: Painter, Sculptor, Writer
Pairs Well With: Entertainer, Mystic, Slacker
This career covers everything from 
landscape painters to pulp writers, digital 
illustrators, interior designers, you name it. 
Most artists need another career to pay 
their bills! In some settings, artists may be 
more ‘in tune’ with psychic and supernatural 
phenomenon, as befits their tangential way 
of thinking.

ATHLETE
Alternative Names: Boxer, Fitness Instructor, 
Gymnast
Pairs Well With: Entertainer, First Responder
Athletes have honed their physical abilities 
to a professional level, whether sports figures, 
marathon runners, gym rats, etc. Aside from 
justifying having high Strength and Agility, 
their fitness comes in handy on adventures—
especially when fleeing the supernatural!
Athletes are good at physical feats, 
enduring pain, and pushing for maximum 
effort. Outside of combat, they can add 
their rank to sports-related actions, such as 
a gymnast performing a backflip or a power 
lifter shoving a rock aside. 
Attributes: Strength and Agility
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Acrobat, Athletic, Cat-like Agility, Great 
Strength, Strength Feat

COP (Martial Career)
Alternative Names: MP, Security, Sheriff
Pairs Well With: Agent, First Responder, 
Investigator 
This career covers law enforcement, 
including semi-professionals like security 
guards. ‘Calling for backup’ isn’t going 
to be an option during most adventures, 
however; the PC will be operating outside 
their usual duties. 
Rank corresponds to formal rank within a 
police department, so a rank 0 cop would 
be a rookie, 2 a sergeant, etc. A police 
detective will also have ranks in investigator.
Cops are good at intimidation, surveillance, 
and criminology. At the GM’s option, they 
can add their rank to attack rolls when 
innocents are in danger. 
Attributes: Strength and Mind
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Alert, Contacts, Fear Resistance, Hard to Kill, 
Hard to Shake 
Addicted, Code, Hatred of . . ., Stressed, 
Taciturn

CREATURE
Alternative names: Ghost, Lycanthrope, 
Vampire
Pairs Well With: Any
Taking ranks in this career normally requires 
the creature origin, unless a character is 
Turned during play (see Powers, pg. XX). 
Creatures can be anything allowed by the 
GM; dhampir, revenants, unseelie fae, etc. 
Rank reflects both the ‘experience’ of being 
a creature (such as having knowledge of 
vampire clans and customs) as well as the 
potency of affiliated boons and powers. 
Rank also implies social status among the 
same creatures; a rank 4 werewolf, for 
example, could bully rank 1 ‘cubs’ with 
relative impunity.  
With the notable exception of angels, the 
creature career is most certainly corrupting 
(see Everywhen, pg. 80), requiring an 
additional flaw for each rank past the first. 
Attributes: Strength and Agility
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Varies, see Creature Boons/Flaws, pg. 
XX, also Recommended Boons/Flaws for 
Creatures, pg. XX
CRIMINAL (Martial Career)
Alternative Names: Gangster, Thief, Thug
Pairs Well With: Homeless, Slacker, Worker
Most criminals populate a city’s seamy 
underworld, but a few white-collar types ply 
their trade from gleaming skyscrapers. In an 
urban fantasy setting, this career includes 
fences who specialize in mystic artifacts, 
dealers with vials of undead blood, human 
consiglieres for the vampire mafia, etc. 
Criminals are good at stealth, defeating 
security (like locks), and emotional 
manipulation. Certain subtypes can have 
different skills; second-story thieves might be 
good at climbing, pickpockets filching, etc. 
At the GMs option, criminals can add their 
rank to attack rolls when striking unnoticed. 
Attributes: Agility and Mind
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Alert, Contacts, Detect Deception, Glib, 
Stealthy
Addicted, Bounty, Enemy, Greed, 
Untrustworthy
DRIVER/PILOT
Alternative Names: Rider, Sailor, Trucker
Pairs Well With: Cop, Soldier, Worker
This career covers vehicle operation at a 
professional level, including everything from 
a stretch limo chauffeur to a helicopter pilot. 
A ‘weekend flyer’ of single prop planes, yet 
to log all their flight hours, would be rank 0.
Aside from their chosen vehicle operation, 
driver/pilots are good at maintenance, area 
knowledge, and pulling off jaw-dropping 
stunts. In vehicular combat (see Everywhen, 
pg. 41), driver/pilots can add their rank to 
maneuver rolls. 
Attributes: Agility
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Ace, Cat-like Agility, Keen Sense, Master 
Gunner, Vehicle
Addicted, Arrogant, Boastful, Obsession, 
Temper

ENTERTAINER
Alternative Names: Actor, Influencer, 
Musician
Pairs Well With: Artist, Professional (Journalist), 
Slacker
Entertainers are performers at the 
professional or semi-professional level, and 
include influencers producing content for 
podcasts or U Tube channels. Generally, 
the higher the rank, the more ‘famous’ a 
character is. Popular entertainers can have 
fans among NPCs, who occasionally show 
up during adventures.  
Entertainers are good at performing in their 
medium, emotional manipulation, and 
moving among various subcultures (i.e., the 
underground music scene, theater venues, 
pop culture conventions, etc.). ‘Star-power’ 
can sometimes grant access to restricted 
people and situations.  
Attributes: Appeal
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Artistic, Attractive, Glib, Striking Appeal, 
Wealthy
Addicted, Amorous, Arrogant, Notorious, 
Spendthrift 
FIRST RESPONDER
Alternative Names: Firefighter, Paramedic, 
SAR
Pairs Well With: Cop, Physician, Ruralist 
This career covers professionals ‘first on the 
scene’ in emergencies, charged to protect 
human life. Though cops would fall under 
this description, the career is intended for 
people like firefighters and EMTs, who aren’t 
necessarily armed and/or don’t have law 
enforcement powers. 
Responders are good at first aid, assessing 
threats, and staying calm under pressure. 
They can stabilize the dying (see Everywhen, 
pg. 47) as physicians of the same rank. 
Depending on their job, responders can 
have additional skills, such as a search and 
rescue officer being good at tracking. 
Attributes: Mind and Agility
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Alert, Comforting Words, Fear Resistance, 
Field Medic, Hard to Shake
Addicted, Code, Obsession, Stressed, 
Taciturn
HOMELESS
Alternative Names: Drifter, Unhomed, 
Vagrant
Pairs Well With: Ruralist, Slacker, Worker
For whatever the reason, homeless people 
have been ‘outdoors’ long enough to 
survive in the urban wilderness. Not all are 
unemployed; drifters wander from city to 
city, taking whatever work they can find. 
Because of their vulnerability, homeless are 
often preyed on by predatory creatures.

MYSTIC
Alternative Names: Medium, Occultist, 
Psychic
Pairs Well With: Academic, Priest, Socialite
The mystic career delves into esoteric, often 
forbidden knowledge concerning the true 
nature of reality. Some versions, such as a 
scholarly occultist who studies strange lore 
but doesn’t wield arcane powers, can have 
a normie origin. Others, like mediums and 
psychics, have harnessed the powers of the 
mind (see Magic, pg. XX), and must take the 
exotic background, as well as the Sensitive 
boon.

PRIEST

Pairs Well With: Academic, Mystic,

Priests who have access to Faith Points (see

True Believer boon. The career includes both

calling, usually organized around a church 
or other congregation. Evil cultists are 
considered sorcerers (see pg. XX). 
Priests are good at public speaking, 
comforting people, and lore. Unlike other 
professions, they tend to be more accepting 
of supernatural phenomenon. At the GM’s 
discretion, priests can add their rank when 
making resolve checks against supernatural 
threats

PHYSICIAN
Alternate Names: Medic, Nurse, Psychiatrist
Pairs Well With: Academic, Professional, 
Scientist
Not restricted to M.D.’s, this career covers 
health care professionals able to provide 
treatment beyond basic first aid, as well as 
specialists like surgeons and psychiatrists. 
In a world where treating bullet wounds 
requires a police report, having a physician 
PC around can be a (literal) life saver. 
Physicians can stabilize the dying and speed 
lifeblood recovery (see Everywhen, pg. 
47). Additionally, they are good at first aid, 
diagnosis, and treating poisons. 
Physicians with access to a hospital or 
mobile surgery can get a hefty bonus to 
their task resolution rolls.
Attributes: Mind
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Comforting Words, Excellent Facilities, Field 
Medic, Knowledge, Tools of the Trade
Addicted, Code, Combat Paralysis, Skeptic, 
Stressed

Pairs Well With: Academic, Physician,

This is a catch-all for different careers 
requiring a formal education, generally 
considered white-collar (cf. worker). This

RURALIST (Martial Career)
Alternative Names: Explorer, Hunter, 
Survivalist
Pairs Well With: Athlete, Creature, First 
Responder
This career covers a range of outdoor-
related skills, which come in handy as soon 
as PCs leave the big city. Note that ‘hunter’ 
nominally refers to a person who hunts game 
instead of monsters, though tracking, stealth, 
and staying downwind are still useful with 
the latter!
Ruralists are good at survival in harsh 
environments, orienteering, and traversing 
rough terrain. They also have knowledge of 
native wildlife. At the GMs option, a ruralist 
can add their rank when detecting or 
setting ambushes in the wild.
Attributes: Strength and Mind

SCIENTIST
Alternative Names: Chemist, Physicist, 
Researcher
Pairs Well With: Academic, Physician, Techie
Though likely baffled by the supernatural, 
scientists, especially the ‘mad’ variety, show 
up in urban horror. They can be corporate 
types working on a zombie virus, paranormal 
researchers talking to ghosts, or even a 
loner making flesh constructs in a hidden 
laboratory. 
Scientists are good at deductive reasoning, 
research, and experimentation. Like 
academics, they have a broad knowledge 
base that goes beyond their particular field. 
Scientist accrue downtime project points 
(DP) for research projects (see Equipment, 
pg. XX). 
Attributes: Mind
Suggested Boons/Flaws:
Contacts, Excellent Facilities, Genius, 
Knowledge, Tools of the Trade
Arrogant, Out of Shape, Skeptic, Slow to Act, 
Tedious
SLACKER
Alternative Names: Conspiracy Theorist, 
NEET, Otaku
Pairs Well With: Artist, Entertainer, Student
Not so much a career as a way of life, 
slackers consume a lot of popular culture 
and/or spend time on the internet to the 
exclusion all else. There is always a chance 
this can be useful, such as a conspiracy 
theorist who uncovers a real conspiracy.
Slackers are good at getting into arguments 
with complete strangers, recalling pedantic 
information, and knowing where to look for 
dubious sources of intel. 
Attributes: Mind

SOCIALITE
Alternative Names: Dilettante, Idle Rich, 
Trust-Fund Baby 
Pairs Well With: Entertainer, Professional, 
Slacker
Socialites are generally drawn from the 
‘upper crust’ of society, and spend their

WHAT, NO SLAYER 
CAREER?
Though it might seem like a glaring omission, 
there is no ‘slayer’ or ‘monster hunter’ career 
presented on the main list. This is purposeful. 
Generally, careers aren’t character classes 
like in other RPGs. They’re more akin to real 
life occupations rather than a collection of 
special abilities.
However, creatures aren’t really a career, 
though for mechanical reasons the Urban 
Fantasy Codex treats them as such. And 
how about a setting where monster hunting 
has become a profession? John Steakley’s 
novel, Vampire$ (the basis for a John 
Carpenter movie), deals with just such an 
occurrence.
There are two potential solutions. One is 
a more elegant, ‘composite’ approach, 
using careers as building blocks, along with 
boons and flaws, to create a larger heroic 
concept. 
Example
Lucas is an archetypal monster hunter. The 
fact that he’s a creature himself just adds to 
the irony. Lethal even when not in werewolf 
form, he ‘thropes only after cornering his 
prey and conventional methods fail. Or 
when the moon’s full. 
Lucas was originally a parks warden in British 
Columbia, before being savaged by a loup 
garou. He takes 2 ranks in ruralist, as well as 1 
rank in creature (werewolf). Along the way, 
he’s learned how to fix offroad vehicles, 
which translates to worker (mechanic) 
at rank 1, and studied up on zoology 
(academic, rank 0).
These skills, along with his normal boons 
Forest-Born and Beast-Friend, as well as his 
deadly compound bow, round out Lucas’s 
monster hunter persona. 
The second approach is to bow to genre 
demands and create a new career. 
Note that this is a slippery slope, as it has 
traditionally led to power bloat, and the 
proliferation of ‘splat books’ within other 
game systems. Also note the career overlap 
that tends to occur with such a specialized 
calling.

CHARGEN SUMMARY
Option: Factions
The GM may have a list of important 
factions established for a setting. If a 
player takes a career that could logically 
have ties to one (or more) of these 
groups, they can opt to identify their 
relationship—if any. 
For example, if a player takes a rank in 
creature (vampire), the GM can show 
them a list of vampire clans already 
established in the area; the PC might 
belong to one of the clans, hate them 
dearly, have no knowledge of them, etc.
Alternately, the GM may allow players 
to invent their own factions during 
character creation. This allows players to 
contribute to the world-building process, 
as well as increasing their ‘buy-in’ with 
a setting. In the example above, the 
vampire PC could belong to a clan 
designed by the player, who would then 
work with the GM on how it ‘fits’ into the 
existing hierarchy.
In some campaigns, a GM may wish 
to have PCs start out all belonging to 
the same faction. An example would 
be a group of agents working for a 
government watchdog agency, keeping 
discrete tabs on the supernatural, or 
journalists hunting wild stories for a 
tabloid. Normally, however, having PCs 
aligned with different factions makes for 
more interesting dynamics. 
Finally, relationships with factions are 
fluid—they can change from adventure 
to adventure, depending on the PCs 
actions. What happens when a vampire 
violates some sacred bylaw of her clan? 
Or forms a friendship (or love interest) 
with the ‘enemy?’
For full details on factions, including 
examples and guidelines for creating 
them, see chapter X, pg. XX.

Chris wants to make an influencer, a paranormal investigator 
with a struggling U Tube channel. He knows his friends will 
be playing combat-oriented heroes, so in contrast he 
wants someone who can smooth-talk NPCs and wheedle 
information. Chris has already settled on the celebrity 
name Dwayne Famous. 
Dwayne starts with the normie origin. While in college he 
took a paranormal anthropology class and befriended 
a fellow student. The pair began recording and 
uploading their investigations of haunted locales. 
Following an argument, Dwayne’s partner left to 
investigate the abandoned Woodhurst Asylum by 
herself—and hasn’t been seen since.
Chris imagines Dwayne as an all-rounder and puts 1 point 
in each attribute. He wants a higher Appeal, though, and 
thinks he might enhance this with a boon.
Dwayne’s backstory suggests several careers. He takes 
academic at rank 0 to represent his college experience. 
Next, he chooses investigator at rank 1 and entertainer at 
rank 2, reflecting his ‘style over substance’ approach. Chris 
isn’t sure about Dwayne’s earlier life; he decides to leave 
this undefined by taking a mystery career (see pg. XX) at 
rank 1. 
As noted earlier, Dwayne isn’t much of a combat guy. 
Because self-preservation is his primary focus, he puts 2 points 
in defense. Along these lines, he puts 1 point in initiative so 
he can react quickly. Finally, because urban exploring is 
dangerous, he puts 1 point in ranged; Dwayne carries a 
9mm for emergencies.
Now for boons and flaws. First up, Dwayne takes Striking Appeal to raise this 
attribute to 2. He also takes Glib, to reflect Dwayne’s knack for schmoozing. 
Finally, because Dwayne knows a lot of people, he takes Contacts tied to his entertainer 
career.

and Obsession (he desperately wants to find his partner). The GM notes both these flaws, as 
they will provide hooks for upcoming adventures. 
Finally, Dwayne’s careers and Greed flaw suggests a possible faction affiliation to the GM

be the break Dwayne’s been looking for, but he suspects it comes with a catch!

With the emergence of a certain vampire-
themed RPG in 1991, the idea of ‘playing 
the monster’ gained broad acceptance. 
Though broached earlier (Monsters! 
Monsters! came out in 1976), the floodgates 
were opened for players to foray into 
verboten territory. 
Fiction writers from May Shelley to John 
Gardner have underscored the humanity 
in monster characters, depicting them as 
deeply flawed or ‘broken,’ rather than evil. 
This approach makes playing creatures 
much more palatable. In game terms, a 
creature’s flaws are emphasized just as 
much as their boons or powers, keeping 
them from becoming ‘fanged superheroes.’ 
In fact, flaws distinguish creatures more 
than their special abilities, as the latter tend 
to be homogeneous. For example, many 
creatures have superhuman strength and 
resistance to injury, but the disadvantages of 
being a zombie or lycanthrope vary greatly. 
THE DEXTER EFFECT
Some creature types, such as demons 
or supernatural slashers, are so bad it’s 
difficult to imagine them as viable player 
characters. NPCs sure, but the problems of 
role-playing evil ‘heroes’ have long been 
known.

Corporeal
Most creatures are living beings with 
a physical body. Their powers and 
limitations can vary widely.
CREATURE TYPES
All creatures fall under one of 3 general 
types: corporeal, incorporeal, and undead. 
Undead
This is a corporeal creature; sometimes a 
spirit inhabiting a dead body, sometimes 
more. 
•	 Undead automatically stabilize if 
taken below 0 lifeblood. They are 
‘killed’ or destroyed at -6 lifeblood, 
though the Resurrect power allows 
some to come back unless certain 
conditions are met (i.e., staked, etc.). 
•	 Undead ‘naturally’ heal lifeblood 
like other characters. If taken to 0 or 
less lifeblood from fatigue damage, 
however, they will rise again, fully 
healed, in 2 rounds.   
•	 Undead don’t need to breathe 
and are immune to poisons and 
mundane diseases. They don’t age 
further from the time of their death.  
Incorporeal
These are spirits who lack a physical 
body, placing restrictions on their ability 
to interact with the material world.
•	 Incorporeal creatures have a full 
range of attributes—including 
Strength and Agility, for use when 
they Manifest. 
•	 Incorporeal creatures are normally 
invisible, inaudible, and insubstantial. 
They use resolve in place of lifeblood, 
unless they have completely 
Manifested or are Possessing a 
host (see Powers, pg. XX). When 
insubstantial, they can’t be harmed 
by most physical attacks, though 
magic and psionic damage still 
affect them. 
•	 If brought to 0 or lower resolve, 
incorporeal creatures dissipate. They 
can reform at 1 resolve or higher.

•	 If a Possessed host or Manifested 
physical form is killed, incorporeal 
creatures cannot reform for the 
remainder of the scene. 
•	 Incorporeal creatures can’t be 
‘killed’ outside of certain rituals (see 
Magic, pg. XX). Laying a ghost to rest 
will permanently ‘kill’ it, but exorcising 
a ghost or demon just drives it away. 
•	 Incorporeal creatures can fly or float 
at walking speed.
Regardless of type, all creatures are resistant 
to resolve attacks from mundane (i.e., non-
supernatural) sources. See pg. XX for more 
details.
CREATURES & CAREERS
During character creation, the GM sets 
the maximum starting rank for creatures. 
This is an effective way to ‘balance’ player 
characters, as most creature boons and 
powers scale by rank. PCs can later spend 
XP to increase their creature rank, gradually 
becoming more powerful—and losing 
what’s left of their humanity. 
Aside from ghosts, creatures can spend 
XP to take ‘normal’ careers like other 
characters. A vampire crime boss, for 
example, can spend XP on ranks in criminal, 
to reflect their growing larcenous skills. 
Careers can also be used to represent 
a character’s life before joining the 
ranks of the supernatural. Generally, it’s 
recommended at least one such career be 
identified in this manner.
Finally, though it should go without saying, 
a character can only be one creature. 
No rank 2 vampire, rank 1 mummy heroes 
allowed!
 
CREATURE BOONS & 
FLAWS
Creatures have access to potent boons, 
but are also limited by powerful flaws. These 
are listed on pg. XX. Creature PCs can take 
a number of boons equal to their creature 
rank + 1. For each creature boon taken, a 
character must also take a creature flaw. 
This applies to boons acquired through XP, 
once play begins. 
Many creature boons scale by rank. For 
example, a rank 1 vampire gains +1 to 
Strength from the Supernatural Strength 
boon, while a rank 6 vampire gains +6!
Creature boons differ from creature powers 
(see pg. XX), in that they are always in 
effect, except where specifically noted. 
So, while a vampire might use the Change 
Shape power to turn into a bat, or the Surge 
power to temporarily boost their Agility, 
these effects won’t last. The same vampire’s 
Supernatural Strength boon is ‘always on,’ 
however, and adds to their lifeblood total.
Note: The creatures in parenthesis at the end 
of these descriptions indicate those typically 
associated with the boon/flaw. As always, 
these should be considered suggestions. If 
you feel a particular boon/flaw fits with your 
creature’s concept, go ahead and take it!
Anchor (Flaw)
You are spiritually tied to a specific person, 
place, or object. You can only regain CP 
and resolve when resting around this Anchor 
(Ghosts, Revenants).
Archaic (Flaw)
Because of your extreme age, most of your 
career knowledge is seriously behind the 
times. For example, being a Renaissance-
era merchant doesn’t completely transfer 
to a modern professional (entrepreneur), 
though some viable skill would remain 
(GM’s call). Only 1 rank in your most recent 
career is considered modern (Fae, Ghosts, 
Mummies, Vampires).

You have less career knowledge than

Chosen (Boon)
You come from a particularly strong 
bloodline, creator, etc. Gain a bonus die on 
activation rolls for creature powers (Any).
Creator (Flaw)
You have someone (a Rival level NPC) 
who made you; typically a sorcerer or 
mad scientist. This person isn’t necessarily 
an enemy, nor your ‘master,’ but your 
relationship with them is complicated, and 
causes problems during adventures. Cf. 
Scion (Constructs, Zombies).
Dayblind (Flaw)
You are hampered by direct sunlight; 
you must wear sunglasses, hats, etc. to 
effectively see during the day. Even then, 
you suffer a penalty die on most actions 
unless not in shade, the interior of a 
building, or the weather is overcast (Ghouls, 
Vampires, Warm Vampires).
Daysleeper (Flaw)
You are more vulnerable during the day. 
Any creature powers attempted suffer a 
penalty die on the activation roll. Your rank 
is considered 1 less (minimum 0) for purposes 
of your creature boons (Ghosts, Ghouls, 
Vampires, Warm Vampires).
Dependent (Flaw)
You need ‘food’ on a daily basis (blood, 
flesh, human fear, etc.) or you start taking 
damage; 1 pt. of lasting lifeblood per 
day that you go without. This damage is 
immediately healed upon feeding (Demons, 
Ghouls, Vampires, Warm Vampires, 
Zombies).
Automaton (Flaw)
You are not completely free-willed. You may 
have directives ‘programmed’ by a creator 
(e.g., “Protect the Jewish community in 
Prague”) or simply follow strong instincts 
(“Brainssss”). Take a penalty die when 
attempting to not follow these imperatives. 
This counts as 2 flaws (Constructs, Mummies, 
Revenants, Supernatural Slashers, Zombies).

CREATURE BOONS & FLAWS LIST
Eldritch Potency (Boon)
Your connection to supernatural forces 
is especially strong. Gain a bonus to your 
CP total equal to your creature rank. Cf. 
Chosen (Any).
Feral (Flaw)
Your ‘human’ side is immersed in a more 
animalistic state, and you have no formal 
clan or creature affiliations as a result. You 
take a penalty die on interactions with 
other creatures, who recognize you (rightly) 
as dangerous (Dhampir, Lycanthropes, 
Supernatural Slashers, Vampires, Warm 
Vampires, Zombies).
Frenzy (Flaw)
When feeding on the substance of your 
heart’s desire (blood, flesh, etc.) you are 
distracted and must make a Mind roll to 
break away, even when directly threatened  
(Ghouls, Vampires, Zombies). 
Hideous (Flaw)
Your features are horrifying to behold— 
among humans anyways. Normal people 
might run screaming in non-combat 
situations. This flaw can be mitigated with 
a disguise, motorcycle helmet, etc. and 
doesn’t apply to humans who know you well 
(Any).

Higher Authority (Flaw)
You have an obligation to a spiritual 
authority (good, bad, or in-between) that 
occasionally causes a hindrance. The GM 
secretly rolls a d6 at the beginning of each 
session; on a ‘1’ this power will make an 
inconvenient demand on you (Angels, 
Demons).
Holy/Unholy Vitality (Boon)
This boon simulates supernatural resilience, 
knitted into your flesh. Because your bodily 
tissues are not wholly of this mundane world, 
you are resistant to normal weapons like 
guns and knives. 
H/U Vitality is treated as armor protection, 
equal to d6-2 + your creature rank/2, round 
down. So, a rank 1 vampire would have d6-2 
protection, while a rank 6 vampire d6+1. This 
protection is considered 0 vs. attacks you 
are vulnerable to, such as fire for vampires or 
silver bullets for lycanthropes. Holy Vitality is 
vulnerable to desecrated weapons (see pg. 
XX). 
You automatically stabilize when taken 
below 0 lifeblood, but are still ‘unconscious’ 
(incapacitated) as normal, and ‘killed’ at -6 
lifeblood or lower. 
In some cases, H/U Vitality can be bypassed 
by taking a negative modifier to an attack 
roll. For example, negating a zombie’s 
Unholy Vitality requires a -2 penalty to hit it in 
the head.  
H/U Vitality does not provide protection 
against magic spells, most psionic effects, or 
magic weapons (Any).
Host (Boon)
This boon is used by incorporeal creatures 
with the Possession power. You can begin 
an adventure with a ready ‘vessel,’ a Tough 
level NPC whose body you will be using 
for the duration. The Host may be willing, 
unwilling, and/or completely oblivious about 
their role. See the Companion boon for 
more details (Demons, Ghosts).

Inherent Power (Boon)
You can use creature powers without having 
to make an activation roll. You must still 
pay the full CP cost, however. An example 
would be a lycanthrope so versed in shape-
changing they don’t have to roll for the 
Force Change power.  
This benefit only applies to powers of 
Magnitude equal to your creature rank/2, 
round down. So, a rank 2 werewolf with the 
Inherent Power boon can automatically 
Fast Heal (a First Magnitude power) without 
having to make an activation roll.  
For NPCs with this boon, Mind affecting 
powers like a vampire’s Beguiling Gaze 
become player-facing (see Mechanics, pg. 
XX) (Any).
Inhuman Anatomy (Boon)
You don’t take extra damage from Vital 
Blow (see Everywhen, pg. 26) as your major 
organs no longer function, or your ‘life force’ 
is spread throughout your body (Constructs, 
Ghosts, Zombies). 
Natural Weapons (Boon)
You can ‘sprout’ fangs, long claws, etc., 
for use in melee combat. Damage is d6 
+ Strength.  These weapons are otherwise 
concealed (Ghouls, Vampires).
Rot (Flaw)
Your corporal form is decaying, despite 
efforts to slow the process. Aside from 
aesthetic problems, you emit a stench that 
makes it difficult to sneak up on anyone. 
Further, when taking lasting damage, you 
tend to shed fingers, limbs, appendages, 
etc. These can be reattached, but cause 
a penalty die on physical actions in the 
meantime (Revenants, Zombies).
Scion (Flaw)
The creature who Turned you is still in your 
(un)life. This is a Rival level NPC, and though 
they might not be hostile, they may feel you 
‘owe’ them something or otherwise cause 
problems. Cf. Creator (Ghoul, Lycanthrope, 
Vampire, Zombie).
Sunbane (Flaw)
You are very susceptible to sunlight, and 
take environmental damage at the rate of 1 
lasting lifeblood per round if caught outside 
during the day. This cannot be Fast Healed 
and is not protected by Unholy Vitality. 
Sunbane counts as 2 flaws (Vampires).
Supernatural Strength (Boon)
Your muscles are imbued with inhuman 
power. This boon grants +1 Strength per 
creature rank, which adds to lifeblood total. 
Your maximum Strength value can exceed 
6, and your maximum starting Strength is 6 
(Any).
Tag (Flaw)
Your supernatural origins can’t be wholly 
hidden. A Tag is usually subtle, though 
discernable to those who know what to look 
for. Examples include not casting a reflection 
(or shadow), dense hair in the middle of your 
palms, cold flesh, red pupils, foul breath, etc. 
Some Tags can be concealed, but not from 
a concentrated ‘test.’ This flaw can only be 
taken once (Any).
Vulnerability (Flaw)
Creatures are often vulnerable to certain 
objects or situations, such as holy symbols, 
garlic, wolfsbane, salt, etc. Generally, a 
Vulnerability will cause a creature to be 
repulsed, obstructed, or confounded. See 
pg. XX in the Mechanics chapter. 
Each separate Vulnerability counts as a flaw, 
and this can be taken multiple times. For 
example, a vampire repulsed by garlic, holy 
symbols, and unable to cross running water 
would have Vulnerability x 3. With the GM’s 
permission, these Vulnerabilities can be 
identified during play (see pg. XX).

state. Of course, he’s going to need a

FLEXIBLE FLAWS
At the GM’s option, the creature flaw 
Vulnerability can be left undefined during 
character creation. Later, in the course of 
play, the GM can identify situations where 
this flaw might be relevant, and propose it 
to the player. If accepted, the PC gains a 
Hero Point just like accepting a Calamitous 
Failure. From then on, the specific 
Vulnerability becomes ‘canon.’
Example
Koenig needs to pay off 3 creature 
boons; he takes the creature flaws 
Daysleeper and Vulnerability x 2 to settle 
the cost. 
Koenig’s player notices that his Unholy 
Vitality doesn’t protect against fire. He 
decides to take this one step further 
and identifies a Vulnerability to fire. He is 
repulsed by things like bonfires, burning 
houses, and flaming torches. Koenig 
leaves his other Vulnerability undefined.
Later, during an adventure in rural 
Oregon, Koenig encounters a fast-
flowing creek. The GM asks if he is 
obstructed by running water; Koenig’s 
player, who can think of a lot of situations 
where this would be limiting, declines. 
Shortly afterwards, Koenig finds a cabin 
where several witches are holed up. The 
door has a wreath of garlic flowers nailed 
to it. Again, the GM asks if garlic would 
repulse Koenig and he decides that it 
does. The GM awards him a Hero Point 
and the vampire tries to find another 
way inside.

CREATURE POWERS
Creature powers are a special type of 
arcana. These are short duration, spell-like 
effects allowing creatures to change shape, 
temporarily boost attributes, fly, etc. Powers 
normally require an activation roll and cost 
Creature Points (CP) to use, just like Arcane 
Points. These CP are replenished in different 
ways depending on the creature; vampires, 
for example, restore CP by drinking blood, 
zombies by eating raw meat, etc.
Creature powers are limited to a maximum 
of Second Magnitude and don’t have 
casting requirements (though these can 
still be taken to reduce cost; see pg. XX). 
Further, some are instantaneous, meaning 
a power can be used without taking a 
character’s action for the round.  For 
example, a revenant could use Fast Heal 
(an instantaneous power) and attack in 
the same round. Only one power can be 
attempted per round.
Creature power activation roll: Mind + 
creature rank – difficulty modifier(s)
Creature Points (CP) = 10 + creature rank
Because powers usually require an 
activation roll, they have the possibility to 
fail, sometimes spectacularly. The GM can 
use their judgment on a case-by-case basis, 
but a Catastrophic Failure, and by contrast, 
a Mighty Success, can have interesting 
consequences.
Example
Koenig is attempting to Change Shape 
into a bat, a First Magnitude power for 
a vampire. He botches his activation 
roll with a ‘2’ however, and accepts a 
Calamitous Failure in return for a Hero 
Point. 
The GM decides Koenig’s arms are 
replaced with tiny, useless wings, limiting 
his physical actions for the length of a 
scene. 
Rot Wyler uses the Surge power to 
temporarily boost his Strength. He rolls a 
‘natural 12’ for his activation and gets a 
Mighty Success.
The GM rules Rot’s Surge lasts the length 
of combat rather than a single round, 
effectively supercharging him for an 
entire fight!
Powers that subjugate a target’s will (like 
Beguile) use the target’s Mind score as a 
negative modifier on the activation roll, just 
like with spells.

Example
Koenig wants to use Beguile (First

LIMITATIONS ON POWERS
Unlike arcana, a casting requirement is 
not required to use a power. However, 
a character may still wish to apply them 
to reduce CP cost. Minimum cost per 
Magnitude applies (see Everywhen, pg. 82). 
Also, some explanation may be necessary to 
justify the requirement.
The following requirements are permissible 
with powers:
•	 Casting Time
•	 Distracted 
•	 Lunar
•	 Miscast (see Magic, pg. XX)
•	 Wounds
Example
Lucas was hit with several high-power 
rifle slugs while in wereform. Though he 
escaped, he’s suffered lasting damage 
to his lifeblood. As Lucas is low on CP, he 
takes Casting Time to use Fast Heal, a 
First Magnitude power. This reduces the 
CP cost by 1. However, the Fast Heal is 
no longer instantaneous, taking d6 x 30 
minutes of intense concentration as the 
bullets slowly work their way out of his 
body. 
The number and specific powers available 
for a character to use can also be limited 
by the GM, depending on preference. For 
example, a GM can rule a creature only has 
access to 2 + 1 per rank powers, which must 
be identified before play begins. This can 
further ‘balance’ the creature origin, though 
it also limits spontaneity (and fun) during 
play. 
Another potential limitation has to do with 
clans or sects of creatures (see Factions, 
pg. XX). Depending on the setting, the 
GM may wish to limit specific powers as 
being available only to certain clans. This is 
more due to ‘flavor’ than game balance, 
but helps make different creature factions 
distinct. 
Example
The beastly vampires of the Martense 
clan must take Hideous as one of their 
creature flaws. Perversely, they also 
cast reflections and show up on film, as 
if reminders of their condition. On the 
plus side, however, they are the only 
vampires known to have the Assimilate 
power, allowing them to ‘borrow’ 
memories and skills from those whose 
blood they drink.

Non-Instantaneous

as a scratch or cut. First Magnitude does 
d3 + creature rank damage, while Second

melee attack roll, though your target’s 
defense doesn’t apply.

Moderate (0)
Instantaneous

Drain
First and Second Magnitude
Moderate (0), Tough (-2)
Non-Instantaneous
You can inflict resolve damage on a target 
and add the amount to your own. The 
target’s Mind acts as a negative modifier on 
the activation roll. Damage is d3 + creature 
rank for First Magnitude and d6 + rank for 
Second. Any ‘extra’ resolve past your total 
lasts for a scene. 
Fast Heal
First Magnitude
Moderate (0)
Instantaneous
This power gave rise to legends of 
supernatural creatures being invulnerable. 
You can instantly heal lifeblood damage, 
including lasting damage. Fast Heal costs 2 
CP +1 per point of lifeblood healed.
Fast Heal doesn’t work against damage 
that bypasses your Holy/Unholy Vitality. Thus, 
vampires can’t Fast Heal fire damage, and 
lycanthropes can’t Fast Heal wounds from 
silver weapons.
Fear
First Magnitude 
Moderate (0)
Instantaneous 
You use your eerie presence to terrify a 
target; for a moment, your face might 
become bestial, your eyes flare a lurid 
yellow, fishhook-fangs pop from your mouth, 
etc. The target’s Mind is a negative modifier 
on the activation roll.
Success inflicts d6 normal resolve damage 
and causes a target to lose priority as if they 
failed a resolve check (see Mechanics, 
pg. XX). Note that other creatures can be 
affected by Fear.

First Magnitude
Moderate (0)
Instantaneous
Fetor allows you to emit a charnel stench 
that can weaken even supernatural 
opponents. Targets within melee range must 
make a Strength roll, plus their creature rank 
(if any), minus your creature rank. If failed, 
they take d6 fatigue damage and suffer a 
penalty die on most actions for a scene.

Moderate (0)
Instantaneous

Non-Instantaneous

Moderate (0)

You can implant illusions in your target’s

First Magnitude
Moderate (0)
Non-Instantaneous 
Quell allows you to calm your savage 
creature instincts. A vampire, for example,

calm another creature, such as an NPC 
lycanthrope about to rip people apart after

Tough (-2)
Non-Instantaneous
You can be in two places at once, provided 
the locations/scenes are separate. For

sustains damage (or dies), so does the other

Spook
Zero and First Magnitude
Very Easy (+2), Moderate (0)
Instantaneous
More subtle than Fear, this power allows 
you to alter the environment in creepy 
ways. Some examples include causing 
the temperature to drop in a ‘cold spot,’ 
dimming lights or causing flames to flicker 
out, shadows to lengthen and distort, etc. 
Magnitude is either Zero or First, depending 
on the extent of the effect (GM’s call). 
Though Spook doesn’t force a resolve check 
(see Mechanics, pg. XX), it does set up for 
one. Targets take a penalty die on any 
resolve checks that occur close to the use of 
Spook.

First Magnitude 
Moderate (0)
Instantaneous
Surge allows you to temporarily increase 
your Strength or Agility. You can add 1 to 
the attribute, plus 1 for each creature rank, 
at a cost of 2 CP + 1 per additional point 
boosted. The Surge only lasts a single round, 
so make it count! Boosted Strength does not 
add to your lifeblood total. Boosting Agility 
to 6+ allows you to make multiple attacks 
(see Mechanics, pg. XX).

First Magnitude
Moderate (0)
Instantaneous
You move at eye-blurring speeds, faster than 
mortal brains can register. Swift allows you 
to gain priority for a combat, acting before 
even a Legendary Success. If two or more 
creatures are using Swift in the same round, 
the one with the highest Agility goes first; if 
this is a tie, then the higher creature rank 
goes first. If this is a tie, then d6s are rolled to 
see who is microseconds faster! Swift lasts for 
a combat scene.

First and Second Magnitude
Moderate (0), Tough (-2)
Instantaneous
The First Magnitude of this power allows 
you to Teleport from your current location 
to anywhere in sight up to 30 feet. Second 
Magnitude allows you Teleport up to 200 
feet, or anywhere you have been previously 
in the last hour. Among other uses, Teleport 
allows you to appear behind someone 
and attack the same round, ignoring their

Turn
First Magnitude
Moderate (0)
Non-Instantaneous
Turn allows creatures to ‘pass on’ their 
condition once they’ve killed a target (0 or 
lower lifeblood for Rabble and Toughs, -6 
or lower for Heroes/Rivals). This effectively 
gives the target the same creature origin, 
provided they didn’t have one in the first 
place (i.e., you can’t Turn a vampire into a 
ghoul). On a failed activation roll, you can’t 
keep trying to Turn—it just doesn’t ‘take.’ The 
target will return from death in up to 24 hours 
with the creature career at rank 0. 
Unseen
First and Second Magnitude
Moderate (0), Tough (-2)
Instantaneous 
The Unseen power renders you invisible. At 
First Magnitude this affects other people and 
creatures, though the Sense power allows 
the latter a chance to notice you. Second 
Magnitude makes you invisible to security 
cameras and able to bypass electronic 
detection like motion sensors. On a later 
review of video, you might show up as a 
momentary distortion, but that’s it. 
Unseen creatures still make noise. They also 
leave ‘footprints’ in puddles and similar tags. 
Cost is 2 CP + 1 per round that Unseen is 
active. 
Veil
First and Second Magnitude
Moderate (0), Tough (-2)
Instantaneous 
Veil allows you to appear ‘normal’ (even 
with the Hideous flaw) and move about in 
human society without causing a ruckus. 
At First Magnitude this is appearance only; 
smells, touch, and other potential Tags 
(see pg. XX) can still be perceived. Second 
Magnitude takes care of all these details. 
The Sense power allows another creature a 
chance to see through this disguise. Veil lasts 
for a scene. 
Wail
First and Second Magnitude
Moderate (0), Tough (-2)
Instantaneous 
Wail allows incorporeal creatures to make 
a sound-based attack (cf. Corporeal 
Strike). This forces a resolve check (see 
Mechanics, pg. XX) to any who can hear, 
with a negative modifier on their roll equal 
to your creature rank or Mind, whichever is 
higher. Damage is d6 normal resolve for First 
Magnitude and 2d6 for Second. 
Wild Call
First and Second Magnitude
Moderate (0), Tough (-2)
Non-Instantaneous 
This power summons groups of animals 
(vermin, insects, etc.) to your immediate 
area, who perform your bidding. The 
specific kind of animal depends on your 
creature type; for example, bats and rats for 
vampires, scarabs and asps for mummies, 
wolves for lycanthropes, etc. First Magnitude 
summons d6+ creature rank Rabble, while 
Second Magnitude calls 1 per creature rank 
Toughs.
Note that the animals need to be feasible 
for the area, with potential ‘substitutions’ 
(e.g., summoning wild dogs instead of 
wolves in downtown New York). The power 
lasts for a scene.

FREEFORM POWERS
Creature powers certainly don’t have to be 
confined to those described here. In fact, it’s 
recommended players be allowed to come 
up with new powers on the fly, just as with 
arcana (see Magic, pg. XX). 
Freeform powers are a great way to 
stimulate creativity during play, though it 
does require some on-the-spot ruling by the 
GM, especially in terms of Magnitude. If the 
new power seems a little too effective, it 
can always be nerfed later when PCs want 
to use it again.
Examples
Threnody Malaise, a neophyte (rank 
0) vampire, finds herself trapped inside 
a tenement building, with a group of 
slayers in hot pursuit. She spots a stout 
metal fire door that’s probably beyond 
her Strength to yank open, even with 
the Surge power. Regardless, she’d like 
to put that door between her and the 
slayers.
Threnody has heard whispers of a 
vampiric power that turns bones gelid, 
allowing passage through narrow 
openings. She’d like to use this to slip 
through the tiny crack in the door 
jamb. The GM rules Gelid Bones is a First 
Magnitude power and allows Threnody 
to attempt an activation roll. Better hurry!
Rot Wyler is trying to meet with an 
informant at a swank club, but can’t 
get past the doorman due to a strict 
“no zombies” policy. Rot’s player has 
an epiphany; he wants to detach his 
left forearm and have it crawl by the 
doorman when he isn’t looking, with 
a message for the informant clamped 
between pinky and forefinger.
Examples (Continued)
The GM points out that Rot’s arm is 
effectively blind; Rot explains he’ll try 
to guide it by peering in through club’s 
windows and front door. The GM decides 
Detach is a First Magnitude power and 
lets Rot give it a go.

Angels are broadly defined as beneficial 
spirits looking out for the interests of 
humanity in the physical world. 
They can be members of an 
organized celestial bureaucracy 
with formal orders and ranks, 
or virtuous souls who decided to 
‘stay behind’ and attend to worldly 
matters instead of moving on to an 
afterlife.
Normally incorporeal, angels are 
loathe to Possess hosts, and seldom 
have this power. Instead, the more 
powerful ones can Manifest for short 
periods of time, during which they 
can be formidable, indeed. They are 
immortal, and regain CP on their holy 
days of the week, or by lingering at 
sacred sites. The latter restores CP 
at the rate of 1 per hour. 
Note that while inherently good, angels possess free 
will and don’t have to hold back when confronted 
with the forces of darkness (i.e., they aren’t the 
paladins of certain fantasy RPGs). Also, they don’t 
routinely check with ‘on high’ for permission to act—
they’re free agents, at least most of the time, and the 
forces they serve aren’t chatty by nature!
When manifested, angels typically appear as beatific humans, surrounded by an aura of 
light. They can sprout wings to use their Flight power, as well as manifest celestial weapons.

DHAMPIR
Dhampir are hybrid creatures born to a 
living mother and vampire father. This is a 
rare occurrence, with only a few vampire 
bloodlines capable of such a feat. Legend 
holds that most of these offspring are born 
without bones; if true, then the creatures 
described here are among the lucky ones. 
Though not as powerful as their undead 
parent, dhampir aren’t saddled with all the 
drawbacks, either (see Warm Vampires, 
pg. XX). They don’t need human blood 
to survive, though they likely crave it, and 
can use blood to restore their CP totals 
as per vampires. If killed, they will return 
as undead within 24 hours, losing all their 
creature ranks and starting again as a rank 
0 vampire. 
Depending on their relationship with 
their parent(s), dhampir usually ‘pick a 
side’ between the living and the dead. 
Dhampir make useful agents for vampire 
clans, given their ability to walk around in 
daylight, and are not seen as threats to 
the blood supply, since they don’t have to 
partake.
Physically, dhampir are pale and often have unusual eyes, including blood-red pupils. These 
features are easily concealed.

connected to our own through liminal spaces.

Celtic unseelie, Japanese yosei, Russian rusalka,

and specific natural features, such as an ancient 
oak in a crowded city park. 
Fae PCs are likely to be changelings; former 
‘problem children’ swapped from the faerie 
realm at birth. As changelings progress in 
creature rank, they become less human, both 
physically and mentally.
Fae are usually depicted as having fickle, 
dual natures; kind if treated with respect, 
but malevolent when crossed. Even ‘good’ 
fae can have a capricious streak. Their powers 
are usually geared towards trickery and illusions, 
but some, like ogres, possess blunt physical 
strength. Celtic fae are associated with ‘pagan’ 
magic, and thus susceptible to Christian-based 
Vulnerabilities.

GHOST
People who have died tragically, with unfinished business, or who were strongly attached to 
a location can return as ghosts. Being incorporeal places severe limits on a ghost’s ability to 
interact with the material world, though this can be overcome with various powers. 
Ghosts usually have the Anchor flaw; this is a place, object (often their remains), or a person 
they must return to before regaining resolve 
and CP. The Anchor can be used in rituals to 
permanently lay a ghost to rest. 
Ghosts can only spend XP to increase their 
creature rank. They cannot pick up any new 
careers or mundane boons and flaws, as their ‘old 
life’ is closed. 
Though they can be completely free-willed, 
ghosts are often driven by an Obsession involving 
the unfinished business they had in life. When 
Manifested they can appear in a pristine form, or 
ravaged by the wounds that took their life. The 
Veil power allows them to appear completely solid 
and normal.  
People with Second Sight and the Sensitive boons 
can communicate with ghosts without the use 
of powers. When a ghost moves something via 
their Manifest power this may be perceived as 
telekinesis, though in truth the ghost is directly 
manipulating the item; lifting it, throwing it, etc. 
Maximum Rank: 6

LYCANTHROPE
Lycanthropes are often tragic creatures, unable to contain 
their bestial nature and shedding innocent blood as a result. 
PCs and more experienced lycanthropes can 
master this dark side, but always a legacy of 
violence looms over them. 
Two sub-types of lycanthropes, 
werewolves and werecats, are 
described here. Presumably, many more 
versions exist across different cultures. 
All lycanthropes have the Wereform 
‘boon,’ which is inherent and doesn’t 
cost any flaws. This causes them to 
automatically shape-change into their 
representative animals under certain 
conditions.
While in human form, accessing powers 
is harder; lycanthropes take the higher 
difficulty modifier on activation rolls 
(Moderate becomes Tough, Tough 
becomes Demanding, etc.). Also, 
lycanthropes can only use their 
Natural Weapons and Supernatural 
Strength boons while transformed.

1 lycanthrope to transform, during 
which they can only writhe 
in pain. At rank 2 or higher, 
transformation is instantaneous. 
Rank 2 or higher lycanthropes 
can change into a human-
animal ‘hybrid’ that walks 
erect and has usable hands.  
Werewolves involuntarily 
transform under a full 
moon once a month, changing 
back into human shape at dawn. They can use the Force Change power to transform at 
other times, however, including full daylight. The Quell power allows them to control their 
animalistic impulses while changed. 
Werecats transform when another person (purposefully) arouses them, sexually. This can lead 
to a complex personal life. They use their Force Change and Quell powers as werewolves 
do.

RECOVERING CP
Creatures recover CP in specific ways, based on their type. Some ways are essentially 
passive, while others require going on the hunt!
Unlike with arcana, the rate of CP recovery doesn’t change based on the Magnitude of 
the powers used; it’s all considered the same.
Angels—CP are replenished on the holy 
day of their particular culture, or by 
lingering at a sacred site at the rate of 1 
CP per hour. 
Constructs—Defined by the player; 
suggestions include returning to a Creator 
to be ‘recharged’ with life essence, or 
resting in a mystic sanctum. 
Demons—Regain CP at the rate of 1 per 
point of resolve loss inflicted on a target 
using a resolve attack (see Mechanics) or 
by using the Drain power. Regardless, a 
demon will replenish all CP after resting for 
a month. 
Dhampir—CP are replenished as per 
vampires; 1 CP per point of lifeblood drunk 
from a target. This damage is considered 
lasting if consumed from a willing ‘donor.’ 
Fae—CP are regained at the rate of 1 per 
hour spent in the faerie realm. 
Ghosts—CP are regained at the rate 
of 1 per hour spent lingering around an 
Anchor, their physical remains, or favorite 
place. Ghosts can also regain CP at the 
rate of 1 per point of resolve loss inflicted 
through fear or via the Drain power. 
Ghouls—Regain CP by ingesting flesh; 1 
CP per point of lifeblood consumed. This is 
doubled for carrion. 
Lycanthropes—Regain CP by attacking 
people while transformed; 1 CP per point 
of lifeblood damage inflicted. Regardless, 
all CP are regained at the completion of a 
lunar cycle (1 month).

Sometimes called returners, or draugr in northern climes, revenants are undead who suffered

usually results in an animate corpse, though occasionally a violent spirit returns. In the latter

VAMPIRE
‘True’ vampires are undead who appear in a bewildering number of varieties, based on 
the bloodline of their progenitor, i.e., the creature that Turned them. Entire clans or clades 
of vampires are organized around a common ancestor, usually with a family surname to 
identify them (‘Martense,’ or ‘Strang,’ for example).  
Despite their many differences, all 
vampires crave blood to sustain 
themselves and appear to be 
immortal. A handful claim to have 
been present when the first mudbrick 
cities were reared in the Levant; a few 
claim to be older than that. The longer 
they survive, the more potent their 
blood becomes, as the precious fluid 
undergoes constant distillation.
Depending on the setting, vampires 
may have insinuated themselves 
into certain power structures 
(i.e., organized crime, corporate 
hierarchies, etc.). Their chief goal 
is remaining invisible to the bulk of 
society. In modern times, this isn’t 
because they fear angry mobs with 
wooden stakes; if today’s youth-
obsessed culture ever found out 
about the rejuvenating power of 
vampire blood, the creatures would 
either be farmed like dairy cows or 
forced to replicate their numbers 
beyond sustainable levels. 
Players can create original vampire 
clans using the faction rules (see pg. 
XX), subject to the GM’s approval. 
Among other things, vampire clans 
distinguish themselves by the specific 
powers their members can manifest. 
For example, the Strang clan is known 
to possess Flight, an otherwise rare 
power among vampires. Members of 
the Martense clan all have the Hideous flaw, (ironically) cast reflections in mirrors, and must 
use the Veil power to move among humans.

EXAMPLE OF CREATURE CHARGEN
Rose wants to play a young goth vampire named Threnody 
Malaise (a threnody is a poem memorializing the dead). The GM 
is limiting creature PCs to rank 0 at the start of the game, in an 
effort to make heroes more balanced. This means Threnody 
only recently Turned, which Rose factors into her character’s 
concept. 
Naturally, Threnody takes the creature origin. Prior to joining 
the undead, she was a music journalist and fervent devotee 
of city nightlife. Threnody started a relationship with Trey, a 
‘musician’ who moved into her apartment and promptly 
became a deadbeat. Among the many illicit substances Trey 
demanded was Red Thirst—street slang for vampire blood. 
While trying to procure the drug at a concert, Threnody was bitten 
by a vampire and Turned. She has little memory of the event, 
beyond a pair of bright eyes and throbbing music. Instead of 
consoling her about the experience, Trey exulted at having 
found a ‘natural’ source of Thirst. Threnody used her Fear power 
to send him screaming from the apartment.
For attributes, Threnody puts 2 points into Mind, 1 into Agility, and 
1 in Appeal. Rose wants a higher Strength for a vampire, so she’ll 
likely take a boon to round this out. 
Next up are careers. Threnody has creature at rank 0, her most 
recent. From there, Rose works backwards, giving her professional 
(journalist) at rank 2, slacker at rank 1, and artist (poet) at rank 1.  
From her origin, Threnody can pick a single creature boon, and 
takes Unholy Vitality, granting her d6-2 protection. Supernatural 
Strength won’t add anything at rank 0, so that’s out. She takes 
the creature flaw Scion to represent the ‘mystery’ vampire who 
bit her, and who will be turning up again at some point.
For standard boons, Threnody choses Great Strength, 
adding 1 to this attribute, and Hard as Nails, adding 
another 3 to her lifeblood. These boons essentially beef 
her up as a vampire; she didn’t have them prior to being 
Turned. For her pre-vampiric life, she takes the boon Artistic.
For creatures, each boon necessitates a flaw. Threnody takes Destitute (she barely scrapes 
by as a journalist), Fear of Sunlight (though she won’t burst into flames, she doesn’t like going 
out in the day), and Bounty. This last has to do with Trey; he acquired his taste for vampire 
blood while running with a group of slayers, who will soon come calling. 
Finally, Threnody totals up her derived abilities. She has a hefty 14 lifeblood, but must deduct 
1 from her Hero Point total, even as a rank 0 creature. She has (rank 0 + 10) 10 CP for her 
powers, which she’ll likely depend heavily on given her lack of creature boons. Luckily, a 
Mind of 2 will help with activation rolls.
