A Closer Look at the Pax Londinium
"Evening guvnor - how can I help you? Ah, you want to know about the Pax Londin­
ium and how we manage it when we live on the South Side? Say no more! Sit down, 
buy me a pint and we can talk about the wonders of South London.
My name? What my mother called me? That’s been lost to me a long time ago. 
Everyone calls me Spike now. Because of the large spear thing I carry to hook kids 
out of the Thames when they fall in - and they do, far more than you realise! Paid up 
member of the Guild of Water and Light, that’s me. I do a sideline as an advocate 
for people being hauled in front of the fae court for breaking the Pax too, so I’m 
pretty much an expert. Anyway, thanks for the pint and the nuts too. Let’s talk 
about the South Side"
— Spike of the Hidden, member of the Guild of Water and Light, resident of Jacob's 
Island Rookery and London's expert in the detail of the Pax
The Pax is a relatively new law, only coming into effect in 1952 but even so, it has been 
picked apart by fae courtiers - and if you want an agreement picked apart, the fae are the best 
people to do it - and those mortals that specialise in that most shady of legal precedent, limin­
al law. The text of the Pax itself is relatively plain
To ensure safety and order for all, mortal and supernatural, the great powers of London have agreed 
to limit the geography of their Hidden influence.
North of the Thames shall be the domain of the Liminal, whilst South of the Thames is no longer 
open to them.
Liminal beings seeking to travel across the Thames, North to South, will be stopped and turned back. 
Should they succeed in their journey, they will be watched and warned. Should they be found practicing 
magicks or exposing the mortal world to the Hidden world, they will be removed.
Exemptions on passage exist for those with ancient permissions to find their homes to the South, 
those who have been granted passage to the South and those bound by the Law of the Tether to a place. 
However, this exemption only extends to travel and not activity.
In the first passage, the debate has been around the phrase ‘their Hidden influence’ and 
what it exactly means. The spirit of the phrase would imply an agreement between fae, 
wizards, wolves, ghost courts, the Knowledge, the Hidden (we were invited, for once) and the 
mortal powers.  What about those that are not included? What about vampires? Are gutter 
mages included in the agreement simply because the Council of Merlin agreed? Common 
implementation suggests they are, but many would argue that they are not bound by the signa­
ture of a group that would not have them as members and that was made many years before 
they were born!
The second passage carries two questions - how far up and down stream does this division 
apply? And what exactly does ‘is no longer open to them’ mean? Many point to this as the 
power that allows the removal of Liminals from the South Side, but some argue it is simply a 
reiteration of the prohibition from travelling.

The third passage is sometimes referred to as the Queen’s Slippery Slope, as it moves from 
the transgressor being stopped and then, if they avoid that, being watched, and warned, and 
then if that doesn’t work, being removed. Many have argued that this ‘slope’ infers that there 
is no real punishment for travelling to the South Side, so why inhibit it? Others argue that in 
that case, the ‘watched and warned’ section could be dropped too? However, it is here that the 
Council of Merlin stepped in to give … forthright clarification. The consequences of removing 
both clauses would require a horde of groups of Liminal hunters, scouring the South Side 
for those that have broken the Pax and that, in all likelihood the burden would fall upon the 
Council to do this through their agents and knights. They would be far happier limiting the 
potential number of cases. Far happier…
The final passage lists the three exemptions: ancient pact, temporary pass, and the Law 
of the Tether. Here, the main debate is over who exactly can grant permission to pass to the 
South Side. This is popularly limited to the Queen of Hyde Park, the leader of the Council of 
Merlin, any of the four Guild leaders of the Mercury Collegium and the Archdeacon of the 
London Preceptory of the Order of St Bede. However, there is nothing that specifies this lim­
itation. Envoys between Hidden rookeries often traverse the bridges carrying permits signed 
by the leader of the rookery and they are mostly accepted. Mostly. 
There is some thought that the vague nature of the Pax illustrates that the Queen meant to 
have ambiguity built into it to keep everyone on their toes, and that she has other plans for 
the South Side. Of course, no-one has taken her up on this or speaks loudly of it around the 
fae court because they quite enjoy breathing.

What’s it like living on the South Side?
Let’s get one thing straight from the start - there are plenty of supernatural folk who live 
and thrive on the South Side. We just have a different attitude from the North Side folks. 
Face it, the Hidden World is dense on that side of the river and a lot of people there like 
to act like a cross between a bad reenactment troupe and a turn of the millennium LARP. 
Fae knights dueling in rooftop gardens for the honour of their lovers, packs of werewolves 
in leather jackets being bouncers in pubs, mages dropped straight out of Jonathan Strange 
and umpteen mortals sniffing around the fringes with their trench coats and intense stares. I 
swear I caught one with a katana once. An actual friggin’ katana.
No, we don’t have any of that shit on the South Side. It’s all just a bit more relaxed and 
chill. And that’s how we like it.
A Bluffer’s Guide to Breaking the Pax Londinium
There’s a test that people like to take right, called the Marshmallow Test. Some nonsense 
about willpower and what not. The South Side is like the marshmallow. The more some 
supernaturals get told they can’t go somewhere, the more they want to go. It’s part of 
the ‘human’ condition I suppose. And you wouldn’t be here talking to me if you weren’t 
interested in popping over, so let’s get to the point yeah? How can you get to the South Side?
Well, for a start, getting to the South Side and doing Liminal business in the South Side are 
two different things. 
One of the easiest ways to break the Pax is to simply drive across one the bridges in a car. It 
is highly unlikely that you’ll be stopped and certainly the trolls will leave you alone. P Division 
might well be a different kettle of fish though, as they almost certainly do have cameras on the 
bridges and an active link to the DVLA records. So, you steal a car, naturally.
Something a little more legal, but slightly riskier, is to use public transport. Bus journeys 
would appear to be as safe as car journeys, but for many of the cross-river routes, buses are 
rumoured to have agents of the great powers riding and watching. If they recognise a Liminal, 
they will follow them and issue their warning. Taking the Tube is another possibility but that 
is far more fraught. Any Liminal found by one of the watchers the Council of Merlin puts on 
the Tubes, masquerading as buskers, could find themselves in all manner of trouble as they 
wander the tunnels for hours, trying to find the true exit.
I would be remiss to not mention the services of the Guild of Tunnellers, the Hidden that 
move things around London through the lost tunnel networks under the city. Tradition holds 
that anyone accompanied by a Tunneller will not be confronted by the trolls and this is true. 
The small print of that tradition is that it exists between the trolls and the Hidden; anyone 
else can try to stop the Tunneller and their client.

There are other ways as well, but that involves the geography of London. Riddle me this 
… as you head south, when does London stop? When does London start? Is it the boroughs? 
The M25? The idea of ‘Greater London’? Who knows? All I know is that the Queen of 
Hyde Park is a fae who is still amazed that we don’t all use horse and cart and as such, her 
understanding of the far borders of London are notoriously vague. So, she focuses a huge 
amount of importance on Liminals moving across the river in Central London and very little 
about people yomping up the South coast. The obvious routes are covered - if you think you 
can travel up from Brighton and not be seen, you have another thing coming - but a lot of the 
less obvious routes are totally open.
In a similar vein, coming in from the sea along the river is another good way to get onto the 
South Side without attracting the attention of the trolls. The trick, if it isn’t too obvious, is 
not to land too close to the bridges. If you land east of the Thames Barrier or west of Putney 
Bridge and you are pretty certain not to raise the ire of the Queen. 
So, let’s assume you’ve got across? How can you work on the South Side and not break the 
Pax? Well, like every other law in the land, the easiest way is to not get caught. There’s no such 
thing as magical CCTV so if you keep it discreet and well, if you’ll pardon the pun, hidden, 
you should be fine.  
You could also just get permission to work on the South Side, but that’s never an easy thing 
to do. The Queen of Hyde Park is known to give out passes for knights and others to operate 
on the South Side, and sometimes - very rarely mind you - she’ll give someone permission 
to settle amongst a community, if they serve that community in some way. There will be 
restrictions - she’s a Fae Queen, so there are always restrictions - but it is a clear route to the 
South Side.
There are some rules that even the Queen of Hyde Park can’t break, and the law of the 
tether is one of them. Your roots matter so if, as a Liminal, you are tethered to somewhere 
or something on the South Side, you have the ancient right to be there. I, for example, am 
tethered to the Jacob’s Island Rookery and no troll on the river would dare keep me away 
from my tenement. This is particularly important for ghosts, who nearly always have a strong 
tether to some place or other and you don’t want to cross them when they want to go back 
and get their shit together!
Maybe the simplest way, but also possibly the riskiest, is to be employed to be on the South 
Side, keeping others out. Hook yourself up with Davy Moore and his lot in the Mercury 
Collegium, or join P Division, or be a member of the Order of St Bede and work out of their 
stronghold at Southwark Cathedral. Do that and you’ll have licence to wander around the 
South Side, all puffed up on your own importance, sticking your nose into people’s business 
as you see fit. No-one needs to know that you’re going into business for yourself and getting 
up to some mischief if you’re careful about it.

Places to Visit on the South Side
The Fairs
South London has always had a tradition of fairs, and this continues to this day - the right 
to hold fairs lies with a power older and darker than the Queen of Hyde Park and the Council 
of Merlin. They’re rare nowadays, happening only around special lunar events but when they 
do happen, they do allow some of the South Side dwelling Liminals a chance to let down 
their hair. 
During a fair, merchants of all sorts will converge on the area, selling every item imaginable 
(and some quite unimaginable!) Food vendors emerge from the shadows, rolling our carts of 
sweet-smelling morsels and unctuous soups. Musicians roam the streets and poets stand on 
street corners telling tales older than London itself. On these nights, the Liminals rule the 
streets and the mortals? They just don’t seem to notice. It’s magic right? 
The Imperial War Museum
The Imperial War Museum sits on the site of the old Bethlem Hospital - known to the rest 
of the world as Bedlam. I’ll just run that by you again - you put a monument to the insanity 
of war on top of the ruins of one of the most notorious asylums for the insane, something 
referred to as a ‘symbol of man’s inhumanity to man, for callousness and cruelty’ and you 
expect everything to be hunky dory? What sort of imbecile does that?
Well, in 1932, a gentlemen called Harold Harmsworth, the 1st Viscount Rothermere, 
offered up the land of the Imperial War Museum to be built there. Rothermere was a right 
piece of work and was known to those within the British establishment clued-in to the 
Hidden World as more than just a Nazi sympathiser. They were never able to pin anything on 
him or find out why someone who may have been aware of the consequences would do such a 
stupid thing, but it goes without saying that rumours abound that the Imperial War Museum 
was designed to be some sort of sleeping occult time bomb in the middle of London. Or, you 
know, somewhere to take the kids when it rains
So, what’s the truth? I have no idea. Any Liminal being worth the name stays well away 
from the place. There must be a Ghost Realm there, but no-one has ever found the door and 
frankly, no-one really wants to imagine the conjoined horrors that lie within if they did!
Jacob’s Island Rookery
The place I call home was formerly known in the days of Dickens as Jacob’s Island but now 
it’s a proper swanky piece of land on the south bank of the Thames. It’s all new apartments 
and houseboats. Gentrified, is what they call it right? Well, if you know the right alley to go 
down and the right words to whisper to the right rats, you can step into the Hidden World 
and reach my Rookery, Jacob’s Island and enter the squalid mire ruled by ‘Lord’ Sykes.
It is, to be fair, quite the actual shit hole. It is a criss-cross of poor crumbling tenements, 
muddy tidal ditches, open drains with all manner of effluent draining directly into them and

rickety bridges that haven’t been fixed properly since … well, I can’t remember anyone trying. 
The place stinks as well, almost like a rotting corpse and sometimes the sediment is stirred up 
on the ditches and red mud turns the water the colour of blood. Sometimes you even get the 
odd dead animal floating by when the weather is nice and hot. It is an absolute blessing to be 
tethered there, honestly…
Sykes is a proper old school Rookery Lord as well. He runs by his own rules and keeps his 
people in line with a mixture of benevolent dictatorship and sheer brutality. Not that it 
makes him any different from the other petty chancers and amateur tyrants that run the show 
in most rookeries. Thing is, at least Sykes knows to keep his nose clean with the Queen o’the 
Park. He counts dozens of members of the Guild of Water and Light, and Tunnelers as part 
of his rookery.
Aladdin’s Cave
Aladdin's Cave is, to us that sleep on the South Side, as Ahmed’s VHS Wonderland is to 
you on the North Side, but with less twee chic. No Council of Merlin panic button at the 
Cave - more likely anyone trying it on in this abandoned railway station turned junk shop will 
have to tackle the band of eagle-eyed mortal antique hunters scouring for a bargain. It used 
to be the Lewisham Road station on the defunct Nunhead to Greenwich Park railway so it’s 
a big place, apparently rammed with a random assortment of furniture and bric-a-brac but 
there’s all manner of magical potential in there. You just need to know where to look.
The cave has been passed down through a family of divining gutter mages who have been 
considered exempt from the dictates of the Pax for as long as anyone can remember. They 
keep their magic restricted to checking the provenance of the items that come into their 
collection and keeping the small clan of Boggarts who move up and down the railway line, 
delivering interesting items to them, in check.
