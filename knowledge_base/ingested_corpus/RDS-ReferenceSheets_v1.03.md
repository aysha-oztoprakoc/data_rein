RULE REFERENCE
Core Mechanic
Stats: Characters have four stats:
•	 Strength (STR) is physical power
•	 Dexterity (DEX) is physical agility
•	 Will (WIL) is mental power and social actions
•	 Intellect (INT) is mental agility and social perceptions 
Stats are represented by dice sizes from d4 to d10. For example, if you are strong 
but clumsy you may have a STR of d10 and a DEX of d4.
Difficulty: GM sets the difficulty of whatever you are facing between d4 and 
d12, with d8 being the most common.
The Check: GM rolls a red die based on the difficulty. Player rolls a die based 
on the stat being used (such as rolling your STR die to kick in a door or rolling 
your WIL die when bartering for goods).
•	 Player roll higher = Success
•	 Red die higher = Failure
•	 Tie = Special. Roll again. On this second roll:
•	 Player roll higher = Critical Success 
•	 Red die higher = Critical Failure
•	 Tie = Difficult success
Dice Bumps: Many things in this game will give you dice bumps. This means 
you increase or decrease the size of the die you roll, such as bumping a d6 up to 
a d8.
Karma and Momentum
Karma: Whenever a crit success or crit fail occurs, the winner of the crit (PCs 
or GM) gets a Karma Token.
Players or GM may spend their Karma tokens to do a number of things. Each 
thing on this list costs 1 Karma token:
•	 Reroll a failed Check (this option is only available outside of combat).
•	 Take an extra Quick or Full Action in your turn in combat.
•	 Gain a dice bump before rolling.
•	 Reroll a die. Karma may not be used to reroll the same die a second time, 
but it may be used to reroll the other die in a Check.
•	 The outcomes of a Check, in order, are:
crit fail — fail — difficult success — success — crit success
You may spend a Karma token to alter the outcome of a Check by one 
step in either direction.
•	 Recover 2 HP, 2 SP, or 1 of each.
•	 After a successful hit, spend 1 Karma to increase damage by 2 or to add a 
debuff in addition to doing damage.
•	 Small narrative changes to reality, if approved by the GM.
•	 Players and GM may come up with other uses, with GM approval. 
PC Karma Tokens are kept in a single pool that any player may use.
The players and the GM are limited to 5 Karma tokens each. If you already 
have 5 Karma tokens and you are supposed to gain another, you get nothing.
Momentum: Like Karma, Momentum is a pool of tokens held by the party as 
a whole rather than by each individual. Momentum may not be spent to gain 
benefits. It is only used to build up to Karma.
When the party reaches 5 Momentum tokens, discard them and gain 1 Karma 
token.
In combat, Momentum is earned by skipping actions. Outside of combat, 
Momentum is earned on most Checks rolled by the PCs. If the Check succeeds, 
PCs earn 1 Momentum. If the Check fails or is a difficult success, the GM earns 
1 Momentum. Crit success and crit fail earn Karma rather than Momentum.

Armor and Weapons
Armor & Shields: Wearing armor gives the attacker’s die a bump down when 
you are defending against attacks. You may not wear armor unless you first take 
the Armor Mastery Talent.
Shields do not count as armor. Instead, having a shield allows you to block all 
damage from a single source once per combat.
Weapon Types: Weapons are classified as light (daggers, whips, rapiers), medium 
(swords, flails, maces), heavy (greatswords, battle axes), or ranged (bows, pistols, 
throwing knives).
Each weapon type has a special ability and its own crit success rule.

Barehand Combat: If you fight without a weapon you may attack with STR 
or DEX, your choice, but your attacks get a bump down and do -1 damage.
Combat
Peril Rating: The peril rating is defined by the GM (typically 3 or 4) to set the danger 
level of the game. Attacks done by PCs do damage equal to the peril rating. Enemies do 
damage based on the peril rating, though some enemies get -1, +1, or +2 damage based 
on their difficulty.
Initiative: Before combat begins, one player makes a Check for the PC team. The 
player will either use DEX or INT, GM’s choice, based on whether reaction speed or 
perception better fits the situation. Team with the higher roll goes first.
Actions: On your turn in combat you get a Quick Action and a Full Action.
•	 Quick Actions are anything that do not require significant effort or concentration, 
such as:
•	 Move one range.
•	 Block – One of your defense Checks this turn gets a bump.
•	 Any other action that does not require significant effort or concentration, such 
as drawing a weapon or drinking a potion.
•	 Full Actions are anything that takes significant effort or concentration, such as:
•	 Attack an enemy in range. You may do a specialized attack instead of a normal 
attack:
•	 Focused Attack: Reduce damage by 1, 2, or 3 (to a minimum of 1) to get a 
respective 1, 2, or 3 bumps to the attack die.
•	 Debuff Attack: Reduce damage by 2 to impart a debuff along with doing 
damage.
•	 Sprint two ranges.
•	 Defend – All your defense Checks this turn get a bump.
•	 Anything else that takes significant effort or concentration, such as healing an 
ally or casting a spell.
Momentum: If you do not take a Quick Action in your turn, you earn 1 Momentum. 
If you do not take a Full Action, you earn 2 Momentum. The GM also earns 
Momentum tokens for Quick or Full Actions not taken by enemies.
Debuffs: Certain things may cause negative statuses that last for one round. Common 
debuffs are:
•	 Daze: Bump down on all actions you take.
•	 Knock Down: Bump up on all actions taken against you.
•	 Root: Cannot move on your next turn.
•	 Stagger: Any damage dealt to you is increased by 1.
•	 Weaken: Any damage you deal is reduced by 1.
Daze, Stagger, and Weaken can stack, meaning you can have multiple copies of a debuff. 
You lose 1 stack of each debuff at the end of your turn. Having multiple stacks does not 
increase the effect, just how long it lasts.

CHARACTER REFERENCE
Races
Centaur: Stalwart. Move twice as one action.
Dwarf: +2 HP, -2 SP. Can see in darkness.
Elf: Increase WIL (not over d8).
Goblin: -2 HP. Stealthy or Trap Master. Bite is light weapon. Highest number on your 
die in a melee defense Check = bite for 1 damage.
Giantblood: +2 HP. Armor Mastery. Armor.
Halfling: -2 HP. Immune to non-magical poisons and diseases. Once per combat you 
may dodge, negating all damage from a single source.
Human: -2 SP. Increase one Stat that is not d8.
Android: -2 SP. Select an extra Skill or Talent at character creation.
Lupin: Tracking. Normal barehanded combat rules do not apply to you. When fighting 
without a weapon your teeth and claws count as medium weapons.
Nymph: -2 HP, +2 SP. Choose:
•	 Spirit of growth: Heal, Cure, and Resurrect spells cost 1 less SP, minimum of 1.
•	 Spirit of vengeance: Empower Attack spells cost 1 less SP, minimum of 1.
Q’Ori: May walk on vertical surfaces at half speed, as long as the surface is sturdy 
enough to support the weight.
Shyleeth: -2 HP. Deft or Perception. Extra arms allow them to hold additional items, such 
as a torch or a shield, while wielding a two-handed weapon.
Skills
Agile: Skilled at non-combat Checks involving things like jumping, climbing, swimming, 
maintaining balance, and acrobatics. Skilled at Initiative Checks that use DEX.
Assault: Skilled at both physical and magical attack Checks.
Barter: Skilled at Currency Checks.
Block: Skilled at both physical and magical defense Checks.
Brute Force: Skilled in any non-combat Checks involving brute force. This does not 
include STR Save Checks.
Charming: Skilled in any Check involving positive social actions, such as convincing or 
influencing.
Deft: Skilled in non-combat Checks involving manual dexterity. This includes picking 
locks, stealing, and doing slight-of-hand.
Detect Magic: Skilled at detect magic Checks.
Direction Sense: Skilled in any Check involving finding your direction, such as finding 
your way through a dungeon or cave system or retracing a path you have taken. When 
you are outdoors, you may find true north without a Check.
Frightening: Skilled in any Check involving negative social actions, such as deceit, 
threats, coercion, intimidation, and distraction.
Intuition: Skilled in any social perception Check, including telling whether someone is 
trying to lie to you, trick you, or deceive you.
Knowledgeable: Skilled in knowledge Checks, with the exception of knowledge of 
nature, which requires the Naturalist Skill.
Naturalist: Skilled in any knowledge Check involving nature such as knowledge of 
plants or animals, including monsters. Also Skilled in any Check involving surviving in 
the wild such as finding or creating shelter, foraging for food, and finding water.
Perception: Skilled at perception Checks, including finding things that are hidden. 
Skilled at Initiative Checks that use INT.
Stalwart: Skilled at Save Checks.
Stealthy: Skilled in any Check involving sneaking and hiding, including finding 
someone who is sneaking or hiding.
Tracking: Skilled in any Check involving tracking someone or something or covering 
your tracks.
Trap Master: Skilled in any Check involving creating, locating, and disarming traps. You 
are also Skilled in Save Checks against traps.
Talents
Arcanum: You have the ability to cast Arcanum spells.
Bulwark: You have the ability to cast Bulwark spells.
Armor Mastery: You may not wear armor unless you 
have this Talent.
Barehand Mastery: Your barehand attacks are now considered light weapons and 
follow all the rules for those weapons instead of the normal Barehanded Combat rules. 
Even as light weapons, you may still choose STR or DEX for your barehand attacks.
Controller: When you do a debuff attack, you only need to reduce damage by 1 to 
apply a debuff instead of reducing damage by 2.
Empowered: +2 SP.
Language: You may purchase a language as a Talent, allowing you to speak, read, and 
write that language. Available languages are up to the GM, based on the setting.
Night Owl: You have trained yourself to use other senses to compensate for sight in 
low light situations. You do not suffer a bump down when your vision is impaired.
Resilient: +2 HP.
Talk to Animals: You understand animals and how they communicate, allowing you to 
speak with them. This form of communication is very simplistic. The GM may require a 
Check to communicate or understand more complex concepts.
Taunt: Once per turn, if the GM declares that an enemy is going to attack one of your 
party members and you are also within range of that enemy, you may choose to have the 
enemy attack you instead.
Bulwark vs. Arcanum:
In terms of classic RPGs, 
think of Bulwark as cleric 
magic and Arcanum as 
wizard magic.

Casting Spells
Rules for casting Bulwark and Arcanum spells:
•	 Bulwark casts spells involving health, growth, protection, defense, nurturing, etc. 
(cleric magic). Arcanum casts everything else (wizard magic).
•	 You may cast whatever spells you can imagine, as long as the effect is small. GM 
can say is a spell effect is too large to cast. Sample spell lists are commonly used 
spells, not exhaustive lists.
•	 If a Check is needed to cast a spell, the Check uses WIL.
•	 Spell target must be visible and within Far range.
•	 Casting spells costs SP. You do not spend SP if your Check to cast fails.
Sample Bulwark Spells
Buff: (2 SP, may also be cast with Arcanum) Give temporary buffs to allies in combat. 
This costs 2 SP and does not require a Check. The buff lasts only for the ally’s next turn. 
When you give a buff, select one of these effects:
•	 The ally gets a bump on all Checks made during their next turn.
•	 The ally gets a bump on all defense Checks made until their next turn.
•	 Any attacks the ally makes get +2 damage.
•	 The ally gets an extra Quick or Full Action.
Any buff you give only lasts for the person’s next turn. The GM may allow you to spend 
more SP to give additional buff effects, as long as all the effects are being given to the 
same target. When giving multiple buff effects with one Buff spell, each effect after the 
first only costs 1 additional SP.
Outside of combat, Buff may be used to grant a bump on a single Check.
Cure: (1-4 SP) Cure ailments (poison, disease, broken bone, turned to stone, etc.) 
instead of healing HP. SP cost is up to the GM based on the severity of the ailment. It is 
up to the GM whether healing an ailment requires a Check.
Fortify: (2 SP) Make an inanimate object tougher. Once cast, any attempt to harm the 
object has a bump down. Likewise, Fortify may be used to make breaking into something 
more difficult, even if the object is not being harmed, such as fortifying a lock so it is harder 
to pick. An object that is man-sized or smaller can be fortified without a Check for 2 SP. This 
lasts one week. To fortify larger objects, give a stronger fortification (bump down twice), or 
have the spell last a longer time, the GM will require more SP and/or require a Check.
Heal: (1-4 SP) Spend 1-3 SP to heal yourself or any adjacent target for 1d4 HP plus 1 
HP per SP spent. May add 1 SP to heal at Near or Far range.
Resurrect: (6 SP) Once per day, you may resurrect someone who has been dead for less 
than 48 hours. The person revives with 1 HP.
Resurrection may not be allowed in darker, grittier games.
Style Shift: (2 SP, may also be cast with Arcanum) Use a different Magic Style for one minute.
Undead Bane: (1-4 SP) Cast this spell on up to 4 undead. Cost is 1 SP per undead 
being affected. No Check to cast. Each affected undead makes a Save Check against the 
caster’s WIL, running away in fear if they fail. Undead with a difficulty of d10 or higher 
are immune to the spell. This spell may only be cast once on each undead.
Sample Arcanum Spells
Buff and Style Shift are both described in the Sample Bulwark Spells list. They may be used 
by anyone with Bulwark or Arcanum.
Bolt: (0 SP) Make a ranged attack Check using WIL.
Control: (2 SP) You may not take control of the body of a living being. That is too large 
of an effect. But you may alter someone’s physical state. This spell causes physical effects 
such as petrify, sleep, uncontrollable sneezing, etc. Physical effects cost 2 SP, require a 
successful Check, last for about 10 minutes, and end if the target is harmed. The GM 
may declare higher difficulty enemies (d10 or d12) are immune to these effects.
You may also use Control to affect someone as if you had touched them physically, 
such as shoving them or grabbing someone who is about to fall.
Control does not apply debuffs in combat. For that you use a debuff attack.
Empower Attack: (1-6 SP) You may magically empower attacks. This may be used 
with physical attacks and with magic attacks. This is part of the attack Action and does 
not require a separate Action.
You may add 2 SP to get a bump on the attack Check.
You may add 1 or 2 SP to increase the damage of the attack by the same amount.
You may add 2 SP to apply a debuff of your choice along with doing damage.
Illusion: (1-4 SP) Create illusions that last for up to 10 minutes. Difficulty of the Check 
and SP cost depend on the illusion’s complexity and size.
Invisibility: (1-2 SP) Make one person or object of horse size or smaller invisible. Costs 
1 SP and lasts for 10 minutes, or 2 SP and lasts for an hour. You lose invisibility if you do 
anything that would overtly draw attention to yourself, such as attacking someone.
Light: (1 SP) Cast on empty air to create a light about as bright (and that lasts about as 
long) as a torch.
Shift: (1-3 SP) Movement spell. Common effects:
•	 For 1 SP, spell’s target may levitate for 10 minutes.
•	 For 2 SP, teleport a willing target up to 50 feet.
•	 For 3 SP, spell’s target may fly for 10 minutes.
Telekinesis: (1-3 SP) Magically pick up and move inanimate objects. Causes you to 
strain as though you were actually moving the object. Does not normally require a 
Check, but the GM may require a Check if you are trying to use an inanimate object to 
do a difficult task. The difficulty of any Check and the amount of SP is up to the GM. 
But generally, heavier objects or more controlled movement cost more SP.
Telepathy: (1-2 SP) You may not take control of someone’s mind, nor can you directly 
read someone’s mind. Those effects are too large. You may make mental “suggestions” 
to nudge someone into the way you would like them to think. Or you could read the 
overall flavor of someone’s thoughts (anger, jealousy, devotion, etc.) rather than the 
specific thoughts. Most commonly used to read emotions.
