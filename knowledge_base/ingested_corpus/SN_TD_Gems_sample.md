ABOUT T REA SURE DRES SING 
Treasure Dressing supplements enable you to easily add 
interesting, flavoursome and unique treasures to your 
campaign. This helps you bring the world to life in your 
players’ imagination and adds anticipation, wonderment and 
enchantment back into their characters’ lootings. 
PERSONAGES AND LOCATIONS  
Many of the items listed in this supplement reference specific 
persons or locations. Some belong to the Lonely Coast, 
Duchy of Ashlar or Picaroon Peninsula mini-campaign 
settings; others are placeholder names. 
ABOUT THIS B OO K 
Treasure Dressing: Gems & Jewellery contains descriptions of 
scores of interesting and unusual pieces of jewellery. Use 
these descriptions for the pieces your characters come across 
during their adventures, either as part of a treasure hoard or 
as those worn by an NPC or monster. 
UNIQUE PIECES IN YOUR CAMPAIGN 
Adding a history and special appearance can go a long way 
to making a gem, jewel or piece of jewellery remarkable and 
memorable. In a world where treasure is often just a 
commodity only valuable for what it can do for the characters, 
unique items achieve a special value. 
	
If you plan to add such a treasure into your campaign, 
consider letting the characters hear rumours of the gem or 
piece of jewellery in question (and others) time before they 
finally encounter it. Powerful, famed items may have an even 
greater reputation than the hero (or villain) possessing them 
and could be the subject of many tall tavern tales. 
	
Alternatively, you can add details of such an item into a 
character’s background—perhaps an elf has heard rumours of 
the Crown of Cth’s location and wants to adventure to gain 
the skills and knowledge necessary to recover it. Making the 
item the focus of a personal quest makes its final acquisition 
so much more meaningful for the character.

GE MS 
Gemstones, or jewels, are pieces of minerals often cut or 
polished to make jewellery or to decorate other items. 
Ranging in value from ornamental stones worth a few gold 
pieces up to valuable gems worth thousands, they are 
popular with adventurers for their portability and the ease 
with which they can be hidden. 
CATEGORIES OF GEMS 
Gemstones fall into four basic categories of quality and value: 
•
Ornamental Stones: 10 gp. 
•
Semi-Precious Stones: 50 gp. 
•
Fancy Stones: 100 - 500 gp. 
•
Gemstones: 1,000 gp or more. 
Size & Cut: A stone’s size and cut can substantially modify its 
base value. For example, jasper is a semi-precious stone 
usually worth around 50 gp, but a tiny or poor-quality jasper 
could be worth only 20 gp, while a large or high-quality stone 
could be worth 100 gp. 
	
Rarity: The rarity of a given type of stone in a campaign 
area could also raise or lower its value. 
	
Use: A gem’s perceived value can also depend on its 
supposed magical application. For example, most wizards 
crave a pearl worth 100 gp or more (for the identify spell). 
Other stones may be highly sought after for other reasons; 
diamond dust is a component in many spells, and in a region 
plagued by disease, amethysts, diamonds, and rubies may be 
sought for their protective properties. 
GEM GLOSSARY 
These terms describe the ways gems interact with light: 
•
Opaque: Not transparent or translucent; the gem does 
not reflect light. 
•
Subtranslucent: The gem is translucent only at the 
edges or in thin slivers. 
•
Translucent: The gem allows light to pass through 
partially or diffusely. 
•
Transparent: The gem permits the uninterrupted 
passage of light. 
MAGICAL PROPERTIES OF GEMS 
Certain kinds of gems are reputed to have magical 
properties. While these reputed properties may have no 
actual in-game powers, they can be used to add a handy 
extra level of verisimilitude to treasure hoards (and magic 
items in particular). For example, amethyst is believed to 
strengthen the immune system and so would make an 
excellent addition to a periapt of proof against poison. 
Agate: The Fire Stone; builds courage and fortitude. 
Amethyst: Strengthens the immune system; heals the soul, 
body and mind. 
Bloodstone: Calms fears; aids in overcoming obstacles; a 
warrior’s stone. 
Carnelian: Energises the physical, emotional and mental self; 
wards off evil thoughts and inspires bravery. 
Chalcedony: Inspires courage and grounds the wearer in 
reality. 
Diamond: The master healer; dispels negativity, purifies the 
body. 
Garnet: Brings love and compassion; enhances imagination. 
Jade: Fosters courage, justice, clarity, modesty, wisdom and 
nurturing. The stone of longevity. 
Jasper (Red): Powerful divination stone. 
Lapis Lazuli: The Night Stone; good for nocturnal activities; 
augments psychic abilities. 
Malachite: Brings peaceful sleep; empowers divinations, 
especially those pertaining to one’s heart’s desire. 
Moonstone: Inspires flexibility, wisdom and balanced 
emotions. 
Onyx: Aids balance and self-control; a protective stone. 
Opal: Aids balance, eyesight and improves self-esteem and 
happiness. 
Peridot: A good anti-toxin gem; cleans the body of 
impurities, stimulates the mind and banishes lethargy; 
attracts wealth. 
Quartz: Good for the brain and soul; aids communication. 
Ruby: Preserves the body and improves mental health. 
Topaz: Enables communication with other planes. 
Tourmaline: Dispels fear and negativity. 
Zircon: The Prudent One; protection against sleeplessness 
and depression. Makes the owner 
more pleasant and wise.

J EWE LL E RY 
Virtually any item can be adorned with gems and jewels. 
People wear jewellery for many reasons, including vanity, 
decoration or as a mark of status or belonging. 
JEWELLERY ITEM BY BODY PART 
Virtually any item can be augmented with a gem or two: 
Head: Circlet, comb, coronet, crown, diadem, earring, 
eyeglasses, eye patch, hairpin, hatpin, headband, 
monocle, nose ring, tiara, torc 
Throat and Neck: Chain, choker, holy symbol, locket, medal, 
medallion, necklace, pendant, periapt, talisman, unholy 
symbol 
Chest: Brooch, clasp, pendant, pin 
Waist: Belt buckle, belt 
Arm, Wrist and Hand: Armband, bracelet, bracers, ring 
Foot: Anklet, toe ring 
JEWELLERY BY MAIN COMPONENT 
Some substances are often used to create jewellery: 
Body Part: Bone, fang, ivory, horn, scale, tooth 
Metal (Common): Brass, bronze, copper, electrum, gold, 
silver 
Metal (Rare): adamantine, mithral, platinum 
Other: Crystal, glass, stone
