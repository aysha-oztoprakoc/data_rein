REALWORLDSORCERY
This document is intended for GM’s wanting a real world feel
for sorcery in their games.
RealWorld Sorcery forSavage Worlds
Magick
"THE OLD spelling MAGICK has been adopted throughout in
order to distinguish the Science of the Magi from all its
counterfeits"
~ Aleister Crowley, Magick Part II (1912)
THIS TREATSISE is an attempt to replicate Real World Magick
(whether you believe in True Magick or not) for the Savage
Worlds Setting you wish to have a real world feel. As quoted by
Aleister Crowley above, you will see the word Magick used
sometimes. This is the correct spelling. Magic, on the other
hand, is the type of illusion, sleight of hand, or trickery used by
stage magicians.
Sorcery
FIRST, ALL Magick is Sorcery. You can call your character by any
moniker you wish - whether a Magic-User, Necromancer, Spell-
caster, Witch, Wizard, or whatever. In the end ALL Magick is
Sorcery and ALL Spell-casters are Sorceror's.
Rituals
SECOND, ALL Sorcery is Ritual. The Sorceror performs a ritual to
cast the Magick Spell, or Power in Savage Worlds parlance.
The Sorceror MUST take the Ritual Skill (linked to his Spirit).
There is NO Edge required to perform Sorcery. The Arcane
Background has been replaced by the Ritual Skill.
There is NO Arcane Resistance Edge.
Pact
NEXT, the Sorceror does not manifest the effects of the Ritual -
this is the realm of the Sorceror's Patron.
The Sorceror MUST make a Pact with a Supernatural Entity, his
Patron. Whether it is The Devil, a Demon, a Spirit, an Angel,
whatever - it is this Patron that “manifests” the Rituals effects.
The Patron may manifest the effects through the Sorceror,
however it is normal for the ritual effects to be manifested
through an “agent” of the Sorceror’s patron or the patron itself.
Skills
THE SKILLS required by the Sorceror are as follows:
FIRST AND foremost the Ritual Skill. The Ritual Skill is linked to
the Sorceror's Spirit Attribute. The Ritual Skill is the Skill you roll
when performing any Sorcery.
Next, Occult: The Occult Skill is linked to the Sorceror's Spirit
Attribute. The number of Rituals your character will possess or
know at any one time is his Occult Skill dice-type divided by 2
For example: Michael Scott, the noted 13rth century Wizard
(Sorceror) has an Occult Skill of d8, this gives Scott four (4)
known Rituals.
No Power Points
SORCERY USES the No Power Points Rule. The No Power Points
Rule used here is a tweak.
Sorcery takes time - you CANNOT speed up the process. I
cannot stress this point any stronger.
Why? This forms part of the real world feel of the rules.
Sorceror's in the real world do not swagger around with an
arsenal of spells dealing death and destruction like a human
B52 bomber or battleship.
Playing a Sorceror (or whatever you call your character) in the
real world is NOT the same as typical fantasy priests and
wizards. Sorceror's do not bring down fiery doom upon their
foes [although there is an exception later on in the listed
Rituals], nor do they cast spell after spell in rapid succession.
Real World Magick is extremely powerful, dark, and ALWAYS a
force NEVER EVER to be trifled with.
The Power Points denote the number of Turns/Rounds required
to perform the Ritual.
Success or Failure
For a Sorceror to successfully perform a Ritual he MUST:
For each Round/Turn required to perform the Ritual, attempt a
successful Ritual Skill Roll.
A Failed Ritual Skill Roll means the Ritual as a whole has Failed.
There are NO shortcuts, for every Round/Turn of the Ritual a
Ritual Skill Roll MUST be rolled - NO EXCEPTIONS
The effects of the Ritual manifest on the Round/Turn following
the final Ritual Skill Roll.
Example: The effects of a Ritual that requires 3 Rounds to
perform will manifest on the 4th Round.


The Costsof Performing A Ritual
Exhausted: Regardless of the number of Turns/Rounds a Ritual
requires to complete, and whether the Ritual itself was a
success or a failure, the Sorceror is Exhausted. That means, he
takes two levels of FATIGUE. As noted in the Savage Worlds
Core Rules, when a character is Exhausted (two levels of
Fatigue) he MUST takes two (2) Game Hours of complete rest
to recover.
If anyone assisted in the performance of the Ritual, they too will
incur two levels of Fatigue.
Corruption Points: The Sorceror MAY incur Corruption Points
for performing a ritual. If performing a ritual incurs Corruption
Points it will be noted in the Ritual Stat Block.
Sanity Points: The Sorceror MAY incur Sanity Points for
performing a ritual. If performing a ritual incurs Sanity Points it
will be noted in the Ritual Stat Block.
Critical Failure: Should any participant of a ritual roll a Critical
Failure on his Ritual Skill Roll, the participant takes three levels
of Fatigue. If the participant becomes Incapacitated, he is
helpless and may be unconscious (GM’s call) for 2d6 hours.

Should a Ritual Fail, the participants are afflicted by the above
costs of the ritual performance plus the participants MUST roll
on the Backlash Table

The Ritual will need to be performed again.
Dealingwith theDevil(Deadlands)
A Sorceror MAY choose to engage with The Devil Himself. This is
extremely perilous, but has great potential as well. Dealing
with The Devil is an action which can only be attempted once
per year.
The potential is the Sorceror MAY perform ANY Ritual available
in the Setting. The process is as follows:
1. Spend a Benny: This is The Devils cost
2. Choose a Ritual: Choose any Ritual allowed in the Setting
3. Gamble: Roll vs. your Gamble Skill.
* A Raise allows the Ritual to be performed by The Devil his very
self!
* Add 4 to your Corruption.
* A Failure: You add 4 to yhour Corruption.
* A Critical Failure: The Devil gets your soul!


Corn Dolly
Anyone with an understanding of sorcery knows of a sinister
method to create a magickal link to a victim. This is the Poppet
or Corn Dolly.
A Corn Dolly is a small representation of the intended victim,
constructed by the Sorceror, from corn.
Sometimes other easily available materials such as wood or clay
are used. In this case, the doll is known as a Poppet (for game
purposes, Corn Dollies and Poppets are identical).
During the construction process, which needs no particular craft
skills and takes one hour, the Sorceror must concentrate on the
intended victim. Most importantly, the sorcerer will need an
item that has an intimate connection with the victim, such as a
fragment of his clothing, a nail-clipping or lock of hair, or some
bodily fluid like blood or other. This item or substance must be
incorporated into the Corn Dolly somehow.
Once the corn dolly is complete, the Sorceror may use spells
against the victim from any range, even if he is unable to
physically see the victim.
The Sorceror must hold the Corn Dolly in his hand when
performing the ritual to gain the benefit, and retain it about his
person for the duration of the ritual, or else the ritual instantly
fails or ends.
Evil Eye
The Evil Eye, or Overlooking, is a curse brought about by a
malevolent glare, usually inspired by envy.
Warding the Evil Eye takes the form of spitting in the persons
eye, or making the sign of the cross.
In Game Terms, making the Evil Eye results in a curse on a
victim or a blight on crops or animals.
A curse takes the form of a -2 on ALL Rolls.
For a Blight on crops and animals see the Ritual Blight Crop.
The range of the evil eye is the unassisted sight of the person
Overlooking.
Lifting the curse or blight can be achieved by the Dispel Ritual,
or the death of the person who made the Evil Eye.
The Evil Eye is a Special Ability and is only available to Wild Card
Villain’s.
Sacrifices
A SACRIFICIAL victim can significantly increase the chances of a
ritual succeeding.
* Chickens and small birds give a +1 to the Ritual Roll
* Goats and Cattle give a +2 to the Ritual Roll
* A human victim gives +4 or more to the Ritual Roll (see the
ritual description)
Performing Rituals
THE Sorceror chooses the Ritual (Power) he wishes to perform,
and as an Action, declares that he is performing the Ritual for
that Power.
The Sorceror notes the Power Points of the Ritual (Power) he is
performing.
A Ritual for a Power that has 2 power points requires the
Sorceror to successfully roll his Ritual Skill over two successive
Rounds/Turns to be successful.
If successful, consult the particular power to determine the
results.
The Sorceror is Exhausted. The Fatigue wears off after 2 Hours
of complete bed rest.
Magick in the Real World is not a matter of uttering a few
words and waving one’s hands. Magick is complex and requires
time if it is to work properly.
The Sorceror may take no other actions during the Turn,
including movement, he loses his usual PARRY score if attacked.
Once the Sorceror finishes the Ritual, the effect of the Power
manifests on the FOLLOWING Round.
Demons and Spirits
A COMMON use of Sorcery is to Evoke ("call out") demonic
forces to do the bidding of the Sorceror. This type of Ritual is
often performed by Demonologists, Necromancers, Warlocks,
and evil Wizards.
In the Bible King Solomon enslaved Lower Spirits (Demons) and
used them for noble purposes. This was done by calling upon
God and His Servants (the Angels) to command these Lower
Spirits (Demons). Solomon then "Called Out" the Lower Spirit
(Demon) in question.
Demons and Spirits are notoriously fickle and frequently
attempt to mislead the Sorceror or struggle for release.
Rule: When asking a Lower Spirit (Demon) a question, or
commanding a Lower Spirit (Demon) to do something the
Sorceror MUST make an Opposed Roll: The Sorceror's Occult
Roll vs. the Lower Spirits (Demons) Spirit.
A Raise means the Lower Spirit (Demon) answers the question
or performs the task as instructed, albeit grudgingly.
A success means the Lower Spirit (Demon) performs the task
but will try to twist the meaning of the command and thus the
result.
A failure means the Lower Spirit (Demon) will try to return to
where it came from, and will usually exact vengeance upon the
hapless Sorceror.
Demons are ethereal meaning demons cannot be seen and
cannot affect the physical realm. However, if commanded to do
so the Demon makes a Spirit Roll to do so. If the result is a Raise,
the Demo MAY manifest a physical action on the Earthly realm -


Lower Spirits (Demons) CANNOT manifest physically themselves
- that is the realm of Possession.
Note: The GM should play the Lower Spirit (Demon) and act as
deviously as he can!

Corruptionand Sanity
Corruption
AS IT TURNS out, Sorcery is inherently corrupting to the
Sorceror's body, mind and soul - who would have thought!
You see dealing with demonic, paranormal, and supernatural
entities is not apparently a normal pastime. Each time a
Sorceror contacts his patron, a demon, or performs a ritual the
action May have a corrupting influence on the Sorceror.
For real world proof, one only has to briefly read about Aleister
Crowley, the self proclaimed Beast.
Crowley was a drug addict, and a sex fiend, amongst his other
bestial personal habits.
The stat block of the ritual will note any Corruption Points that
is inflicted upon the Sorceror if he attempts to perform the
ritual regardless if the ritual was a success or failure.
Additionally, any ritual that affects a victim may also have
Corrupting effects on the poor individual.
For every Point of Corruption, the Sorceror reduces his
Charisma by 1 Point.
Should a Sorceror accrue as many Corruption Points as his
Sanity Points (or more) he takes a Hindrance such as a Habit
(addiction), or a Quirk (a fetish of some sort). The GM may also
impose other Hindrances as he sees fit.
Sanity
SANITY TOO is a potential danger to the budding Sorceror. As
with corruption, dealing with demonic and supernatural entities
and performing rituals May have an effect on the Sorceror's
Sanity.
The effect is noted in the Ritual stat block and description. Any
victim of the ritual MAY also loose sanity points.
Sanity loss has the notation x/xdx. The first value is the
automatic loss, if any. The second value is a dice roll should the
Sorceror and/or victim fail a Guts Roll for Sanity.
Example: A Ritual has the following Sanity loss 1/1d4. The
Sorceror loses an automatic 1 Sanity Point for performing the
ritual. The Sorceror MUST then attempt a Guts Roll for Sanity.
Should the Sorceror fail the Guts Roll he MUST Roll a 1d4 for an
additional amount of Sanity Points loss.
From the above description, the reader will note performing a
Ritual is indeed a perilous action, and something not attempted
at a whim or without careful forethought.

Rituals(Powers)
LISTED below are real world Rituals. Each Ritual has the
following statistics:
Ritual Time: This is the number of Rounds/Turns required to
perform the ritual. The time is based on the Power Points of the
Power.
Corruption Points: The number of Corruption Points (if any) for
preforming the Ritual
Sanity Points: The number of Sanity Points (if any) for
preforming the Ritual
Range: The maximum distance the victim of the ritual can be
from the Sorceror when the ritual is first activated (it remains in
effect even if the victim moves out of range until the end of the
rituals duration).
Example: A Range of Smarts, for a Sorceror who has a Smarts
Attribute of d10, would be means 10".
If a ritual lists three ranges, such as 12/24/48, these are read
just like ranged weapon ranges, and subtract the standard
penalties for each range bracket (0/–2/–4).
The Sorceror’s Ritual Skill Roll acts as both performing and
attack for these ritual types.
Duration: How long the effects of the ritual will last. Some
rituals have a Duration based on the Rank of the caster.
A ritual with a Duration of 1 minute/Rank, for instance, lasts
just 1 Game Minute for a Novice character.
If performed by a Heroic character, the ritual has a Duration of
4 Game Minutes as the performer has four Ranks under his belt.

see demons have one and only one mission in life (and a very
long life indeed).
From the beginning - and here we mean the biblical Genesis -
the demons reason for being is the downfall of Man (humanity).
And demons are quite good at it. Look at the world around you
now, at this very moment - NOT GOOD.

ConjureApollonius ofTyana [Grave Speak: Necromancy]
Available: from Biblical days to today
Time: 8 Turns
Corruption Points: 1
Sanity Points: 1
Range: Touch
Duration: 2 Turns/Rank
Trappings: Runes carved on bodily remains, Black Candles,
Leather Books, Ouija board
Raises the ghost of the dead. The Sorceror MUST possess the
body of the deceased he wishes to contact.
If successful the phantom of the dead appears and may be
questioned. The Sorceror may ask one question, and one
question alone.
The spirit of the deceased may not be friendly, but is impelled
to answer the question - although the answer may be in the
form of a riddle or may be obscure requiring research to
decipher.
The answer will be only what the deceased knew during its life
up to the point of its death and nothing more.
If the ritual fails the Sorceror is afflicted by a wound if the ghost
can make a successful attack.
A critical failure conjures up a Demonic or other hostile entity.
This entity, although it CANNOT affect the Sorceror physically,
may try to mislead the Sorceror for it is not obliged to answer
truthfully.

Invisibility
Available at any time from the ancient Egypt period onwards
Time: 1 Turn
Range: Self
Duration: until desired
Trappings: Call Out Harpocrates
With a success this ritual invokes the Egyptian God Harpocrates.
This is NOT the fantasy Invisibility Spell. The Sorceror is not
invisible is the truest sense of the term, but the Sorceror is
simply NOT noticed by those around him.
The Sorceror WILL be visible to those who have a specific
reason to look for him, though those who do MUST attempt a
successful Notice Skill Roll at -4.
Should someone bump into the Sorceror while invisible, they
will not see the Sorceror (perhaps thinking they are clumsy).
The Ritual is for a specified time only. If performed for a
recipient other than the Sorceror, that recipient MUST return to
the Sorceror at a specific time to remove the effects of the
ritual. Failure to do so will result in the recipient being lost
forever.
MithrasLiturgy
Available to initiates of the Mithras cult from the Roman Empire
onwards, in very rare editions to other Sorceror's from the 4th
century, and widely available in English and German from the
late 19th century.
Time: 4 Turns
Range: Small Burst Template
Duration: 1 minute per Rank of Sorceror
Trappings: Performed in a Cave
Any character present during the ritual may charge an amulet
with the power of Mithras.
When this amulet is worn, and triggered by the person wearing
the amulet by spending a Benny, the person receives +1 to be
immune from being Shaken for the duration of one minute per
Sorceror's rank.
Requires the ritual to be performed in a Cave.
