Primal Forces
Magic System Expansion 
Effective 10/06/2025 — Created by Justin Fritz
Greetings, and welcome to Primal Forces! This exciting expansion for Advanced Armory marks a 
major milestone in the development of our magic system, bringing with it a host of both new and 
revised spell cards. What began with the Advanced Armory Starter Deck spells quickly surged into 
something greater—we carried that momentum forward to fully realize the Book of Air, Book of 
Flame, Book of Stone, and Book of Water.
Along the way, we developed several improvements to the system—visual upgrades, streamlined 
text, and expanded mechanics originally planned for Arcane College. Rather than hold these 
back, we’re releasing them early with this expansion. What follows is a summary of the new 
icons, visual components, and supporting rules, along with context for how they fit into your 
game.
Looking for a complete overview of how magic works in Advanced Armory? Explore the Arcane College 
chapter of the Heroes Guidebook, available now as a digital download on DriveThruRPG.

Revised Cards
If you already own Advanced Armory spell 
cards, you’ll find that most revisions are 
primarily cosmetic or visual. Common 
textual information that appeared on nearly 
every card is now conveyed through icons 
and layout, leaving more space for unique 
spell text and mechanics. Unless otherwise 
noted in this document, new and revised 
copies of a spell (identified by name and 
serialization) are fully interchangeable with 
your existing cards.
Worried about having extra copies? Don’t be. 
During early playtesting of Advanced Armory, 
we quickly discovered that multiple copies 
of spells were not only handy but often 
necessary. Players with more than one hero 
in their deck boxes, along with the demands 
of the Quest Master, meant we often printed 
several decks just to keep things running 
smoothly. We’re confident that even your 
duplicate cards will find a welcome place at 
the table.
Optimizing Card Text
One of the key improvements to our spells 
is the reduction of redundant text on spell 
cards. Streamlining the language allows us 
to use larger font sizes more consistently, 
making cards easier to read at the table. 
You’ll see these changes across the new cards, 
with examples explained below.
Discard After Use
By default, all spells in Advanced Armory are 
discarded after use: cast the spell, resolve its 
effects, then discard the card. Exceptions and 
other game effects may apply. Because this 
rule is universal, the phrase “Discard after use” 
has been removed from the new card designs.
Line of Sight (LoS)
Many spells require a caster to 
see their target before casting. 
Traditionally, this was stated 
in the spell text. To streamline 
presentation, we’ve introduced a new icon to 
represent Line of Sight, which now reinforces 
or replaces this text on spell cards.
Wherever you see this icon, line of sight to 
your spell’s target(s) is required.
Optional Game Mechanics
The following game mechanics are entirely 
optional. Use them to add tactical variety 
and narrative depth to your campaigns—
or ignore them if you prefer to keep things 
simple.
Instantly Destroyed
Some spells and abilities are so deadly that 
they take effect instantly. They cannot be 
countered, interrupted, or prevented in any 
way. When a creature or object is instantly 
destroyed, it is removed from play with no 
chance of being saved.
Maximum Spell Range
Some gaming groups take their adventures 
beyond the dungeon and into wide-open 
spaces. In these cases, a spell’s maximum 
range equals your Mind score in squares (or 
inches, if you are not using a grid).
Primal Magic
The four most fundamental forces of the realm 
are Air, Fire, Earth, and Water. Artifacts, 
elementals, and spells aligned with one of 
these elements carry the Primal keyword.

Spell Tier Frames
One of the hallmarks of our magic system is 
the Tiers of Magic, which define the core 
power levels of all spellcasters. To make 
these tiers easier to recognize at a glance, 
we’ve added new escalating frame art to each 
spell card. When combined with existing 
keywords, these frames make it simple to 
identify a spell’s tier.
This change is purely cosmetic. If your original 
spell cards do not include the new frames, 
they remain fully valid and interchangeable. 
There is no need to rebuy or reprint unless 
you simply want the updated look.
Spell Range
One of the hallmarks of Advanced Armory is 
our range grid system. These grids provide 
a simple, visual representation of where you 
can attack, use a skill, or cast a spell. As the 
system evolves, we continue to add depth 
and granularity. To that end, this expansion 
introduces color-coded range profiles.
Green Range
When a range grid on a card displays green 
squares, you choose a single target 
occupying one of those squares. Individual 
card text may allow you to (or automatically) 
choose more than one target.
Red Range
When a range grid on a card displays red 
squares, all creatures (and other eligible 
targets) 
occupying 
those 
squares—and 
meeting 
any 
other 
requirements—are 
affected by the ability, attack, or spell.
Color Blindness
We recognize that some players may have 
difficulty distinguishing between red and 
green grids. To make this easier, all new 
spells with a Red Range will now include a 
star symbol next to their keywords for quick 
reference.

Area of Effect (AoE)
Area of Effect spells and abilities affect 
multiple squares at once. The following rules 
establish how they function in Advanced 
Armory, including profiles, environments, 
and common edge cases.
AoE Basics
Focal Point: When casting an AoE spell, you 
choose the spell’s center (usually the caster, 
but it may also be a targeted creature, object, 
or square on the map).
Profiles: Each AoE uses a range profile 
that shows which squares are affected. All 
applicable creatures in those squares are 
affected.
Walls & Barriers: AoE effects do not pass 
through walls, closed doors, or natural 
barriers unless the spell text says otherwise. 
AoE spells will ignore furniture unless the 
spell text says otherwise.
AoE Range Profiles
Cone: Choose an adjacent square, then face 
that direction. The spell expands outward 
from the caster in a cone shape. All creatures 
the cone touches are automatically hit.
Small Blast: Target a 2×2 group of 
squares. All applicable creatures in those 
squares are affected.
Large Blast: Target a 3×3 group of 
squares. All applicable creatures in those 
squares are affected.
Line of Sight (Except Self):
Automatically hits all creatures within line of 
sight except the caster.
Line of Sight (Including Self):
Automatically hits all creatures within line of 
sight, including the caster.
Ray (Straight Line): Choose an 
adjacent square, then face that direction. 
The effect extends outward in a straight line 
from the caster. All creatures the line passes 
through are automatically hit. The line ends 
when it strikes a wall, closed door, or scenery 
object (such as furniture).


5
Ring: The AoE centers on the caster, 
affecting all applicable targets in surrounding 
squares.
Room: The spell affects all creatures within 
the room (or corridor) where it is cast.
X Marks the Spot: Some AoE spells 
display an X at the center of their grid. These 
spells require you to target a specific creature 
or object, which becomes the center of the 
AoE.
AoE in Enclosed Spaces
Rooms: When cast in a room, an AoE extends 
outward from its center until it meets a wall, 
natural barrier, or a closed door. Effects do 
not cross into adjacent rooms or corridors.
Corridors: In corridors, an AoE expands in 
all cardinal directions relative to its center, 
ending when it meets a wall or obstacle.
Maximum Range: Maximum range rules 
do not apply indoors or in enclosed 
environments.
Additional Considerations
Verticality 
(Height/Levels): 
AoEs 
do 
not expand upward or downward unless 
explicitly stated in the spell text.
Doorways: If centered in a doorway, the AoE 
applies only in the direction the caster is 
facing. Declare facing to the Quest Master 
before casting.
Friendly Fire: AoEs affect all creatures in 
their area, including heroes, unless the spell 
specifically says otherwise.
Obstructions: Large, solid objects inside the 
AoE (pillars, statues, furniture, etc.) do not 
block the effect. The AoE fills its profile 
regardless.
Multiple AoEs Overlapping: Effects stack. 
Resolve in the order cast. Interrupts resolve 
first; if multiple interrupts occur, resolve in 
casting order.
Moving AoEs: Some spells create ongoing 
hazards (e.g., Snow Devil). These are treated 
like summoned creatures for rules purposes.

Harnessing Elementals
The Nature of Elementals
Elementals are as fundamental to the living 
realm as the energies from which they are 
born. These beings exist primarily as pure 
consciousness—the raw will of nature given 
form. Wind elementals drive the shifting 
currents of the sky, stirring and calming 
storms. Water elementals churn the tides 
and shape the rivers. Stone elementals roll 
down mountainsides, clearing overgrowth 
and renewing the land. Fire elementals blaze 
through fields and forests, burning decay so 
that new life may rise. Together they flow 
through the leylines—the world’s living 
veins—guiding the balance of all ecosystems 
in an endless, unseen struggle for harmony.
Invoking the Primal
Since the dawn of magic, mortals have sought 
to call upon these ancient forces. From the 
smallest candle nymph to towering mountains 
that walk, elementals have answered the call 
of those bold enough to summon them. Yet 
coaxing an elemental into physical form 
is far easier than commanding its will. The 
Arcane College teaches only the most basic 
conjurations—limited to elementals of 
minimal intellect and manageable strength.
Tenets of Command
•	 Elementals are monsters and act under 
the control of the creature who invokes 
them (you).
•	
If your Mind score ever equals or falls 
below that of your elemental, the 
creature immediately breaks free and 
comes under the Quest Master’s control.
•	
You may control only one elemental at 
a time.
•	
While actively controlling an elemental, 
you may not cast spells of the same 
element. Doing so disrupts your 
concentration and causes the summon 
spell to end immediately.
Laws of the Bound
•	 Elementals take no actions on the turn 
they manifest; their bodies require time 
to assemble fully. During this time, they 
may defend themselves. They become 
active at the start of your next turn.
•	
Once active, elementals may move, 
attack, and defend, and perform simple 
narrative interactions.
•	
Elementals cannot cast, search, equip, 
or use items (including potions or other 
consumables).
•	
Elementals 
cannot 
be 
healed 
or 
resurrected. When their Body score 
reaches zero, the summon is broken and 
they are immediately discarded.
•	
Elementals are immune to Mind damage 
and automatically pass any Mind Check.
•	
Elementals are immune to Body damage 
from sources of their own element.
•	
Elementals always deal magical damage 
matching their elemental type (Air, Fire, 
Earth, or Water).

Magical Walls
The Nature of Conjured Barriers
Walls 
created 
through 
magic—whether 
of stone, ice, flame, or other conjured 
substance—are among the most versatile 
defenses in a mage’s arsenal. Though they 
often appear solid and physical, these barriers 
are sustained entirely by the caster’s will and 
by the elemental essence that shapes them. 
As such, they obey rules distinct from their 
mundane counterparts.
General Guidelines
The following rules apply to all magically 
conjured walls unless the spell itself states 
otherwise:
•	
Line of Sight: Magical walls block line 
of sight for both heroes and monsters. 
Spells and ranged attacks cannot pass 
through them.
•	
Stopping Effects: Any spell, ability, 
or game effect that “stops when it hits a 
wall” also stops when striking a magical 
wall.
•	
Placement: When a wall spell creates 
multiple tiles, squares, or segments, they may 
be placed onto any empty squares within line 
of sight. They do not need to be adjacent; a 
skilled mage can scatter their barriers across 
the battlefield as tactical cover or traps.
Tier I Wall Spells (Mage-Level)
Mage-level walls share a single pool of Body 
Points, regardless of how many sections are 
created. The wall breaks once this shared 
pool is depleted—whether the damage was 
dealt to one block or distributed across many.
Tier II Wall Spells (Wizard-Level)
Wizard-level walls are more stable, each 
section sustaining its own separate pool of 
Body Points. The break threshold applies 
individually, allowing stronger mages to 
construct more enduring fortifications.
Environmental Interaction
Game 
Masters 
may 
choose 
to 
allow 
environmental reactions based on the wall’s 
elemental type.
•	
Fire Walls may ignite nearby flammable scenery.
•	
Ice Walls may melt into water if exposed to heat or 
flame.
•	
Stone Walls may collapse into rubble when 
destroyed, potentially blocking movement.
•	
These effects are optional but add a satisfying layer 
of tactical and narrative flavor.
Appendix
Arcane Surge
Whenever you cast a spell above your current 
Tier, you suffer an Arcane Surge.
1.	 Roll a D6. You take mind damage equal 
to the result.
2.	 If you roll a 6, you also suffer D6 body 
damage.
3.	 If you are unlucky enough to roll 
another 6, all other creatures in the same 
room suffer 1 body and 1 mind damage.
Books of Magic
Though the exact nature of magic varies 
from realm to realm, it is generally accepted 
that it requires the use of energy. The most 
popular form of energy throughout the

fantasy genre is elemental energy. This is the 
primary means by which we organize spells 
in Advanced Armory. Books of Magic are 
collections of individual spells with the same 
primary elemental energy type.
•	
Books of Magic are spell groups organized by 
elemental energy type.
•	
Any mage class may train (unlock) new books 
of magic during advancement.
•	
Once a book is unlocked, you may learn/
purchase spells from known books.
Schools of Magic
Specialized mage classes have their own 
unique abilities that have been tailored to 
suit their specific needs. These classes have 
developed special schools that teach their 
specific skills and spells. Though this can 
limit a new mage’s versatility, specialist 
abilities are often more powerful.
When a specialist advances, they may learn 
new spells from their own school or unlock 
the traditional books of magic.
•	
Non-specialist mage classes cannot train in 
specialist schools of magic.
•	
Spells from specialist schools can only be 
learned by their matching specialist classes.
•	
Specialist mages may learn any book of magic 
during advancement.
Mind Checks
Basic Mind Check: Roll 1D6 for each of 
your Mind points (up to your max total). If 
at least one die rolled is a 6 or better, then the 
check is passed.
Opposed Mind Check: Two or more 
participants roll 2D6, then add their total 
Mind score.
Total Mind Score: This is your naked Mind 
score on your hero profile, plus advancement 
upgrades, plus equipment, spell, and ability 
bonuses.
Subgroups Within the Party
As your adventures continue, your hero 
may gain allies beyond the main party 
— companions, mercenaries, familiars, 
summoned creatures, and other followers. 
When this happens, you effectively control a 
small “sub-party” of creatures.
To maintain clarity during combat and 
exploration:
•	
You may not mix or interleave the actions of 
multiple creatures you control (including 
your Hero).
•	
Each creature you control must complete its 
full turn — movement, actions, and effects 
— before another of your creatures may begin 
its turn.
•	
Once all of your creatures have acted, your 
entire turn ends and play proceeds to the next 
player as normal.
Quest Masters may allow a player’s creatures 
to act in any order they choose, as long as 
each takes a full turn before another begins.
