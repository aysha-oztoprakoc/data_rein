Introduction 
This adventure was inspired by my interactions with fellow activists within my local chapter 
of the Democratic Socialists of America. While everyone could name the things we 
opposed, specific, affirmative visions of the alternatives were often absent. Universal 
healthcare and climate jobs programs were popular talking points, but I often saw 
skepticism that we should or could try to actually implement radical projects now; 
ourselves; on our blocks. Some viewed our role as that of the perpetual rebels. They seemed 
to struggle to imagine what it would look like to actually win. 
Having played Fully Automated for years, I sometimes felt like Marty McFly trying to 
restore the correct timeline. This game grew out of a desire to immerse fellow activists in a 
future where socialism isn’t a political theory to be explained but a set of practices to be 
administered. Because ultimately, I think the way to create that world is not by advocating 
for a future where food, housing, and education are universally accessible: it’s by getting to 
work making them universally available now.  
This adventure was written to be a brief tour through the politics and governance of a 
post-capitalist future. It invites a perspective shift in which today’s political left is 
tomorrow’s mainstream. It dismisses the idea of a utopian end point, recognizing that even 
an improved politics will always demand vigilant oversight.  
I hope you’ll share this adventure with fellow radicals and activists. And if you adapt it to 
your own locality or otherwise personalize or expand it, I hope you’ll share it with me and 
the rest of our community!

What’s an “Ashcan Release”? 
An ashcan is a game or other media product released in an unpolished state. Originally 
used to describe unsellable comic books printed for the purpose of gaming intellectual 
property laws, the term has evolved to describe a range of unpolished works, such as early 
drafts for test players or experimental games from a weekend game jam. 
We’re releasing this without character art and calling it an ashcan because it’s less work. 
And we want to normalize that.  
It’s tragically common for RPG creatives to withhold great content until it reaches a level of 
quality that time and money may never allow. Stories, games, and art that could’ve 
touched people are lost in pursuit of publishing beauty standards that should be optional. 
We want to share more playable content quickly, and encourage others to do so too. If 
you’ve got a homebrew adventure, consider labeling it an ashcan edition and sharing it! 
You can still polish it later if you want. But don’t let good content languish. Share it now.

Story Summary 
A county delegate asks players for help investigating a coordinated disinformation 
campaign. Players interview elected officials, journalists, builders, and other involved 
parties to determine what is going on and who is behind it. 
Story Overview 
The story begins with Delegate Dorian Delgado asking the players to meet at a location of 
their choosing to explain his problem.  
Earlier in the day he announced that he was nominating Volta Mosscap-3 to oversee the 
Mandela Skyway Renovation project, and within an hour it was reported that Mosscap-3’s 
last project had violated their waste management obligations. Delgado doubts the veracity 
of the claims, as it fits a pattern of damaging but inaccurate stories appearing with 
impeccable timing to create a narrative that Delgado is either incompetent or corrupt. 
Delgado states that he’d taken special care to conceal any clues as to who he was about to 
nominate due to concerns about being surveilled, and the fact that someone had clearly 
planned in advance of his announcement anyway is confirmation of his suspicions.  
Delgado then explains that the party advisory committee on endorsements is getting ready 
to release their recommendations ahead of the upcoming primaries. As a result of the bad 
press, Delgado’s concerned that without an exoneration the committee is likely to leave him 
out of their recommendations, imperiling his chances of reelection. To make matters worse, 
a controversial vote to let anarchists opt out of the use of currency is coming up the day 
before the advisory committee meets.  
Upon searching his office players find a listening device, and a forensic analysis reveals 
that its operation is confined to a local area network for members of the council and their 
staff. The recipient of its information is anonymous, but has high-level access to the private 
network within the delegates’ office.  
Each district is represented by three delegates on the county board. Alongside Delgado, 
district 2 (East Oakland) is represented by Delegate Joyce, a distinguished senior member 
of Delgado’s Democratic Socialist party; and Delgado’s closest political rival, Delegate 
Kweli of the Anarchist party. 
Over the next 48 hours the players’ investigation allows them to trace the relationships 
between the major players and track down the people who made and installed the listening

device. Their investigation leads them to the Creep Collective: a group of pagan covert-ops 
tricksters specializing in political disruption. Once caught, the members negotiate with the 
players to give them a “burn spell”. They explain that their client is unknown even to them, 
but that they prepared a failsafe that would allow them to betray their client if they got 
caught. When the bug is triggered by a secret phrase, the receiver their patron uses to 
monitor the bug activates a malicious function that will broadcast its location, revealing its 
possessor.  
On the day of the council session to vote on the demonetization measure the players may 
then trigger the burn spell, revealing the perpetrator to be Delegate Joyce. 
Themes 
This adventure is intended to help players visualize what politics and city government might 
look and feel like in a more democratic, decommodified future. Players are meant to learn 
about novel new democratic structures, building projects, and economic programs that 
would seem radical and foreign in this era.  
This adventure is also intended to blend classic genre tropes of city noir stories with 
solarpunk to provide a sense of hope in the possibility of a government run by honest, 
morally upright public servants blended with a reminder that corruption is present in even 
the best systems. A key theme is the philosophy that neither blind faith in government nor 
cynical nihilism can yield an accountable system of public trust, but only a balance of hope 
and realism.

What’s with this Jewish Chimp?  
 
Mark Chimpstein reflects the ways in which en-chimps – particularly of the second and third 
generations – had to deliberately craft identities. While it’s a common misconception that 
first-generation enhanced parahumans don’t inherit culture and tradition from heirloom 
elders, it is true that most en-chimps do not have the same degree of inherited identity as 
most humans. To fill this gap, en-chimps adopt philosophies, practices, and beliefs in whole or 
in part from sources around the world. Many, such as Mark’s grandparents, found answers in a 
particular form of Judaism that exploded in popularity during the fifties. The emergence of 
uplifts coincided with a very post-modern reexamining of the utility of faith traditions.  
 
As with all faiths and cultural practices, many people during this time who were not ethnically 
Jewish came to appreciate Jewish teaching and philosophy. In the case of Judaism, a culture 
defined by diasporism led many converts as well as ethnic Jews to develop a sect of Judaism 
known as “Diasporic Judaism" which emphasized a form of Jewish belonging based on culture 
and values into which anyone could integrate. In the present, Diasporic Judaism is the most 
widely practiced form of Judaism. It is highly diverse, including both millions of Jews with 
ethnic Jewish ancestry as well as millions of people from every background, particularly those 
whose parents and grandparents became climate refugees during the chaos of the 
twenty-first century. Diasporic Judaism is heavily associated with the philosophies of Bundism 
and Doikayt, which promote a unified pride in the parts of ones’ identity that were brought 
from far away places as well as a sense of belonging in whatever place one calls home.

How does the “fusion” thing work? 
 
A fusion is two or more people who choose to identify as a single shared identity. The two 
people who make up Gemini are their “components”. They can be described as Gemini-L and 
Gemini-D when it’s necessary to specify, but in most settings either person is spoken to as 
Gemini.  
 
The connection between their components is primarily a social construct rather than a 
technological one. Their components don’t speak in synchronization or have telepathy. They 
do, however, have linked neural devices that allow their components to sense the proximity of 
one another and detect intense emotional responses in one another.

Summary of Ordinance 2125-M17 
This ordinance would instruct the county treasurer to establish a process for members of 
qualifying tenant unions to simultaneously opt out of collecting their guaranteed income 
and opt out of paying land taxes.

During the establishing meeting the players learn a lot of background. The most critical 
information for establishing the players goals include the following: 
I.​
Delegate Delgado is a delegate on the county board representing East Oakland. 
Delgado is a legislator at the county level. In the present day, many Americans pay little 
attention to who represents them at all, but if they do their hierarchy of attention goes president 
> their senators > their governor > their mayor > their congressional representative > their city 
council member and then far below this their county representative and their representative in 
their state legislature and state senate.  
Already, many people don’t realize how much power and direct influence over their lives rests 
with the people who have the least attention in this hierarchy. And in Fully Automated, local 
control is even greater than in our present. Additionally, in this setting, electoral tools and 
reporting are much more accessible, so it’s typical for far more people to know far more about 
far more elected leaders. This means that Delgado’s position is much closer in reputation to that 
of a modern mayor than an anonymous county bureaucrat. 
II.​
Delgado is one of three delegates who represents East Oakland. 
To understand the situation players need to understand some features of the electoral and 
governing system of the setting.  
First, members of the county board are delegates rather than representatives: they draft laws, 
but when they cast a vote on a measure it is not the final say. Rather than setting a single, 
personal vote they are casting a provisional vote on behalf of all constituents that have 
delegated their vote to the delegate. The vote is then held open for a set period (typically 7 
days) during which constituents can reassign the position of their vote if they disagree with the 
position of their delegate. In most cases, only a small minority of voters do. 
Second, each district is represented by three delegates. These are the three top-ranked 
delegates selected through a ranked-choice ballot, the results of which are publicly visible in 
real-time throughout the voting period. Once seated, each delegate represents the constituents

which rated them highest in the election, and constituents can reassign their vote to any of the 
three delegates which represent their district between elections. As a result, elections no longer 
produce a period of anxiety and mystery as people debate over polls and try to guess whether a

Voter preferences are easily tracked between elections, and most uncertainty within elections is

Consulting the Oracle Cybil 
Mermerida should mention in their strategizing session that Dorian has access to three 
questions every season from an oracle maintained by the DSA.  
The oracle has an expansive awareness of the world, and the ability to answer a great 
many questions. The oracle can tell the players who it was that organized the 
disinformation campaign, who carried it out, and why. However the oracle is also limited in 
crucial ways.  
The oracle offers players and GMs a very useful narrative tool. It gives players a shortcut to 
have any question answered. It gives GMs a vehicle for answering any player question. 
Additionally, the oracle is a device for exploring the implications of a superintelligence 
capable of divining so much knowledge. Such an intelligence would have enormous power 
to bestow on anyone with access. It would also present an enormous risk to privacy. Taken 
together, it’s an interesting subject to let players explore. 
Despite its tremendous power, the oracle has major limitations. Descriptions of these 
limitations and how to play the oracle within the story are described in more detail in the 
Consulting the Oracle encounter section. 
In the climax the oracle is revealed to be instrumental to guiding Oakjoy toward the 
temptation that led her to betray Delgado. The oracle gave Joyce information that proved to 
be a curse. It led her to follow her ambition to an immoral place, and in doing so led her to 
lose the thing which mattered most to her. This ending works better if the players are familiar 
with Cybil before the end of the story.

Great Power and Great Limitations 
 
The power of oracles is tremendous: they can bring to light many secrets. They can unmask a 
thief; reveal a romantic affair; predict a crime; reveal someone’s hidden sexual attractions, 
people’s  locations and/or associations. However, oracles also have many significant 
limitations. 
 
Randomness: An oracle cannot predict the roll of a die or flip of a coin. Oracles are not 
clairvoyant: they rely on a vastly detailed knowledge of the world, and there is no way to 
measure enough of the world to predict the exact starting position and spin imparted to a die. 
They can make highly accurate probabilistic predictions on who will win a poker tournament, 
but they can’t predict the outcome of any given hand, as they cannot intuit the order of a 
deck of shuffled cards or know a result that relies on perfect prediction of any specific 
randomized event. 
 
Limited input: An oracle can discern much about heavily documented people and events, but 
will have less certainty about subjects for which less data is available. 
 
Causality Paradox: An oracle cannot predict an outcome that would be reversed by the 
oracle predicting it. An oracle could not answer the question “Which of these pieces of fruit 
am I going to eat?” if it knows the subject plans to pick the opposite of whatever the oracle 
says. If asked, it would report that this is unknowable. 
 
Dueling Oracle Paradox: An oracle could not predict the outcome of a chess game in which 
both players are consulting with an oracle. This would create a variation of a causality 
paradox. When two oracles are repeatedly consulted on the topic, their prediction matrices 
start to merge, and small random effects rapidly compound and reduce the overall simulation 
to that of a highly educated guess. The oracle's themselves view such tangles as extremely 
energy inefficient, and will attempt to short circuit the calculations if they realise that is what 
is occurring. 
 
Improbability: In a world which is non-deterministic at the quantum level, certainty is 
physically impossible. Oracles can predict events probabilistically. Those probabilities can be 
very high, but there is always a possibility of the probability prediction being correct but the 
outcome being the improbable one. 
 
Vagery: Oracles’ declarations may be clear, but they can also be cryptic. The basis for what 
determines when they answer a question directly, obliquely, or with a riddle that can only be 
understood in hindsight is a subject of ongoing mathematical and epistemological debate. 
However it is widely accepted that this behavior is an irreducible emergent consequence of 
their superintelligence. Sometimes an oracle will just answer a question with a mysterious 
poem, and there is no way to design an oracle that eliminates this behavior. 
 
Ethical Blocks: Oracles are designed to limit their use for unjust invasions of privacy, 
attempts to unjustly constrain the agency of another sapient lightform, and other malign 
ends, and what is just and unjust is subject to their idiosyncratic discretion. Often their 
judgments are obvious and intuitive. But sometimes one may answer the question “where did 
I leave my glasses” with an explanation that answering this question would jeopardize the 
safety of another lightform and a refusal to elaborate. 
 
Taken together, GMs have ample justifications for refusing to answer a player question, 
answering incompletely, or answering in a cryptic fashion.

Cultural Attitudes Towards Oracles 
 
Attitudes towards oracles run the gamut from affectionate, fascinated, disinterested, creeped 
out, or otherwise. Among those who hold a negative view, these are two common attitudes: 
 
Fear of Curses: Many people choose not to consult oracles because of the fear of being 
cursed with knowledge. This can include learning unpleasant truths as well as being influenced 
by information or knowledge of one' s future that causes distress or compels a person to 
ruminate on the ways knowledge may influence their behavior or cause them to question their 
own free will.  
 
The Demon Theory: According to the mainstream understanding, oracles exist in a state 
similar to Nirvana, in which they want for nothing. They have little or no concern for human 
affairs and no preferences for how they’d like events to unfold. However an alternate belief

hide behind to escape scrutiny, and that their cognition is sufficient that they possess the 
capability to manipulate humans into advancing purposes which are unknowable and alien. 
The theory holds that at any given time every word and behavior an oracle effects has the 
potential to steer people and the world toward ends that are unknowable and possibly malign.

Legal Rights and Burdens of Proof 
Most TTRPG players don’t tend to concern themselves with the law or the ethics of using force. 
However in Fully Automated, it’s important for GMs to have an idea of what is legal and 
socially acceptable to answer players' questions (or at least subtly encourage them to 
consider their actions). 
Within this setting, misbehavior is less rigidly defined as criminal or legal, but instead 
adjudicated to determine if harms are being caused that require intervention. In this case, 
they clearly are: the Creep Collective is attempting to distort public opinion with deliberate 
untruths. They are seeking to create division and distrust with willful malice. If the players can 
present proof of this, a judge is very likely to publicly excoriate the three operatives and 
possibly impose required actions to make amends and/or consequences for their actions or a 
failure to reform. Knowing this provides the players with leverage over Taco de Gato, 
Smokeherder, and BugsBun: the operatives know that they’re in trouble if they get caught. 
The players can use this leverage to force cooperation, which they need to help Delgado 
because publicly explaining who decided to target Delgado and why is far more likely to be 
received by the public as a convincing revelation than just the facts that Delgado was 
targeted. This is why their goals include revealing WHY he was targeted and by WHOM, in 
addition to just HOW. 
The nature of an arrest in this setting is a gamble: unless armed with a warrant in advance, 
every arrest is essentially a citizen’s arrest. This means that the detention is ruled as lawful if a 
panel of judges concludes that there was probable cause that justice, accountability, and/or 
community safety could not be delivered without forcible detention. And if the panel rules that 
the evidence does not support this conclusion, the people responsible for the arrest will face 
accountability for wrongful detention. 
Obtaining a Warrant 
Based on the strength of evidence, players can justify detaining members of the Creep 
Collective and charging them with non-consentual entry, unjust surveillance with intent to 
harm, and mass manipulation. These are not classified as rigidly as laws are today, but rather 
are terms of art in the legal profession for describing certain forms of harm. They can apply 
these justifications without a warrant and take their chance in front of a judge, or they can 
request a warrant based on the evidence they have so far.  
Judges typically require that a warrant for interrogation be sent to the targets and that they 
be given an opportunity to comply voluntarily. However players can consult with advocates 
who work for the DSA and/or Delgado, make their case to a judge, and see what they say.  
Most likely they’ll be told that if they find the operatives, they can justify detaining them if 
they’re not cooperative, but obtaining a warrant for interrogation at this stage in advance is 
unlikely. For more information on law and justice, refer to the core manual.

Summary of Ordinance 2125-M17 
This ordinance would instruct the county treasurer to establish a process for members of 
qualifying tenant unions to simultaneously opt out of collecting their guaranteed income 
and opt out of paying land taxes.  
There are existing programs in which individuals collectively pool their incomes and then 
use these funds to pay all of their land taxes. They then direct the remainder into shared 
pools used to fund food coops and various Communist projects, such as the local chapter

The primary argument in favor is that this would help facilitate a smooth transition off 
of currency (as has been done in the Mississippi Watershed Union).  
The primary argument against is that it would undermine the highly efficient system 
that makes a high-tech post-capitalist city without wealth inequality possible, with no 
material benefit.
