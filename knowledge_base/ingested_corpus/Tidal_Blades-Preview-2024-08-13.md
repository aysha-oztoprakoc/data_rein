A SHORT HISTORY
Every Navirian remembers the gigantic cloud, 
black as cuttlebeard ink, as it started forming 
on the horizon. It cast an unusual stain on the 
pristine sky—after all, storm season wasn’t 
expected for another few months. Before long, 
wind unleashed its fury like a battle cry, rain 
hammered the floating docks, and drifters had 
to moor their houses in haste before the first 
waves flooded the bay. In its wake, the storm 
brought an army of sea monsters, the likes of 
which had never been seen before in Naviri. 
It was by all accounts a most fantastic storm, 
one that would be remembered as the Great 
Battle.
As most ran for safety, a few heroes 
remained, standing strong against the 
thundering sky. They were known as the Tidal 
Blades, elite guardians of the Floating Islands. 
Commander Braze, Master Takaro, Masinki 
Bubba—they and so many others were known 
by name and adored. For two hundred years, 
generations of these talented fighters had 
kept Naviri safe and prosperous. Wielding 
the most sophisticated weapons designed 
by the Engineers of the Citadel of Time, they 
patrolled the reef barrier day and night, 
keeping all horrors from the ocean at bay.
But this storm was like nothing the city had 
ever encountered. Despite their incredible 
skills, the Tidal Blades were no match for the 
endless hordes of sea monsters invading 
the reef on that day. The city’s leaders were 
summoned. There was one desperate measure 
that could save the city, but it came at a 
terrible price. Facing the imminent destruction 
of Naviri, the leaders tasked the Tidal Blades 
T
idal Blades, and its setting of Naviri, has 
a unique feel and sensibility. It’s a game 
and setting that shines bright with 
community, helpfulness, joy, delight, science, 
and celebration. In this fantasy water world, 
wonder blends with advanced time-altering 
technology. Crocs, turtles, and fish-folk cohabit 
peacefully with humans in a vast floating city. 
People trade with fruit and travel between the 
various docks on watercrafts, powered by small 
energy devices known as shells.
Despite the paradise-like setting, Naviri 
is in dire need of heroes. Powerful sea 
monsters have started breaking out of the 
hyperdimensional barrier that protects the 
area. And of course, just like any city, Naviri 
has its share of smaller dangers—dark corners, 
dark dealings, and dark characters—that all 
need to be dealt with.
While there are definitely obstacles 
to overcome and challenges to face, the 
focus of the game is on the ways in which 
characters and the community can work 
together to keep their beloved home safe 
and thriving. Characters may find themselves 
gathering hard-to-find ingredients for a 
much-beloved local chef, tracking down a 
group of thieves who’ve stolen an unstable 
piece of technology, or battling a giant sea 
monster with a volcano on its back—all while 
racing watercrafts across the sea, attempting 
to befriend local wildlife, or competing in 
contests of skill and strength.

CAN I PLAY A TIDAL BLADE?
In the world of Naviri, Tidal Blades are the elite 
guardians of the Floating Islands. Can you play 
one of these powerful heroes?
The short answer is yes, you can—but 
probably not right out of the gate (although 
that is a rare possibility, as you’ll see below). 
That’s because Tidal Blades require a lot of 
work, training, experience, and dedication, 
all of which typically take time. In game 
terms, Tidal Blades are considered high-tier 
characters, and most games start with tier 1 
characters.
In most cases, the clearest route to 
becoming a Tidal Blade is to complete one or 
more character arcs that will lead you to that 
level of fame and glory. Establishment, Learn, 
Master a Skill, Transformation, and even Fall 
From Grace could all be character arcs that 
lead to becoming a Tidal Blade.
Letting your GM know that you want your 
character to become a Tidal Blade means that 
they can give your PC lots of opportunities 
to interact with those who can help your 
progress. For example, your GM might have 
Elders give your character Tidal Blade–related 
quests, provide more chances to enter 
contests and races, or create adventures 
that invite your character to explore the 
Fold looking for treasures in the form of lost 
weapons, artifacts, and rare shell devices.
STARTING OUT AS A TIDAL BLADE
It is possible that you can work with the GM 
to begin the game as a Tidal Blade, but this 
would be a rare case, and would likely occur 
only in one of the following backgrounds. In 
all of these instances, you probably would still 
need to work your way up through the tiers 
to reach full “Tidal Blade” status. GMs might 
consider some of the various optional rules, 
such as Getting an XP Advance and Skills From 
Backgrounds, as ways to make this work.
Angler’s Blade: Angler is forming his own 
secret, elite team of Tidal Blades outside the 
influence of the Citadel, and you’re one of 
them. Angler’s goal is to push the limits of 
technology and make super fighters. You’ve 
been sent on a mission to study the Fold and 
capture new monsters, so Angler can study 
their abilities and create even more powerful 
fighting gear. However, you’ve been keeping 
your role hidden, as the Citadel doesn’t 
look kindly upon rogue Tidal Blades.
Shameful Past: You were a Tidal Blade 
long ago but fled that life for personal 
reasons. In these troubled times, you have 
come back to Naviri to redeem yourself. You 
will have to work hard to regain everyone’s 
trust, but your goals are noble and you want 
to put your past behind you.
Out of the Fold: You were one of the lucky 
few Tidal Blades close to the border when the 
Fold was deployed, and over the years you 
managed to break free. The hyperdimensional 
barrier has left an enduring strain on your 
physical and mental abilities. In fact, you have 
forgotten much about your past and will need 
to work hard to regain the full scope of your 
previous skills.
In most cases, a Tidal 
Blade character is likely 
to be a Fighter type, but 
any type could take the 
title, provided the GM 
and player work together 
to create a character 
that fits the setting.

Amare’s character carries a small knife 
hidden in his bag in case of trouble. As a light 
weapon, it inflicts 2 points of damage.
For his species, Amare chooses Salamander, 
because of their quick-thinking skills and 
their crafting abilities. He adds 4 to his 
Intellect Pool (raising it to 18) and is trained 
in crafting, but is hindered in Might defense 
skills. For his abilities, he chooses Michronic 
Efficiency, which helps him get more out of 
the michronic devices he uses.
Amare chooses Driven for his descriptor, 
because he wants a character who is highly 
motivated. Driven increases his Might Pool to 
10 and gives him training in Intellect defense 
tasks, so he can protect his all-important mind. 
Each day, he can also choose a new skill to be 
trained in that will help him reach his goal. At 
first, Amare is sad that his descriptor gives him 
an inability in perception tasks, but he realizes 
that the flaw fits his character well—a character 
so driven and in their own head would likely 
miss some things going on around him.
For his focus, Amare chooses Studies 
Anomalies, which gives him training in 
monster lore of all kinds. 
For his character arc, Amare chooses Join an 
Organization. He wants to become a member 
of the Engineers, hopefully his first step on the 
long journey to becoming an Arcanist.

CHARACTER FOCUS
F
ocus is what makes a character unique. 
Ideally, no two PCs in a group should have 
the same focus. A focus gives a character 
benefits when they create their character 
and each time they ascend to the next tier. 
It’s the verb of the sentence “I am an adjective 
[species] noun who verbs.”
This chapter contains more than two dozen 
foci to choose from, such as Brandishes a Shell 
Shield, Delves the Fourth Dimension, and Sails 
the Howling Sea.
When you choose a character focus, you 
get a special connection to one or more of 
your fellow PCs, a first-tier ability, and perhaps 
additional starting equipment. Each focus also 
offers suggestions to the GM and the player 
for possible effects or consequences of really 
good or really bad die rolls.
As you progress to a new tier, your focus 
grants you more abilities.
Each tier’s benefits are independent of and 
cumulative with benefits from other tiers 
(unless indicated otherwise). So if a first-tier 
ability grants +1 to Armor and a fourth-tier 
ability also grants +1 to Armor, when the 
character reaches fourth tier, a total of +2 to 
Armor is granted.
At tier 3 and tier 6, you are asked to choose 
one ability from the two options provided.

THE CITY AND WORLD
Naviri is a word typically used to mean both 
the city itself and the surrounding areas. 
To distinguish the urban center from the 
surrounding regions, it is sometimes called 
“the city of Naviri,” “the Floating Islands of 
Naviri,” or “the Blue City” (usually by outsiders).
The city consists mainly of a handful of 
permanent large docks, including New Mingu, 
Radiant Bay, Vines, Trawl, and Remo’s Dock, 
each with its own unique customs and mores. 
For most Navirians, the dock they were raised 
in is an important part of their culture, belief 
system, and so on. In addition to the urban 
center, a large number of floating docks also 
drift in and out of the city throughout the 
year, providing wares, tradespeople, crafters, 
and mercenaries for hire.
Most stationary docks are somewhere 
between 2 to 3 miles (3 to 5 km) in diameter, 
with a few floating houses along the 
periphery. Drifting docks, such as Remo’s 
Dock, tend to be smaller, around 1,000 feet 
(300 m) long. Main docks can have as many as 
3,000 people, while smaller ones host around 
100 people or less.
Beyond Naviri, one finds more dangerous, 
less-welcoming places like the Fury Isles, the 
Scattered Reefs, and the Droskani Desert.
T
he city of Naviri and its surrounding area 
is a place with a long and complicated 
history that has grown into a complex 
and vibrant community, thanks to the hard 
work of its inhabitants. This gazetteer chapter 
is designed to provide a wide variety of 
information at a glance about everything 
from Naviri’s history and possible future to its 
inhabitants, important festivals, seasons, and 
languages. It’s a good resource for both the 
GM and the players, as you begin to explore 
Naviri and discover more about its unique 
inhabitants and their way of life.
In general, Naviri is a tropical world filled 
with islands, warm sunshine and warm 
waters, and strong communities. Most of the 
inhabitants are nonhuman, aquatic-based 
species like frogs, salamanders, turtles, and 
chameleons. The few humans in Naviri have 
mostly settled here from a faraway city 
called Stoen.
In many ways, Naviri is a bit of a paradise—
people take care of each other, there are few 
real dangers, and everything is made just a 
little easier by the community’s embrace of 
time-based technologies. It’s a life filled with 
festivities and celebrations, contests that 
challenge even the toughest participants, 
and leadership that works hard to care for its 
community.
Beyond the paradise-like community of
