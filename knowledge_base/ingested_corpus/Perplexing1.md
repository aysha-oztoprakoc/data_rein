Kill! Loot! LEVEL!
Repeat as necessary.
It is a fact that we as gamers must face; a
gaming campaign, at its heart, is an incredibly
long corridor with monsters and shinies
strewn throughout at regular intervals. GMs,
DMs, Storytellers, and their ilk exist to mix
this basic premise with interesting plothooks,
puzzles, and homebrew mechanics. The end
result, if done well, is a memorable bit of
gaming whose highlights are talked about for
years to come.
The primary issue with producing this
memorable content is that pushing the
concept of, "Kill! Loot! Level!" into the
background is a ridiculously difficult feat to
pull. Players are hardwired for it, some more
than others. This means that anything that is
meant to pull the player away from this
concept must be designed to engage the player
in an intriguing fashion and fire upon a part of
the mind that is not satisfied by, "Kill! Loot!
Level!" In other words, one must design in
such a way that the player pushes, "Kill! Loot!
Level!" away on his own because this new idea
is THAT cool.
It is the author's opinion that puzzles are a
fantastic method to accomplish this.
Perplexing Puzzles is a series of playtested,
highly modular puzzles laid out in a logical
fashion so that they may be read quickly and
implanted into almost any campaign. Care has
been taken to make these puzzles as system
neutral as possible, but certain references to
rules interactions in Dungeons and Dragons is
unavoidable at times.

Chromatic Crystals
What is it?
Chromatic Crystals is an extrapolation of the
classic "Lights Out" puzzle. Given a target
combination, the party utilizes a set of
colored rods that cause crystals in a grid to
change color to match the target
combination.
Entering the Puzzle
Chromatic Crystals is best used as a puzzle
that unlocks a door when solved. As such, the
best way to enter the puzzle is to simply have
the party walk into a room dominated by it.
Interpreting the Puzzle
There are four colors in Chromatic Crystals:
red, blue, green, and clear. In addition, there
are three rods: red, blue, and green. The
properties of the rods are as follows.
Red:
Turns clear crystal to red.
Turns green crystal to clear.
Blue:
Turns red or green crystal to blue.
Turns blue crystal to clear.
Affects crystals adjacent to crystal touched.
Green:
Turns blue crystal to green.
Turns red crystal to clear.
Affects crystals adjacent to crystal touched.
Note that rods do not function on adjacent
crystals if its primary target won't change.
Example of Rod Functionality

shown in the above configuration, it affects
not only the crystal touched, but also the
crystals to the left of, to the right of, and
above. Since the blue rod turns red or green
to blue and turns blue clear, we get the
following configuration.
Variants of this Puzzle
A particularly amusing way to work this
puzzle into a campaign is to have the three
rods strewn throughout a dungeon. This
allows the party to get used to the rules of
each rod in turn and makes the prospect of
complicated three­rod puzzles at the end of
the dungeon a little less daunting due to
puzzle experience gained leading up to it.
Advanced variants are presented on page 8.

Advanced Variants
Crystalline Chaos
A rather annoying variant to unleash upon a
hapless party is the inclusion of a hovering
crystal in the center of the room.
Every five moves the party makes, roll a d4
for a 3­crystal puzzle, a d8 for a 6­crystal
puzzle, or a d10 for a 9­crystal puzzle. If a
number greater than the number of crystals
in the puzzle is rolled, then nothing happens.
Otherwise, the crystal corresponding to the
number rolled randomly changes color based
on the table below.
Crystalline Chaos Color Change
d6
1: Clear
2­3: Red
4­5: Blue
6: Green (Clear if a two color puzzle)
The Golem
Perhaps the party needs a "final exam" that
includes a boss fight! Simply take the
statblock for a golem in the appropriate CR
range and add the following abilities to it.
Red Crystal Overload (Su):
As a full­round action, this creature can
unleash a wave of destructive energy, dealing
1/2 CR d6 points of force damage to all
enemies within 30 ft with a DC 10 + 1/2 HD
Reflex save for half.
While charging this ability, this creature
glows red and can have its color changed by
crystalline rods. Should its color be blue or
green when this ability resolves, it executes
Blue Crystal Overload or Green Crystal
Overload instead. Should its color be clear,
this ability fizzles instead.
Blue Crystal Overload (Su):
As a full­round action, this creature can fire
a ray of energy. Treat this as a ranged touch
attack that deals CR d6 points of force
damage to a single target.
While charging this ability, this creature
glows blue and can have its color changed by
crystalline rods. Should its color be red or
green when this ability resolves, it executes
Red Crystal Overload or Green Crystal
Overload instead. Should its color be clear,
this ability fizzles instead.
Green Crystal Overload (Su):
As a full­round action, this creature can
flood itself with intense energy, granting it
fast healing 3*CR for 3 rounds.
While charging this ability, this creature
glows green and can have its color changed
by crystalline rods. Should its color be red or
blue when this ability resolves, it executes
Red Crystal Overload or Blue Crystal
Overload instead. Should its color be clear,
this ability fizzles instead.
Wild Card
This variant facilitates puzzle completion by
granting the party a single multicolored rod.
When touching a crystal with this rod, the
player simply states the color it shall become
and it is so. Upon use, this rod crumbles.
