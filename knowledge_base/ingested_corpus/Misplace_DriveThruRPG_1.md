ntroduction
I
The Misplace is a supplementary plane of existence, built to 
be slotted into any campaign where your players want (or 
deserve) to have their world turned on its head, either as a short 
expedition to retrieve a valuable item, lost character(s), or pertinent 
piece of information, and/or as a larger, pervasive element of your 
world. As a broader worldbuilding element, The Misplace aims to 
add an existential danger to teleportation and recorded history 
within your world, acting as an interplanar void where all things 
forgotten, misplaced, discarded, or neglected can end up. Although 
the Misplace is home to plenty of entities that pose visceral physical 
threats, the true horror of the plane aims at unravelling your player 
character’s identities, making expeditions into the plane’s depths a 
great opportunity for roleplay and character (un)building.

The plane is pre-built with its own pre-existing, discarded history 
and current, fleeting ecosystem, but feel free to discard any elements 
that do not fit within your worldbuilding, or indeed add elements of 
your own world that may have wound up here.

hat is the misplace
W
The Misplace is both a crossroads and the offroad, the path 
beyond the torchlight, the place beyond the map, beyond 
The Misplace exists below, and feeds from, the Prime Material plane 
above it, a sort of extraplanar basement where things that are neglected 
or misplaced fall to gather dust.  In this way, The Misplace has no solid 
reality of its own, the space a barren landscape of fractured places, 
histories, and peoples, made up loosely of the dregs of the reality 
overhead. Unlike most planes, The Misplace is not self-contained, but 
like a shattered globe is cut open at one end, the top containing a 
thin atmosphere and the bottom of the plane giving way to the Void 
beyond all planes, where things go when they exist nowhere, in the 
minds of no one. 
	
With the Limbostratus above – a great roiling storm that 
appears from below as if the surface of choppy water – the spires of 
rock across the landscape and the inky depth below, the plane appears 
to be the bottom of an ocean bereft of its water, left only with an 
empty darkness. 
	
Given its unstable nature, The Misplace is in a constant 
state of collapse, slowly bleeding off into the Void below it, kept fed 
only by the flotsam and jetsam of the Prime Material plane above. 
Appearance-wise, the plane is made up of things from broken-
up streets, to shattered buildings, to long-lost glades that float like 
fragments above the Void.  These all, in turn, will crumble, replaced 
by the next people or place to fall on the great wheel of time into the 
Void below. In effect, The Misplace is a great and horrid alembic of 
meaning, containing in its depths the fragments of time, place and 
thought it is currently digesting, mulching, to oblivion.

reparing for travel
P
The true danger of The Misplace lies in its ability to strip 
a character’s personality and individuality. Mechanically, 
this requires some dissection of characters in advance, in the form 
of Core Memories.
core memories
•	
Each player drafts a list of up to 5 Core Memories 
where possible, each a specific and significant moment 
in their character’s past that has contributed to who 
they are. To each Core Memory, have each player 
assign a particular trait or personality quirk their 
character possesses related to it. The connection need 
not be one-to-one and can instead be representative.
•	
Core Memories function as a mechanical way to allow 
personalities to break down, but also form an incredibly 
valuable commodity in the meaning-starved realm of 
The Misplace for a desperate adventurer.
mementos
•	
Each player selects one of their Core Memories (this 
could be any, but ideally the most foundational in 
both background and its associated trait) and assigns 
a Memento to it, a trinket or small object that stands 
in as representative of that memory in some way.
•	
A Memento acts as a guiding rod within The Misplace, 
ensuring that even when all is lost, you still possess some 
shred of yourself. Protecting your Memento ensures its 
associated Core Memory remains, but also provides 
protection from Unwinding and similar effects.

G
Used as a more pervasive element of worldbuilding, the following 
can act as ways in which an unfortunate creature finds their way 
into The Misplace:
As a place one can end up when forgotten or lost, it is 
exceedingly difficult to reach The Misplace on purpose. 
losing your way
•	
When a creature attempts intraplanar travel (and/or of 
an object) via the Teleport spell and rolls a Mishap three 
times in a row, all targets of the spell are instead sent 
to The Misplace, arriving in an approximate area as if 
they had rolled for ‘Similar Area’ as part of the spell.
•	
When a creature attempts interplanar travel (and/or of 
an object) via the Plane Shift spell without the use of a 
teleportation circle or to a plane they have not visited 
previously, roll a d100. On a 1, all targets of the spell are 
sent to The Misplace.
•	
When a creature attempts to cast either of the above 
spells and is successfully counterspelled, roll a d100 
and subtract the difference between the successful roll 
and the DC of the Counterspell. On a 1 or lower, the 
countered spell refracts and shatters reality, sending all 
creatures and objects within 10ft to the Misplace.
•	
When any number of creatures attempt to teleport via 
any means to the same space at the same time, roll a d100 
and subtract the total number of creatures attempting to 
do so. On a 1, all teleporting creatures are sent to The 
Misplace.

from the reality they rely on, finding no destination, no surety, 
no solace. Spells such as Plane Shift cannot target The Misplace, 
the plane beyond any definition of a ‘destination’ one could set 
a course to. Mechanically, one may lose their way in many ways,

losing your mind
The Misplace lies outside of sanity, beyond comprehension. From 
a drunkard stumbling down an alley in his stupor to an archmage 
delving into secrets beyond his imagining, all who lose their 
minds may stumble into The Misplace, as concepts crumble to 
abstraction. Mechanically, one may lose their mind in many ways:
•	
If a creature becomes unconscious or dies while its mind 
is cast elsewhere, such as during a Scrying or Dream spell, 
or whilst seeing through another creature’s eyes such as via 
the Find Familiar spell, their mind may become severed 
from their body and sent to The Misplace. Likewise, 
having your tether cut during the Astral Projection 
spell may cause the same effect. Your body remains in a 
catatonic state during your detainment in The Misplace.
•	
When a creature enters the dreams of another, such as by 
the Dream spell, and the host dreamer dies, each other 
creature within its dreams must make a Charisma saving 
throw against the initial spell or effect’s DC, or have 
their mind lost to The Misplace. Your body remains in a 
catatonic state during your detainment in The Misplace.
•	
If using alternative rules for reviving creatures that allow 
for failure  and the ritual, effect or spell fails, the creature’s 
soul may be cast adrift and sent to The Misplace. Your 
body, if still present, remains in a catatonic state during 
your detainment in The Misplace.

losing your legacy
If no written, oral or otherwise-recorded history exists of a creature 
or location, it may turn up in The Misplace, in actuality or as a 
manifestation of the original. 
	
Such manifestations are incomplete - A once famous 
champion may manifest as a faceless caricature, a house may 
manifest entirely empty, bar the room its denizens once gathered 
within to tell stories together. Likewise, if the last few memories 
of the creature or location are misinformed, biased or otherwise 
inaccurate, the manifestation may reflect the same discrepancies, 
rather than the entity’s true nature. This avenue of getting to The

enter the plane:
•	 Obscuranaurum: An unknowable time ago, a nameless 
archmage whose horrific practices alerted the local law 
and, soon, had the world against him, attempted to design 
an artifact to evade justice. In a way, it worked. Now a 
cursed artifact found only deep within the recesses of 
a dungeon, The Obscuranaurum is a sphere of ornate 
arcane design, a handprint delicately impressed on its 
surface. Any creature that places their palm against the 
sphere is removed from historical record, the artifact 
inadvertently functioning as a key to The Misplace.
•	 The Circle of All-Severing: A dark and dreadful ritual 
developed over countless lifetimes by an organisation 
struck from reality. This is the surest and ‘safest’ way to 
enter The Misplace.

then days, perhaps months or years, concentrated within a single blip 
of existence. And then, with a sudden and overwhelming feeling of 
falling, as one feels before sleep, the ritual’s participants end up in 
The Misplace.

Despite its apparent hostility, The Misplace is still home 
to a number peoples and cultures. Many of these peoples 
split off and turn to isolation like Maereids, while others like the 
Remembrancers band together and value community above all 
else, both looking to sustain themselves for as long as possible 
within the fleeting plane.
the remembrancers
denizens
The Remembrances is a term used for the various nomadic groups 
that roam The Misplace, traveling through the Shallows and the 
Depths alike. There are roughly a dozen groups, each with their 
own traditions and history stretching back many years.
	
The Remembrancers see themselves as custodians of The 
Misplace, trying to maintain a sense of memory about the plane 
and recording not only their own history, but also that of the plane 
itself. They do this through the Weaves, tapestries their Weavers 
create that tell their people’s story. 
	
According to the Weaves, the Remembrancers were, long 
ago, a single group of people. They joined together through various 
means, but above all for the sake a community, a place where people 
would care for each other, in so hostile a place. Even as people 
forget who they are and what they stand for, the Remembrancers 
are able to hold on to the sense of community, helping each other 
in all things.
	
As the Remembrancers grew, they were forced to split into 
groups due to the absence of food. Having hundreds upon hundreds 
of people traveling through The Misplace together made it hard

As they years have passed, more and more Remembrancer groups 
have been formed. 
	
Each group is led by the Master Weaver, the person

Each Weaver has at least a dozen apprentices that create minor 
Weaves of their own – small tapestries detailing family histories, 
embroideries, and so on. On top of this, the Weaver and their 
apprentices also transmit their group’s history orally in great

the maereid
While the Remembrancers band together to find meaning, the 
Maereid are more solitary people, who nonetheless share the same 
sentiment among them: That their time here is finite, and thus they 
should revel in all that they can find. Clinging on to no assurance, 
the Maereid are those who travel willingly away from the Shallows 
into the Depths, hoping to find meaning at the very end of things.
	
Collectors of discarded items, memories and ideas, the 
Maeried are a patchworked people. Found with mismatched 
clothing, half-complete poems, found objects, the Maereid value 
futility, taking the various discarded parts of The Misplace into 
themselves in the hope that through them, they find new meaning. 
Macabre to many, they also seem to view beauty in the plane’s 
collapse, mercurially dancing between traps and gaps into the 
Void.

the researchers of hollow-tower
A crooked tower floats in the spaces between land, drifting and 
lilting calmly across the inky skyscape. The scholars who reside 
within are curious, but rarely seen; only venturing out when they 
require more understanding. Hollow-Tower is home to any who 
offer insight, and a prison for the half-witted. The denizens are the 
descendants of a group of foolhardy wizards, who ventured into 
The Misplace for understanding, and instead found only a cage. 
Now the descendants continue their work in solemn desperation, 
having never known anything else. Those who know their ancestors’ 
secret either work themselves to the bone to find an escape, or go 
mad. Either way, they relish one thing: knowledge. 
	
The Researchers of Hollow-Tower are not hostile, nor
