ome years ago, I was thrilled to be selected 
as part of the 5E design team. Back then, 
years before publication, we called it “D&D 
Next.” During one incarnation, the primary design 
team was headed up by Monte Cook. Perhaps 
you’ve heard of him? The team also included 
Robert Schwalb. The three of us really gelled. 
It was a heady time. Also, sometimes a bit silly. 
During one afternoon pitch session, we laughed 
about including a new character class whose 
distinguishing class feature was gaining a pig 
companion at level 7. You probably had to be there. 
But I later ordered us all t-shirts printed simply 
with the words “Pig 7.” (Sadly, I don’t think I ever 
distributed those shirts. Sorry, guys!)
An always-evolving design perspective required 
an equally robust response in actual words on 
paper. In subsequent phases of design, I relished 
creating new drafts of the classes, species, spells, 
items, and more. I lost count of the number of 
different versions of wizard, warlock, druid, and 
other classes I created. I corralled my monthly 
gaming group into becoming an outside playtest 
group. That was sort of mean in retrospect; having 
to incorporate new rules for their characters every 
time we started a new session was definitely a big 
ask on my part.
All of which is to say, it was a nostalgic delight 
to return to 5E player character (PC) mechanical 
design when we launched Path of the Planebreaker. 
(And then a few weeks later for this book, to look 
at everything I’d just done all over again, but from 
a Cypher System Rules perspective.) It was all the 
more fantastic because I did so under the auspices 
of creating PC options for exploring the freaking 
multiverse! Talk about the perfect design prompt. 
Interested in starting a new character who’s got a 
mysterious cosmic map imprinted on their skin? 
Look no further than the traveler, a new PC species. 
Curious about a druid who draws their power from 
the unnatural flora and fauna of the Abyss? Check 
out the Shepherds Demons focus. Or maybe a 
fighter who once fought in a secret war at the edge 
of the universe is more your style; if so, try the Was 
Scarred by the Psychic War focus. Or maybe…
I could go on. But I won’t. Just open the book 
and dive in. A multiverse of character options 
awaits you.

CHIMERAN
A mélange of possibility, chimerans are as 
changeable as the day. They require only a night’s 
rest to gain the attributes of some other species, 
if not in outlook, then at least physically, as long 
as they have a bit of hair or blood to work with. 
Rumored to be the mortal spawn of a demon lord 
or a variety of doppelganger, chimerans are often 
feared by people who realize what they can do. 
However, given the chimerans’ ability to radically 
alter themselves, most of the time they can pass 
among others without arousing any suspicion 
that they’re anything other than what they 
appear as that day. On the other hand, chimerans 
embodying a particular species don’t gain any 
cultural knowledge, including the language. Other 
individuals of the same species a chimeran is posing 
as may eventually suspect that something is off 
about the stranger among them.
A
cross the many planes, a multitude of species 
exist. Players making new characters have 
plenty of interesting possibilities to choose 
from, including travelers, chimerans, inkarnates, lava 
elves (also called Laghristi), surk, and vlatons.
Because these species were introduced in Path 
of the Planebreaker, these writeups include more 
depth and story than are typically included in a 
species descriptor for the Cypher System.

SECRET OF EMBODIMENT
The secret of embodiment that makes a chimeran what they are is truly a deep and mysterious one, 
possibly something that even a chimeran doesn’t fully understand. In addition to having a sample 
of another creature’s body, the secret involves meditating in a particular way when consuming the 
sample. But the heart of the secret is that only certain people seem capable of acting on it. Even if 
the process were fully explained on a pamphlet that was widely distributed, maybe only one among 
hundreds reading it might be able to act on the knowledge. To everyone else, it’s just so much 
empty wishing.
The options for how a PC learned the secret are many, including the following.
Nightmare: The character had an especially vivid dream in which they snipped a bit of beard hair 
from a passed-out dwarf. Then, following the logic of dreams, they of course consumed the hair and 
became a dwarf in body, though not mind. Upon waking, the chimeran eventually tried the process, 
just to see.
Whispered by a Demon: While on an adventure, the character got separated from the others. 
During that time, a horned shadow, a disturbing bloodstain on the wall, a scroll the character picked 
up, or some other corrupted source imparted the secret of becoming a chimeran.
Library Visit: Deep in the stacks of the Library of Worlds in Timeborne, the character found a treatise 
on “self” as a slippery concept, with a particularly exhaustive example of that principle. This provided 
an impetus for the character learning the secret.
Accident: During a particularly vicious combat, a spray of blood sheeted the character’s face. After a 
long rest, the character woke up as an apparent member of the same species that they’d just fought.

When a chimeran takes on a new form, they 
might go from human embodiment to that of an 
elf, a dragonborn, or a gnome, gaining the gross 
physical attributes of the new species. But anyone 
who knows the chimeran can see the resemblance 
between their previous embodiment and their 
newest one. If a given chimeran has large eyes, 
an expressive mouth, and a high forehead when 
embodied as a human, they have something 
of those features as an elf, a dwarf, or a tiefling, 
too, just translated accordingly. And if they ever 
return to a previous form, they regain the same 
features as their last embodiment of that species, 
including height and weight. Even if they vary their 
gender from their previous embodiment, some 
resemblance remains.
Perhaps it has to do with their changeable 
embodiments, but chimerans can be moody. 
Those who don’t learn to get a handle on this 
trait find themselves going through friends 
and opportunities at an alarming pace. These 
individuals can end up living mean lives of deceit 
and criminality. However, chimerans understand 
that changing forms so often can be a shock to 
their mental equilibrium, so they often practice 
daily meditation where they seek to concentrate 
on compassion and equanimity. Those who master 
these techniques seem anything but moody, but 
instead contemplative and kind.
A chimeran comes into their ability to embody 
other species later in life. They’re originally born 
as a halfling, dwarf, tiefling, elf, or some 
other known species. It’s only years later 
that they learn the secret of embodiment, 
gather their first samples of another 
kind of creature, and switch their form 
to gain an advantage in a specific 
circumstance. Once someone embodies 
a different species for the first time using this 
secret, they’re no longer part of their original 
species; they’ve become a chimeran, even if they 
later predominantly choose to embody their 
original species.
According to one legend that chimerans pass 
among themselves, the children of Proteus, a 
long-vanished god of change, were drowned in the 
sea by a divine rival. Grief-stricken, Proteus faded 
away, presumably finding his final peace. However, 
his children survived, taking on the shapes of sea 
beings, but forgetting themselves in so doing. That 
heritage was passed down the ages to the present. 
Chimerans are not deviants; they are the heirs 
to Proteus.
Chimeran birth names are those of their birth 
species. Later, a chimeran could end up using 
different names to match the species they are 
currently living as, if their purpose is to fool 
others. However, many chimerans keep their 
original names.
Chimerans gain the following characteristic:
Embody Species: Changing into another kind 
of creature requires preparation. First you must 
collect a bit of skin, hair, blood, nail clippings, or 
some other portion of once-living flesh from the 
humanoid species you wish to embody. Then, 
anytime during or immediately after a ten-hour 
recovery, you can you ingest a shred of this material. 
You need to ingest only a single hair, a drop of 
blood, or a nibble of flesh. You fall unconscious, and 
over the course of about ten minutes, you lose the 
form and characteristics (including those from your 
species descriptor, if any) of whatever species you 
were previously embodying and gain the new form 
and characteristics associated with the sample you 
ingested, after which you awaken.
Once the process is complete, you have 
exchanged physical traits from the old species and 
the new one. These include increases or decreases 
to your Pools, modifiers based on your size, special 
resistances (such as a Helborn’s additional Armor 
against fire), and other abilities derived from your 
physical or mental physiology. You don’t gain 
the exact likeness of the creature from whom 
you obtained the bit of flesh; you instead retain 
recognizable facial and other features translated 
into your new form.
When a chimeran embodies 
a species for the first time, 
the player and the GM 
should work out exactly 
what species abilities they 
gain, and the player should 
make note of these things 
for future embodiments of 
that species.

ASPIRES TO ANGELIC PERFECTION
Some people are so enamored of the righteous 
path that they swear a binding oath to achieve 
angelic perfection. They wish to become in mind, 
body, and spirit as close to the celestial beings that 
directly serve the gods as they can. Devas, solars, 
and planetars are the heights sought by anyone 
who Aspires to Angelic Perfection—sometimes 
called celestial novitiates. The sublime grace and 
promise of divine contact that these higher beings 
possess is something celestial novitiates believe 
can be gained in their own mortal lifetimes, as long 
as they hold tightly to the tenets of their oath. The 
following tenets seek to align the novitiate’s spirit 
with that of the celestials they hope to one day 
transubstantiate into.
Tireless Devotion. Do not let weakness of the 
flesh stand in the way of achieving stated ends. 
Do not be swayed by devotions other than to your 
divine benefactor.
Lawful and Good. Law and good are beyond 
compare, but one tempers the other. A law of 
the land may not be good in all cases, and good 
may sometimes be brought about by creating 
appropriate laws.
Infallible Judgment. Knowing the difference 
between a lapse in judgment from which 
redemption is possible, and that which is truly evil, 
is a discernment that you must constantly strive 
to perfect.
Remorseless Against Evil. For that which is 
judged truly and irredeemably evil, you must act 
severely and with the knowledge that your action is 
the will of the gods.

F
lavors introduce a group of special abilities the 
GM and players can use to alter a character 
type, customizing it more to their liking, or 
tweaking it to make it more appropriate to the 
genre or setting.
Flavors include stealth, technology, magic, 
combat, and skills and knowledge, and as of this 
publication, planar.
At a given tier, abilities from a flavor are traded 
one for one with standard abilities from a type. 
So, to add the Fiery Touch planar flavor ability to 
a Warrior, something else—perhaps Bash—must 
be sacrificed. Now that character can choose Fiery 
Touch as they would any other first-tier Warrior 
ability, but they can never choose Bash.
PLANAR FLAVOR
Characters with planar flavor typically are from 
fantasy settings (although anything is possible). 
They excel at understanding the multiverse, dealing 
with creatures that cross planar divides, and using 
their own knowledge of dimensional anomalies to 
empower themselves. An Explorer with planar flavor 
might be a planeblazer, and a Speaker with planar 
flavor could be a disciple of an entity from a higher 
(or lower) dimension.

CHEST OF SOUL SILVER
Level: 1d6 + 2
Form: Collection of 1,000 soul silver coins in a chest 
or other container
Effect: The user makes a wish, and it is granted, 
within limits. The level of the effect granted 
is no greater than the level of the artifact, as 
determined by the GM, who can modify the 
effect accordingly. (The larger the wish, the more 
likely the GM will limit its effect.) When the coins 
deplete, all that remains is the chest or other 
vessel that previously contained them.
Depletion: Automatic
CIRCLET OF SHADOW EXCLUSION
Level: 1d6 + 3
Form: Grey metallic circlet designed to be worn like 
a crown
Effect: Wearer is immune to the despair effect of 
spending time in the Shadowfell.
The circlet has other properties, which can be 
triggered as part of another action.
•	 The wearer radiates bright light that extends 
an immediate distance for one hour. Creatures 
outside that radius looking in don’t perceive 
the bright light, instead perceiving whatever 
the prevailing illumination conditions 
happen to be. Within the area, the bright light 
temporarily suppresses any magical darkness 
it intersects.
•	 The wearer and each living creature within 
immediate range they choose gain +5 to 
Armor against attacks that cause decay and 
necrosis of flesh for one hour.
•	 For one minute, the wearer and each living 
creature within immediate range they choose 
regain 1 point of health or 1 Pool point while 
they remain within immediate range.
Depletion: 1 in 1d10 (check each use)
Normally, soul silver coins are provided 
only by the Mantis, but enough have 
been dispatched by her as rewards for 
completing her bounties that they can 
be obtained through other means and 
in other locations as well.
The wearer enjoys the following benefits.
•	 Defense rolls to resist disease are eased by 
two steps.
•	 Navigation tasks are eased.
•	 The boots adjust to perfectly fit any creature 
of up to twice the size of a human. They are so 
comfortable that they provide a +3 bonus to 
the wearer’s recovery rolls.
•	 The wearer can journey into places where 
many others fear to tread. As part of another 
action, they can gain +5 to Armor against one 
of the following energy types for one hour: 
acid, cold, fire, electricity, or sonic. Once used, 
this property can’t be used again until the 
next dawn.
•	 As part of another action, the wearer can 
activate these boots by calling on Mudarak’s 
grace. This allows them to walk across empty 
air, moving up to long range as their action, 
for up to one hour. Once used, the wearer 
can’t use this property again until after a 
ten-hour recovery.
•	 The wearer can use their action to teleport 
to any location they have previously visited 
(including on other planes of existence) up 
to once per day. They can take one other 
creature with them. Once used, the wearer 
can’t use this property again until after  
ten-hour recovery.
The boots were built to stand up to almost 
any environment, making them difficult to 
destroy accidentally. However, they can be 
destroyed if the wearer locates the place where 
Mudarak died on a distant world of the Material 
Plane. Even then, the wearer doesn’t so much 
destroy the boots as the boots become undone 
in the presence of proof of their creator’s demise.
Depletion: 1 in 1d20 (Check each time the boots 
are used to plane walk; if depleted, the boots 
disappear, and reappear somewhere else in the 
multiverse, possibly looking for Mudarak.)
