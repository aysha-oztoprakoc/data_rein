Introduction 
Welcome to the seventh issue of Further Horizons for the 
Transdimensional Adventures rules. 
The original plan was to make issue #7 a magic-themed issue, but with 
the release of the new post-apocalyptic book I decide to include more 
material tying into that – making this a combined Mutants & Magic 
theme.

attention in this issue is the Blasted Lands, one of the worlds in the 
World Book, as part of the Magic theme. This issue also includes

The Making of Apocalypse Essentials 
Out now is Apocalypse Essentials, briefly mentioned last issue. This is 
something of a hybrid product: it works as either a supplement for 
Transdimensional Adventures focussing on post-apocalyptic gaming, or 
a standalone product as it includes enough essential content that it 
can be used to run a game without owning the full Transdimensional 
Adventures core rules.  
To function as a standalone product it repeats a certain amount of 
content from the core rules – both core rules (combat, skills, etc.) and 
also Survivor-related content like mutations, classes, modern weapon 
rules for ‘ancient artifacts’. The system is slightly streamlined by 
removing the ‘origin’ (homeworld) step in character generation, as 
well as simplifying the advantage system to just use Talents. The mutation system integrates content 
from earlier issues of FH (sometimes with more revisions/expansions e.g. more Unusual Powers). 
Additional post-apocalyptic content includes: 
• 
More mutant subspecies and mutant powers e.g. additional Mutant Talents. 
• 
More post-apocalyptic monsters. 
• 
Expanded post apocalyptic equipment and economy system. 
New mutant varieties include the Small Folk, Denier, and Preserved One, as well as Multimutant 
which lets a player crossbreed two mutant varieties - this also lets a player create mutant animals 
that are part animal folk, part another mutant variety e.g. catfolk telepaths or minotaur/monstrous 
wasteland mutant.  
Building the rules has actually been a fairly interesting process which has involved both taking core 
rules and removing references to other worlds or magic, taking ‘generic’ tables like diseases and 
making them specific, integrating extra material from later articles or other products, expanding 
material, and genuine new material. As such it should be useful to someone who owns the normal 
core rules by adding new options and clarifying areas that are vague in the core rules due to limited 
space – specific details like starting equipment, monsters and encounter tables, and 
finding/analyzing ancient relics get covered in more detail. 
In terms of compatibility with other systems the World Book itself might be used with virtually any 
system - maps and other content are mostly systemless – but ideally one that can handle multiple 
genres; Essentials on its own isn’t perfectly suited to use with the World Book, since many of the 
worlds will have some elements it doesn’t really cover in detail e.g. stats for fantasy races, monsters 
or spells. Around half of the worlds there may work, possibly with a little fudging: Modern and 
Survivor worlds from the world book can possibly work (skill lists and classes are technically different 
but its possible to make something vaguely resembling a Modern character), while Exotic worlds 
may default to the Primitive class.                                                Ω

Class Features: Witch Hunter 
 
Witch hunters are characters trained to hunt evil magic-users, particularly witches. Most often these 
may be a branch of a religious order, but they might also be self-trained specialists hired to find the 
source of problems in a community or agents of a local government. 
 
Skills: Limited Skill – Metagenetics (witch finding), Insight, Folklore 
As one of these skills is a limited skill, they are short of the usual 3 skills for a class and gain bonus 
skill points to buy specializations at levels 2,4,6,8,10: these are drawn from the following list: One 
WP associated with deity worshipped or nonlethal weapon (such as mancatcher or net); Intimidate – 
Seek Confession; Folklore – identify magic item; Herbalism – create antitoxins. 
Metagenetics: As true witches gain a minor mutation, witch hunters gain some lore around witch 
marks: primarily they can use Metagenetics in place of Perception to spot a mutant– with 
Metagenetics the hunter can spot non-blatant mutations, perhaps finding the witch with darkvision 
when their eyes reflect light like a cat at night, or spotting Regeneration by a character’s perfectly 
scarless and unblemished skin. However they lack any deeper understanding of mutations or mutant 
varieties – it being likely their local equivalent of the Malleus Maleficarum states all mutations are 
the handiwork of demons. 
See also Sign of the Witch in this issue. 
A witch hunter may also find some knowledge of Combat useful (although NPCs may rely on guards 
or support from their order), and possibly Herbalism (in case a witch tries to poison them or to 
evaluate the handiwork of such). 
 
Class features: 
Resist Magic: a witch hunter can resist magic 1/day, gaining a +2 bonus on their resistance roll. This 
can be considered a special form of the Determination talent, which can only be used to affect magic 
resistance rolls. 
Also, choose two of the following additional abilities. 
• 
Extra Resistance: The character can use Resist Magic 2/day. 
• 
Exorcist: The character can banish demons with a lengthy ritual. This works as the 
Banishment spell but requires a Folklore roll. A given exorcist can attempt this only once 
against a particular demon unless their modifier increases (from levelling up or from a bonus 
due to an item or the like). 
• 
Martial Order: The character gains a bonus weapon proficiency. 
• 
Semi-spellcaster: The character can gain ranks in the Invocation skill, casting spells as a 
priest. They can only purchase this as a Limited Skill; the character learns Detect Magic plus 
only one spell per skill rank, and is limited in spells known as a priest would be. 
• 
Redeemer: The character can remove corruption from a willing witch (with a lesser pact 
only) with a successful opposed roll of [Metagenetics+Cha] – opposed to the patron demon’s 
Metagenetics skill check. If successful the witch loses the Invocation skill and their class 
features (e.g. Dark Gift), becoming a non-classed character. However, the ex-witch also gains 
2 points of Luck (max. 5); if Luck is already 5 the character gains the Incredible Luck 
advantage (FH #5). The demon is likely to consider the witch still their property, but will have

to ingest them personally. A multiclass witch trades Multiclass for Lucky rather than 
becoming non-classed, although this gives the same adjustments. 
Disadvantages: Witch hunters are not infrequently Zealots, suffering from some other 
psychological complication, or Cursed. They may have lost family or been wronged by 
spellcasters before taking up their current profession. A witch hunter can choose an extra minor 
disadvantage (beyond the usual 1 major/2 minor) in order to gain a 3rd class feature. 
Conversion Notes: This is typically a class for Dark Age characters. It can be used as a Survivor 
class (e.g. with the Apocalypse Essentials rules) although with some conversion – witch hunters 
being particularly twisted fanatics who are superstitious and hate psionics and/or mutants - 
perhaps even technology. The character’s Resist Magic becomes Resist Psionics. Without magic 
the character cannot have the Semi-spellcaster, Exorcist or Redeemer class features - the 
character’s initial class features will be Resist Psionics, Extra Resistance and Martial Order. 
The character would gain the full Metagenetics skill (a normal skill for post-apocalyptic 
characters), while instead gaining a limited version of Folklore (a Dark Age/fantasy world skill 
covering superstitions, purported magical phenomena and the like). Folklore is treated as being 
exclusively a class skill to witch hunters within the Essentials rules framework; a Scavenger who 
was formerly a witch hunter could buy access to this as a class feature, in which case note the 
skill is purchased at half cost (as a Limited Skill).                                   Ω

Metabolic Weakness: This is a Major physique drawback. More insidious than most physique 
defects, rather than a major structural change (as in Jellyfish or Small) the character merely has 
defects in metabolic pathways that generate energy, although the effects are still severe. The 
mutant’s altered metabolism causes muscle weakness and pain, and also affects generation of 
energy for mutant powers. They suffer the effects of Increased Fatigue (minor), and also recover lost 
gamma points at half the usual rate (i.e. a night’s rest recovers ½ their normal maximum).  
Multitentacled: This is a Major Movement & Manipulation 
drawback. Similar to e.g. Quadruped, this is a high-severity 
mutation which afflicts a character with increased 
penalties compared even to a other Major drawbacks, 
but also compensatory bonus abilities. Both of the 
character’s limbs are tentacles (as Missing Manipulator: 
Tentacle). Offsetting this, the character also gains two 
additional tentacles – four total - each is equivalent to Extra Arm with 
power limitation: Tentacle only. At GM option a character might be able 
to perform some more complex manipulation tasks by using all four of their tentacles at once to 
manipulate an object. The character suffers a penalty of -4 to Disguise checks if actively concealing 
them (additional tentacles may be tucked away, or a single sleeve might hold two at once). In 
combat a character can make 4 attacks dealing unarmed damage, three suffering an offhand penalty 
as normal (-2), or between them all a characters tendrils may manipulate a single one-handed 
weapon or object, virtually as if a character had four oversize ‘fingers’ – this is awkward and the 
character is at half Dexterity (rounded up). Using all the tentacles is at full Strength, otherwise a 
character’s Strength is divided between their tentacles with a minimum Str of ½. e.g. at Str 4 each 
tentacle has Strength 1, while a Str 2 character’s tentacles have Str ½ each. 
True Octopoid: If desired a player making an Animal Folk (Octopoid) character can take this 
Drawback, in exchange also gaining Extra Leg x 2 – this gives the character 8 limbs, 4 ‘arm’ tentacles

and 4 ‘leg’ tentacles (grouped at the lower end of the character and shorter/less flexible - not useful 
for manipulating objects). If using the disadvatnages in the core rules the character is (like a Mutant 
character) barred from taking any additional Disadvantages.   
Impaired Clotting: A Major Health Drawback, this works somewhat like a combined Vulnerability to 
both Slashing and Piercing damage, but the character takes only normal damage initially, with bonus 
damage delayed until later. After a hit the character takes damage at 1/round until additional 
damage equals 50% of the initial damage, or the bleeding is stopped. A character can attempt to 
staunch the bleeding for a round with an action and a Medicine check (TN 15), with bleeding ceasing 
after 3 successful checks are accumulated (TN 15). A medikit can stop bleeding with one successful 
check to dispense clotting agents. Taking a critical hit will increase the bleed rate to 2/round. A 
character with the Regeneration power can’t choose this drawback. 
Situational Learner: This is a Major mental drawback. The character learns by rote and generalizes 
lessons especially poorly. If using a skill in an unusual way (outside of normal functions distinctly 
explained in the rules) the character is treated as untrained. Any specializations the character has 
must be defined more narrowly than usual: Weapon Proficiencies will apply to a single weapon 
rather than a category of weapons e.g. ‘Medium Sword’ instead of ‘Blades’, Survival specializations 
will function in a specific area rather than all terrains of a type (e.g. ‘Delwar Swamp’ instead of 
‘Swamp’), or a Vehicle skill will apply to a single model of vehicle (the character is readily confused 
by different controls, brake sensitivities, etc.). 
Haphazard Learner: This is a Minor Mental Drawback. A character must be able to take psionics (i.e. 
Will 4+ or the Psychic Talent advantage) to choose this as a drawback; the character rolls randomly 
instead of selecting for all of their psi-powers, where from the Psionics skill or Innate Psionics.  
Dread Calling: This is a Major Psychic Drawback. This mutation makes the character either 
particularly susceptible or particularly interesting to an outside force that calls to and wants to either 
totally control or consume the character, using them as a slave or wearing their skin. This can be run 
similarly to a Doom (see FH #6) if the GM needs detailed interpretation guidelines; the relevant skill 
to delay the process is likely to be Mental Endurance. 
Missing Jaw: The character is missing their lower jaw. This is a Major Appearance drawback. It 
interferes with a mutant's speech, giving them a -4 penalty on Persuasion rolls, as well as preventing 
them from eating hard food. The character suffers only a -1 on Disguise checks to pass for human 
and a partial mask is enough to conceal it unless the mutant speaks, however. 
Some mutants with this may have had a jaw bone which rotted away due to radiation sickness. A 
water-breathing mutant might have this from a ‘reversion’ to earlier evolution (the jaw suggested to 
have started out as a gill arch).                                                    Ω

Metagenetics: Magic & Mutant Powers/ Sign of the Witch 
Metagenetics is a regular feature for covering mutant powers or other aspects 
of mutants. 
 
 
The existing Transdimensional Adventures rules do lean on the mutant rules as synonomous with a 
powers system, with other races often sharing abilities with mutants (e.g. natural weapons, size 
variations, darkvision, and so on). This is something that really goes against my core preference 
which was for mutants/mutations to be largely SF-themed/post-apocalyptic specifically and 
something that didn’t mix with magic. There are exceptions - some spells have unavoidably dipped 
into the mutation system i.e. Curses resembling mutation defects, witches gain an actual mutation 
themed as the horrifying work of devils; the chaos tainted mutant type is suggested to exist as due 
to wizard experimentation – but these are somewhat reluctant exceptions, either forced into 
existence by game systems standardization or because a certain amount of ‘genre violation’ is 
permitted within each origin, so that it is possible to create characters who are a bit diverse without 
all of them coming from different planets, if that happens to better suit the GM’s campaign. 
In general, mutations and magic can be thought of as different “power sources”. In some cases (due 
to game standardization) a spell and an ability can have similar effects – a wizard can cast a spell that 
gives someone darkvision that is the same as an elves’ (or a mutants). Mutant energy – as seen in 
either post-apocalyptic monsters or superheroes – doesn’t work according to magic principles 
however, and mutant powers aren’t vulnerable to being “dispelled” by magic (for example). The 
exception may be the ‘chaos tainted’ mutant type: if the player wants they can decide that their 
character (created by magical experimentation) has powers that likewise are magical. If the GM is 
using the mutant rules to create other creatures within an equivalent point budget (Using the 
‘Unusual Creatures as Mutants’ rules), these also could use a ‘magic’ power source. This makes these 
vulnerable to dispelling and the like, but potentially can have compensatory benefits. This could give 
a character benefits in some circumstances – a character could have a +2 bonus on power rolls on a 
‘high magic’ world for example – but is much more likely to result in problems (taking the same 
penalty as arcane magic if the world is low magic, being vulnerable to dispelling) and so may count 
as a power limitation. Treat the power rating of a power as its level if necessary for adjudicating 
dispelling or similar effects. A chaos-tainted characters’ drawbacks can be viewed as a sort of Curse 
in this case, but nothing short of a Wish can remove these (as usual). 
The rarity of mutants and mutant powers on Dark Age worlds (as well as psionics, which can be 
duplicated by mutants with the Innate Psionics power) also means that there are relatively few 
spells which have been researched that interact with mutations. A few exceptions exist: 
• 
Discern Shapechanger works against shapeshifting creatures regardless of the source of their 
shapechanging power, being designed to work against monsters which may have what 
amounts to an innate mutant power. 
• 
Magical Cure Disease does work on mutant diseases, which are similar enough to normal 
diseases, although with difficulty due to these usually having a high severity. Removing a 
drawback like Plague Bearer will also remove one of a character’s powers as noted in the

• 
A Wish directly alters reality and can accomplish many things lesser magic cannot, including

One world in the World Book is an exception to this – the Burning Kingdoms which has radiation and

From the other side, very few mutations also interact with magic specifically. Notably a character 
can gain Magic Sense (or Spirit Sense which is quasi-magical) with Enhanced Senses, but these are 
recommended only for magic-created mutants. One example form of Mutant Skill Prodigy can affect 
saves vs. magic. Overall however, very few mutations interact in any way with the magic system. 
Sign of the Witch 
In Transdimensional the witch (warlock) is a Dark Age class which as part of its niche has mutations 
(rather than being a semi-sorcerer with extra attack and miscellaneous features that can duplicate 
virtually every other class a la 5e; I do like the 5E warlock, but Transdimensional characters are 
already more flexible intrinsically). 
A Witch, gains a bonus minor mutation – the Dark Gift which is the sign of their patron demon or 
creature. This is one way to add more mutations to a Dark Age character, otherwise difficult, but as 
these are literally ‘the mark of the devil’, they remain rare (and unwelcome as far as most are 
concerned). Natural mutants from other worlds visiting dark age worlds may be thought to be 
demon-marked, since having a mutation means they bear ‘the sign of the dark one’. 
In most cases a magically-added mutation is no different to a natural one, although it will be less 
haphazard, and may be something less natural to a character’s form - a normal mutant might grow a 
third eye in their forehead while a witch might have it added to the palm of a hand; a witch may gain 
a bestial or monstrous feature that would be rare in an mutant who is largely human-like in 
appearance rather than wholly monstrous. 
As noted in the core rules a witch doesn’t randomly roll their mutation but what they get is up to 
their patron – playing the part of the patron, the GM can enforce additional requirements on a 
character who bargains hard to get a particularly strong ability. Without Metagenetics a prospective 
witch may not really know what mutations are possible - unless they’re a fallen witch hunter who 
has become multiclassed - but demons do have the skill (being interdimensional) and can try to 
translate odd requests into a known mutation type. A character can also receive a random mutation 
if they don’t wish to agree to any unusual pact terms, representing the vagaries of a demon feeling 
playful (a minor mutation is innately beneficial but may have detrimental effects however - most 
often in terms of looking human, but demons understand this may compromise a servant’s utility). 
Common mutations for witches include darkvision, Minor Power (Regeneration, Ooze or Size 
Increase are most common), extra body parts (e.g. gills, pouch, poison glands), or metabolic change 
e.g. reduced sleep requirement. Some mutations are most likely to be given in specific 
circumstances – for example, a mutation that makes a witch immune to disease might be granted if

an ability that will help them situationally on a quest the demon wants performed (Gills; Metabolic 
Change – Atmosphere Tolerance; Invulnerability to the energy type used by the guardian monster of 
a place; Wings).   
Greater Pacts 
A patron can fully transform a warlock into a mutant (the race) but this is more expensive of their 
energy (life force) – one can have fewer active greater-pact warlocks active at one time. From a 
player POV, a ‘greater pact’ is arguably less beneficial than a lesser pact, since instead of gaining a 
minor ability a character is instead replacing a set of racial abilities, with a different set (Mutant) that 
is the same cost. However, mutant as a race is potentially quite powerful; its balanced overall, but 
with extra abilities funded by drawbacks a non-mutant character’s performance over time is more 
flat, while a mutant’s abilities over time is a series of peaks and dips where moments of ‘peak 
performance’ using their powers let them accomplish things a normal character couldn’t, before the 
superman is felled by an allergy to some normally harmless substance.     Ω

Unearthly Arcana: Magic Items of Specific Worlds 
Unearthly Arcana is a regular feature for magical items or 
spells. 
 
This article deals with some example item 
common to specific worlds listed in the World Book. 
Characters rich enough to afford an item (Resources) might 
start with items listed here if they come from a world in 
question (applying any relevant homeworld modifiers) or 
have visited it previously. 
Per the core rules, a character can have Item Access as a ‘known world’ benefit, as described in 
‘Finding New Worlds’ in the core rules (Chapter 9); a character can gain this initially as a benefit from 
the Veteran advantage. Gaining an item from below is therefore slightly difficult (requiring both 
Veteran and in most cases Resources to pay the GP cost). The GM can consider waiving some 
requirements if appropriate for a character’s background. Being uncommon, optionally a PC who has 
the ‘Uncommon Feature’ ability (FH #6) may use that in place of Item Access to have found or 
purchased an item (meaning what is unusual about the character is the object they own, rather than 
an advantage or race, and in which case Uncommon Feature can’t be used to also qualify for another 
rare ability). In this case the character is likely to be a dimensional traveller, or the item itself could 
have been moved around in some fashion, such as by another dimensional traveller like the 
shopkeeper in FH #1. 
Characters may also find items on their adventures, of course: this does not require any specific 
character resource be expended, aside from any time/effort questing for an object.  
 
Worlds listed here include: 
Arden: Key to the Undercity 
The Burned Kingdoms: Renewal Elixir 
Neras (The Blasted Lands): Darkblade, Grazul’s Elixir. 
Skan-Doran: Book of the Sixth Age 
Ygg [not in world book]: Medallion of the Quest 
 
(Note: Some items listed in previous columns such as artifacts are unique, and so from a specific 
world initially, but which world is often at GM discretion for campaign reasons; a specific item can 
also move around, of course. These by contrast are all item types that are specific to a world due to 
either specific materials being from a world, or construction method only being known on that 
world). 
Ygg is not a world listed in the world book. It may be listed in a future supplement, but I made a 
decision to include some gear from other/unlisted places to emphasize again that the World Book 
planets are just intended as a sample, not as an exhaustive list.

New Items 
 
Magic Items: 
Darkblade (Level: N/A: , GP value – 4,000; Neras): While not entirely unique to the Blasted 
Lands, an equivalent will have a cost multiplier of about x10 elsewhere, if it can be found at 
all. 
A darkblade is ‘dark’ with respect to magic, negating magic in its vicinity. The weapons 
themselves are made from meteoric iron primarily which should simply resemble normal 
steel, but the metal is often blackened to a matt-black finish alchemically – the reason for 
this is lost to time and may be simply theatre or have a practical underpinning, as the 
weapons are often intended to be drawn unobserved in darkness e.g. on assassination 
missions against wizards.  
Casting a spell directly on a character using one requires a raise (5 over the normal TN); this 
applies to ‘area’ effects with respect to the character only, who may be unaffected while 
companions burn depending on a wizards’ exact spellcasting skill result.  
The increase applies to both beneficial and baneful effects as long as the weapon is in hand 
or on a character’s person; even magic items may be affected (If one would normally function 
automatically it should instead make a roll of d20 + item level against the normal TN to cast 
the spell, i.e. one loses an implied ‘reliable’ property instead of requiring a raise to function). 
The weapon itself can be affected by magic with a roll 10 over the normal target number. Other 
magical properties can be added to one but again suffer the 10-point TN increase (whether using an 
actual spell like Transfer Enchantment or normal Artifice, which still involves magical processes). A 
darkblade’s properties themselves can’t be transferred with Steal Enchantment as they are a 
property of the material rather than a separate enchantment. 
While typically given the shape of a weapon, other metal items (shields or miscellaneous items) may 
be forged that have similar effects.  
 
Grazul’s Elixir of Power (GP value: 100 GP; consumable; Neras): This 
glowing liquid is a variety of mana potion made with tiny amounts of 
triphazium, a magic-infused toxic metal naturally existing on Neras. It 
does not have the usual addiction side effect possessed by mana 
potions made from organic materials (see the core book), but is toxic 
– a drinker must make a TN 10 Endurance check to keep it down and 
gain 4 magic points, and takes 1d4 poison damage unless the 
Endurance check is 20+. A character with Eat Anything can regain 
magic points with no check required. A character with Alchemy may 
find the properties of the elixir particularly interesting, its key 
ingredient being extraordinarily rich in magic energy.  
GP cost is a purchase cost (in the Blasted Lands only); it can be produced very cheaply with access to 
triphazium (at around 5 GP per dose for other ingredients and probably 2 GP for a bottle).

Key to the Undercity (L4, 4000 GP, Arden): This blackened silver key is ornamental, the key end sharp 
and trident-like while the other end is topped with a carving of a skull. It marks the character as one 
who has the favour of several of the factions of the undercity of Golkana and the ‘freedom of the 
city’– meaning they are a villain most foul, but an unusually trusted one. Most of the factions have a 
patron god/sin, making a (true) owner of the key one who has several of the vices they espouse. The 
character gains a +2 bonus on Persuasion checks in the undercity (a non-magical benefit). 
 
Medallion of the Quest (L3, 2000 GP, Ygg): Ygg is a world of 
hilltop castles overlooking rugged terrain; individual castles 
are aligned with either the Dark or the Light. A Medallion 
of the Quest is a disc of black or silver metal that shows an 
image of a knight in armour on one side, transforming into 
a suit of gleaming ornate full plate (of the same colour) on 
command or back, in effect the equivalent of the 
Conditioned talent (as the character can turn off armour 
penalties at will). The Medallion is normally given to a hero 
set a specific task, accompanied by a geas spell.  
 
Renewal Elixir (L4, 4000 GP, The Burned Kingdoms): When consumed the Elixir removes all mutations 
and mutation drawbacks from the target, returning a target to their original race (human if not 
otherwise specified). An unwilling target can make a TN 15 Endurance check to resist the effect. This 
functions like the Transform spell, but does not risk a mishap and applies only to mutants, as well as 
being much lower-level and cheaper. Manufactured in the mage-ruled city of Yim, its use is seen as a 
blasphemy by more conservative branches of the Church of Taym who regard mutation as a divine 
punishment. 
Note: As a permanent effect this does not gain a cost divisor for being consumable. The effect is 
permanent and basically irreversible – remutating an affected character is theoretically possible but 
no easier than changing any other human into a mutant, nor would future mutations necessarily be 
the same as their original power set. The GP cost reflects creation costs (and would be the cost paid 
if the item is purchased using Resources) – actual cost may be increased as much as ten-fold by low 
availability and high demand from other buyers trying to save someone from the Church 
(themselves, an adventuring companion, beloved or family member). 
 
Book of the Seventh Age (L10, 200,000 GP, The Dying Lands): The book is a storybook of long-ago, 
the illuminated text seeming more realistic as it is read. Those who fully read the Book vanish, 
according to legend being transported back in time to the Seventh Age, around 8000 moonyears ago 
– at that time the planet being fairly unremarkable. What effects time travelling may have on the

Transdimensional Locations: Darkblade - A Blasted Lands Location 
This adventure location is in the magic-wrecked Blasted Lands (Neras) (not to be confused with the 
radioactive Burned Kingdoms, home of the Warlord in Further Horizons #5). For a map of most of the 
main continent and general description see the World Book (pages 24-26). 
This location could be visited as a result of Destiny (if PCs need to kill a wizard really badly), or as a 
one-off PCs could be recruited and sent here by a wizard (who wants a Darkblade but can’t come 
themselves because of local prejudices) or an interdimensional organization. 
The Blasted Lands (as described in the World Book) has a very high magic level, which led to 
devastation in the Wizard Wars between rival rival magic-users. Arcane magic checks on the planet 
gain a +2 bonus, but magic has become unstable, with spells suffering a mishap (potentially 
disastrous) on a roll of 1-2. The prevalance of magic and monsters means that many NPCs are 
relatively high-level. Surviving settlements have tiny populations compared to most worlds. 
Native people generally detest wizards, but paradoxically magic is easy and commonplace enough 
that characters native to the planet may have levels of Arcane Spellcasting as a custom skill (having 
Magical Aptitude in place of the usual Fast Healing ability for Dark Age characters); the title is 
reserved for those who can work particularly grand magic or seem to display a deeper knowledge of 
how magic works (i.e. members of the actual Wizard class). Specific wizards who have proven 
themselves to be allies of the people or mostly good can overcome this (e.g. the dead wizard 
Eddorak still has some in the town who would say good of him). 
Humans have traditionally relied on magic for many things; some of the races that lack much magical 
ability (dwarves, humanoids) tend to be better with respect to crafts than humans, e.g. smithing or 
mining. 
Travelling in the Blasted Lands: The GM should roll for a random encounter (1 in 6 chance) per hour 
of travel; this does not necessarily mean a fight however; characters may be able to detect and avoid 
monsters ahead if proceeding cautiously. 
Travelling characters may also occasionally be exposed to storms of magical energy, with 
unpredictable effects if shelter is not found; these grow more common going north. 
The History of Starfall Lake: Starfall Lake was created by accident when magic was used to pull a 
great meteor – a very small asteroid - from the sky. The wizard responsible, the ‘astromancer’ and 
mystic Eddorak, was killed. Meteoric iron is used in some items that negate magic – typically 
weapons (traditionally knives, although this may be due mainly to the scarcity of ‘star metal’). 
Actually summoning such an object was thought impossible due to the anti-magic nature of the rock, 
but one wizard found a loophole – using magic to create a gate through which his psionic abilies 
could be manifested and amplifying those with magic. He had intended to cold-forge the material to 
equip a humanoid army with magic-defeating blades…he could still defend himself with psionics, 
after all…but was attacked by his enemies while distracted by the ritual.

Advanced Psionic Powers 
This article lists a number of more advanced psionic powers. Unlike spells, psionic powers do not 
have a true level, but access to these is restricted by prerequisites (other powers). 
Due to prerequisites, these won't appear if rolling a psionic power randomly. If a character rolls a 
power twice then they can choose a power that has it as a prerequisite, rather than re-rolling, 
potentially including any of the new powers listed here. 
 
Disintegrate 
Prerequisites: Telekinetic Rend, Telekinesis 
This power completely destroys a target of up to Medium size, shredding it into its component 
atoms. Non-living objects can also be affected. When using the power, the caster designates a 
‘power level’ it is being used at of from 1-5, which gives the psi-point cost to use the power. 
Something like a spell the psionic must make a successful Psionics check (TN 10 + 2 per power level) 
to use the power; if successful the target must make an Endurance check against the same TN or be 
evaporated, and a target that resists still takes damage equal to the power level (1-5). If the Psionics 
roll fails the power has no effect and the target takes no damage. 
 
Farspeech 
Prerequisite: Telepathy 
This psi-power lets a character dramatically increase the distance they can send or receive thoughts, 
which otherwise functions as normal Telepathy. They may make a Psionics roll, with the TN 
determining the maximum possible distance:

Farspeech costs 1 psi-point per 10 minutes of communication. 
 
Haunt 
Prerequisite: Spirit Sense 
With this power, the character increases their ability to connect to the spirit world, being able to not 
merely see spirits but call up a departed spirit. This requires a Psionics roll (TN 12). A spirit knows 
most of what it knew in life but may be traumatized by its death and may not always give true 
information for its own reasons. If the soul is unavailable the power is not effective (e.g. it doesn’t 
work on destroyed constructs or warlocks). On a complication the character will call forth the wrong 
spirit (which may or may not immediately be obvious).

Identity Theft 
Prerequisites: Telepathy, Domination 
This power lets a telepath rearrange a target’s memories and identity so that they – temporarily – 
become a different person. A target cannot gain any skills or attributes, but their awareness they 
know how to use a skill can be suppressed. Generally a character acts in accordance with their new 
memory set and persona, although something diametrically opposed to their original personality 
allows them a Will+Mental Endurance roll to remember something and hesitate or resist. A 
character might be Persuaded they are being influenced, or may make Int checks to notice any 
discrepancies between reality and their fake memories (at GM discretion). A character also gets a 
Mental Endurance roll (TN 12) to break the effect each day. Casual use of Telepathy detects only 
thoughts of the ‘new’ identity – hence in some cases this power may be used on someone willingly, 
if they need to infiltrate an organization with mind-reading powers- if a Probe attempt is made a 
successful opposed Psionics roll determines the character’s mind has been tampered with.  
A second use of this power can restore a character’s personality and memories.  
 
Matter Transformation 
Prerequisite: Molecular Agitation 
This power lets the psionic interfere with the molecular structure of substances. It doesn’t allow for 
true transmutation of elements but can purify a substance, reverse chemical changes, or change the 
allotropic state of an element e.g. they can turn rust back into steel, purify water, re-strengthen 
titanium that is radiation-damaged or smelt ore with their mind. However, diamonds created with 
the power from carbon are crumbly and cloudy due to impurities and the slowness of the 
crystallization process, and so of limited if any value. The character can’t create complex molecular 
structures (most organic substances). The character has to be somewhat familiar with the sort of 
substance they are trying to produce - the GM can require a Chemistry or Alchemy check to see if a 
character has enough knowledge to attempt to synthesize something (the Advanced Knowledge 
origin feature may also apply). Each pound of matter affected costs 1 psi-point and requires a 
Psionics check (default TN 12 unless a substance is particularly unusual). Magical items are not 
affected by the power, making it possible to detect them with the power, albeit clumsily.  
 
Metabolic Control 
Prerequisite: Mind Over Body 
The character’s control over their bodily functions is such that they can modify their metabolism, 
granting themselves a metabolic feature of their choice. The duplication process operates similarly 
to Minor Power – Adaptability, except that it requires 2 hours of meditation to accomplish the 
change and a successful Psionics roll, and only metabolic powers can be duplicated. The power costs 
2 psi-points to use with the character’s maximum psi-points reduced by this amount for as long as 
the power is maintained. The change can be maintained indefinitely.   
 
Psi-blade 
Prerequisite: Force Generation 
Created by an advanced form of force generation, a psi-blade is a weapon of burning psychic energy 
(rather than merely an object shaped from a force field). A character spends 1 psi-point to create the 
blade, which then lasts for up to a minute (generally, an entire combat encounter). A character using 
the power rolls Psionics; if successful creating the blade is not an action and imposes no initiative 


Further Horizons      23 
 
 
 
penalty, while an unsuccessful check means the character must concentrate (an action) to bring a 
blade into existence. On a complication a blade fails to appear at all. 
In combat, a psi-blade is a Very Light weapon which deals 1d10 points of damage; it requires a hand 
to use but does not have a minimum Strength score to wield or gain a bonus to damage from 
Strength. It is considered to be equivalent to a Tech III (Futuristic) weapon for armour penetration 
purposes. 
 
Teleport Object 
Prerequisite: Teleport 
With this power a character attempts to teleport another object they can see within 100ft. If a 
Psionics roll is made (TN 12) the object can be moved into their hand or next to them, or to a 
random location within 100ft of its original position. If the roll is made with a raise (TN 17), the 
character can accurately move an object to any location within 100ft, or may pay double the psi-
point cost to move an object up to 1000ft away to a specific location, with a margin of error of 1d10 
feet in any direction. 
An object may be up to 100 pounds in weight per psi-point expended (minimum of 1 psi-point). The 
power does not work against living creatures.                            Ω

Adventure: Fortune’s Foe 
 
This is a city-based adventure set in Atlantik City, described briefly in the sample campaign area in 
the Apocalypse Essentials book. 
Characters can start in Atlantik city - they may be citizens of the place, or may be just passing 
through on their way to somewhere else - most likely from the nearby area although some sea travel 
(a dangerous proposition) also passes through here. Dimensional travellers might also show up here, 
of course; these may be slightly harder to integrate into a local adventure. 
If the GM wants, the partial framework for the city here might be useful as a starting point for other 
adventures - characters might find a ship travelling north to the ruins of Old New York, try to find the 
headquarters of a cult, plan a heist on vaults below the temple-casino, fight crime in the city streets, 
or get a job protecting a wealthy citizen from their enemies – but with with limited space this 
adventure concerns itself with one possible adventure set in the city itself; tracking down a wanted 
criminal in the city. Kane is a mutant thug wanted by the city’s democratic government and the 
Casino-Temple of the God of Luck for various crimes including robberies and murders. He has a fairly 
dangerous combination of mutant powers and abilities -most of his family are thought to be just 
minor mutants but he seems to have inherited an extensive array of minor powers - and he has 
escaped from traps in some unknown way previously (Intangibility). 
The pregenerated characters can be integrated into the sample adventure as follows: 
 
Arric: Arrik may have had an encounter with the gang when they were trying to treat Kane’s injuries 
– possibly he had a medikit stolen by the catfolk, instead of her just buying it.  
Bront: New to the city, he was directed to the tavern where he may find other PCs looking for clues. 
Caleb: Caleb is a contact of Roman, who won big at the casino-temple. Roman is (unknown to the 
temple) also a psychic from Mystik Island, having cheated in order to win money to help them raise 
and educate outcast psychic orphans.  
Isabella: Having grown up around the docks, she may have been persuaded by Hank to rescue Kane’s 
cousin Marlin. This may short cut some of the information gathering sequence of the adventure; the 
GM can add more slum encounters, or have Kane’s gang move elsewhere (so that the PCs need to 
get information out of his arrested friend or ambush him at another location). 
Luiza: Basically a cop she may be involved in trying to find Kane as part of her job, or asked to find 
Kane as a test to see if she can join the guard. Alternatively, she may be a friend of a guard injured or 
killed during the gang’s crime spree. 
Mort: Not needing to sleep he may have gotten a job at the Lockhouse, or may be just on the 
lookout for bounty jobs to afford essential upgrades. However, he would realize Kane may be too 
much to deal with on his own. 
Characters a player makes themselves may be integrated similarly if something suggests itself.

A Partial List of City Locations: 
The Marketplace (Bazaar): In this area can be found traders from across the sands or across the seas, 
local farmers selling produce, fishmongers, citizens looking for a bargain, and shops crafting all 
manner of goods - smiths, scribes, tinkers and weavers. If the GM wants, the PCs might find the 
trader from the first adventure here, looking to recruit adventurers for a desert adventure.  
A visit to the marketplace may notice a theft going on (unrelated to the adventure), which could be 
an encounter; most thieves are likely to be 0-level mutants. Occasionally a gang will attempt to 
shake down a merchant for protection money or a customer will confront a merchant over problems 
with products or services.  
The Silver Gull: This old saloon is run by Greeva, an old one-eared mutant she-wolf (3rd level 
enforcer). Tavern brawls erupt occasionally. 
The Slums: The ruined part of the city near the coast is an area inhabited by gangs, the ultra-poor, or 
those otherwise shunned by society e.g. people who have contracted diseases making them 
dangerous (the Plague Bearer drawback). 
The Sewers: These have largely been flooded - perhaps surprisingly for a post-apocalyptic city they 
still work mostly as sewers (and are not inhabited by any but the most desperate aquatic mutants). 
The Docks: These are used by both visitors from other places further along the coast, although sea 
travel is extremely hazardous, and fishermen catching food for the city. No two "fish" are the same, 
with a catch likely to include mutant octopi, lobsters and other creatures as well as seemingly 
normal fish. City ordinances since "the incident" demand any fish displaying significant sentience or 
telepathic abilities be destroyed in case some sort of super-fish appears that could menace shipping. 
Taverns near the docks are especially rough, with significant chances of a brawl erupting between 
sailors and fishermen. The docks may contain one or two actual pirate ships that are taking on 
supplies, selling booty, or making repairs, as unobtrusively as possible. 
The Temple of Luck: Once one of the casinos of the old city, this is now a place of religious worship to 
the Luck God and a den of assorted vices. The temple will extend some credit to losers, giving them 
the opportunity to go deeper into debt. 
The Palace: Another former casino, this is now where the city’s democratically-elected King (or 
Queen) lives, along with assorted bureaucrats.
