SETUP
1.	Choose which game mode you would like to play. 
2.	Place the board in the centre of the table.
3.	Shuffle the Judge cards, then deal one face-up to each player 
until someone is dealt the Starting Player card.
4.	Each player chooses a standee (and the matching Specialist 
Judge ability if using them). In the event of disagreement, use the 
remaining Judge cards to assign them randomly. 
5.	Starting with the Starting Player and moving clockwise, each 
player places their standee on an empty Starting Sector on 
the board (these are the spaces with black text on a white 
background).
6.	Shuffle the Perp, Crime, Sector and Action cards separately. 
Place them face-down beside the board, leaving space beside 
them for face-up piles of discarded cards.
7.	Reveal the top card of the Sector deck. Place a face-down Perp 
card and a face-up Crime card on the matching Sector on the 
board, then place the Sector card in a discard pile. Repeat this 
until there are 6 Perps and Crimes on the board.
8.	Deal 6 Action cards to each player. Return any yellow Alert cards 
drawn during setup to the deck and replace them with a new card, 
then reshuffle the deck if necessary. Players must keep their hand 
of Action cards secret from one another. You’re now ready to start!
OVERVIEW
It’s the 22nd century and Mega-City One, the largest city on 
Earth, is awash with crime. You are Judges, tasked with enforcing 
the law and dispensing justice city-wide. Each turn you’ll move 
through the city, headed for the Sectors containing crimes, then 
revealing the Perps responsible and attempting arrest. You’ll 
have backup from the Justice Department in the form of Action 
cards: events, tools and even obstacles in the fight against crime.
Each round is split into three phases: movement, arrest and 
refill. In each phase players act in turn, starting with the Starting 
Player, then moving in clockwise order. Players do not need to 
wait until their turn to play Action cards, though some cards can 
only be used during certain phases (detailed on page 5).
In the movement phase Judges head towards Sectors where they 
can make arrests or draw additional cards. In the arrest phase 
all Judges who can attempt arrest on a Perp in their Sector must 
do so. In the refill phase new Perps and Crimes are added to 
the board (if necessary), players redraw Action cards, then the 
Starting Player card is passed to the next player on the left.
The player who has the greatest total strength of Crime and Perp 
cards in their score pile at the end of the game is the winner.
The first six pages of this book detail the rules, while the rest is a 
card album detailing the history behind Mega-City One’s various 
Perp, Crimes and Sectors.

MOVEMENT PHASE
Judges will move across the board with 
the goal of revealing Perps during the movement 
phase, before attempting to arrest them in the arrest phase.  
The player with the Starting Player card moves first, then each 
remaining player moves in clockwise order.
THE BOARD
The board shows the heart of Mega-City 
One, divided into 28 Sectors by the black 
and white boundary lines and by the rivers. 
Boundary lines can be crossed freely, while 
rivers can only be crossed if there is a 
bridge present.
The Sectors with black text on white 
backgrounds are Starting Sectors, where 
the Judges will begin the game. 
The Justice Department Hospital in the 
centre of the board is where Judges will be 
sent when they are injured in combat. Only 
Judges (not Perps or Aliens) can enter the 
Hospital. It has a red border.
MOVEMENT
On your turn in the movement phase, you may move your Judge 
up to two adjacent Sectors in any direction. You may not cross 
the river unless there is a bridge between the two spaces. More 
than one Judge can be in the same Sector.
A Judge cannot leave a Sector containing a Perp unless:
	*
There is another Judge in that Sector.
	*
They are forced to leave by an Action card.
	*
They are KO’d during combat.
If you enter a Sector containing a face-down Perp, immediately 
turn the card face up and end your movement there.
After everyone has moved, and nobody wishes to play more 
Action cards, the arrest phase begins.
ARREST PHASE
In the arrest phase, Judges fight Perps in order to bring them to 
justice (and score the points that win them the game). Starting with 
the Starting Player and going clockwise, each player in the same 
Sector as a Perp must attempt to arrest them, resolving a combat. 
Each Judge may only be in one combat per round. If a Judge takes 
part in a special combat, they can’t initiate a separate arrest attempt.
If there are multiple Judges in the same Sector as a Perp (who haven’t 
been in combat already this round), the players must decide whether 
to band together for a joint arrest or attempt competing arrests.
Once every Judge who can fight has done so, the arrest phase ends.
The Judge in Resyk can move up to two spaces. If they enter 
Devil’s Island they cannot move any further, as there is a Perp 
in that Sector. They cannot move from the Academy of Law to the 
Power Tower as there is no bridge across the river.

STRENGTH
Each Judge has a base strength of 6.
Each Perp has a base strength equal to the value on their card, 
as well as any Crime cards in the same Sector.
Both Judges and Perps will add a die roll to their strength. Action 
cards committed in combat will further modify the strength values 
of the participants.
At the end of combat, all cards played are discarded.
ARREST
Most combat happens when a Judge tries to arrest a Perp in their 
Sector. Once combat begins, any player may play Action cards face-
down on the table. Each time a card is added the other players can 
choose to add more cards, but they cannot take back any cards they 
have already committed. Once everyone is finished adding cards, all 
face-down cards are revealed.
Action cards with a red bar are combat cards, which can support or 
sabotage the arresting Judge. Any cards with a number in a shield 
add (or subtract) that value to the Judge’s total strength.
Players may add non-combat cards too. If the player controlling a 
Judge in combat plays a non-combat card with a shield on the bottom 
of the card, that total is added to their strength. If another player has 
added any card other than Escape or Sabotage it was a bluff, and 
that card is discarded with no effect.
Add the total values of all cards to either the Judge or the Perp’s 
strength (as per the card). The Judge player then rolls the black Judge 
die and whichever other player added the most Action cards to the 
combat (or the next player clockwise if tied) rolls the red Perp die. Add 
the results to the Judge and Perp’s strength respectively. If any cards 
gave the Judge or Perp additional rolls then roll again, adding each 
result to the total. The highest total strength wins the combat.

JOINT ARRESTS
If multiple Judges are in the same Sector, they can agree to make a 
joint arrest. Judges who are working together have a strength of 6 
per Judge, but still only roll a single die. Action cards and Specialist 
Ability tokens modify their strength as normal.
If you win the combat, the Judges involved split the rewards: starting 
with the earliest player turn order, each player takes the Perp or 
Crime card with the highest value. This means a third arresting 
Judge will usually receive no points. The Crime Spree Action card 
means there may be two Crime cards with a Perp. In this instance 
a third Judge could take the lowest value card. If there were only 
two Judges involved, the first Judge would take the highest and the 
lowest, while the other Judge would take the middle value.
In a tie, the earliest player in turn order gets the Crime card.
If you lose the combat, all Judges involved are KO’d.
COMPETING ARRESTS
If different Judges (or groups of Judges) do not wish to join together 
for an arrest, they make simultaneous arrest attempts. 
The Judge (or group) that beats the Perp by the highest margin 
makes the arrest. Any Judges (or groups) with a lower strength 
than the Perp are KO’d.
In the event of any ties between Judges, the player who is earliest 
in turn order wins the tie.
If you win the combat, you arrest the Perp: place the 
Perp and Crime cards face-down in front of you as a 
score pile. Cards in this pile count as points at the end 
of the game. You may check your own score pile at any 
time, but not those of other players.
If the combat ends in a tie, you stop the crime but the 
Perp escapes. Add the Crime card to your score pile, 
then the player who rolled the Perp die moves the Perp 
card up to two Sectors in any direction. They cannot 
stop on the Hospital or on a space with another Perp.
If you lose the combat, your Judge is KO’d: knock over 
their standee. You cannot move or play any more Action 
cards this round, and at the end of the round you will 
be forced to discard and redraw your entire hand.

REFILL PHASE AND ENDING THE GAME
REFILL PHASE
This is the end of the round, where new crimes are reported, and 
Judges get new Action cards.
ADD NEW CRIMES
If there are fewer than 6 Perps on the board, draw a Sector card 
and add a face-down Perp and a face-up Crime to the matching 
Sector. Repeat this until there are 6 Perps on the board, or the 
Sector deck is empty.
If playing the Hotdog Run version of the game, discard the top card 
of the Sector deck if no Perps have been added to the board this 
round (see Game Length).
HOSPITALISE KO’D JUDGES
Stand up any KO’d Judges, and move 
them to the Justice Department 
Hospital. They must discard their entire 
hand, then redraw the same number of 
cards as they discarded. KO’d Judges 
who have no Action cards draw 1 card 
in addition to the Action card below, for 
a minimum of 2.
DRAW NEW ACTION CARDS
Every player draws 1 new Action card. Any non-KO’d Judges who 
end their turn on the Hospital or a Starting Sector get the bonus 
indicated by the card icon on that Sector. This is always in addition 
to the card they will draw normally. 
If the deck of Action cards ever runs out, shuffle up the discarded 
cards to form a new deck.
Players can never have more than 6 cards in hand. If drawing 
more cards would bring them over this limit, they do not draw.
Judges voluntarily ending their turn on the Hospital 
may discard as many Action cards from their hand 
as they choose before drawing, then draw new 
cards equal to the number they discarded plus 1 
additional card. 
Judges on a Starting Sector draw 1 or 2 additional 
cards at the end of the round, for a total of 2 or 3.
ALERT CARDS
If any player draws a yellow Alert card, they must play it immediately 
then draw a new card to replace it. Some effects will last for the 
next round, while others (like the Alien) can last even longer.
PASS THE STARTING PLAYER CARD
The Starting Player passes that card to the 
player to their left, who begins the next round.

ENDING THE GAME
The game ends after the final round. There are three options for 
when the final round is triggered: choose how long you want the 
game to last from the Game Length options. 
The Hotdog Run is recommended for learning the game, while 
the Day Shift is recommended for normal play.
When the final round is triggered, play one last round of the 
game. At the end of that arrest phase the game ends. 
Add the total strength of all cards in your score pile. The player 
with the highest total wins.
If tied, each tied player has one final combat against the others 
to decide the victor, playing any remaining Action cards they 
have. Non-tied players may not add cards to this combat.

ACTION CARDS
ACTION CARDS
The Action cards represent the 
unexpected twists and turns of life 
in Mega-City One. They are split into 
four types, indicated by the coloured 
bar across the top of the card: 
Combat (red), Movement (green), 
Alert (yellow) and Special (blue). 
You do not have to wait until your 
turn to play an Action card, and 
can play them on any player, 
including yourself.
Most cards include an alternative option at the 
bottom of the card, which can be used instead of the main action 
to help yourself in combat or to dodge other card effects.
COMBAT
Combat cards are played face-down 
during combat (usually in the arrest 
phase). Most add to the Judge or Perp’s 
strength – the amount added (or 
reduced) is shown in the badge in the 
top right corner:
If the badge shows a die, then roll the Judge die an extra time and 
add that amount to the Judge’s total.
Once a combat has begun, you can only play cards face-down 
as part of that combat, and you must do so before the dice 
are rolled.
MOVEMENT
Movement cards can be played on any 
Judge (including your own) during the 
movement phase. Most will reduce or 
increase the distance that Judge can 
move, while Barney cards will send 
the Judge directly to one of the Starting 
Sectors. 
If that Judge has moved already this 
round, any reduction or increase applies 
to their movement next round.
A Judge who has been moved by a Barney card can still move this 
round if they have not already done so.
You cannot play an action card while a Judge is mid-movement.
ALERT
Alert cards must be played immediately 
when drawn. The player who drew the 
card chooses how the effect is applied, 
then draws a different card to replace it. 
The Alien Escape card adds an Alien 
token to the board, who must be fought 
by any Judges in its Sector during the 
arrest phase instead of a Perp. It has a 
base strength of 12 and wins ties, but 
does not add a die roll and is not worth 
any points at the end of the game. When 
fighting the Alien other players may add combat cards as usual. Cards 
which help Perps have no effect, but cards which hinder Judges still 
do. If the Alien is defeated, remove the token from the board.
Quarantine locks off an entire Sector for one round, making 
it impossible to enter or leave, but also rendering Judges there 
immune from the effects of any Movement or Special cards. A 
Barney card cannot force a Judge to enter a Quarantined Sector. 
However, if the Hospital is Quarantined, KO’d Judges are still sent 
there during the refill phase.
Bridges Closed prevents Judges and Perps (but not the Alien) from 
crossing the river for one round. 
SPECIAL
Special cards can be used during either 
the movement or arrest phase, unless 
there is a specific timing mentioned on 
the card.
When using a Tip-Off card to move 
a Perp, you may move through other 
Perps and Judges, but cannot end the 
movement in the Hospital or in a space 
containing another Perp.
The Block Mania card forces two Judges 
to immediately fight each other, regardless of where they are on the 
board, and neither can be in combat again this round. Any player 
may add combat cards in front of either Judge. The Judge with the 
lowest strength is KO’d. If tied, both Judges are KO’d.
The Robodoc card is used when you are KO’d for any reason. You 
avoid the KO, and instead move to an adjacent space.
ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS
Most cards have a black bar along the bottom of the card with 
an alternative option which can be used instead of the main 
action on the card. Alternative options can only be used on your 
own Judge: they cannot be used to help other players.
If the bar contains a badge, the card can be played during your 
own combat to add that value to your strength.
If the bar instructs you to ignore the effect of a Boing or 
Barney card, you can immediately play this card to cancel its 
effect. Both cards get discarded.

numbers, but many other types of Judge come to the service of the city.

then you can choose which specialist you would like to play as, or they can be randomised as suggested in part 4 of Setup.

describes one or more abilities that let you override the usual rules of the game in unique ways.
The badge shows that Judge’s base strength, replacing the default 6.
