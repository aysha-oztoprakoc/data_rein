INTRODUCTiON
Welcome to Grimsbury is a tabletop roleplaying game set in the fictional English region of Grimsbury during the postwar 
1950s up to the 1990s. Players take the roles of visitors to the Grimsbury region, investigating occult activity, errant 
scientific experimentation and otherworldly encounters. With a backdrop of folk horror and real Magick, the players 
must work to survive the forces arrayed against them.
Fans of The Wicker Man, Dr. Who, Children of the Stones and Folk Horror Hauntology will feel right at home in 
Grimsbury. It might be the Go-Go 60’s in “Swinging London” but out in Grimsbury things are odder, more sinister and the 
mystery is waiting to be discovered. Create a character using the 20 supplied Archetypes. Or create your own Archetype by 
customising your Skills, Talents and Abilities. Explore unknown places, investigate, encounter, fight and try to survive 
uncanny forces and beings from HERE and (THERE)
TO PLAY
You need this Rulebook, a Counselor to guide the story and Players with open minds and the willingness to trust each 
other around the table as they delve into the world of Grimsbury.  And you need a pile of six sided dice (D6), paper and 
pencils (with erasers) to keep track of the game.
This Second Edition Player’s Guide contains the essentials needed to equip both Players and Counselors for the world 
of Grimsbury. This book contains the following:
• 
Character Creation rules including 20 Archetypes, 12 Skills, 40+ Talents and character life-path mechanics.
• 
Rules and mechanics for Magick, Combat, Damage, Stress & Panic.
• 
Grimsbury Town and Region details including locations, personalities and a description of another realm (THERE) 
• 
Regional Map with various landmarks called out
• 
Blank Character Sheets and a sample character illustrating how to build your own characters.
• 
A curated soundtrack to help set the mood

Ancient Albion Magick & Folk Horror
In Grimsbury, Magick is real and the old 
ways are still followed. There are whispered 
rumours of legendary spirits appearing 
amid full moon rites. Annual harvest 
celebrations take on a menacing air as 
masked residents prowl the streets. Summoners call to 
spirits and summon them forth to do their bidding.
Ley Lines, Gates & Magickal Locations
Geomancy - magickal earth energy - flows 
through Ley Lines around Grimsbury - a 
major nexus for this power. Those that can 
harness this energy can transport 
themselves to other locations and other 
dimensions. And the standing stones are more than 
mossy echoes of the past - they whisper secrets and grant 
power to those that listen.
Here & (THERE)
The barriers between this reality HERE and a 
stranger realm (THERE) have rubbed thin 
over the millennia. This had led to overlap 
and bleed-through between the two realms. 
(THERE) is populated by powerful entities 
and otherworldly creatures driven by their own goals and 
morality outside of our understanding.
Errant Scientific Experimentation
The first domestic UK nuclear power plant 
went online in 1957; right down the river 
from Grimsbury. The scientists from 
Germany, Russia and the U.S. who helped 
to design and built this Facility have a new  
source of incredible raw power to experiment with. The 
mice were teleported but where did they end up?
Wyrd Personalities
Grimsbury is a crossroads for odd characters 
from Here and (THERE). Walk into any pub 
or down side streets and you will surely 
encounter one of these personalities. Some 
might have a piece of the puzzle or at least a 
nudge towards the next destination as players 
attempt to explore and survive Grimsbury.
Secrets & Lies
Grimsbury is full of factions and groups 
that have their own goals and morality. 
These groups might assist or hinder the 
players depending on the circumstances. 
Don’t expect to get the full truth from 
anybody and sometimes the goals of the players fit into 
greater plans in unforeseen ways.

CHARACTER CREATiON
Your character is your alter-ego in the world of Grimsbury. These facets help you define, customize and personalize your 
character to be exactly who you want them to be:
AGE
The age of your character determines starting Ability Scores, Skill and Luck Points and Financial Level bonus. The older 
you are - the more skills you have but age has probably dulled some of your abilities and you know you burned a few Luck 
Points by your 50’s.  
Ability Points are spent on the four Ability Scores, Mysticism for Archetypes that have access to Magick and can raise 
your starting Financial Level.
Skill Points are spent to learn Skills.
NOTE: For a “crunchier” version of character crea
on use the Hardcore Ability & Skill Point columns. This keeps your characters a bit closer to 
the “normal” side of things and less “heroic”.
ABiLiTY SCORES
Your character’s strengths and weaknesses are described with the five Ability scores below - each has a value between 1 
and 10. During character creation, assign no less than 1 and no more than 5 points to any Ability Score. NOTE: Depending 
on your character Archetype - Mysticism might not be available.
STRENGTH (STR)
Raw muscle power, lifting, close-quarters fighting, speed, climbing and endurance.
AGILITY (AGI)
Aiming, shooting, throwing, fine motor skills, sneaking, making and crafting.
KEN (KEN)
Historical knowledge, specialised knowledge, detection, perception, mental will & grit.
CONNECT (CNX)
Ability to connect with and manipulate others, Self-Awareness. Healing.
MYSTICISM (MYS) Not available for all Archetypes
Ability to understand and harness Magickal power. Not all Archetypes have this Ability.

AGENT
An agent might work for an obscure government agency 
like the Chimera Taskforce or a Faction - as someone who 
“gets things done”. 
Could be a planted employee in the bank or The 
Grimsbury Facility operating under deep cover. Might be 
a sleeper agent waiting to be activated by a certain 
codeword. They know their mission and are honour and 
duty bound to execute.
Agents know about (THERE) and are aware of the 
otherworldly entities that walk among us - however 
Agents do not practice Magick.
Typical Activities: Gathering intelligence, Trade-craft, 
Surveillance,  investigation, Documentation, Acquisition, 
Nullification. Infiltrate the cult. Sabotage the device, 
plant the bomb, kidnap the target, eliminate the threat, 
send a message, deliver the package.
Ability Score Adjustment: CNX +1, AGI +1, LUCK -1
Skill Adjustments: Fight-Ranged +1, Detect / Observe 
+1, Sneak +1
Possible Flaws: Has a nemesis who seeks to thwart their 
efforts. Impulsive, might act without thinking about the 
consequences. Made enemies who plot their revenge. 
Identity loss, the mission is their identity - what happens when the mission is over? Amoral - sacrificing 100 souls to save 
1,000 is a good outcome.

checks. This includes vocal mimicry.
Unphased  Stress Checks are made with a 2D bonus
Under the Wire  You can get into places others can’t. Add 2D bonus to Infiltration or Bugging activities.

ARCHAEOLOGiST
The Archaeologist has a background in academic 
research as well as field “dig” experience. They can 
examine ancient artifacts, scrolls, rubbings and obscure 
writings to attempt to piece together past civilizations 
and their practices. With this knowledge in hand they 
can go onsite and conduct the fieldwork necessary to 
unearth the past.
Sometimes fieldwork requires subterfuge and a certain 
amount of grit and scrappiness. Getting permits, access 
and permission from governments and landowners is 
always tricky. Archaeologists have a wide global network 
of colleagues, competitors, diggers, fixers and logistics 
experts to call on. This network might include forgers, 
fences, smugglers and mercenaries. But to the victor go 
the spoils… and the professional credit and reputation.
Typical Activities: Historical research, ancient 
languages, cartography, land surveying, brokering, 
fencing, smuggling, organizing the dig, identifying 
artifacts, invoking the power of the artifact, publishing 
the article.
Ability Score Adjustment: KEN +2
Skill Adjustments: Detect +1, Focus - History +1, Focus - 
Archaeology +1
Possible Flaws: Most Archaeologists believe in the higher goal of advancing human knowledge - but just barely.  Gaining 
the professional glory of unlocking an unknown part of human history or discovering a treasure laden tomb is always 
calling the Archaeologist out of the library and into the field. An Archaeologist might be greedy or covetous or have a 
rival. They also might be cursed from a past encounter. They might hate the field and want to be shut in the library. They 
might sell part of what they find on the black market to fund their lifestyle.
Archaeologist Talents
Obscure Reference - Once per day the Archaeologist can glean a piece of relevant information from some historical 
relic, writing or object as fits the story-line.
Boots On The Ground    The Archaeologist is versed in intense 
fieldwork such as organizing Digs, Survey Trips and Relic Hunting, etc. 
Speaks the local language - Easy to find a foreman, workers, permits, etc.
Bookworm   The Archaeologist gets a 2D bonus when looking for 
specific information within a relevant library or data store. If the 
information isn’t there to begin with this talent won’t make it appear.
What is this thing?   Has studied thousands of different relics, their 
history and usage. Receives 2D bonus on all checks to identify an object.
Exact Replica   Knows how to use ancient materials - or make newer 
materials look ancient - to create exact replicas of items such as tablets, 
scrolls, weapons, etc.

ARiSTOCRAT
You are from old money - land and horses - or new 
money - banking and industry. You have connections, 
assets and wealth at your disposal. Or maybe it’s all a 
front and you’re a bankrupt gambler having borrowed 
and leveraged everything you own. You’re an Heiress, a 
Scion of Industry, an Art Collector, a Curator of Sensate 
Experiences, a Global Explorer or a Huntsman.  Or a total 
poseur. Regardless, give yourself a title and a manor 
home.
Typical Activities: Buy the meals, charter the flight or 
bribe the front desk. Use your wealth, power and 
influence to help those around you. Being able to drive 
sports cars, ride horses and shoot sporting clays is also 
helpful if you get into a situation.
Ability Score Adjustment: Starting Finance Level 3, 
CNX -2
Skill Adjustments: Focus {you choose} +1, Fight-Ranged 
+1, Read -1
Possible Flaws: Sociopath - indifferent to the needs of 
others. Greedy, snobbish - unaware of the plight of the 
“working man”. Awkwardly Extroverted - always buying a 
“round for the pub”. Massively in debt to someone or 
something - this facade is all a sham.
Aristocrat Talents
We Served Together   (see Politician Talents)

Racer  You collect fast automobiles and race them around the circuit. You have excellent driving skills giving you a 2D 
bonus on any driving tricks. Never need to check on regular fast driving.
Cultured  Gain 2D bonus on any checks related to art, literature, music, theater, poetry etc.

ARMY GRUNT - SOLDiER / 
VETERAN
A Grunt is a soldier who does their duty - and beyond - 
under the most adverse situations. Soldiers tackle tough 
tasks and can cover long distances carrying heavy loads. 
They can wait patiently for days and weeks before being 
called into explosive action. 
A Veteran is someone who has seen the front lines, 
experienced the chaos and terror of war and come back 
to everyday life still carrying those experiences. Your 
character might be a veteran of the first or second World 
Wars. Or you might have been stationed in Northern 
Ireland during “the troubles”.
Typical activities: Scouting a location, patrolling an 
area, leading a group through the unknown, raiding a 
base, fighting with firearms, heavy weapons and 
explosives. Waiting for orders and following them.
Ability Score Adjustment:  STR +2, KEN -1
Skill Adjustments: Endurance +1, Fight-Close +1, Fight-
Ranged +1, Willpower +1
Possible Flaws: Dishonorable discharge for fighting - a 
hothead. PTSD might paralyze this character if they 
encounter triggering situations or stimuli - real or 
imagined.
Army Grunt Talents
Reloading!   Reloading a firearm only takes a Minor Action not a Major Action.
Snapshot   Use a minor action to shoot a firearm a second time during a combat turn. No bonus dice applied. 2D penalty 
to the roll.
Knife Thrower (REQ: Fight-Ranged 2+) Range for thrown knives is doubled and gets a 2D bonus to hit. 
Iron fist  First unarmed strike in any encounter will do double damage
Medic!   Apply first Aid with a 2D bonus. Requires: Heal / Console > 0
Sharpshooter  (REQ: Fight-Ranged 2+, Finesse 1+) Trained as a sniper. Range is doubled. Add 2D bonus to hit with a 
scoped rifle. Gun damage is doubled plus add your Fight-Ranged skill levels to damage.  You can aim for specific targets 
such as a head or a held weapon or the lock on a door or anything you can see in your scope.

BURGLAR
The Burglar is the sneaky trickster. They might be a 
traditional thief - stealing a relic from a locked bedroom 
safe. Or they might look like everybody else and slip into 
an office to grab sensitive files before stepping out for a 
smoke. 
They might shimmy up a tree to silently slip into a 
hospital window or they might pull up in a Postal Van to 
deliver a package that expels sleep gas. They might plan 
and execute their capers with precision or just bop you on 
your head - take your keys - and help themselves. Same 
outcome - different methods.
Typical Activities: Case the spot, create an infiltration 
plan, set up a distraction, sneak in, grab the goods and 
get out. Use misdirection and disguise. Change 
accounting records, copy computer files, hack into 
security systems, pick locks, crack safes, use a disguise 
kit.
Ability Score Adjustment: AGI +1, CNX -1, LUCK +1
Skill Adjustments: Sneak +2
Possible Flaws: Covetous for a certain type of item like 
stamp collections, antique firearms, timepieces or gems. 
Pursuing these items might cloud their judgment. Might 
be compelled to leave a “tell” to mark their work. Might 
have a police record, a cop walking the beat might 
recognize them. Might have stolen from an entity and have a bounty on their head.
Burglar Talents
Nimble Fingers  (REQ: Finesse 2+) With minimal tools you can pick almost any lock made by human hands. Get a 3D 
bonus on related checks. 
Safecracker Your experience has taught you how to listen to lock mechanisms, hear tumblers falling and recognize other 
signs to help you open almost any kind of safe. Get a 3D bonus on related checks. Requires: FINESSE 2+
Get Away Clean Pursuers have a -2D penalty if they try tracking, spotting or following you as you escape.
Chameleon  You blend in wherever you go, others get a 2D penalty if actively attempting to spot you in a group setting, 
2D bonus if attempting to hide for relevant checks.
Know a Guy  You have a network of scrappy connections that can hook you up with almost anything. Put the word out 
and something will happen within 2-48 hours.
Wheelman  You get a +2D bonus on any Driving checks. Once per day you can hotwire any automobile .

COMMANDO ±
Commandos are blunt objects used against the wyrd. 
They started out as Soldiers / Grunts but were singled out 
for training in the occult and supernatural combat. They 
graduated from regular combat missions and were 
assigned to an obscure Ministry of Defense Agency.
They were trained in the use of arcane weapons that 
combine the kinetic force of regular arms with Magickal 
effects. They are conditioned against exposure to the odd 
and unsettling. They have high Stress Points and stay 
frosty while battling the most otherworldly creatures.
Typical Activities: Magickal ambush, search & destroy, 
plant the arcane device and arm it. Provide covering fire. 
Guard the escape. Create and use improvised Magickal 
weapons and explosives. Gate destruction.
Ability Score Adjustment:  Magick Allowed. MYS +1, 
STR +1, LUCK -2, CNX -2 , Stress Points +2
Skill Adjustments: Fight-Close +1, Fight-Ranged  +1, 
Focus-Explosives +1. Choose a Magickal Focus Area - 
Level 1
Possible Flaws: Cowboy - solo operator. Past conflicts 
might have led to a Hex or Curse that needs to be 
removed. Might have PTSD that affects them in certain 
situations. Nihilistic, prone to self-sacrifice. Unable to 
maintain relationships - few family or personal ties. Zealot.
Commando Talents
Boomsauce  Add 2D bonus to any activity that has to do with explosives or demolition.
Berserker  1D6 times per day the character can do two attacks per turn until primary target is killed.
Unphased   Stress Checks against the Odd are made with a 2D bonus
Tactical  You can size up a situation and get a 2D bonus for checks related to setting an ambush or preparing your squad 
with instructions
Stay Frosty   Your cool presence gives you and your allies a bonus for Stress Checks and Panic Effect rolls. 6 Bonus Dice 
total can be distributed per day.
Sacrificial Lamb   You wear an explosive vest under your uniform that can destroy anything (including you) within 30 
feet. Some missions you don’t come back from.

HEDGE WiTCH/WARLOCK ±
A Hedge Witch or Warlock is a Magick practitioner who is self 
taught or learned informally from another Magick user. They 
are not affiliated with an established coven. They work alone or 
with a handful of friends. They usually have strong natural 
talent that bubbles up during moments of extreme stress or 
emotion. They are powerful but can lose control. Usually 
descended from a powerful Witch or Warlock hiding 
somewhere in their family tree. The term “Gutter Mage” is a 
demeaning term - fighting words from a condescending 
Magick user.
Typical Activities: Day to day Magick. Cure a hangover or 
shoplift a new outfit. Resurrect a pet dog or heal a sprained 
ankle.
Ability Score Adjustment: Magick allowed. MYS +1, Stress 
Points -1
Skill Adjustments: Magick - Prestidigitation +1, Magick - 
Illusion / Disguise +1. Choose 2 areas of Magickal Focus at level 
1 each.
Possible Flaws: Established Covens do not like Hedge 
Witches in their territory or hanging around. They will use 
magickal annoyances to move them along. Possibly on the run 
from a coven. Possibly angered someone or a Faction - has an 
enemy. Possible Addiction, or Mental Health Issues, Abusive 
upbringing. Selfish or Narcissistic.
Hedge Witch/Warlock Talents
Spell Slinger  Knows offensive ranged spell attacks. Can add 
Fight-Ranged skill level to MAGICK CHECKS where 
appropriate.
Boosted!  Can trade a HEALTH POINT for increased Magickal 
effect. 
Shimmerslip  Once a day - as a Bonus Action - they can slide 
between dimensions) with anybody or  anything they are 
touching and instantly reappear in a spot they can see up to 100 
feet away .
Tapped In  Can absorb Magickal energy from Ley Lines, 
Objects, Magickal creatures or individuals to recharge Magick 
Points.

making up 12 Core Skills. Your character acquires skills by purchasing them using Skill Points during character creation.

Leveling Up Skills
Skills improve over time. The more your character uses a particular skill the faster they can level up. Track skill usage 
during play sessions - depending on the outcome of the Skill Check a player will earn 1 or 2 usage points.
•

from a level 1 skill to a level 2 skill requires 20 USAGE POINTS. Once a new skill level is attained - the USAGE POINTS for

FOLK MAGiCK
Folk Magick - like its name is for everyone - all who believe may practice it. 
There is no requirement to have MYS points to practice Folk Magick
Folk Magic (AKA root work, granny magic, low magic) is older than the “High Magick” practiced by Witches & Sorcerers 
and “powered” differently. Unlike Witches & Sorcerers who are imbued with their Magickal power - Folk Magick is not 
inherent in an individual but is invoked through a pact or relationship with a source. A source can be a Spirit, a Place or a 
Belief and has the Magickal power - the character is simply requesting a small “channeling” of that power for their needs.
These pacts or relationships between characters and their “sources” might have been formed in childhood or they might 
have been earned by dedicating time and effort to honor a spirit or a place.  The character has gifted or sacrificed 
something to their source and has earned the RIGHT to call upon the source for aid. Maybe the character 
composed a song or a poem about a certain stone circle. Or the character gives blood sacrifice to a long-forgotten deity. 
They might hold an ongoing belief or have a family relationship to their source. 
Folk Magick usage is limited to D3 times a month.  It requires something related to their Magick source - a charm to use 
when they call upon their source. A pebble from a path, a stone from a cairn atop a hill, a vial of water from a river, a plant 
root from a field, a pine cone from a forest, a winged maple seed “helicopter”. 
During character creation the player should create a Folk Magick Source this is important to the character and likely a 
part of their backstory. Then decide on how this source was activated - describe how the character gave a Gift or Sacrifice 
and what is the resulting Type of Folk Magick that the character can access using their particular Charm item. 
Following are suggestions for Folk Magick Sources - Gifts and Charms:
Using Folk Magick
Folk Magick is more ephemeral than High Magick and sometimes an attempt to use it will just fail. Each use of Folk 
Magick must be rolled to see if it is successful, a single Success (6) means it worked otherwise it fails.

Types of Folk Magick
Blessing - you can pull power from your Source and give 
yourself or someone you see a +1D bonus on their next 
check or attack in Combat. You must verbally 
acknowledge your source and how you use this Blessing.
Healing - you can pull power from your Source and heal 
yourself or someone you touch 1-3 Health Points.
Protection - you can use your Source to give a +1D 
penalty to an attacker and reduce damage taken by 1-3 
Points. Usable on one attack.
Concealment - you can use your Source to give a +2D 
bonus to your next Sneak check.
Pointing the Way - your Source can subtly guide you 
towards a destination. State out loud where you are trying 
to go and you will get a sign indicating the way to go. A 
breeze, a bird, a fox, a gentle tug in the right direction.
Working with Animals - you can calm animals and give 
them simple commands like GO, STAY, BARK, QUIET. 
The number of Successes rolled will be the duration in 
hours. 
Finding Food or Water - you know which Fungi are 
edible,where to find springs & game trails.  The number 
of Successes rolled will be the duration in hours. 
Gleaning Knowledge - you can apply this to a place or a 
thing and learn about recent visitors to a place or 
historical events. You can learn how an object was created 
and by whom.
Influence - you can convince someone of something 
you’d like them to believe. You can cover a lie, make them 
have a crush on someone. The number of Successes rolled 
will be the duration in hours. 
Banes
For each of these longer duration effects the caster needs 
a lock of hair, a vial of blood, a precious item or 
something relating to the targeted individual to make 
these work:
Jinx is a small bane imposed on a person - perhaps they 
are always losing their keys or cannot remember a certain 
individual’s name. This might last a week at most. There 
is a 50/50 chance this will result in a Black Mark.
Hex is more powerful than a jinx and has deeper 
consequences - the end of a marriage, the loss of a job, 
etc. This will give the character a Black Mark
automatically and will prevent the character from calling 
on their source for three months.
Curse is the most powerful manifestation of dark Folk 
Magick. A deadly illness, the death of a loved one, the 
inability to speak or see. This will require a blood sacrifice 
and give the character two Black Marks automatically. 
Giving a curse will prevent the character from calling on 
their source for a year.
Other kinds of Dark Folk Magick
Confusion - you can cloud the memory of an event, offer 
up wrong directions or instructions. The target will ask 
clarifying questions and you get 2D bonus at Manipulate
skill checks. The number of successes on the roll is the 
number of hours this lasts.
Fear - the target becomes irrationally frightened of a 
person or a place or a situation and they will seek to flee 
and avoid the subject of their fear. The number of 
successes on the roll is the number of hours this lasts.
Loathing / Indifference - you can make the target 
dislike or feel indifferent about something or someone 
that they previously held dear. The number of successes 
on the roll is the number of hours this lasts.
Harm - you can touch someone and they will get one of 
the following: Cramps Headache, Burning & Itching All 
Over, Diarrhea, Hair Falling Out, Constipation, Flu.The 
character can decide when the subject will feel the effects 
- instantaneously or up to 1 hour in the future.
The Dark Side of Folk Magick
Folk Magick has a dark side and can be used to harm or influence people. Instantaneous results are possible and longer 
duration effects like jinxes, hexes and curses can be conveyed. Using this kind of Folk Magick too often can result in a 
Black Mark and potentially keep the Character from accessing the other more benign uses of Folk Magick. Each time 
your character uses Dark Folk Magick roll a Stress Check to see how this affects them.
If your character succeeds at their Folk Magick roll they have a 50/50 chance of earning a black mark.

HEALTH POiNTS    HEALTH POINTS = STR + AGI + ENDURANCE Skill
Your character might fall off a wall or be hit by a thrown ax or be punched in the face by an automaton - resulting in an 
injury. Health Points record this physical damage. Starting Health Points are the sum of your STRENGTH + AGILITY + 
ENDURANCE skill. When a character takes physical damage it will first be subtracted from Health Points. Once Health 
Points reach 0 any additional damage is subtracted from the STRENGTH or AGILITY ability scores. (See DAMAGE rules)
STRESS POiNTS    STRESS POINTS = KEN + CNX + WILLPOWER Skill
Similar to Health Points - Stress Points represent the ability of your character to resist the uncanny, the horrific  and the 
unknown that they encounter in Grimsbury. It is related to mental and emotional damage that can occur during 
encounters in this world. Your starting Stress Points are the sum of your KEN + CONNECT + WILLPOWER Skill scores. 
When a character takes mental, emotional or stress damage it will be first subtracted from Stress Points. Once Stress 
Points are reduced to 0 - additional damage will be applied to KEN + CONNECT ability scores. 
REPUTATION  This is an optional mechanic for the game.
Witnesses, survivors and bystanders talk. Always assume people will gossip and share what they saw. If your character’s 
actions are observed they will gain a Reputation recorded as Black Marks and Gold Stars.
Reputation might impact interactions with NPCs. If the character is attempting to  Manipulate, Read, Persuade, 
Investigate or Question an NPC or Personality, the Counselor should look at Black Marks or Gold Stars relevant to that 
interaction and assign a penalty or bonus to any checks made.
As your character builds a reputation - either good or bad- they gain allies that are aligned to their methods or enemies 
that are opposed to their actions. Your character might broaden their influence and power within a faction or a region. 
They might get into politics or start a new faction with their own followers. Start a cult! Start a yoga camp! 
EXAMPLE: Lilith recently exposed a network of crooked barristers and police - this earned her a Gold Star and advantage on checks 
when dealing with other like-minded folks. If she encountered someone  associated with that crooked group - that same Gold Star 
would give her a penalty on any checks.
Black Marks
When you do bad things like harm people, spread rumours, torture or instigate bad situations, your character earns Black 
Marks. Here’s what might happen when you earn too many Black Marks:
• 
1 Black Mark - Think about what happened. Is that the path you want to take? Personal crisis.
• 
2-3 Black Marks - Close relations might confront or shun you. Police might be aware of your actions.
• 
4-5 Black Marks - Shunned by many. Gain attention from an aligned faction or individuals.

6-7 Black Marks - Gain followers. Possibly on the radar of Factions like Police, Chimera Group, The Conclave, The 
White Lodge, etc. Black Lodge candidate - they might reach out. Possible bounty on your head.
• 
8-10 Black Marks - Dark reputation, Black Lodge member, followers willing to die for your goals. Large Bounty of 
your head from a Faction.
Gold Stars
When you do good things and help people, creatures or situations become better your character can earn Gold Stars. 
Here’s what might happen if a character is on the path to earning Gold Stars.

LUCK
Luck points represent the fickle hand of fate or fae or the divine intervening in your life and giving you a brief reprieve 
before your glorious end. It’s the book in your pocket that absorbs a bullet, the prickly feeling you get right before you 
open a door, blowing on the dice and rolling boxcars.
Luck Checks
Luck can be used by the Counselor for a variety of things. 
Can you get a cab? Does the dress come in red? Does the 
library have a certain book? You don’t always have to roll for 
these things but if you need to - make a Luck Check. Roll a 
pool of D6 equal to the number of Luck Points you have - a 
success means things work out your way.
Burning Luck
A player can “Burn Luck” to attempt to turn the tides of 
fate… to make that fingertip grab of the final rung on the 
ladder as you fall. Burning Luck has its downside. Each 
time Luck is Burned the player must roll a Stress Test.
Luck can be burned in the following ways:
Re-rolls  You can burn a luck point for a complete re-roll of 
a Skill check, a Combat attack, a Magick check or any other 
roll. Even if the first check was pushed. Luck trumps all. 
You can burn luck anytime you want in the game to affect 
the present.
Retcon / Flashbacks  Luck can be burned if you want to 
retcon a past action that will affect the current situation. 
Like a flashback you say what you did and how it happened: 
“I hired a cab to wait for us outside the museum at 6pm” or 
“I remembered to pack my shortwave radio set” or “I spent Sunday afternoon researching that topic at the library”. The 
Counselor will need to adjust the present situation based on that new information.
Folk Magick  You can burn a Luck point to use an additional Folk Magick action - it will succeed automatically - roll for 
effect.
Regaining Luck
Luck points can be added by spending Character Points awarded by the Counselor. Luck points organically replenish over 
time - refilling over the course of a year of in-game time. And they will reset at the end of a specific adventure. So be 
careful of when and how you use them.

ROLLiNG SKiLL CHECKS
Sometimes your character needs to do something out of 
the ordinary. To determine if the action succeeds or fails 
the player rolls a skill check. It is up to the Counselor to 
determine which actions require a rolled check. Typically 
checks are made when the character is attempting 
something beyond the realm of “normal activity”. Rolling 
a Skill Check involves assembling and rolling a Dice 
Pool of D6. Depending on the difficulty of the check it 
might require a single six or multiple sixes to be a Success. 
The more sixes rolled - the higher the effect. No sixes 
rolled in the pool is a Failure.
Success has varying degrees. Rolling 4+ sixes is a Critical 
Success and has a more intense effect. Example effects are 
provided for each skill but these should be seen as guides 
and suggestions - not fast and firm rules.
NOTE: To keep the game flowing - it’s important not to 
slow things down with unnecessary checks. Assume 
characters can do normal things and only check if their 
ac
on is beyond normal ac
vi
es.
Who rolls? Player or Counsellor?
Usually the Player will roll Skill Checks for their Character 
actions - especially if the outcome is immediately known. 
EX: Jumping between rooftops, defusing a bomb, digging 
out an artifact, etc.  If the roll doesn’t succeed you know 
you failed - you fell - the bomb went off - a tool broke.
However - the Counsellor can choose to roll checks when 
the character is trying something where the result is not 
immediately apparent:
EX: You attempt to Manipulate someone by lying to 
them, the Counsellor rolls for you - and you fail - however 
the subject might not immediately react. So you THINK 
you lied successfully.  You and your character do not know 
the outcome.
EX: You attempt to use Finesse  and Focus-Art to create 
a forged painting by a well-known artist. The Counselor 
rolls for you and you fail - the forgery looks fine to you but 
you never know the outcome of the roll until you try to 
sell it.
NOTE: Different Game Runners / Counselors handle this 
in different ways - some allow their players to make all 
of their own rolls but some

You Can Only Check Once
Your character has only one chance to perform their 
action - you can’t fail and then try again and again until 
you succeed. However, if you fail your initial rolled check 
you can Push Your Roll to re-roll with the added risk of a 
possible consequence (described below). You can also 
Burn Luck to get a full re-roll (described below).
Degree of Difficulty
Checks start with the Counselor indicating the Degree of 
Difficulty for a Success. If the attempted action is way 
beyond the character’s expected performance then 
Succeeding will require a higher number of Successes (6s) 
in the roll. 
Group Skill Checks
If a group of characters are trying the same action at the 
same time then they can try to succeed as a group. There 
might be a total number of Successes needed by the group 
to pass - individual group members might fail the roll but 
as long as the group hits the target number of Successes 
then everybody passes.
Storytelling the Skill Use
As your character is performing their action - the player 
should describe HOW they are using their Abilities and 
Skills. What does it look like? Use your character’s 
personality and abilities to add color to the narrative.

In Grimsbury, Magick is real but has been obscured and 
eclipsed by science and commerce. As human activity has 
become more disconnected from agriculture and the seasons 
- and more focused on capitalist mechanistic productivity - 
we’ve lost those ties to primal geomantic, mystical forces.
Many still hold onto secret Magickal knowledge. Arcane texts 
are taught and passed on Sorcerer to Sorcerer. Covens meet in 
forest clearings and church basements and  entities from 
(THERE) share some of their knowledge to worthy folks from 
HERE.
Magickal power has many sources such as the earth-bound 
Ley Lines of Geomancy, the celestial residue of the Old Gods, 
the inherited power of ancestors, Druidic practices and the 
Divine. Following are the cornerstones of the Grimsbury 
Magick System.
Does the Public Know About Magick?
The public in cities and urban settings think of Magick as 
Folk Magick - something old and quaint. Faeiries, little 
people, fortune-tellers, love potions and the like. They 
have no awareness of the ongoing Magickal battle 
between the Light and the Dark. They are quick to explain 
away Magickal events with “science” or “atmospheric 
weather effects” or “I was a bit tipsy”
Magick Is Real
The practical use of Magick is more expansive than spells. 
It can involve ritual, sacrifice, chemistry, celebration, 
performance (singing & dancing), gathering, geomancy 
and other aspects besides just “spell slinging”.  Magick 
manifests real power but how it works is where things get 
interesting. 
Magick Is Not Clearly Defined
Magick encompasses the use of Magickal items and 
performing Magickal actions or rituals.  To use Magick - 
tell the Counselor what you are trying to do. If you are 
casting a spell or setting up a summoning ritual - explain 
how that happens, what it looks like - and what resulting 
effect you would like to happen. If you are conducting a 
ritual - explain where you are doing the ritual and what 
material components you are using. Explain the result of 
your ritual Magick. Then discuss how many Magick 
Points you want to spend on this action and the scope of 
the effect. This is a chance to introduce amazing, 
cinematic moments to the gameplay - details matter.
Magickal Actions Should Fit The Character
Spells should make sense for the character archetype and 
their magickal focus areas. You can’t expect a Potion 
Maker to be able to do Gate Magick - at least not very 
well. There are primary areas of Magickal Focus but don’t 
let them limit you. If you want your character to be versed 
in another type of Magick then work with your Counsellor 
to make it happen. This system doesn’t define specific 
spells or rituals - feel free to bring your own ideas or spells 
from other systems but the Magickal actions need to 
make sense for the character. 
The Environment Affects Magick
If the Moon and Sun and Stars are in alignment. If the Ley 
Lines are surging and you are in close proximity. If you are 
using an established circle or gate you might not need to 
spend as many Magick Points on your action. Most times 
this will not be the case but if you are waiting for the right 
moon cycle to work a  Magickal ritual or if you travel to 
the right spot where Ley lines converge you might get that 
bonus. Timing and location can add a lot to a Magickal 
action.
NOTE: When using Magick, the Counselor should ask 
"What do you want to do? How much do you want to 
spend and risk to do it?" The Counselor will have to 
develop the “scale” of point cost vs Magickal effect with 
the players. And try to keep some level of consistency.

Using Magick Burns Magick Points
Magick points power your Magickal actions. Each Magick Point spent on a Magickal Action adds a D6 to your dice pool 
when you roll for Magickal Effect. As you use your Magick Points - remember to mark them off your character sheet. 
MAGICK POINTS = KEN + MYS
Magickal Effect Rolls
A Magickal Effect Roll determines if your minor earthquake actually hit the intended building or veer the other way and 
hit a house? Either way - your character created an earthquake and you roll to see how it works out. Magick Effect Rolls 
are similar to Skill Checks - grab the number of dice equal to your MYS ability score. Then add dice for the relevant 
Magick Skill Level. Then add in dice equal to the number of Magick Points you are spending on this action - these should 
be a different color in your dice pool. Once that dice pool is assembled - roll away. 
MAGICK EFFECT DICE POOL = MYS + MAGICK SKILL+ MAGICK POINTS SPENT
                                                                                       |  1s = Consequence   |
You roll for the effectiveness of your Magickal action. 
1s don’t matter unless you rolled on Magick Point dice - then your character has a Consequence. 
6s on your Magick Roll add more effect.
A roll with no Success (6) is not a failure. The Magickal Action worked as intended but no bonus effect. 
A roll with one or more Success (6) means the Magickal Action had a stronger impact on its intended target. The more 
successes in the roll - the stronger the effect - the effect aligns more closely with your intentions..
A roll with a Bane (1) on Magick Point Dice results in a Consequence. Each time Magick is used there is a chance the 
character invokes a Consequence. Magick is powerful but can lead to a grim consequence for your character.  
No Magick Left
When your character uses their last Magick Point that takes them to zero - they will be fatigued for 2D6 minutes and will 
have a 2D penalty to all skill checks and attacks. However a character with 0 Magick Points can still use Magick. For each 
Magick Point they want to “borrow” they have a 1 in 6 chance of dying within 24 hours. Want to “borrow” 3 Magick 
Points? That’s a 50/50 chance of dying. This roll should be shared only with the Counselor and player. This death should 
be cinematic and amazing. 
Regaining Magick
Only rest and meditation can help a character regain their Magick Points. Rest is like a sick day - staying home, in bed, 
reading, studying, meditating. Rest is not adventuring or exploring or visiting with others. Be mindful of how and when 
you spend your Magick Points - they take time to regenerate. One day of rest restores 1 Magick Point. Certain Talents, 
items and abilities can enable faster regeneration or the ability to absorb Magickal energy from other sources.
Acquiring Mysticism
Even though your character archetype might not allow you to create your character with MYSTICISM - over time, with 
exposure to the odd and other personalities that might help - you can see if this your character was able to tap into 
MAGICK and earn a point of MYSTICISM. This would be a full-time focus over the 2 year period. Work with the 
Counsellor on how this might turn out.

MAGiCKAL FOCUS AREAS
When a character takes a level of Mysticism they can choose an area 
of Magickal Focus. It is possible to have multiple areas of Magickal 
Focus - a character could have Enchant Items 1 and Brew Potions 1
as unique specializations. Or a character could have deep focus in just 
a single area such as Summoning 3. 
Following describes the areas of Focus as well as how each can be 
used by a Character in the game. 
Some Magick can be wielded on the spot - “Spell Slinging” in the 
traditional Wizard sense. Some Magick requires a ritual to invoke its 
power. Ritual Magick is stronger but takes more time, more 
forethought and relevant components that are consumed by the 
ritual. Those components are sometimes hard to find or procure. And 
sometimes Ritual Magick requires a sacrifice such as a prized 
possession, a relationship, blood or a life. These details are up to the 
Counselor and player to work through.

chemist, distiller using Magick as a catalyst. The resulting potions are 
bottled effects that can heal, harm and transform. These require 
ingredients, process and time. The resulting material might work as 
intended or do something completely different -  drink and find out! 
The process involves sourcing and preparation of ingredients,

Dimensional Travel / Gates
Identify, create and use gates to travel quickly between 
places and realms. Summon brief gates that wink open 
then shut behind you. When invoked as a ritual the 
character can create permanent gates between two points. 
With more Magick, gates stay can open longer, are more 
protected and hidden and a character can alter gate entry 
“permissions” or even permanently seal gates shut.
Do Harm
Cause damage to someone or something. Identify the 
target, say the kind of Harm you are doing and how it 
affects them. Depending on the kind of entity you are 
targeting you will want to do different kinds of damage - 
disease, rot, wasting. Roll the number of D6 you spend on 
Magick Points and do that amount of damage.
In the field, a character must touch the target. To Do 
Harm from a distance, the character conducts a Ritual to 
transmit the Magickal Harm effect. This requires one of 
their possessions and some part of them like a baby tooth 
or hair or nail clippings. A character that uses Do Harm 
extensively could possibly end up with some long-term  
consequences and Black Marks on their reputation.
Enchant Items
This focus allows a Magick user to place a Magickal effect 
on an object. The resulting item will hold a Magickal 
effect, spell or some other Magickal aspect. This involves 
an uninterrupted multi-stage ritual with the item to be 
enchanted. Must have a spell or Magickal source to pull 
from in order to imbue the item. Must include some 
sacrifice of something valuable to invoke permanency 
otherwise the duration of the enchantment is limited. 
Coin or Blood. The process is very costly - usually takes all 
of the Magick you have to make it a permanent effect. 
Enhance / Buff / Bless
Your presence, encouragement and coaching positively 
impact those around you. You can help them overcome 
Stress damage or to do better in the moment. For each 
Magick Point spent - roll a 1D6 to remove that much 
Stress from the target or add 1d to the target’s dice pool for 
a Skill Check or Combat roll. Can be conveyed through 
spoken words or silently through touch.
Force - Telekinesis
Use your mind and Magick to push open a door, move a 
boulder, shove a person or deliver a blow to them. Can be 
blunt force or focused energy like a force bullet. Lift the 
keys off the peg and float them into the cage. Flip the 
lever and start the countdown. 
Can be instantaneous - cast like a spell or done at a 
distance through a ritual.  The more Magick points spent 
means more weight, speed, stealth or distance can be 
used. How much do you spend for the desired outcome? 
Foresight / Portents / Visions
This skill allows the character to get brief visions of the 
past or the future based on their inquiries and 
questioning. Visions might be incomplete or without 
proper context but should be considered “clues” in the 
game narrative. Can be used “on the spot” to get brief 
glimpses of a possible future or can be invoked in a more 
lengthy, complex ritual that provides more concrete 
details. The more Magick Points spent on the check 
means you can see further into the past or future - and 
more detail is revealed. 1 Magick Point spent means you 
can see something that will happen or happened an hour

Heal Up
Heal physical damage for yourself or someone else. Might  
be like First Aid for a wound or miraculous healing like 
curing someone’s cancer. The scale of the healing depends 
on how many Magick Points you spend and risk. In the 
field you can restore STR, AGI & Health Points equal to 
1D6 for each level of Heal Up plus 1D6 for each Magick 
Point spent.
You can’t bring someone back from the dead. You can’t 
reattach a severed limb. But with an expensive ritual you 
might be able to cure lung cancer or heal up burns 
without scarring.
Illusion & Disguise
Make someone or something look like something else. 
Can be as minor as camouflaging a doorway to look like a 
wall or altering someone’s features to resemble someone 
else. A major use might hide a river or a street or an island 
from view. Can be cast like a spell or invoked through a 
ritual using an image of the desired effect. Depends on 
the scale of the Illusion and how much you spend.
The more Magick spent means the illusion is more 
believable, lasts longer and resists being dispelled.
Manipulate Fire
Manipulate or summon flame - create a campfire or spray 
flame from your hands across a building or group of 
people. Fire effects can be at a small or large scale

You are the firestarter.
Magickal & Occult Research
Academic knowledge about Magickal and Occult 
practices. Includes knowledge of Magick, Runes, Symbols,  
Items, Rituals, Residue, Manifestation and any other 
Magickal aspects.
Can be used to determine the nature of an object or what 
a symbol means. More complicated Rituals can be 
conducted to learn the exact nature of Magickal items or 
the contents of a tome in an alien language. 
Mental Control
Make someone believe something - make a city believe 
something. Make someone do something or believe and 
act on something in the moment. Or establish the

maintain control. 
Magick Points spent determine how long can you keep up

Pull water out of the air into a globe around a target’s

Lights flicker, doors slam shut, drawers open and close, 
spooky noises, phantom figures appear. Also  known as

Read Minds
Entering the minds’ eye of the subject and knowing 
exactly what they know. From grabbing their surface 
thoughts to delving deeper into their memories - the 
more you spend, the deeper you can go but the riskier it 
is for both parties. A quick scan of surface thoughts is 
easy and undetectable in the moment. Digging out 
hidden or repressed memories requires more time and 
effort and has a higher risk of detection.
Any 1s rolled by the Magick user means the subject has 
detected the intrusion and can fight the intrusion.   
Possible Stress Damage to both parties if there is a fight. 
Possible Stress Damage to the Magick User if they are 
exposed to damaging memories or visions.

individual from elsewhere into the same plane as the 
character. The summoned creature or individual is bound 
to the character for a period of time. Then they return to 
their original location. If the summoned creature is upset 
them they might stick around and muck about.

When a character is wounded by weapons or other means 
such as falling or being impaled on a graveyard fence they 
will take Damage. Damage is first subtracted from 
Health Points. Once Health Points reach zero (0)  
additional damage is applied directly to their STR and 
AGI ability scores. Damage can be split between the two 
ability scores. As STR and AGI decline your character will 
use the new, diminished values for Skill Checks and 
Combat. As your character is wounded their ability to 
perform is reduced. 
Unconsciousness & Death
If either STR or AGI reaches zero (0) - the character goes 
Unconscious. If the character does not receive first Aid 
or Stabilization within 1 hour - they will be permanently 
Dead.  If both STR and AGI reach 0 - the character is 
permanently Dead.

The Grimsbury region is known for rolling hills, ancient 
forests, karst cave systems and Grimsbury Town proper. 
The town straddles the confluence of Two Rivers that flow 
out to sea approximately 20 miles away. Circling the town 
is a set of rolling foothills containing flint nodules and 
many neolithic sites. The area was settled thousands of 
years ago by nomadic hunters who found plentiful game, 
fish and shelter in the area. Early settlers left many 
mysterious monuments, artifacts and signs on the 
landscape. It’s not uncommon to find flint chips and 
hunting points in the dirt after a strong rain.
Surrounding everything is a deep Elm, Oak and Maple 
forest. The Forest is not listed in any of the National 
Trust guides, maps or park listings - it seems to be 
overlooked when those items return from the printer.
In the 1800’s and early 1900’s there were dozens of mills 
and a thriving economy that took advantage of the free 
water power provided by the rivers. After WW2, the war-
time industrial plants tried to convert to civilian 
production but manufacturing slowed and moved to 
cheaper locations leaving Grimsbury a forgotten region.

In the mid-1950s the construction of The Grimsbury 
Facility was started several miles downstream from 
Grimsbury town. This was the first commercial nuclear 
power plant in the UK. The mines up north still sent their 
rumbling trains laden with coal down along the river but 
the tall cooling towers of the Grimsbury Facility spoke of 
real progress. The new scientists and engineers working at 
the Facility taking up temporary housing in the town are 
finding the local dialect impenetrable and the customs 
odd. What are all of these twig figures hanging off our 
trees and cars?
The invisible geography is also interesting. The Grimsbury 
region sits at the intersection of scores of Ley Lines - 
arcane geomantic energy vectors that connect standing 
stones, forgotten monuments and other sacred geography. 
GRiMSBURY TOWN
Grimsbury Town itself is unremarkable with a high street 
you might see in any English town this size. Several 
chippers, radio sales and repair, a fabric store, a few off-
brand fast-food chains, the Sainsbury’s grocer, a butcher, a 
few home goods vendors and the scattered pub make up 
the most noticeable businesses.
But slip down an alley into the quieter streets and you 
might start to feel an uncanny unease. That crawling 
feeling of being watched and observed. Looking around 
there is nothing odd to be seen - perhaps a lace curtain 
falls back across a 2nd story window or a door is quietly 
shut or a sloshing gurgle is heard from a storm drain. 
Players will encounter personalities on these side streets 
and alleys— each has a piece of the puzzle or at least a 
nudge towards the next destination as they attempt to 
explore and survive Grimsbury.
GeTng Around Travel in the Grimsbury area is simple. 
You are assumed to have a bicycle and bus and train 
fare cards. To get someplace fast you need to hire a taxi 
or get access to a car. Magickal travel is also a possibility.
GRiMSBURY LOWER TOWN
The Lower Town sits at river level - under the steep bluffs 
of New Town. This is the site of the oldest habitation in 
the area. It’s made up of claustrophobic alleys and streets 
winding around 2 and 3 story warehouses, row houses 
and older brick buildings. Here is a port area where 
various boats and ships are docked and barges call at the 
rail terminal to offload their goods.
Along the riverwalk there are dozens of houseboats 
lashed up against the wharf pilings where various 
personalities dwell. There are several pubs and cafes along 
the riverwalk where one can grab a meal or a drink. Nice 
for private meetings. Ferries cross the river every 15 
minutes taking passengers and their bicycles to different 
destinations.
Sea birds circle in the sky and roost along the pier posts 
looking for handouts, dropped chips or other 
opportunistic meals. They squawk and call to each other.

GRIMSBURY SETTING: (THERE)
(THERE) is the name for another realm of existence outside our normal reality and physical space, it’s not a fairy land or a 
whimsical “Land of the Fae”. It’s a middle-ground, a nexus, a routing point where many planes of existence come together. 
(THERE) is a place of many possibilities.
(THERE) exists alongside and sometimes overlaps HERE. Areas with more overlap can mirror each other - looking similar 
but not exactly like our world. The close proximity in these areas results in increased bleed-through and “mystickal 
encounters” or other paranormal phenomena. Magickal Gates are the most well known ways to move between HERE, 
(THERE) and other REALMS. From (THERE) even stranger REALMS can be accessed with the right knowledge and 
powers.
NOTE: (THERE) is a place that is a broad canvas that allows players and the Counselor to create places, personali
es and 
experiences that exist outside of our normal world and don’t follow the natural order of things. The details in this book are meant 
to sketch out a framework for the place and the kinds of encounters one might have.
You Really Are (THERE)
Crossing into (THERE) physically moves the player into that existence - this is not an “out of body experience” or a “mind 
trip”. If a player goes (THERE) it is like they have disappeared or “vanished without a trace” from HERE. “I saw her walk 
down that alley and into this doorway… and there’s no trace of her!”. If a player dies (THERE) they are a missing person 
case that likely is never solved by anyone HERE.
Nothing Will Sustain You
Being (THERE) extracts a cost; few creatures can be fed or 
nurtured  - you can eat the food but nothing can sustain you. You 
will starve to death if you stay too long. Magick might stay the 
effects but how long can you keep that up? For most creatures 
(THERE) is a place of temporary transition used to move between 
REALMS. For other creatures who don’t require sustenance like 
humans, (THERE) is a place of refuge and a potential home base 
since it is convenient to many REALMS and neatly overlaid with 
Terra HERE. You need to plan for travel and bring sustenance 
especially if you are there for longer than a day or two. Track the 
time and mark it off. Many of the skeletons you see are people 
who were trapped without food and drink.
(THERE) is Unnatural And Has an Unsettling Effect
Everything looks muted and faded. Uncanny visions and 
scenes are everywhere. You need to actively stifle the panic as 
it rises. How am I going to return? Each time you enter 
(THERE) and for each 24 hours you spend, roll a check to see 
if you are becoming Touched. Subtract a die from the check 
for each day after the first 2.  
Touched by THERE  When a character is Touched, they

will seek Gates or access to Gates to return (THERE) in an 
almost involuntary way. There are ways to remove this 
affliction but it is different for every individual.

There are several hundred shapeshifters who can take

The White Lodge
The White Lodge is a universal fellowship for practitioners 
who follow the “right-handed path”. It is concerned with 
preventing the schemes and attacks of agents of the Black 
Lodge and promoting benevolence and peaceable 
existence. The White Lodge exists as a concept across time,

Lodge member Dion Fortune (a nom de plume).
The Black Lodge
The Black Lodge is the mirror group of The White Lodge

Magickal power no matter the cost. During the Second

members are not monsters, thugs or goons. They look and 
act like any person and often participate in many “noble” or 
“charitable” organizations. However their true motivations 
are power, greed and fortune. Their rituals require blood 
and their members hold each other to the highest secrecy. 
Any hint or whisper about the group is punishable by a 
horrible, long death.

PLAYiNG THE GAME
A play session is conducted in-person or using a virtual 
tabletop (VTT) platform such as Roll20.net. In the play 
session there are players who develop and guide their 
characters through a scenario or module, and there is a 
Counselor who helps narrate and direct the play sessions. 
The Counselor knows the details of the adventure session 
and voices the Non-player characters (NPC) and 
personalities encountered and helps to guide and develop 
the narrative.
You don’t need any fancy miniatures, tokens, maps or dice 
to play - you only need a notepad, pencil and standard six 
sided dice referred to as a D6. Ideally you will also have 2 
dice that are different colors - Black and Red - those come 
into play as your character is crossing between Here and 
There. And bring an open mind and willingness to trust 
those you are playing with.
PLAYERS
The people playing the game - you, me, sitting around a 
table or plugged into a VTT like Roll20 with our character 
sheets and dice in front of us.
CHARACTERS
The people IN the game - your alter-egos or personas that 
are adventuring forth in Grimsbury. These characters are 
voiced and directed by the players. Your character doesn’t 
have to be anything like you - the player. Try picking a 
character type that is interesting to you - that you want to 
explore. There are many character types to choose from, 
soldiers, detectives, magick users, scientists, researchers 
and students - you pick the character type you want.
Work with the other people in your game to develop a 
rounded group - if all of you pick the same character type 
you might be strong in one or two areas but weak in many 
other areas. That might hinder your ability to navigate 
and deal with Grimsbury.
And remember to play your character like they have no 
external knowledge of the scenario or the world. The 
player might know “things” but would your character 
know the same “things”. Just because a player knows 
something doesn’t mean the character would operate with 
that knowledge. This can be a tricky line to toe but try to 
avoid meta-gaming.
THE COUNSELLOR
Arguing with the Counsellor is not wise.
If you are The Counselor for your game you have a lot of 
responsibility. You have the hardest role but some say it’s 
the most fun.  The Counsellor describes the world around 
the characters - telling the players what they see, smell 
and hear - providing the right level of detail so the players 
can propel their story forward. They also voice all of the 
“Personalities” or NPCs encountered - directing their 
words and actions around the characters.
The Counsellor interprets the scenario, the application of 
the rules and determines outcomes. Sometimes that’s

Know The Rules
That’s easy - just read this guide and understand the

Be Prepared & Be Prepared to Improvise

different “scenes” your players will encounter and sketch

PLAY MODES
Welcome to Grimsbury has different play modes each 
with its own pacing and ideas for Character development.
One-Shots
 These are play sessions usually completed in 3-6 hours 
and require a lot of “railroading” by the Counselor - 
scenes should be pre-determined and scheduled. The 
Counselor will need to push the players along where 
needed. Characters are pre generated or disposable and 


97
most times there’s no need to worry about Finances, 
Reputation, Character Points, Skill advancement or 
tracking Stress.
Mini-Campaigns
These are extended one-shots or mini-campaigns that 
might last 2-4+ sessions. These require less “railroading” 
and can allow for more investigation, character 
development, exploration, random encounters and 
Grimsbury flavour scenes. The characters can be pre 
generated or from an ongoing campaign. It’s up to the 
Counselor if Finances, Reputation or tracking Stress are 
important. Probably don’t need to worry about Character 
Points or Skill advancement.
Ongoing Campaigns
 In an ongoing campaign players focus on the narrative as 
well character development. There might be a longer 
story arc coupled with sandbox exploration of the 
Grimsbury region and weird random encounters. 
Finances, Skill advancement, Character Points, Stress and 
Reputation will be a much larger part of the gameplay and 
the fun as characters acquire powerful and interesting 
objects and perhaps a multi-dimensional row house 
(THERE). They might become political leaders or 
business tycoons or start a coven made up of PCs and 
NPCs working to strike down a rival. The ongoing story is 
up to those around your table.
Winning
For us players - to play is to Win! But there is no 
“winning” for your characters, there’s only survival to face 
the next adventure. Survival can lead to growth, 
acquisition of items or wealth and gaining new power. 
Example: develop a new skill, acquire a magic object, 
develop a deep friendship or relationship, reveal a path to 
another place, get a unicorn.
Failure means loss, insanity, death or worse. Example 
failures: soul is trapped in another body, soul is trapped in 
a object, banished to a plane of eternal pain, transformed 
into a frog, total amnesia
Setting up the Game - Session 0
During Session 0 the Counselor will work with players to 
understand the kind of adventure they want. Do they 
want a mystery or investigation? Do they want combat? 
Do they want otherworldly encounters? How much Role 
Play do they want while playing?
Go through Character Creation and work on a 
collaborative back-story where characters are rewarded 
for weaving a narrative web of how they might know each 
other.
Discuss Campaign Style - what do players want to get 
out of it? What kind of adventure do they want to have? 
“Crunchy/Survival” with time tracking and penalties for 
lack of sleep and food? Or “Cinematic” with more focus 
on action? Horror? Combat? RPG Focus? Romance? 
What blend of those styles do they like?
Discuss Safety and any topics that are off-limits
Discuss Scheduling - does play proceed only if all players 
are available or do you just need four out of five players 
available to proceed?
Prepare for Session 1 - The Arrival and The Gathering - 
bring characters into the universe, have them meet up 
and start the adventure.
Notes About Safety
Discuss potentially sensitive topics up-front before the 
first play session. Session 0 is usually a good place to 
discuss these topics. Respect for your other players and 
the Counselor is paramount. Even though this is a 
“fantasy game” taunting, bullying or verbal abuse should 
never be allowed at the table. Here are some tools of the 
trade to help ensure player safety:
Lines - these are topics that will NEVER come up. These 
are triggering concepts, topics or images that should 
NEVER be included in the game by the Counselor or 
other players. Things like rape, torture, slavery. Call them 
out and respect those Lines.
Veils - these are topics that can come up but are not 
detailed - the activity takes place “off screen” and is not 
dwelled upon.
X-Card - even when Lines and Veils are agreed upon - 
sometimes a triggering topic that wasn’t previously 
mentioned comes up and makes a player uncomfortable. 
Simply placing an index card marked with an X on the 
table indicates this topic is off limits and play needs to 
move in a different direction.
Cinematic Moments
During play sometimes there is a pivotal moment in the 
story or a specific scene where everything comes together 
- it might involve an amazing timely roll with a major 
level of success. The Counselor and players can call for a 
cinematic moment and describe their actions like a 
Hollywood Director describing a scene.

APPENDiX N - TOUCHSTONES
The world of Grimsbury has many inspirations - any one of which will help you get into the mood of the game. Start with 
BBC, Granada and ITV television shows like Children of the Stones, The Owl Service, The Changes, The Sandbaggers and 
earlier Dr. Who. Movies like The Wickerman, The Blair Witch Project, Midsommer and The Thing. Music from the BBC 
Radiophonic Workshop, the Ghostbox label especially groups like Belbury Poly, The Advisory Circle, Pye Corner 
Audio and the Focus Group. Additional musical inspiration comes from Broadcast, the Soundcarriers, Moon Wiring 
Club and The Espers. Books that inspire this game come from C.S. Lewis’ Narnia series, Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell by 
Susanna Claarke, A Year In The Country by Stephen Prince, The Ancient Science of Geomancy by Nigel Pennick, 
Stonehenge Decoded by Gerald S. Hawkins and A Folk History of England. The Kraken by China Mieville. The Dresden 
Files by Jim Butcher. Magicians by Lev Grossman. Additionally the wonderful Scarfolk ethos by Richard Littler informs 
quite a bit of the oddness one might find in the world of Grimsbury. RPG systems that have been impactful on this 
journey: Cairn, Liminal & Liminal Horror, Call of Cthulhu, Vaesen, Delta Green, The Hateful Place - all of these are 
amazing. The creative Counsellor should feel free to adopt the Grimsbury setting into any of those systems.
