# Silver_Age_Quickstart.pdf

 
 
QUICKSTART 
This is a Mark of the Odd game based on Into the Odd, created by Chris McDowall. It also uses the Liminal Horror 
SRD under a CC-BY-SA 4.0 license. Everything in this document, except for the Silver Age logo and the “Welcome 
to Hattie” information, is also available under a  CC-BY-SA 4.0 license. 
This Quickstart PDF assumes you are using the character starters. 
 
Welcome to Hattie 
It’s 1991 in Hattie, TX, a town of 900 souls, three 
churches, two dried-out oil fields, a juvenile 
detention center, and one pack of werewolves. 
That last bit’s you. 
Named for a legendary madame who the State of 
Texas shut down 135 years ago, Hattie’s halfway 
between Abilene and San Angelo—45 miles from 
anywhere. You’ve got to drive to the city to buy 
anything they don’t sell at The Farm & Town 
Quickstop. 
Hattie’s your home. You know the walkways to 
Porter Ridge, the sideroads to Copper Mountain, the 
2 AM specials that aren’t on the menu at Juanita’s 
Cafe, and the faces of businessmen who drive out 
for an hour or more for whatever goes on in The 
Silver Palace Roadhouse. 
What Makes a Werewolf 
Maybe you were raised by werewolves (or maybe by 
wolves), always knowing who you were. Maybe your 
family forgot their lycanthropic nature or 
suppressed it. 
You might know some old legends and think you 
changed because of a blessing or curse, or because 
you were an unwanted child born on Christmas. No 
one thinks people who get bitten by wolves become 
wolves themselves, but maybe your first change 
came after you did. 
Maybe you first changed when puberty started. 
Maybe it was just after the first time you got high, 
drunk, kissed, or laid. It’s different for all of you. You 
know what happened and when, even if you’ll never 
know exactly why. 
But a werewolf is more than how they came to be. 
Pack werewolves are those that claim a territory, a 
turf. It becomes part of them. They anchor to it and 
protect it, keeping it safe from the predators and 
parasites of the world, being the monster heroes 
their territories might not deserve but desperately 
need. 
Werewolf Forms 
When you shift, you drop what you carry. What 
you’re wearing falls or rips away. If you need to do it

fast, you can shift as an action. The werewolf’s 
forms are: 
●​The human walks on two legs, speaks with 
language and words, and fights with fists and 
feet (d4 damage each) and guns and blades. 
The human form has d6 Essence. 
●​The wolf walks on four legs, speaks with howls 
and barks, and fights with its two foreclaws and 
teeth (d6 damage each). The wolf form has ​
d6 Essence. 
●​The hybrid walks on two legs, speaks in a mix of 
words and howls, and fights like a wolf (d8 
each). 
When normal humans and animals (other than a 
werewolf’s Anchors) see a werewolf shift or in 
hybrid form, they take d6 stress and must make a 
check or flee. This never affects humans tagged as 
“awakened” or “hunter.” 
True Form 
Every werewolf has a True Form, and any of those 
forms may be it. “True” could mean a lot of things, 
not just what the world called you when you were 
born. When you’re in your True Form, add Moonlight 
to your Essence each time you roll. In addition, 
temporarily increase your Trickery by Shadow. 
Stats & Checks 
When you take action despite the risks, say how 
you’re avoiding those risks and roll a check. 
●​Ferocity: Avoiding the risks through physicality, 
brawn, athleticism, presence, intimidation. 
●​Trickery: Avoiding risks through stealth, agility, 
maneuvers, charm, and lies. 
●​Love: Caring wisely and well; understanding 
your territory, pack, and people; keeping your 
head when they’re under threat; managing 
stress. 
When you act and success and failure are both 
possible (and interesting), the Keeper will ask you to 
roll a d20. Don’t roll until the Keeper asks for it. Equal 
to or under the stat? It works. Nope? It goes wrong: 
fail and face the consequences, or, with the Keeper’s 
permission, succeed but face worse. Your Keeper 
will let you know how bad it gets, maybe even give 
you a choice. 
When an NPC acts against you, they roll a d20 and 
go over your more relevant stat. If they do, they 
succeed. If not, things go badly for them. 
If it’s possible to do a thing, 1s always succeed. No 
matter how good the odds were, if failure is possible 
and interesting, 20s always fail. 
Essence 
Will, joy, anger, rage, and hope in defiance of a world 
that insists you must not exist—Essence keeps you 
alert, gets you the jump, keeps you going in the 
midst of a fight. When you enter your true form, add 
Light to Essence. 
Always write your Essence in pencil—it will fluctuate 
during play as they shift, regenerate, and take 
damage and stress. You’ll have a chance to reroll 
Essence each time you Regenerate. When you 
reroll, you never have to take the new number if it is 
lower than your current Essence. 
When you take damage and stress, it comes off 
your Essence before it starts doing real damage. 
Pushing & Hindering with Love 
When a werewolf fails a roll to protect their 
packmates, territory (and its innocent denizens), 
anchors, or anyone else they love, the player may 
re-roll the check against Love instead. If they 
succeed at the second check, they’ve succeeded on 
the check. 
If a werewolf willfully makes a check that actively 
harms or willfully ignores the needs of their territory 
or others they love—if it’s part of a bigger 
strategy—the Keeper may ask them to reroll the 
check against Love instead. If they fail the second 
check, they’ve failed the check. 
Endurance, Skills & NPCs 
Instead of stats and Essence, NPCs, whether 
werewolf, human, or otherwise, have Endurance and 
Skills. When they take damage, it comes from 
Endurance. When they take a risk and defy the 
odds, they roll a d20 equal to or under their 
Endurance. When an NPC’s Endurance drops to 0, 
they’re out of action. 
In addition, NPCs and opponents have stability, 
armor, and, sometimes, skills. Skills add to their rolls 
against another character.

Resources 
Broke. You have little or no money. You can pick up 
bread, bacon, milk, margarin, and maybe some beer 
or frozen dinners. On your own, 2-3 times each 
month, you can afford a trip Jaunita’s for a full meal, 
a trip to The Silver Palace for beer (and, if you want 
it, company), enough gas to drive into Abilene or San 
Angelo for errands and a cheap meal. 
Struggling. You have just enough to get by, but not 
much to spare. You can afford some better food and 
a few treats. You can hit Jaunita’s, The Silver Palace, 
or a trip into a nearby city 1-2 times a week. 
Stable. You have enough to get by and afford some 
small luxuries. You don’t have much to set aside, 
though. You can get to Jaunita’s, The Silver Palace, 
or a neaby city 3-4 times each week. You can afford 
to take a date (or a Broke or Struggling friend) with 
you. 
Comfortable. You have enough to get by, maybe 
even afford some luxuries. You can get out around 
town as much as you like, even take a couple of 
friends with you 2-3 times a week. If you try to push 
your luck with your Resources, routinely living 
beyond your means, your Keeper may restrict or 
reduce them.

PHASES, MOONLIGHT & SHADOW 
A werewolf’s Moonlight and Shadow depend on the phase of the Moon currently in the sky (or that will next rise). 
●​
New Moon: Shadow 5 
●​
Crescent Moon: Moonlight 1/Shadow 4 
●​
Waxing Quarter Moon: Moonlight 3/Shadow 2 
●​
Waning Quarter Moon: Moonlight 2/Shadow 3 
●​
Gibbous Moon: Moonlight 4/Shadow 1 
●​
Full Moon: Moonlight 5 
 
The Moon also affects werewolves in the following ways, whether they can see the Moon or not: 
●​
When a New Moon rises, werewolves erase all Silver in their Burdens. 
●​
When a Waxing Quarter Moon or Full Moon rises, werewolves erase all Exhaustion in their Burdens. 
●​
When a Waning Moon sets, werewolves erase 1 Silver from their Burdens. 
●​
At each Moon’s quarter (New, Waxing, Full, Waning), regain 1d6+Moonlight Love.

BURDENS 
Whether you carry it on your body or your heart, 
what you carry with you is a Burden. Even if you love 
it. Even if it protects you. 
Werewolves can take on 12 Burdens. 
Small items you can wear, like clothing, rings, or 
simple jewelry, aren’t Burdens. Items you carry are. 
Several items that can fit in a pocket or a small 
container may just be one Burden, while items that 
need two hands to carry may be two. 
Wounds, Anchors, Exhaustion, Worries, and Silver 
are also burdens. So are Heartbreaks. 
Worn 
When you carry more Burdens than you should, you 
become Worn. Even if you set those extra burdens 
down, you’re still Worn until you get food, water, and 
a night’s sleep. 
Pick up more physical burdens when you’re Worn? 
You must explain how you’ll carry them. The Keeper 
may turn your Exhaustion into Heartbreaks or 
Wounds if you keep at it for long. 
 
 
Take another Wound or more Exhaustion after 
you’re Worn? You drop. Someone will need to carry 
you. 
Take another Worry or Heartbreak after you’re 
Worn? Choose either Frenzy, Haze, or just drop like it 
was Exhaustion.  
Silver pierces your skin after you are Worn? Make a 
Love check or die.

WHEN THINGS GET TENSE 
Discussions, Confrontations, 
Reactions 
When your Keeper doesn’t know how an NPC or 
creature will react to your werewolves,  they may roll 
2d6: 
●​2-3 Immediately Hostile 
●​4-5 Tense/Wary 
●​6-8 Curious/Suspicious 
●​9-11 Friendly 
●​12 Helpful 
 
Fights 
First Round 
During the first round, each werewolf (and each 
other participant). When a fight starts, each player 
werewolf in the fight rolls a d6. If it is higher than 
their Essence, their foes get the jump on them. If the 
result is equal or lower, they have the jump on their 
foes. When you have the jump on someone, they 
have to commit to an action and tell their opponents 
what it is before they decide what they are going to 
do. You can only change your action if it becomes 
impossible. 
Surprise Round 
If the werewolf was being tricked, stalked, 
ambushed, or hunted by foes they weren’t 
adequately looking out for, there’s a chance the start 
of the fight will completely surprise them. During the 
first round, anyone whose foes get the jump on 
them does not get to act. 
After the First Round 
After the first round, things are simpler: werewolves 
go first then the opponents respond. If something 
happens to shake things up — the opponents' 
reinforcements arrive, the building catches fire, the 
authorities show up — start the first round again. 
 
Each round, each werewolf, person, animal, or other 
entity in the conflict can make a check or attack and 
then move. Make a check when your werewolf 
doesn’t want to risk doing harm — you want to 
restrain someone, pull them out of the fight, or talk 
everyone down. Attack when you’re ready to take 
that risk. 
Attacks 
In Silver Age, there is no rolling to hit. Each round, 
every character targeting a specific foe rolls their 
attack dice to deal harm all at once. The highest 
number rolled is the damage dealt (subtracting 
Armor), +1 for every other die rolled (+2 if the result 
is 6+). The Keeper may allow players to pull any die 
that’s 5+ to be pulled from the pool to do one of the 
following: 
●​
Stop the target from moving until they 
break away from their attackers 
●​
Knock the target down to the ground 
●​
Push the target back up to 5 feet (as long 
as there’s space to move) 
●​
Hinder their next attack 
Any die pulled from the pool does not count for 
damage or for pulling punches. 
Harm, Damage, and Stress 
When a conflict starts, you don’t make checks to try 
and hurt each other. Instead, you and your 
opponents roll your dice to deal harm. Weapons, 
claws, teeth, and the terrifying presence of a hybrid 
werewolf all deal harm of some sort, either damage 
— blows that wear on your body — or pain — fear 
and stress that wears on your soul. 
Harm, no matter the type, reduces your Essence 
first. When that’s gone, you it them out of your 
Attributes. 
●​
Damage reduces your Ferocity (though 
some opponents may target Trickery 
instead), making it harder to succeed at 
checks and easier for others to succeed 
against you. 
6 


 
●​
Stress reduces Love, making it harder to 
care for your family, friends, neighbors, 
territory, and pack wisely and well. 
Weapons 
●​
Human fists and kicks deal d4 damage. 
●​
Most simple weapons (a wolf’s claw, a 
blade, a thrown rock) do d6 damage. 
●​
Larger or more damaging weapons (a 
longsword, a hunting arrow, a hybrid’s claw, 
a large axe) do d8. 
●​
Especially big (chainsaw, sledgehammer, 
car bumper) or powerful (bullet) do d10. 
Impairments & Enhancements 
If you’re in a bad spot before you attack (attacking 
through cover, tangled in restraints, or can’t sense 
your target), your attack is Impaired. You only deal 
d4 damage. 
If you’re in a good spot before you attack (surprise, 
high ground, a barrage or flurry), your attack is 
Enhanced. You deal d12 damage no matter what. 
Multiple Attackers 
If multiple attackers target the same opponent, roll 
all damage dice and keep the highest result. 
Dual Weapons 
If you’re in wolf or Hybrid form, you roll your damage 
dice for both claws and your bite. If you’re in human 
form and attack with two weapons, roll both 
damage dice and keep the highest result. In human 
form, the weapon in your off-hand is Impaired. 
Armor 
When you want to avoid damage, you put Armor 
between yourself and your attacker. Armor can be 
something you wear, the thick skin of the hybrid 
form, or physical materials in the environment you 
keep between yourself and the attack. Reduce any 
damage you take from your Armor. 
Critical Damage & Wounds 
When you take more damage than your Essence, 
you take Critical Damage. Subtract any remaining 
damage from your Ferocity, then check Ferocity. If 
you fail, you have a wound. 
●​
If a hit takes you down to 3 Ferocity or less, 
it’s a Mortal Wound. You need medical 
treatment or regeneration fast, or you will 
die, no matter what your stats say. 
●​
If you lost 5 or more Ferocity, it’s a Major 
Wound. You’ll need medical treatment or 
regeneration. Major wounds badly scar 
normal humans. 
●​
If you lost 3-4 Ferocity, it’s a Minor Wound. 
You can cope with first aid and maybe an 
emergency room trip, but if you don’t 
regenerate or get the right care, it’ll leave a 
lasting scar. 
●​
If you lost 1-2 Ferocity, it’s a Flesh Wound. 
It’ll likely heal on its own in a day or so. 
If your Ferocity drops to 0, your werewolf is either 
dead or will be very soon. 
Damage & Feats 
When everyone who attacks a target rolls a combat 
die, they can choose to do feats instead of damage 
for each die 4 or higher. Feats can include pushing 
someone away, knocking them over a precipice or 
through a breakable wall, grabbing someone, 
pinning someone you’ve grabbed, or scaring the 
fuck out of them. Feats well used can lead to attacks 
being impaired or enhanced. As long as it makes 
sense, you can do it. They can try to undo or resist 
the following turn. 
The highest die you don’t use for Feats is the die you 
use for damage. Want to make a feat without 
dealing damage, say how, and the Keeper will ask for 
a Ferocity or Trickery check instead to see if it 
works. 
Playing Defense 
When you move, you can try to find some cover. 
Cover increases your Armor and sometimes makes 
it impossible to hit you. Getting to the ground during 
a firefight could give you +1 armor from things like 
bullets or hurled rocks. 
When your foes Deal Damage, you can: 
●​
Dodge a Shot. After foes roll damage for 
anything hurled or shot, remove one of 
their dice and make a Trickery check. If you 
fail, either take Exhaustion or the Keeper 
chooses someone or something near you 
to take the damage instead.

●​
Block a Strike. After foes roll hand-to-hand 
damage, remove one of their dice and 
make a Ferocity check. If you fail, take 
Exhaustion. 
●​
Intercept or Draw Fire. After foes roll 
damage against anyone adjacent to you, 
you can draw the fire or intercept 
Moonlight number of dice on yourself. Then 
make a Love check. If you fail, you take 
Exhaustion. 
Wild Attack 
After you roll damage on your attack, you can 
choose to get wild and reckless with your strike. 
Enhance one die you roll this round to deal damage. 
Anyone dealing damage to you this Turn must also 
Enhance one of their attack dice. 
Pulling Punches 
If you don’t want to kill someone, take 1d6 Stress 
and deal Stress to your target instead of damage. If 
multiple werewolves attack the same target, the 
one who rolled the highest result on their attack 
makes the call. 
Retreat 
Need to pull yourself and up to one other person out 
of a doomed situation and know a safe place to go? 
Say where you’re going and how you’re getting away, 
then make a Trickery check to ensure it happens.

STRESS & EXHAUSTION 
Dealing Stress 
When you lash out at someone’s soul or sense of 
reality, Deal Stress. When you deal Stress, subtract 
Stability, then reduce Essence. 
Stability 
For your first session, your werewolf’s Stability is 
equal to your number of Anchors. 
When you want to avoid Stress, you rely on your 
Stability. Most werewolves connect with Anchors 
for Stability, but sometimes it comes from other 
sources. Reduce Stress with Stability. 
Critical Stress & Heartbreaks 
When you take more Stress than you have Essence 
left, take the rest from Love. Then check Love. If you 
fail, you take Heartbreaks. The Keeper may assign 
one or give you a choice. 
●​Worries. Choose an anchor, beloved place, or 
other non-pack friend, acquaintance, or 
connection, and worry for their safety. Worry is 
a Burden until you can see they’re truly safe for 
now. 
●​Vision. You see an ominous or unwelcome 
vision of something that has happened or is just 
about to happen that you could not otherwise 
know of. The Keeper says what you see. Take 
the vision as a Burden until you investigate its 
truth for yourself. While you actively investigate 
it, take +1 Stability. 
●​Communion. Choose a Packmate or Anchor. 
They choose a Worry, Vision, or other 
Heartbreak that can be cleared. Add it to your 
own Burdens and take +1 Stability until you clear 
it for them. 
Avoid Heartbreaks by entering Frenzy or Haze (if you 
aren’t already in them) 
If Love drops to 0, you drop out, leaving territory, 
anchors, and pack behind. 
Curses 
Certain opponents may impose special or specific  
Heartbreaks. The Keeper will tell you if so. 
Exhaustion 
When you do something that says, “Take 
Exhaustion,” write Exhaustion as one of your 
Burdens. 
When deprived of needed sleep, food, or water for 
longer than 24 hours, take Exhaustion. Silver’s Gifts 
also exhaust you, as do all sorts of things that 
happen while you live in the world. Each time you 
take Exhation, it’s a new Burden. You remove one 
Exhaustion after a full day of water, food, and 
comfortable rest without meaningful exertions 
beyond your home.

REGENERATION 
Regenerating 
Critical Damage and wounds need time and care to 
heal. The worse they are, the longer it takes. But 
you’re a werewolf. Werewolves regenerate when 
they shift form, take an action to regenerate during 
a fight, or have a moment to pause and catch their 
breath. 
When you regenerate, choose one: 
●​If you’ve lost Essence since your last 
regeneration, you can reroll Essence. 
●​Recover d6 Ferocity or Trickery Damage. 
●​Remove Minor or Flesh Wound. 
●​Reduce a Mortal Wound to a Major Wound. 
●​Replace a Major Wound with Exhaustion. 
If you roll dice as part of your Regeneration, reduce 
the roll by the Silver in your Burdens (minimum 1). 
 
 
Regaining Love 
During each Moon’s quarter, regain 1d6+Moonlight 
Love. Each time you take a Vow to an anchor or 
denizen of your territory, or  clear a Worry or Vision 
for yourself or packmate, take 1d4+Moonlight.

ANCHORS 
Deep in their souls, werewolves are torn between 
human and wolf, duty and impulse, town and wild. 
They ground themselves by selecting humans, 
canine animals, and sometimes even packmates as 
anchors. 
Each Anchor is a burden. The connection between a 
wolf and their Anchor is as deep as the connection 
between a pack and its territory. Each Anchor 
grounds the werewolf’s soul, giving them +1 to 
Stability (maximum of 2). Anchors live in or regularly 
visit the werewolf’s territory. 
Even if they do not understand that their loved one 
or friend is a werewolf, ordinary human Anchors 
never take Stress from seeing their werewolves 
change or in hybrid form. 
Anchors, Risks & Action 
It’s nearly impossible for a werewolf to bond with 
someone who willfully avoids action. Anchors 
should be active and present in the game. Players 
should avoid taking Anchors that they wouldn’t want 
to see actively taking risks or participating in 
mysteries and scenarios. 
 
 
Anchors & Stress 
When an Anchor is in immediate danger, and you 
cannot go to their aid, take d6 Stress—even if you 
didn’t know about the threat or are unclear about 
what’s causing it. If a werewolf has not spent 
meaningful time with their anchor since the last new 
or full moon, they take d4 Stress each time the 
moon enters a new phase. 
Changing Anchors 
Each New Moon, a werewolf may either replace 
existing Anchors or name a new one if they have 
fewer than two. This usually reflects relationship 
changes—breaking up, falling out, or moving on.  
Heartbreaks 
When an Anchor is lost or abandoned before the 
New Moon, the werewolf takes d12 stress, and a 
new Heartbreak replaces it: Loss.  
Werewolves cannot remove Loss, but during the 
next New Moon, if the werewolf is not Worn and 
takes a full day of rest, they may combine separate 
Losses into a single Burden.

SILVER 
The touch of silver is too intense for pleasure and 
too beautiful for pain. Each time Silver breaks your 
skin, take Silver as a Burden. If the Silver pierced 
your skin after an unexpected attack, choose Frenzy, 
Haze, d6 Stress, or check Love to keep it together. If 
you fail, Frenzy. If you pierce your skin with Silver, 
take Silver and d4 damage—if you want to enter 
Haze or Frenzy intentionally, you can. 
Subtract the Silver in your Burdens from any dice 
you roll when regenerating. 
Silver’s Gifts 
All werewolves can convert Silver to Exhaustion to 
add a d6 to any attack with their fists, claws, feet, or 
bite. 
Each werewolf has at least one more of Silver’s 
Gifts—ways they can use Silver for Power. 
In addition, any werewolf can convert Silver to 
Exhaustion and use five minutes to work any 
divination rituals. 
Augury. Ask the Keeper questions equal to your 
Shadow that they can answer with “yes,” “no,” or 
“uncertain.” The Keeper can choose to give a more 
detailed or specific answer. 
Awareness. Ask the Keeper how many intelligent or 
sentient entities are within 100 feet of you (seen or 
unseen), how many are human, animal, or other, and 
how many have hostile intent toward you, your 
anchors, or your packmates. Some entities may be 
able to hide themselves from you. 
Message. Send a telepathic message with a number 
of simple sentences equal to Shadow to a person or 
creature you know within your territory or to any 
packmate anywhere that you choose. 
Commune. Name an Anchor, packmate, or 
packmate’s Anchor. The Keeper will tell you if they’re 
in your territory, and if they are, say whether they are 
facing (or will soon face) dangers. 
Cleansing Silver 
Remove one Silver from your Burdens when a 
waning moon sets. Remove all Silver from your 
Burdens when a New Moon rises. 
Ecstatic Frenzy 
When a werewolf frenzies: 
●​Shift to Hybrid until Frenzy ends 
●​Moonlight 5 (regardless of Lunar Phase), Armor 
+1, 2d6+5 Essence 
●​Take d8 Stress each turn you do not attack 
●​Must use all dice (2d8 and d12) for each attack 
●​Each attack affects anyone and anything 
adjacent to the frenzying werewolf unless they 
pull punches 
●​Any dice not used for stunts are added together 
as damage 
Check Love to Exit Frenzy on any round you didn’t 
deal damage, but replace all Silver in your Burdens 
with Exhaustion. 
Euphoric Haze 
When a werewolf gains this dazed, euphoric state: 
●​Shift to True Form until Haze ends 
●​Shadow 5 (regardless of Moon Phase), Stability 
+1 
●​Convert all Exhaustion to Silver 
●​Check Ferocity to take any action other than 
using Silver’s Gifts. In Haze, speaking more than 
6 words is an action. 
●​To use Silver’s Gifts during Haze, remove Silver 
without converting it to Exhaustion. 
To exit Haze, take Exhaustion and either wait until 
the next time either the Sun or Moon crosses the 
Horizon, or take d6 Stress and clear it immediately. 
Remove all Silver from your Burdens when you do.
