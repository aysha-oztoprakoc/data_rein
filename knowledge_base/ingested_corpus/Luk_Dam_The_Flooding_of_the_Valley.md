Introduction
 The Setting
Atheles is a harsh world, especially the Eastlands. Acid 
rain and electric storms pock the landscape. Disease and 
sickness curse its inhabitants. Magic destroyed the world in 
the Great War and disappeared soon after. But now magic 
has started to make its presence felt again, along with 
creatures, people, and monsters touched by magic.
Who should play?
This is a SagaBorn Roleplaying Game compatible 
adventure for 3-6 characters of 4th level.
What do I need to play?
This adventure, some friends, the SagaBorn Roleplaying 
Game 1.5 Core Rulebook, and some refreshments and you 
are ready to go. Some dice may help too.
What campaign setting is appropriate?
Though intended to be set in the Dark Return setting, this 
adventure can easily be adapted to fit in any fantasy RPG 
setting, though will fit most readily in a “low fantasy” 
setting or remote region of a world where the inhabitants 
have not had much exposure to magic or non-human NPCs. 
An experienced Gamemaster should have no difficulty 
converting the module for play in another campaign world 
or alternate FRPG system.

The Adventure
StoryGuide Information
Things have not been comfortable in the northern lands 
around Kowal lately. Orlocks have been invading the 
farmlands in the northwest. They have been spotted in large 
scavenging parties roaming the hills and plains of the wilds. 
A group of adventurers sent by the Duke discovered that 
many of the orlocks originate in the Svarin Valley, located 
under the Luk Dam. The valley is littered with caves, and 
the orlocks must have started a hive or three there. 
Adventure Hook:
Read the following to the players:
Duke Zadeki, the ruler of Kowal, has hired you to protect 
the city. The Luk Dam, which holds back a tributary of 
the Weron River, arches over the Svarin Valley. Built long 
ago by dworvs, the dam lessened the flow of the river, 
opening up more land for farming. The Duke’s plan is to 
open the old valves and flood the valley, driving out or 
eliminating the orlocks. You are given a long brass rod 
that functions as the “key to the wheel,” but the Duke’s 
factor* doesn’t know precisely what that means.
The pay is 150 gp for each adventurer.
What Happened:
There are indeed many 
orlock hives in the valley. 
Something amiss with 
one of the hives is causing 
all of them to be overly 
aggressive and warlike. 
They have not only warred 
among themselves but have 
also struck out at nearby 
farms and settlements, 
causing many deaths among 
those who live in the lands 
north of Kowal.
Goal:
The adventurers must travel to the old dworven facility 
and open the valve to flood the valley. At the end of the 
adventure, a group of orlocks come to try and persuade 
them not to destroy all the orlock homes by opening the 
valves. What they decide can lead into other adventures in 
and around Kowal.
*What is a factor?
Factors are people who get 
things done. They are often 
skilled at persuasion and 
politics and are ultimately 
problem solvers. They can 
work for nobles; they can be a 
street fixer; they could work in 
law enforcement or against it. I 
find the use of a factor as a way 
to get my players interested in 
various quests and events in 
the campaign.

The Valve Facility
Read the following to the players:
A narrow overgrown path leads up to the valve access 
marked on your map. Ahead you can see a dark opening 
in the side of the cliff, a stone door crumbled around the 
entrance. A 6’ hallway, carved from the stone, leads into 
the darkness.
1 The Entry Room
Read the following to the players:
The corridor opens into a large room, which looks to 
have been carved from the granite of the mountain itself. 
Halfway across the room, a stone staircase leads up to a 
higher level where a campfire burns dimly. Pillars stand 
on either side of the stairs, the gray stone as pristine as 
the day it was carved, though it is now covered in crude 
graffiti. As you survey the room, you hear a skittering 
sound. Something is up there, causing a chill to run up 
your spine, but so far, nothing but the flickering light can 
be seen.
The goblins will stay hidden, though when anyone enters 
the room, the chief shouts out, “Strak no be nour, lef noos 
row!” If an adventurer can speak goblin, it translates 
(roughly since it is a local dialect), “This is our home; 
leave now!” If they decide to converse, the goblins will 
be very distrustful but will listen. On a DC 20 Persuasion 
check, the goblins will allow the adventurers to continue 
into the dungeon unhindered. They do not know how to 
open the metal doors and will stand, weapons drawn, until 
the intruders move on or leave. Otherwise, the goblins will 
attack.
The staircase in the center of the room leads up to the 
raised area, which is 8’ taller than the entrance section.
Strategy:
The Shaman will use snare and mage fire to hold the 
adventurers back while the others fire down with their 
bows. They will also climb the pillars to get out of melee 
range and fire arrows down from above. If pressed, two 
goblins will attack with swords while the others use ranged 
weapons. They will do their best to use the cover of the 
pillars and height to their advantage.

The Double Doors
Read the following to the players:
At the top of the platform, there are two doors facing each 
other across the raised platform. The large wall between 
has a mural of a giant dragon curled around a red sphere. 
Engravings of dworvs holding bowls of water towards the 
dragon in offering fill the rest of the wall.
The doors are metal and ornately adorned with abstract 
designs. No hinges, knobs or locks are evident.
A DC 20 Knowledge check (a dworv or dweran gets +2 
to this check) will show that the dworvs are giving water 
offerings to Trund, the god of craftsmanship. This is a 
common theme in the dworvs’ large mechanical structures.
While they may believe this is a puzzle, it is merely a latch 
to open the doors. The heroes must push the red sphere into 
the wall to unlock and open the doors. The doors have 65 
HP and an unlock DC of 30. If there are any with dworven 
heritage in the party, give them a few hints that this doesn’t 
align with any riddle or puzzle from the Dworv culture.
When the button is pushed, read the following to the 
players:
You hear a soft click as the sphere slides into the wall. 
The sound of water running down tubes trickles behind 
the wall. Three other soft clicks sound from each metal 
door. Then silence.
The doors have unlocked and are easily opened with a 
slight push. In an hour, the sphere pops back into place, 
and the doors become locked again. The doors are easily 
unlocked from similar spheres on the other side.

 2/3 The Valve Rooms
Read the following to the players for either room:
A short hall leads down to a square room. Pipes run 
everywhere, and though the stone floors may be worn, the 
pipeworks look to be in great shape. A large gear with a 
hand crank is on the far wall, barred in position.
(Room 3 only) The floor is partially flooded here; a slimy 
green water fills the corner.
As the characters start to explore, read the following:
As you are cautiously entering, you hear a noise echo 
down the hallway leading out of this room. 
Clank. Clank. A pause. 
A dim light filters down the hall. 
Clank. Clank. The light is slowly getting brighter.
Give the players a moment to decide what to do. When 
ready, read the following:
A large steel monstrosity moves into the room. Its metal 
body reminds you of a spider combined with a wood 
furnace. Two large empty eyes turn towards you, their 
glow a pale yellow. Six long arching legs clank as they 
clank along the floor. Other arms or tools protrude from 
the bottom of its body, tubes running back and forth.
A small hole between its eyes emits a red light, filling the 
room with a hum. The light scans over you, a thin line of 
focused light stopping on any bit of metal and lingering 
on weapons. The red light goes out, and loud clanks fill 
the room as it advances towards you.
It is a dworven drone, an automaton left to take care of 
the facility. On a DC 18 Awareness check (those with 
dworven heritage gain +4 to this check), they notice that it 
has large dworven runes inscribed along its side. It labels 
it as “maintenance drone 239” in Dworven. A 
DC 
22 Awareness shows smaller text, that are 
some commands it follows, though 
the words have slime and muck 
covering them. Only one command 
word can be read per round. It is 
up to the StoryGuide to decide 
which the adventurers see first.
The drone follows these commands if spoken in the 
Dworven language:
Stop - The drone stops and awaits orders
Clean - The drone leaves the intruders and begins its 
maintenance pattern.
Sleep - The drone sits down and “powers down”
The drone will not accept any other commands, nor will it 
leave the valve facility.

4 The Trapped Door
Read the following to the players:
A large metal door with a beautiful engraving of a 
waterfall and valley looms to the ceiling. There are no 
handles, locks, or hinges present.
The only way to open it is to spin the gear hand cranks in 
the adjacent rooms to unlock it. If both cranks have been 
turned, the door easily pushes open. It will reset and lock 
after the door is closed again.
The crafters left a magical trap on the door to prevent 
anyone who was not part of the workforce from entering. 
As the Dworven Builders Guild 183 has long since 
disbanded, it is a DC 30 Knowledge check to know of the 
common practice of trapping doors against non-Union 
workers. If they make the knowledge check, they will also 
know 
the simple password, in Dworven tongue, “build 
more.” If heroes all fail their knowledge 
checks, it is up to those with trap sense 
to notice it.
Trap Circle of Flame: DC 22 to 
find and disarm, 2d6 damage 
to all within 15’, DC 11 Reflex 
save to take half damage.

The Orlocks
Right before the adventurers turn the wheel, read the 
following:
“Stop! Know that what you are about to do will kill many 
hives and murder many orlocks.” says a tall armored 
figure who walks in the door behind you. Three tall 
figures, their faces stern and angled, stare at you with 
large eyes. Their shiny organic armor flows over their 
body and matches the chitinous weapons they carry.
If the heroes have not encountered any before, these beings 
are orlocks. While they have weapons drawn, they are not 
immediately attacking. They followed the heroes here, 
spying to see what they were doing in their territory.
Goal:
The orlocks (Greik, Felik, Feaka; all Orlock Predators) are 
from a hive that wishes no harm to Kowal or its inhabitants. 
They will argue against the mass murder of all orlocks in 
the valley and offer to help eradicate the offensive colonies. 
They will do their best to show that they have the best 
intentions, though they have no foolproof strategies for 
curbing the attacks from other hives. If the heroes decide to 
open the dam gates, the orlocks will attack.

Two Choices
Opening the Valve
Goal:
It will take an action to insert the key and then three rounds 
to turn the valve to the open position. Each round requires 
a DC 16 Athletics check and is a full-round action. Also, 
anyone who takes damage or becomes grappled while 
attempting to turn the valve must succeed at a Heroic 
Action against their opponent to continue, or they will be 
interrupted. The moment anyone begins to go for the valve, 
the orlocks will gasp, shout “Nooo!” and then attack.
If they open the valve, read the following to the players:
You hear a loud grinding, and the room begins to rumble.  
A moment later, what sounds like a river’s worth of water 
rushing down beyond the wall fills the room.
Helping the Orlocks
Goal:
The orlocks do not know why so many of the tribes have 
become aggressive, but they are truthful in their intent to 
find out and help. If the heroes decide to listen to them, 
they will launch into another adventure, but that is another 
story...
